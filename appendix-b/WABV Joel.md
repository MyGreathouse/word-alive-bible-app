# WABV: Joel

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-RUACH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Joel is the second of the twelve books of the Minor Prophets in the Protestant order that the WABV text follows. It is in Hebrew and is almost entirely poetry. The book has no date, and the WABV text does not decide when it was written. It has no king named in it. The chapter and verse numbering is the English numbering. The Hebrew Bible has four chapters. Hebrew 2:1-27 is English 2:1-27, and Hebrew 3:1-5 is English 2:28-32, so Hebrew chapter 4 (verses 1 to 21) is English 3:1-21. The book falls into three parts in the WABV text: the locusts and the drought (chapter 1), the day of the LORD, the call to return and the promise (chapter 2), and the judgement of the nations and the blessing of Zion (chapter 3). The headings of the parts are not in the Hebrew. The four kinds of locust (1:4; 2:25) are given by their Hebrew names in the footnotes, and the WABV text uses "cutting", "swarming", "hopping" and "destroying" for them, as the footnotes record. The Hebrew text of Joel is generally clear, but a few verses are hard, and the footnotes record the other readings. The names of God are given as in the earlier books: "the LORD" for YHWH, "Lord" for Adonai, and "the Almighty" for Shaddai. The WABV text does not decide whether the locusts are an actual plague, a figure of an army or both, and the footnotes record only the language.*

## Part One: Chapter 1, the locusts and the drought

### Chapter 1

**1** The word of the LORD that came to Joel son of Pethuel.

**2** Hear this, you elders. Give ear, all inhabitants of the land. Has such a thing happened in your days, or in the days of your fathers?

**3** Tell your children about it, and let your children tell their children, and their children the next generation.

**4** What the cutting locust left, the swarming locust has eaten. What the swarming locust left, the hopping locust has eaten. What the hopping locust left, the destroying locust has eaten.

**5** Wake up, you drunkards, and weep. Wail, all you wine drinkers, over the sweet wine, for it is cut off from your mouth.

**6** For a nation has come up against my land, powerful and without number. Its teeth are lion's teeth, and it has the fangs of a lioness.

**7** It has laid waste my vine and splintered my fig tree. It has stripped off its bark and thrown it down. Its branches have turned white.

**8** Lament like a young woman dressed in sackcloth for the husband of her youth.

**9** Grain offering and drink offering are cut off from the house of the LORD. The priests mourn, the ministers of the LORD.

**10** The field is ruined, the ground mourns, for the grain is ruined, the new wine dries up, the oil fails.

**11** Be ashamed, you farmers. Wail, you vinedressers, over the wheat and the barley, for the harvest of the field has perished.

**12** The vine dries up, the fig tree withers. Pomegranate, palm and apple, all the trees of the field are dried up, and gladness dries up from the children of mankind.

**13** Put on sackcloth and mourn, you priests. Wail, you ministers of the altar. Come, spend the night in sackcloth, you ministers of my God, for grain offering and drink offering are withheld from the house of your God.

**14** Consecrate \[H-QADOSH\] a fast. Call a solemn assembly. Gather the elders and all the inhabitants of the land to the house of the LORD your God, and cry out to the LORD.

**15** Alas for the day. For the day of the LORD is near, and as destruction from the Almighty it comes.

**16** Is not the food cut off before our eyes, joy and gladness from the house of our God?

**17** The seeds shrivel under their clods. The storehouses are desolate. The granaries are torn down, for the grain has dried up.

**18** How the animals groan. The herds of cattle are perplexed because there is no pasture for them. Even the flocks of sheep suffer.

**19** To you, LORD, I call. For fire has devoured the pastures of the wilderness, and flame has burned up all the trees of the field.

**20** Even the wild animals pant for you, because the water brooks are dried up, and fire has devoured the pastures of the wilderness.

*Review note: Joel 1 was drafted in WABV wording. It is the heading (1:1), the call to hear and the four locusts (1:2-4), the lament of the drinkers and the farmers over the nation like a lion that has come up (1:5-12), the call to the priests to mourn and to consecrate a fast (1:13-14), and the day of the LORD and the cry over the drought and the fire (1:15-20). The whole chapter is in poetry, and the verses are set in lines. In 1:14 "consecrate" translates qaddeshu and is tagged H-QADOSH. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapter 1. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 1:7 (the sense of the branches), 1:8 (*betulah*, "a young woman"), 1:17 (the words for the seeds and the storehouses) and 1:19-20. The rendering of *betulah* in 1:8 is open to the team's decision. The four locusts of 1:4 (*gazam*, *arbeh*, *yeleq* and *chasil*) are given in the footnote, and it is not certain whether they are four kinds of locust or four stages of one. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Two: Chapter 2, the day of the LORD and the promise

### Chapter 2

**1** Blow the trumpet in Zion. Sound the alarm on my holy \[H-QADOSH\] mountain. Let all the inhabitants of the land tremble, for the day of the LORD is coming. It is near,

**2** a day of darkness and gloom, a day of clouds and thick darkness. Like dawn spread on the mountains, a great and powerful people. Their like has never been before, nor will be again after them, through the years of all generations.

**3** Fire devours before them, and behind them a flame burns. The land is like the garden of Eden before them, but behind them a desolate wilderness, and nothing escapes them.

**4** Their appearance is like the appearance of horses, and like war horses they run.

**5** As with the rumbling of chariots they leap on the tops of the mountains, like the crackling of a flame of fire devouring stubble, like a powerful army drawn up for battle.

**6** Before them peoples are in anguish. All faces grow pale.

**7** Like warriors they charge. Like soldiers they scale the wall. Each goes on his way, and they do not break their ranks.

**8** They do not jostle one another. Each marches in his path. They burst through the weapons and are not halted.

**9** They leap on the city, they run on the wall, they climb up into the houses, they enter through the windows like a thief.

**10** Before them the earth quakes, the heavens tremble. The sun and the moon are darkened, and the stars withdraw their shining.

**11** The LORD utters his voice before his army, for his camp is exceedingly great. Mighty is the one who carries out his word. For the day of the LORD is great and very terrible. Who can endure it?

**12** "Yet even now," declares the LORD, "return \[H-SHUV\] to me with all your heart, with fasting, with weeping and with mourning."

**13** Tear your hearts and not your garments. Return \[H-SHUV\] to the LORD your God, for he is gracious and compassionate, slow to anger, rich in faithful love \[H-HESED\], and he relents \[H-NACHAM\] from sending calamity.

**14** Who knows whether he will turn \[H-SHUV\] and relent \[H-NACHAM\], and leave a blessing behind him, a grain offering and a drink offering for the LORD your God?

**15** Blow the trumpet in Zion. Consecrate \[H-QADOSH\] a fast. Call a solemn assembly.

**16** Gather the people. Consecrate \[H-QADOSH\] the congregation. Assemble the elders. Gather the children, even nursing infants. Let the bridegroom leave his room, and the bride her chamber.

**17** Between the porch and the altar let the priests, the ministers of the LORD, weep and say, "Spare your people, LORD, and do not make your heritage a reproach, a byword among the nations. Why should they say among the peoples, 'Where is their God?'"

**18** Then the LORD became jealous for his land and had compassion on his people.

**19** The LORD answered and said to his people, "See, I am sending you the grain, the new wine and the oil, and you will be satisfied. And I will no more make you a reproach among the nations.

**20** I will remove the northerner far from you, and drive him into a parched and desolate land, his front into the eastern sea, and his rear into the western sea. His stench will rise, and his foul smell will go up, for he has done great things.

**21** Do not fear, land. Be glad and rejoice, for the LORD has done great things.

**22** Do not fear, you animals of the field, for the pastures of the wilderness are green. The tree bears its fruit. The fig tree and the vine give their full yield.

**23** Be glad, children of Zion, and rejoice in the LORD your God, for he has given the early rain for your vindication \[H-TSEDAQAH\], he has poured down for you abundant rain, the early and the latter rain, as before.

**24** The threshing floors will be full of grain, the vats will overflow with new wine and oil.

**25** I will repay you for the years that the swarming locust has eaten, the hopping locust, the destroying locust and the cutting locust, my great army that I sent among you.

**26** You will have plenty to eat and be satisfied, and you will praise the name of the LORD your God, who has dealt wondrously with you. And my people will never again be put to shame.

**27** You will know that I am in the midst of Israel, and that I am the LORD your God and there is no other. And my people will never again be put to shame.

**28** And afterward, I will pour out my Spirit \[H-RUACH\] on all flesh. Your sons and your daughters will prophesy, your old men will dream dreams, and your young men will see visions.

**29** Even on the male and female servants in those days I will pour out my Spirit \[H-RUACH\].

**30** And I will show wonders in the heavens and on the earth, blood and fire and columns of smoke.

**31** The sun will be turned to darkness and the moon to blood, before the great and awesome day of the LORD comes.

**32** And everyone who calls on the name of the LORD will be rescued. For on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there will be those who escape, as the LORD has said, and among the survivors those whom the LORD calls.

*Review note: Joel 2 was drafted in WABV wording. It is the alarm and the army like a day of darkness (2:1-11), the call to return with all the heart (2:12-17), the answer of the LORD and the promise of the grain and the removal of the northerner (2:18-27) and the pouring out of the Spirit and the signs of the day (2:28-32). The numbering is the English numbering: English 2:1-27 is Hebrew 2:1-27, and English 2:28-32 is Hebrew 3:1-5. The whole chapter is in poetry, and the verses are set in lines. In 2:1, 2:15 and 2:16 "holy" and "consecrate" are tagged H-QADOSH. In 2:12, 2:13 and 2:14 forms of shuv ("return", "turn") are tagged H-SHUV. In 2:13 "faithful love" is tagged H-HESED, and in 2:13 and 2:14 "relents" translates nicham and is tagged H-NACHAM. In 2:23 "vindication" translates tsedaqah and is tagged H-TSEDAQAH. In 2:28 and 2:29 "my Spirit" translates ruchi and is tagged H-RUACH, and the capital letter is the draft's choice, as in Ezekiel 36:27 and 39:29 and Isaiah 63:10-11. The words "the LORD has compassion on his people" (2:18, *va-yachmol*) are not tagged. The words of 2:13 ("gracious and compassionate, slow to anger, rich in faithful love") match Exodus 34:6 and the other places where the same phrase is used, and they are given the same wording. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapter 2. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 2:8, 2:20 (the northerner), 2:23 (*moreh li-tsedaqah*, "the early rain for your vindication" or "the teacher for righteousness") and 2:26-27. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The army of 2:1-11 is rendered as written, with no comment on whether it is the locusts, a human army or both. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Three: Chapter 3, the nations and the blessing of Zion

### Chapter 3

**1** For see, in those days and at that time, when I restore \[H-SHUV\] the fortunes of Judah and Jerusalem,

**2** I will gather all the nations and bring them down to the Valley of Jehoshaphat. And I will enter into judgement with them there, on behalf of my people and my heritage Israel, because they have scattered them among the nations and divided my land,

**3** and have cast lots for my people, and traded a boy for a prostitute, and sold a girl for wine and drunk it.

**4** What are you to me, Tyre and Sidon, and all the regions of Philistia? Are you paying me back for something? If you pay me back, I will return \[H-SHUV\] your payment on your own head swiftly and speedily.

**5** For you have taken my silver and my gold, and have carried my precious treasures into your temples.

**6** You have sold the people of Judah and Jerusalem to the Greeks, to remove them far from their own border.

**7** See, I will stir them up from the place to which you have sold them, and I will return \[H-SHUV\] your payment on your own head.

**8** I will sell your sons and your daughters into the hand of the people of Judah, and they will sell them to the Sabeans, a nation far away, for the LORD has spoken.

**9** Proclaim this among the nations: Consecrate \[H-QADOSH\] war. Stir up the warriors. Let all the soldiers draw near and come up.

**10** Beat your ploughshares into swords, and your pruning hooks into spears. Let the weak say, "I am a warrior."

**11** Hurry and come, all you surrounding nations, and gather yourselves there. Bring down your warriors, LORD.

**12** Let the nations rouse themselves and come up to the Valley of Jehoshaphat, for there I will sit to judge all the surrounding nations.

**13** Put in the sickle, for the harvest is ripe. Come, tread, for the winepress is full. The vats overflow, for their evil is great.

**14** Multitudes, multitudes in the valley of decision. For the day of the LORD is near in the valley of decision.

**15** The sun and the moon are darkened, and the stars withdraw their shining.

**16** The LORD roars from Zion and utters his voice from Jerusalem, and the heavens and the earth shake. But the LORD is a refuge for his people, a stronghold for the people of Israel.

**17** So you will know that I am the LORD your God, who lives in Zion, my holy \[H-QADOSH\] mountain. And Jerusalem will be holy \[H-QADOSH\], and strangers will never again pass through it.

**18** And in that day the mountains will drip sweet wine, and the hills will flow with milk, and all the streambeds of Judah will flow with water. And a spring will come out from the house of the LORD and water the Valley of Shittim.

**19** Egypt will become a desolation, and Edom a desolate wilderness, for the violence done to the people of Judah, because they shed innocent blood in their land.

**20** But Judah will be inhabited for ever, and Jerusalem to all generations.

**21** I will avenge their blood that I had not avenged, and the LORD lives in Zion.

*Review note: Joel 3 was drafted in WABV wording. It is the gathering of the nations in the Valley of Jehoshaphat and the charge against Tyre, Sidon and Philistia (3:1-8), the call to war, the harvest and the winepress (3:9-16), and the blessing of Zion and the end of Egypt and Edom (3:17-21). The numbering is the English numbering: English 3:1-21 is Hebrew 4:1-21. The whole chapter is in poetry, and the verses are set in lines. In 3:1, 3:4 and 3:7 forms of shuv ("restore", "return") are tagged H-SHUV. In 3:9 "consecrate" and in 3:17 "holy" are tagged H-QADOSH. In 3:10 the words "beat your ploughshares into swords, and your pruning hooks into spears" are the reverse of Isaiah 2:4 and Micah 4:3, and the words for the tools and the weapons are given the same rendering as in those books. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapter 3, and none in the whole book. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 3:2 (the name of the valley), 3:3, 3:8, 3:14 (*emeq ha-charuts*, "the valley of decision" or "of threshing") and 3:21 (*ve-niqqeti damam lo niqqeti*, "I will avenge their blood that I had not avenged", or "I will declare innocent their blood that I had not declared innocent"). The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the sale of the boy and the girl (3:3), the sale of the sons and daughters of Tyre (3:8) and the shedding of innocent blood (3:19) are rendered as written, with no comment. The call to war (3:9-11) and the winepress (3:13) are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. The book as a whole has no date, and the footnotes do not give one. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Footnotes

- **1:1** "Joel" translates *Yo'el*, "the LORD is God", and "Pethuel" translates *Petu'el*. The book gives no date.
- **1:2** "Elders" translates *zeqenim*.
- **1:4** "The cutting locust", "the swarming locust", "the hopping locust" and "the destroying locust" translate *gazam*, *arbeh*, *yeleq* and *chasil*. They may be four kinds of locust or four stages of one, and the rendering of each is uncertain. See also 2:25.
- **1:5** "Sweet wine" translates *asis*.
- **1:6** "A nation" translates *goy*, and "my land" translates *artsi*. "A lioness" translates *lavi*.
- **1:7** "Splintered" translates *qetsafah*, and "its branches have turned white" translates *hilbinu sarigeha*.
- **1:8** "A young woman" translates *betulah*, and "the husband of her youth" translates *ba'al ne'urehah*. The rendering of *betulah* is open to the team's decision.
- **1:9** "Grain offering" and "drink offering" translate *minchah* and *nesekh*, and "the ministers" translates *meshartei*.
- **1:10** "Is ruined" translates *shudad*, "the new wine" translates *tirosh*, and "fails" translates *umlal*.
- **1:11** "Be ashamed" translates *hovishu*, a word-play with *hovish*, "dries up", in verse 12.
- **1:14** "Consecrate" translates *qaddeshu*, "a fast" translates *tsom*, and "a solemn assembly" translates *atsarah*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **1:15** "Alas for the day" translates *ahah la-yom*, "the day of the LORD" translates *yom YHWH*, and "destruction from the Almighty" translates *ke-shod mi-Shaddai*, a word-play on *shod* and *Shaddai*.
- **1:17** "The seeds shrivel under their clods" translates *avshu perudot tachat megrefoteihem*, in which the words are rare and the sense is uncertain. "Storehouses" and "granaries" translate *otsarot* and *mamegurot*.
- **1:18** "Are perplexed" translates *navokhu*.
- **1:19** "Pastures" translates *ne'ot*, and "flame" translates *lehavah*.
- **1:20** "Pant" translates *ta'arog*.
- **2:1** "The trumpet" translates *shofar*, "sound the alarm" translates *hariu*, and "my holy mountain" translates *har qodshi*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **2:2** "Darkness", "gloom", "clouds" and "thick darkness" translate *choshekh*, *afelah*, *anan* and *arafel*.
- **2:3** "The garden of Eden" translates *gan-Eden*, and "nothing escapes them" translates *ve-gam-peleitah lo-hayetah lo*.
- **2:4** "War horses" translates *parashim*.
- **2:8** "They burst through the weapons" translates *ve-ad ha-shelach yippolu*, and "are not halted" translates *lo yivtsa'u*. The words are rare and hard.
- **2:9** "Like a thief" translates *ke-ganav*.
- **2:11** "Terrible" translates *nora*, also "awesome".
- **2:12** "Return" translates *shuvu*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **2:13** "Gracious and compassionate, slow to anger, rich in faithful love" translates *channun ve-rachum erekh appayim ve-rav-chesed*, as in Exodus 34:6, Psalm 103:8, Nehemiah 9:17 and Jonah 4:2. See entry H-HESED. "He relents" translates *ve-nicham*, and "from sending calamity" translates *al-ha-ra'ah*. See entry H-NACHAM.
- **2:14** "Who knows" translates *mi yode'a*, "he will turn" translates *yashuv*, and "relent" translates *ve-nicham*. See entries H-SHUV and H-NACHAM.
- **2:15** "Consecrate a fast" translates *qaddeshu tsom*, as in 1:14. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **2:16** "Consecrate the congregation" translates *qaddeshu qahal*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **2:17** "The porch" translates *ulam*. "A byword among the nations" translates *limshol-bam goyim*, which may also be read "for the nations to rule over them".
- **2:18** "Became jealous" and "had compassion" translate *va-yeqanne* and *va-yachmol*, which are in the narrative tense, and some read them as future ("the LORD will be jealous"). The second word is not tagged.
- **2:20** "The northerner" translates *ha-tsefoni*, which is hard. "The eastern sea" and "the western sea" are the Dead Sea and the Mediterranean. "He has done great things" translates *hig'dil la'asot*.
- **2:23** "The early rain for your vindication" translates *ha-moreh li-tsedaqah*, in which *moreh* may be "early rain" or "teacher", so that the words may be read "he has given you the teacher for righteousness". See entry H-TSEDAQAH. "The latter rain" translates *malqosh*.
- **2:25** "I will repay you" translates *ve-shillamti lakhem*. The four locusts are as in 1:4, in a different order.
- **2:26** "Dealt wondrously" translates *hifli la'asot*.
- **2:27** "There is no other" translates *ve-ein od*. The clause "my people will never again be put to shame" is repeated in verses 26 and 27 in the Hebrew.
- **2:28** The verses 28 to 32 are Hebrew 3:1-5. "I will pour out" translates *eshpokh*, "my Spirit" translates *ruchi*, and "all flesh" translates *kol-basar*. See entry H-RUACH. The capital letter is the draft's choice, and the team should decide it with Isaiah 63:10-11. See Acts 2:17-21.
- **2:30** "Wonders" translates *mofetim*, and "columns of smoke" translates *timrot ashan*.
- **2:31** "The great and awesome day of the LORD" translates *yom YHWH ha-gadol ve-ha-nora*.
- **2:32** "Calls on the name of the LORD" translates *yiqra be-shem YHWH*, "will be rescued" translates *yimmalet*, and "those who escape" translates *pletah*. See Romans 10:13.
- **3:1** The verses 1 to 21 are Hebrew 4:1-21. "When I restore the fortunes" translates *asher ashiv et-shevut*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **3:2** "The Valley of Jehoshaphat" translates *emeq Yehoshafat*, "the LORD judges", and no valley of this name is known. "I will enter into judgement" translates *nishpatti*, "my heritage" translates *nachalati*, and "divided my land" translates *chillequ*.
- **3:3** "Have cast lots" translates *yadu goral*, "traded a boy for a prostitute" translates *va-yitnu ha-yeled ba-zonah*, and "sold a girl for wine" translates *ha-yaldah makhru ba-yayin*. It is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **3:4** "The regions of Philistia" translates *gelilot Peleshet*, and "are you paying me back" translates *ha-meshallemim attem alai gemul*. "I will return your payment" translates *ashiv gemulkhem*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **3:5** "My precious treasures" translates *machamaddai*, and "your temples" translates *heikhaleikhem*.
- **3:6** "The Greeks" translates *benei ha-Yevanim*, literally "the sons of the Yevanim" (Ionians).
- **3:7** "I will return your payment" translates *ve-heshivoti gemulkhem*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **3:8** "The Sabeans" translates *Sheva'im*.
- **3:9** "Consecrate war" translates *qaddeshu milchamah*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **3:10** "Ploughshares" translates *ittekhem*, and "pruning hooks" translates *mazmerotekhem*. The words are the reverse of Isaiah 2:4 and Micah 4:3.
- **3:11** "Hurry" translates *ushu*, which is rare, and "bring down" translates *hanchat*, which is hard.
- **3:13** "The sickle" translates *maggal*, "the winepress" translates *gat*, and "the vats" translates *yeqavim*. See also Revelation 14:14-20.
- **3:14** "The valley of decision" translates *emeq ha-charuts*, in which *charuts* may mean "decision" or "threshing sledge" (see Isaiah 28:27).
- **3:16** "A refuge" translates *machseh*, and "a stronghold" translates *ma'oz*.
- **3:17** "My holy mountain" translates *har qodshi*, and "holy" translates *qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH. "Strangers" translates *zarim*.
- **3:18** "Sweet wine" translates *asis*, as in 1:5. "The Valley of Shittim" translates *nachal ha-Shittim*, "the valley of the acacias", east of the Jordan.
- **3:19** "Innocent blood" translates *dam naqi*, and the verse is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **3:21** "I will avenge their blood that I had not avenged" translates *ve-niqqeti damam lo niqqeti*, in which *niqqah* means "to treat as innocent" or "to avenge". The words may also be read "I will declare innocent their blood that I had not declared innocent", and the verse is hard.
