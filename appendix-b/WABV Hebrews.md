# WABV: Hebrews

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[G-DIATHEKE\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Hebrews is the nineteenth book of the New Testament in the order that the WABV text follows, and the first of the writings that follow the letters of Paul. It is written in Greek, and the WABV text is drafted from a critical Greek text, with the Byzantine tradition noted in the footnotes where it differs, as the Charter requires. It does not name its writer, and it has no greeting at the start, and it ends like a letter (13:22-25), and it calls itself "a word of exhortation" (13:22), and it is best read as a sermon that was sent in writing. Its Greek is among the most polished in the New Testament, and it quotes the Greek Old Testament (the Septuagint) throughout, and where the Greek differs from the Hebrew the WABV text follows the Greek that the writer quotes and the footnote records the matter. Who wrote it, who the first readers were and where they lived (the title "to the Hebrews" is old but is not in the text) are debated, and the WABV text does not decide. Its themes are the Son who is greater than the angels, than Moses and than the priests of Aaron, the high priest according to the order of Melchizedek, the new covenant and the one sacrifice, faith, and the call to hold on, with four warnings (2:1-4, 3:7-4:13, 6:4-8, 10:26-31) that are among the hardest passages in the New Testament and that are rendered as written. The chapter and verse numbering is the usual numbering. The WABV text of Hebrews is drafted with the voice standard (Part 46 of the review package), and it sets the poetry and the long quotations of the Old Testament in lines where the standard requires it. Where the text of a critical edition is not certain, the draft says so in the footnote and asks the team to confirm the reading. It makes a number of fixed choices that follow from its Greek, and they are written down here so that they are used throughout. *Diatheke* is rendered "covenant" and is tagged G-DIATHEKE, and the footnote records that at 9:16-17 it may mean a will. *Hiereus* is rendered "priest" and *archiereus* "high priest". *Teleioo* and its family are rendered "bring to completion", "complete" and "perfect", and the footnote records the choice in each place. *Hagiazo* is rendered "make holy" and *hagios* "holy", and are tagged G-HAGIOS, and "sanctify" is not used. *Hilaskomai* (2:17) and *hilasterion* (9:5) are rendered "make atonement" and "place of atonement", as at Romans 3:25. *Pistis* is rendered "faith" and *pisteuo* "believe", and both are tagged G-PISTIS, and *apistia* "unbelief". *Eleos* is rendered "mercy" and is tagged G-ELEOS. *Charis* is rendered "grace" and is tagged G-CHARIS. *Doxa* is rendered "glory" and is tagged G-DOXA. *Sozo* and *soteria* are rendered "save" and "salvation" and are tagged G-SOZO. *Pneuma* is rendered "Spirit" or "spirit" and is tagged G-PNEUMA. *Psyche* is rendered "soul" and is tagged G-PSYCHE. *Euangelion* and *euangelizo* are rendered "good news" and "bring good news" and are tagged G-EUANGELION. *Ekklesia* is rendered "assembly" and is tagged G-EKKLESIA, and the team should decide between "assembly" and "church". *Basileia* is rendered "kingdom" and is tagged G-BASILEIA. *Dikaiosune* is rendered "righteousness" and is tagged G-DIKAIOSUNE. *Agape* is rendered "love" and is tagged G-AGAPE. *Zoe* is rendered "life" and is tagged G-ZOE. *Metanoia* is rendered "repentance" and is tagged G-METANOIA. *Nomos* is rendered "law" and is tagged G-NOMOS. *Lutrosis* (9:12) is rendered "redemption" and is tagged G-LYTRON. *Aion* is rendered "age". *Adelphoi* is rendered "brothers", and the footnote records that in many places it may include the sisters, and the team should decide. *Christos* is rendered "Christ" and is tagged G-CHRISTOS. *Idou* is rendered "look", as in the Gospels. The headings of the parts are not in the Greek. The markers beginning with G- (for Greek words) are defined in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. The wording of the WABV text is drafted from the Greek and is not taken from any other English translation.*

## Part One: Chapters 1 to 4, the Son, the great salvation, Moses and the rest of God

### Chapter 1

**1** Long ago, at many times and in many ways, God spoke to our fathers by the prophets,

**2** but in these last days he has spoken to us by a Son, whom he appointed the heir of all things, through whom also he created the ages.

**3** He is the radiance of the glory \[G-DOXA\] of God and the exact imprint of his nature, and he upholds the universe by the word of his power. After making purification for sins, he sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high,

**4** having become as much superior to angels as the name he has inherited is more excellent than theirs.

**5** For to which of the angels did God ever say, "You are my Son, today I have become your Father"? Or again, "I will be to him a Father, and he shall be to me a Son"?

**6** And again, when he brings the firstborn into the world, he says, "Let all God's angels worship him."

**7** Of the angels he says, "He makes his angels winds, and his ministers a flame of fire."

**8** But of the Son he says, "Your throne, O God, is for ever and ever, and the sceptre of righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\] is the sceptre of your kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\].

**9** You have loved \[G-AGAPE\] righteousness and hated lawlessness. Therefore God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness beyond your companions."

**10** And, "You, Lord, laid the foundation of the earth in the beginning, and the heavens are the work of your hands.

**11** They will perish, but you remain. They will all wear out like a garment,

**12** like a robe you will roll them up, like a garment they will be changed. But you are the same, and your years will have no end."

**13** And to which of the angels has he ever said, "Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet"?

**14** Are they not all ministering spirits \[G-PNEUMA\] sent out to serve for the sake of those who are to inherit salvation \[G-SOZO\]?

### Chapter 2

**1** Therefore we must pay much closer attention to what we have heard, lest we drift away from it.

**2** For if the message declared by angels proved to be reliable, and every transgression or disobedience received a just retribution,

**3** how shall we escape if we neglect such a great salvation \[G-SOZO\]? It was declared at first by the Lord, and it was attested to us by those who heard,

**4** while God also bore witness by signs and wonders and various miracles and by gifts of the Holy \[G-HAGIOS\] Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] distributed according to his will.

**5** For it was not to angels that God subjected the world to come, of which we are speaking.

**6** It has been testified somewhere, "What is a human being, that you are mindful of him, or a son of man, that you care for him?

**7** You made him for a little while lower than the angels. You have crowned him with glory \[G-DOXA\] and honour,

**8** putting everything in subjection under his feet." Now in putting everything in subjection to him, he left nothing outside his control. At present, we do not yet see everything in subjection to him.

**9** But we see him who for a little while was made lower than the angels, namely Jesus, crowned with glory and honour because of the suffering of death, so that by the grace \[G-CHARIS\] of God he might taste death for everyone.

**10** For it was fitting that he, for whom and by whom all things exist, in bringing many sons to glory, should bring the founder of their salvation \[G-SOZO\] to completion through suffering.

**11** For he who makes holy \[G-HAGIOS\] and those who are made holy all have one source. That is why he is not ashamed to call them brothers,

**12** saying, "I will tell of your name to my brothers. In the midst of the assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\] I will sing your praise."

**13** And again, "I will put my trust in him." And again, "Look, I and the children God has given me."

**14** Since therefore the children share in flesh and blood, he himself likewise partook of the same things, that through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death, that is, the devil,

**15** and deliver all those who through fear of death were subject to lifelong slavery.

**16** For surely it is not angels that he helps, but he helps the offspring of Abraham.

**17** Therefore he had to be made like his brothers in every respect, so that he might become a merciful \[G-ELEOS\] and faithful \[G-PISTIS\] high priest in the service of God, to make atonement for the sins of the people.

**18** For because he himself has suffered when tempted, he is able to help those who are being tempted.

### Chapter 3

**1** Therefore, holy \[G-HAGIOS\] brothers, who share in a heavenly calling, consider Jesus, the apostle and high priest of our confession,

**2** who was faithful \[G-PISTIS\] to him who appointed him, just as Moses also was faithful in all God's house.

**3** For Jesus has been counted worthy of more glory \[G-DOXA\] than Moses, as the builder of a house has more honour than the house itself.

**4** (For every house is built by someone, but the builder of all things is God.)

**5** Now Moses was faithful in all God's house as a servant, to testify to the things that were to be spoken later,

**6** but Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] is faithful over God's house as a son. And we are his house, if indeed we hold fast our confidence and our boasting in our hope.

**7** Therefore, as the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] says, "Today, if you hear his voice,

**8** do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion, on the day of testing in the wilderness,

**9** where your fathers put me to the test and saw my works for forty years.

**10** Therefore I was provoked with that generation, and said, 'They always go astray in their hearts. They have not known my ways.'

**11** As I swore in my wrath, 'They shall not enter my rest.'"

**12** Take care, brothers, lest there be in any of you an evil, unbelieving \[G-PISTIS\] heart, leading you to fall away from the living God.

**13** But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called "today," that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin.

**14** For we have come to share in Christ, if indeed we hold our original confidence firm to the end.

**15** As it is said, "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion."

**16** For who were those who heard and yet rebelled? Was it not all those who left Egypt led by Moses?

**17** And with whom was he provoked for forty years? Was it not with those who sinned, whose bodies fell in the wilderness?

**18** And to whom did he swear that they would not enter his rest, but to those who were disobedient?

**19** So we see that they were unable to enter because of unbelief \[G-PISTIS\].

### Chapter 4

**1** Therefore, while the promise of entering his rest still stands, let us fear lest any of you should seem to have failed to reach it.

**2** For good news \[G-EUANGELION\] came to us just as to them, but the message they heard did not benefit them, because they were not united by faith \[G-PISTIS\] with those who listened.

**3** For we who have believed \[G-PISTIS\] enter that rest, as he has said, "As I swore in my wrath, 'They shall not enter my rest,'" although his works were finished from the foundation of the world.

**4** For he has somewhere spoken of the seventh day in this way: "And God rested on the seventh day from all his works."

**5** And again in this passage he said, "They shall not enter my rest."

**6** Since therefore it remains for some to enter it, and those who formerly received the good news \[G-EUANGELION\] failed to enter because of disobedience,

**7** again he appoints a certain day, "Today," saying through David so long afterward, in the words already quoted, "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts."

**8** For if Joshua had given them rest, God would not have spoken of another day later on.

**9** So then, there remains a sabbath rest for the people of God,

**10** for whoever has entered God's rest has also rested from his works as God did from his.

**11** Let us therefore strive to enter that rest, so that no one may fall by the same sort of disobedience.

**12** For the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing to the division of soul \[G-PSYCHE\] and spirit \[G-PNEUMA\], of joints and marrow, and discerning the thoughts and intentions of the heart.

**13** And no creature is hidden from his sight, but all are naked and exposed to the eyes of him to whom we must give account.

**14** Since then we have a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus, the Son of God, let us hold fast our confession.

**15** For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathise with our weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin.

**16** Let us then with confidence draw near to the throne of grace \[G-CHARIS\], that we may receive mercy \[G-ELEOS\] and find grace to help in time of need.

## Part Two: Chapters 5 to 7, the high priest, the warning and Melchizedek

### Chapter 5

**1** For every high priest chosen from among people is appointed to act on behalf of people in relation to God, to offer gifts and sacrifices for sins.

**2** He can deal gently with the ignorant and wayward, since he himself is beset with weakness.

**3** Because of this he is obligated to offer sacrifice for his own sins just as for those of the people.

**4** And no one takes this honour for himself, but he is called by God, just as Aaron was.

**5** So also Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] did not exalt himself to be made a high priest, but was appointed by him who said to him, "You are my Son, today I have become your Father";

**6** as he says also in another place, "You are a priest for ever, after the order of Melchizedek."

**7** In the days of his flesh, Jesus offered up prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears, to him who was able to save \[G-SOZO\] him from death, and he was heard because of his reverence.

**8** Although he was a Son, he learned obedience through what he suffered.

**9** And being brought to completion, he became the source of eternal salvation \[G-SOZO\] to all who obey him,

**10** being designated by God a high priest after the order of Melchizedek.

**11** About this we have much to say, and it is hard to explain, since you have become dull of hearing.

**12** For though by this time you ought to be teachers, you need someone to teach you again the basic principles of the oracles of God. You need milk, not solid food,

**13** for everyone who lives on milk is unskilled in the word of righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\], since he is a child.

**14** But solid food is for the mature, for those who have their powers of discernment trained by constant practice to distinguish good from evil.

### Chapter 6

**1** Therefore let us leave the elementary doctrine of Christ and go on toward completion, not laying again a foundation of repentance \[G-METANOIA\] from dead works and of faith \[G-PISTIS\] toward God,

**2** and of instruction about washings, laying on of hands, resurrection of the dead, and eternal judgement.

**3** And this we will do if God permits.

**4** For it is impossible to restore again to repentance \[G-METANOIA\] those who have once been enlightened, who have tasted the heavenly gift, and have shared in the Holy \[G-HAGIOS\] Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\],

**5** and have tasted the goodness of the word of God and the powers of the age to come,

**6** and then have fallen away, since they are crucifying again for themselves the Son of God and holding him up to contempt.

**7** For land that has drunk the rain that often falls on it, and produces a crop useful to those for whose sake it is cultivated, receives a blessing from God.

**8** But if it bears thorns and thistles, it is worthless and near to being cursed, and its end is to be burned.

**9** Yet in your case, beloved \[G-AGAPE\], we feel sure of better things, things that belong to salvation \[G-SOZO\], though we speak in this way.

**10** For God is not unjust so as to overlook your work and the love \[G-AGAPE\] that you have shown for his name in serving the holy ones \[G-HAGIOS\], as you still do.

**11** And we desire each one of you to show the same earnestness to have the full assurance of hope until the end,

**12** so that you may not be sluggish, but imitators of those who through faith \[G-PISTIS\] and patience inherit the promises.

**13** For when God made a promise to Abraham, since he had no one greater by whom to swear, he swore by himself,

**14** saying, "Surely I will bless you and multiply you."

**15** And thus Abraham, having patiently waited, obtained the promise.

**16** For people swear by something greater than themselves, and in all their disputes an oath is final for confirmation.

**17** So when God desired to show more convincingly to the heirs of the promise the unchangeable character of his purpose, he guaranteed it with an oath,

**18** so that by two unchangeable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we who have fled for refuge might have strong encouragement to hold fast to the hope set before us.

**19** We have this as a sure and steadfast anchor of the soul \[G-PSYCHE\], a hope that enters into the inner place behind the curtain,

**20** where Jesus has gone as a forerunner on our behalf, having become a high priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek.

### Chapter 7

**1** For this Melchizedek, king of Salem, priest of the Most High God, met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him,

**2** and to him Abraham apportioned a tenth part of everything. He is first, by translation of his name, king of righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\], and then he is also king of Salem, that is, king of peace \[G-EIRENE\].

**3** He is without father or mother or genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life \[G-ZOE\], but resembling the Son of God he continues a priest for ever.

**4** Consider how great this man was to whom even Abraham the patriarch gave a tenth of the spoils.

**5** And those descendants of Levi who receive the priestly office have a commandment in the law \[G-NOMOS\] to take tithes from the people, that is, from their brothers, though these also are descended from Abraham.

**6** But this man who does not have his descent from them received tithes from Abraham and blessed him who had the promises.

**7** It is beyond dispute that the inferior is blessed by the superior.

**8** In the one case tithes are received by mortal men, but in the other by one of whom it is testified that he lives.

**9** One might even say that Levi himself, who receives tithes, paid tithes through Abraham,

**10** for he was still in the loins of his ancestor when Melchizedek met him.

**11** Now if completion had been attainable through the Levitical priesthood (for under it the people received the law \[G-NOMOS\]), what further need would there have been for another priest to arise after the order of Melchizedek, rather than one named after the order of Aaron?

**12** For when there is a change in the priesthood, there is necessarily a change in the law as well.

**13** For the one of whom these things are spoken belonged to another tribe, from which no one has ever served at the altar.

**14** For it is evident that our Lord was descended from Judah, and in connection with that tribe Moses said nothing about priests.

**15** This becomes even more evident when another priest arises in the likeness of Melchizedek,

**16** who has become a priest, not on the basis of a legal requirement concerning bodily descent, but by the power of an indestructible life \[G-ZOE\].

**17** For it is witnessed of him, "You are a priest for ever, after the order of Melchizedek."

**18** For on the one hand, a former commandment is set aside because of its weakness and uselessness

**19** (for the law made nothing complete), but on the other hand, a better hope is introduced, through which we draw near to God.

**20** And it was not without an oath.

**21** For those who formerly became priests were made such without an oath, but this one was made a priest with an oath by the one who said to him, "The Lord has sworn and will not change his mind, 'You are a priest for ever.'"

**22** This makes Jesus the guarantor of a better covenant \[G-DIATHEKE\].

**23** The former priests were many in number, because they were prevented by death from continuing in office,

**24** but he holds his priesthood permanently, because he continues for ever.

**25** Consequently, he is able to save \[G-SOZO\] to the uttermost those who draw near to God through him, since he always lives to make intercession for them.

**26** For it was fitting that we should have such a high priest, holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners, and exalted above the heavens.

**27** He has no need, like those high priests, to offer sacrifices daily, first for his own sins and then for those of the people, since he did this once for all when he offered up himself.

**28** For the law appoints men in their weakness as high priests, but the word of the oath, which came later than the law, appoints a Son who has been brought to completion for ever.

## Part Three: Chapters 8 to 10, the new covenant, the heavenly tent and the one sacrifice

**3** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] we understand that the universe was created by the word of God, so that what is seen was not made out of things that are visible.

**4** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] Abel offered to God a more acceptable sacrifice than Cain, through which he was commended as righteous \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\], God commending him by accepting his gifts. And through his faith, though he died, he still speaks.

**5** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] Enoch was taken up so that he should not see death, and "he was not found, because God had taken him." Now before he was taken he was commended as having pleased God.

**6** And without faith \[G-PISTIS\] it is impossible to please him, for whoever would draw near to God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him.

**7** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] Noah, being warned by God concerning events as yet unseen, in reverent fear constructed an ark for the saving of his household. By this he condemned the world and became an heir of the righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\] that comes by faith.

**8** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] Abraham obeyed when he was called to go out to a place that he was to receive as an inheritance. And he went out, not knowing where he was going.

**9** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] he went to live in the land of promise, as in a foreign land, living in tents with Isaac and Jacob, heirs with him of the same promise.

**10** For he was looking forward to the city that has foundations, whose designer and builder is God.

**11** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] Sarah herself received power to conceive, even when she was past the age, since she considered him faithful who had promised.

**12** Therefore from one man, and him as good as dead, were born descendants as many as the stars of heaven and as many as the innumerable grains of sand by the seashore.

**13** These all died in faith \[G-PISTIS\], not having received the things promised, but having seen them and greeted them from afar, and having acknowledged that they were strangers and exiles on the earth.

**14** For people who speak thus make it clear that they are seeking a homeland.

**15** If they had been thinking of that land from which they had gone out, they would have had opportunity to return.

**16** But as it is, they desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed to be called their God, for he has prepared for them a city.

**17** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] Abraham, when he was tested, offered up Isaac, and he who had received the promises was in the act of offering up his only son,

**18** of whom it was said, "Through Isaac shall your offspring be named."

**19** He considered that God was able even to raise him from the dead, from which, figuratively speaking, he did receive him back.

**20** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] Isaac invoked future blessings on Jacob and Esau.

**21** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] Jacob, when dying, blessed each of the sons of Joseph, bowing in worship over the head of his staff.

**22** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] Joseph, at the end of his life, made mention of the exodus of the Israelites and gave directions concerning his bones.

**23** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] Moses, when he was born, was hidden for three months by his parents, because they saw that the child was beautiful, and they were not afraid of the king's edict.

**24** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] Moses, when he was grown up, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter,

**25** choosing rather to be mistreated with the people of God than to enjoy the fleeting pleasures of sin.

**26** He considered the reproach of Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt, for he was looking to the reward.

**27** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] he left Egypt, not being afraid of the anger of the king, for he endured as seeing him who is invisible.

**28** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] he kept the Passover and sprinkled the blood, so that the Destroyer of the firstborn might not touch them.

**29** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] the people crossed the Red Sea as on dry land, but the Egyptians, when they attempted to do the same, were drowned.

**30** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] the walls of Jericho fell down after they had been encircled for seven days.

**31** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] Rahab the prostitute did not perish with those who were disobedient, because she had given a friendly welcome to the spies.

**32** And what more shall I say? For time would fail me to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, of David and Samuel and the prophets

**33** who through faith \[G-PISTIS\] conquered kingdoms, enforced justice, obtained promises, stopped the mouths of lions,

**34** quenched the power of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, were made strong out of weakness, became mighty in war, put foreign armies to flight.

**35** Women received back their dead by resurrection. Some were tortured, refusing to accept release, so that they might rise again to a better life \[G-ZOE\].

**36** Others suffered mocking and flogging, and even chains and imprisonment.

**37** They were stoned, they were sawn in two, they were killed with the sword. They went about in skins of sheep and goats, destitute, afflicted, mistreated

**38** (of whom the world was not worthy), wandering about in deserts and mountains, and in dens and caves of the earth.

**39** And all these, though commended through their faith \[G-PISTIS\], did not receive what was promised,

**40** since God had provided something better for us, that apart from us they should not be made complete.

### Chapter 12

**1** Therefore, since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us also lay aside every weight, and sin which clings so closely, and let us run with endurance the race that is set before us,

**2** looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith \[G-PISTIS\], who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is seated at the right hand of the throne of God.

**3** Consider him who endured from sinners such hostility against himself, so that you may not grow weary or fainthearted.

**4** In your struggle against sin you have not yet resisted to the point of shedding your blood.

**5** And have you forgotten the exhortation that addresses you as sons? "My son, do not regard lightly the discipline of the Lord, nor be weary when reproved by him.

**6** For the Lord disciplines the one he loves \[G-AGAPE\], and chastises every son whom he receives."

**7** It is for discipline that you have to endure. God is treating you as sons. For what son is there whom his father does not discipline?

**8** If you are left without discipline, in which all have participated, then you are illegitimate children and not sons.

**9** Besides this, we have had earthly fathers who disciplined us and we respected them. Shall we not much more be subject to the Father of spirits \[G-PNEUMA\] and live?

**10** For they disciplined us for a short time as it seemed best to them, but he disciplines us for our good, that we may share his holiness \[G-HAGIOS\].

**11** For the moment all discipline seems painful rather than pleasant, but later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\] to those who have been trained by it.

**12** Therefore lift your drooping hands and strengthen your weak knees,

**13** and make straight paths for your feet, so that what is lame may not be put out of joint but rather be healed.

**14** Strive for peace \[G-EIRENE\] with everyone, and for the holiness \[G-HAGIOS\] without which no one will see the Lord.

**15** See to it that no one fails to obtain the grace \[G-CHARIS\] of God, that no "root of bitterness" springs up and causes trouble, and by it many become defiled;

**16** that no one is sexually immoral or unholy like Esau, who sold his birthright for a single meal.

**17** For you know that afterward, when he desired to inherit the blessing, he was rejected, for he found no chance to repent \[G-METANOIA\], though he sought it with tears.

**18** For you have not come to what may be touched, a blazing fire and darkness and gloom and a tempest

**19** and the sound of a trumpet and a voice whose words made the hearers beg that no further messages be spoken to them.

**20** For they could not endure the order that was given, "If even a beast touches the mountain, it shall be stoned."

**21** Indeed, so terrifying was the sight that Moses said, "I tremble with fear."

**22** But you have come to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to innumerable angels in festal gathering,

**23** and to the assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\] of the firstborn who are enrolled in heaven, and to God, the judge of all, and to the spirits \[G-PNEUMA\] of the righteous made complete,

**24** and to Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant \[G-DIATHEKE\], and to the sprinkled blood that speaks a better word than the blood of Abel.

**25** See that you do not refuse him who is speaking. For if they did not escape when they refused him who warned them on earth, much less will we escape if we reject him who warns from heaven.

**26** At that time his voice shook the earth, but now he has promised, "Yet once more I will shake not only the earth but also the heavens."

**27** This phrase, "Yet once more," indicates the removal of things that are shaken, that is, things that have been made, in order that the things that cannot be shaken may remain.

**28** Therefore let us be grateful for receiving a kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] that cannot be shaken, and thus let us offer to God acceptable worship, with reverence and awe,

**29** for our God is a consuming fire.

### Chapter 13

**1** Let brotherly love continue.

**2** Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for thereby some have entertained angels without knowing it.

**3** Remember those who are in prison, as though in prison with them, and those who are mistreated, since you also are in the body.

**4** Let marriage be held in honour among all, and let the marriage bed be undefiled, for God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterous.

**5** Keep your life free from love of money, and be content with what you have, for he has said, "I will never leave you nor forsake you."

**6** So we can confidently say, "The Lord is my helper. I will not fear. What can people do to me?"

**7** Remember your leaders, those who spoke to you the word of God. Consider the outcome of their way of life, and imitate their faith \[G-PISTIS\].

**8** Jesus Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] is the same yesterday and today and for ever.

**9** Do not be led away by diverse and strange teachings, for it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace \[G-CHARIS\], not by foods, which have not benefited those devoted to them.

**10** We have an altar from which those who serve the tent have no right to eat.

**11** For the bodies of those animals whose blood is brought into the holy places by the high priest as a sacrifice for sin are burned outside the camp.

**12** So Jesus also suffered outside the gate in order to make the people holy \[G-HAGIOS\] through his own blood.

**13** Therefore let us go to him outside the camp and bear the reproach he endured.

**14** For here we have no lasting city, but we seek the city that is to come.

**15** Through him then let us continually offer up a sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of lips that acknowledge his name.

**16** Do not neglect to do good and to share what you have, for such sacrifices are pleasing to God.

**17** Obey your leaders and submit to them, for they are keeping watch over your souls \[G-PSYCHE\], as those who will have to give an account. Let them do this with joy and not with groaning, for that would be of no advantage to you.

**18** Pray for us, for we are sure that we have a clear conscience, desiring to act honourably in all things.

**19** I urge you the more earnestly to do this in order that I may be restored to you the sooner.

**20** Now may the God of peace \[G-EIRENE\] who brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, the great shepherd of the sheep, by the blood of the eternal covenant \[G-DIATHEKE\],

**21** equip you with everything good that you may do his will, working in us that which is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ, to whom be glory \[G-DOXA\] for ever and ever. Amen.

**22** I appeal to you, brothers, bear with my word of exhortation, for I have written to you briefly.

**23** You should know that our brother Timothy has been released, with whom I shall see you if he comes soon.

**24** Greet all your leaders and all the holy ones \[G-HAGIOS\]. Those who come from Italy send you greetings.

**25** Grace \[G-CHARIS\] be with all of you.

*Review note: Hebrews 11 to 13 were drafted in WABV wording, from the Greek, using the fixed choices set out in the introduction to this book, and with the WABV voice standard (Part 46 of the review package) applied. They are the definition of faith and the creation (11:1-3), faith from Abel to Abraham and Sarah (11:4-12), the strangers and exiles and the better country (11:13-16), Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph and Moses (11:17-29), Jericho, Rahab and the many who conquered and suffered (11:30-38), the better thing for us (11:39-40), the cloud of witnesses and the race (12:1-3), the discipline of the Lord (12:4-13), the call to peace and holiness and the warning about Esau (12:14-17), Mount Sinai and Mount Zion (12:18-24), the warning about refusing him who speaks (12:25-29), the closing exhortations (13:1-9), the altar and the camp (13:10-16), obedience and prayer (13:17-19), the benediction (13:20-21) and the closing and greetings (13:22-25). The numbering is the usual numbering. No new G- marker is used, and the markers are G-PISTIS (11:1-39, 12:2, 13:7), G-DIKAIOSUNE (11:4, 11:7, 12:11), G-CHRISTOS (11:26, 13:8), G-ZOE (11:35), G-AGAPE (12:6), G-PNEUMA (12:9, 12:23), G-HAGIOS (12:10-14, 13:12, 13:24), G-EIRENE (12:14, 13:20), G-CHARIS (12:15, 13:9, 13:25), G-METANOIA (12:17), G-EKKLESIA (12:23), G-DIATHEKE (12:24, 13:20), G-BASILEIA (12:28), G-PSYCHE (13:17) and G-DOXA (13:21). The main points are these, and the footnotes carry the Greek and the sources: the Greek of 11:1 (*hupostasis*, *elegchos*) is rendered as written, and the WABV text does not decide between "assurance", "reality" and "substance"; the quotations follow the Septuagint; at 12:2 "the founder and perfecter of our faith" is rendered as written, with "perfecter" (*teleioten*) used only here, and "our" is not in the Greek; at 12:16-17 Esau and "he found no chance to repent" are rendered as written and are linked with the warnings of 6:4-8 and 10:26-31; at 13:1 "brotherly love" is *philadelphia* and is not tagged G-AGAPE; at 13:10 "an altar" is rendered as written, and the WABV text does not decide what it is; at 13:24 "those who come from Italy" is rendered as written. Several readings are drafted with some uncertainty and are to be confirmed against the critical edition in use: 11:4 (*lalei* or *laleitai*), 11:37 (the verbs in the list), 12:3 (*eis heauton* or *eis heautous*), 12:7 (*eis* or *ei*), 12:24 (*para ton Abel*), 13:21 (*en panti agatho*, *en hemin*). There are no departures from the critical Greek text in chapters 11 to 13. The Greek is hard in some places, including 11:1, 11:3, 11:11, 12:2, 12:17, 12:24 and 13:10. The words on faith (11:1-3), the cloud of witnesses (12:1), the discipline of the Lord (12:5-11), Esau (12:16-17), Mount Zion (12:22-24), the unshakeable kingdom (12:28), "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and for ever" (13:8) and the benediction (13:20-21) are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. The section needs a check against the Greek by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked. With this the book of Hebrews is complete in first draft.*

- **8:12** "Their iniquities" is the reading of the critical text, and some manuscripts add "and their lawless deeds" as in 10:17.
- **8:13** "Obsolete" and "growing old" are rendered as written.
- **9:1** "The first covenant" is the reading of the critical text, and some manuscripts add "tent" (*skene*) after "first". The tent and its furnishings in verses 1 to 5 are rendered as written, and the writer places the golden altar of incense in the Most Holy Place (verse 4), which differs from Exodus 30:1-6 and 1 Kings 6:22, and the matter is debated.
- **9:5** "The place of atonement" translates *hilasterion*, as at Romans 3:25. See entry G-DOXA.
- **9:8** "The Holy Spirit indicates" is rendered as written. See entries G-HAGIOS and G-PNEUMA.
- **9:9** "Cannot bring the conscience of the worshipper to completion" translates *teleiosai*, and "perfect" is the common word.
- **9:10** "Regulations for the body" translates *dikaiomata sarkos*, and "until the time of reformation" is rendered as written.
- **9:11** "Good things that have come" is the reading of the critical text (*genomenon*), and some manuscripts read "good things to come" (*mellonton*), and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use. "Through the greater and more complete tent" is rendered as written.
- **9:12** "An eternal redemption" translates *aionian lutrosin*. See entry G-LYTRON.
- **9:13** "Make holy for the purification of the flesh" translates *hagiazei*.
- **9:14** "Through the eternal Spirit" is rendered as written, and may mean the Holy Spirit or the spirit of Christ. "Purify our conscience" is the reading of the critical text (*hemon*), and some manuscripts read "your conscience" (*humon*).
- **9:15** "The mediator of a new covenant" and "a death has occurred that redeems them" are rendered as written, and "redeems" translates *apolutrosin*.
- **9:16** "Covenant" in verses 16 and 17 translates *diathekes*, and the Greek word means a covenant and also a will or testament, and the WABV text renders it "covenant" in both verses, and it may be read "will" (as in a last will), and the writer may be playing on the two senses. The team should decide, and the matter is linked to the question of G-DIATHEKE and H-BERIT.
- **9:19** The blood of the covenant is as in Exodus 24:3-8, and "the book itself" is rendered as written, and the Greek of the quotation in verse 20 differs from Exodus 24:8.
- **9:22** "Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins" is rendered as written.
- **9:24** "Into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf" is rendered as written.
- **9:26** "At the end of the ages" is rendered as written.
- **9:27** "It is appointed for people to die once, and after that comes judgement" and "will appear a second time" (verse 28) are rendered as written, and "to save those who are eagerly waiting for him" translates *eis soterian*. See entry G-SOZO.
- **10:1** "Bring to completion those who draw near" translates *teleiosai*.
- **10:5** The words in verses 5 to 7 are from Psalm 40:6-8 as in the Septuagint, which has "a body you have prepared for me" where the Hebrew has "ears you have dug for me", and the Greek is rendered as the writer quotes it. See entry G-CHRISTOS.
- **10:10** "We have been made holy through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all" is rendered as written, and the verb is *hegiasmenoi*. See entry G-HAGIOS.
- **10:12** "Offered for all time a single sacrifice for sins" may be read "offered a single sacrifice for sins for all time" or "offered a single sacrifice for sins and for all time sat down".
- **10:13** "A footstool" is from Psalm 110:1.
- **10:14** "Has brought to completion for all time those who are being made holy" is rendered as written, and the verbs are *teteleioken* and *hagiazomenous*.
- **10:16** The words in verses 16 and 17 are from Jeremiah 31:33-34, and the order of "on their hearts" and "on their minds" differs from 8:10. See entry G-DIATHEKE.
- **10:19** "Brothers" translates *adelphoi*, which may include the sisters. "The holy places", "the new and living way ... through the curtain, that is, through his flesh", "a great priest over the house of God" and "our bodies washed with pure water" are rendered as written, and "through his flesh" may go with "curtain" or with "way".
- **10:22** "In full assurance of faith" is rendered as written. See entry G-PISTIS.
- **10:23** "The confession of our hope" is the reading of the critical text (*elpidos*), and some manuscripts read "faith" (*pisteos*).
- **10:24** "Stir up one another to love and good works" and "not neglecting to meet together" (verse 25) are rendered as written. See entry G-AGAPE.
- **10:25** "The Day" is rendered as written.
- **10:26** The third warning in verses 26 to 31 ("if we go on sinning deliberately ... there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins") is rendered as written, and it is one of the hardest passages in the New Testament and is linked with 6:4-8, and the WABV text does not decide. See entry G-ALETHEIA.
- **10:28** "Dies without mercy" translates *choris oiktirmon*, and the Greek word is *oiktirmon*, "compassion", and not *eleos*, and it is not tagged G-ELEOS.
- **10:29** "Trampled underfoot the Son of God", "profaned the blood of the covenant by which he was made holy" and "outraged the Spirit of grace" are rendered as written, and "by which he was made holy" may refer to the Son or to the person who fell away. See entries G-DIATHEKE, G-HAGIOS, G-PNEUMA and G-CHARIS.
- **10:30** The words are from Deuteronomy 32:35 and 32:36.
- **10:31** "A fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God" is rendered as written.
- **10:32** "After you were enlightened" is rendered as written.
- **10:34** "On those in prison" is the reading of the critical text (*tois desmiois*), and some manuscripts read "on me in my chains" (*tois desmois mou*).
- **10:37** The words in verses 37 and 38 are from Habakkuk 2:3-4 as in the Septuagint, with an addition from Isaiah 26:20, and "the coming one" is rendered as written. "My righteous one shall live by faith" is the reading of the critical text (*ho de dikaios mou ek pisteos zesetai*), and the Byzantine tradition has "the righteous one shall live by my faith" (*ho de dikaios ek pisteos mou*), and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use. See entries G-DIKAIOSUNE and G-PSYCHE.
- **10:39** "Preserve their souls" translates *peripoiesin psyches*.
- **11:1** "Faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen" translates *esti de pistis elpizomenon hupostasis, pragmaton elegchos ou blepomenon*, and *hupostasis* may be read "assurance", "reality" or "substance" (as at 1:3), and *elegchos* "conviction", "proof" or "evidence". The WABV text does not decide. See entry G-PISTIS.
- **11:3** "What is seen was not made out of things that are visible" is rendered as written.
- **11:4** "Though he died, he still speaks" is the reading the draft follows (*lalei*), and some manuscripts read "is spoken of" (*laleitai*), and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use. See entry G-DIKAIOSUNE.
- **11:5** The words are from Genesis 5:24 as in the Septuagint.
- **11:6** "Must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him" is rendered as written.
- **11:7** "In reverent fear constructed an ark" translates *eulabetheis*, and "an heir of the righteousness that comes by faith" is rendered as written.
- **11:10** "The city that has foundations, whose designer and builder is God" is rendered as written.
- **11:11** "Sarah herself received power to conceive" is the reading that the draft follows, and the Greek is hard, and may be read of Sarah or of Abraham ("by faith he also, with Sarah, received power"). The WABV text does not decide.
- **11:13** "Strangers and exiles on the earth" and "a better country, that is, a heavenly one" (verse 16) are rendered as written.
- **11:17** "Offered up Isaac" is rendered as written, and "figuratively speaking" (verse 19) translates *en parabole*, and the words in verse 18 are from Genesis 21:12.
- **11:21** "Bowing in worship over the head of his staff" is as in Genesis 47:31 in the Septuagint ("staff"), and the Hebrew has "bed".
- **11:23** "They saw that the child was beautiful" is rendered as written.
- **11:26** "The reproach of Christ" is rendered as written, and Christ is named of Moses as the one anointed or the one to come. See entry G-CHRISTOS.
- **11:28** "The Destroyer of the firstborn" is rendered as written.
- **11:32** The names and the deeds in verses 32 to 38 are rendered as written, and the sources include Judges 4 to 16, 1 Samuel, Daniel 3 and 6, 1 Kings 17, 2 Kings 4 and 2 Maccabees 6 and 7.
- **11:35** "Women received back their dead by resurrection" is rendered as written. See entry G-ZOE.
- **11:37** The verbs in the list "they were stoned, they were sawn in two, they were killed with the sword" differ in the manuscripts, and some add "they were tempted", and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use.
- **11:40** "That apart from us they should not be made complete" is rendered as written, and the verb is *teleiothosin*.
- **12:1** "So great a cloud of witnesses" is rendered as written, and "the sin which clings so closely" translates *ten euperistaton hamartian*, an uncertain word.
- **12:2** "The founder and perfecter of our faith" translates *ton tes pisteos archegon kai teleioten*, and "founder" is as at 2:10, and "perfecter" (*teleioten*) is used only here, and "our" is not in the Greek, and the faith is the faith that Jesus had or the faith that he gives. The WABV text does not decide. "For the joy that was set before him" may be read "instead of the joy that was set before him".
- **12:3** "Such hostility against himself" is the reading that the draft follows (*eis heauton*), and some manuscripts read *eis heautous*, "against themselves", and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use.
- **12:5** The words in verses 5 and 6 are from Proverbs 3:11-12 as in the Septuagint. See entry G-AGAPE.
- **12:7** "It is for discipline that you have to endure" is the reading that the draft follows (*eis paideian hupomenete*), and some manuscripts read "if you endure discipline" (*ei paideian hupomenete*), and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use.
- **12:9** "The Father of spirits" is rendered as written. See entry G-PNEUMA.
- **12:10** "Holiness" translates *hagiotetos*, and "the peaceful fruit of righteousness" (verse 11) is rendered as written. See entries G-HAGIOS and G-DIKAIOSUNE.
- **12:14** "The holiness without which no one will see the Lord" is rendered as written, and "holiness" translates *hagiasmon*. See entry G-EIRENE.
- **12:15** "Root of bitterness" is from Deuteronomy 29:18. See entry G-CHARIS.
- **12:16** "Esau, who sold his birthright for a single meal" and "he found no chance to repent, though he sought it with tears" (verse 17) are rendered as written, and the second may be read "he found no way to change his father's mind" (*metanoias topon*), and it is linked with the warnings in 6:4-8 and 10:26-31. The WABV text does not decide. See entry G-METANOIA.
- **12:18** Mount Sinai and Mount Zion in verses 18 to 24 are rendered as written, and "Sinai" is not named in the Greek of verse 18 ("to what may be touched").
- **12:20** The words are from Exodus 19:12-13, and the Byzantine tradition adds "or shot with an arrow".
- **12:21** "I tremble with fear" is from Deuteronomy 9:19.
- **12:23** "The assembly of the firstborn" and "the spirits of the righteous made complete" are rendered as written, and the verb is *teteleiomenon*. See entry G-EKKLESIA.
- **12:24** "The sprinkled blood that speaks a better word than the blood of Abel" may be read "better than Abel's blood" or "better than Abel does", and the manuscripts differ in the form of "than Abel", and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use. See entry G-DIATHEKE.
- **12:26** The words in verses 26 and 27 are from Haggai 2:6, and "Yet once more" is rendered as written.
- **12:28** "A kingdom that cannot be shaken" is rendered as written. See entry G-BASILEIA.
- **12:29** "Our God is a consuming fire" is from Deuteronomy 4:24.
- **13:1** "Brotherly love" translates *philadelphia*, and it is not tagged G-AGAPE, because it is not *agape*.
- **13:2** "Some have entertained angels without knowing it" is rendered as written, and the allusion is to Genesis 18 and 19.
- **13:4** "Let marriage be held in honour among all" may be read as a statement or as an imperative.
- **13:5** The words are from Deuteronomy 31:6, and in verse 6 from Psalm 118:6.
- **13:7** "Remember your leaders" is rendered as written, and the leaders may be dead.
- **13:8** "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and for ever" is rendered as written.
- **13:9** "Strange teachings" and "foods" and "an altar from which those who serve the tent have no right to eat" (verse 10) are rendered as written, and the altar may be the cross, the table of the Lord or heaven. The WABV text does not decide.
- **13:11** "Outside the camp" is rendered as written.
- **13:15** "A sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of lips that acknowledge his name" is rendered as written.
- **13:17** "Obey your leaders and submit to them" is rendered as written. See entry G-PSYCHE.
- **13:20** The benediction in verses 20 and 21 is rendered as written, and "the great shepherd of the sheep" is from Isaiah 63:11, and "the blood of the eternal covenant" is from Zechariah 9:11 and Isaiah 55:3.
- **13:21** "Equip you with everything good" and "working in us" are the readings that the draft follows, and some manuscripts read "in every good work" and "in you", and the team should confirm the readings against the critical edition in use. See entry G-DOXA.
- **13:22** "A word of exhortation" and "I have written to you briefly" are rendered as written, and "brothers" translates *adelphoi*, which may include the sisters.
- **13:23** "Our brother Timothy has been released" is rendered as written, and may be read "has been set free" or "has been sent away".
- **13:24** "Those who come from Italy send you greetings" is rendered as written, and may be read "those from Italy" (writing from Italy, or Italians elsewhere). See entry G-HAGIOS.
