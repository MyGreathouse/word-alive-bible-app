# WABV: Zephaniah

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-RUACH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Zephaniah is the ninth of the twelve books of the Minor Prophets in the Protestant order that the WABV text follows. It is in Hebrew and in poetry. The book names Zephaniah son of Cushi, and gives his line back four generations to Hezekiah, and dates his word to the days of Josiah son of Amon, king of Judah (1:1), which is between about 640 and 609 before Christ. The WABV text does not decide more than the date the book gives. The chapter and verse numbering is the English numbering, and in Zephaniah it is the same as the Hebrew numbering. The book falls into two parts in the WABV text: the day of the LORD against Judah and Jerusalem (chapter 1), and the call to seek the LORD, the oracles against the nations, the woe on the city and the promise of restoration (chapters 2 and 3). The headings of the parts are not in the Hebrew. Two sets of lines are the same in the Hebrew as other books, and the wording of the lines that are the same is the same in the WABV text: 1:7, which is the same in part as Habakkuk 2:20, and 1:14-15, which are close to Joel 1:15 and 2:1-2. The Hebrew of Zephaniah is mostly clear, with a few very hard verses (for example 1:17 and 3:18), and the footnotes record the other readings. The names of God are given as in the earlier books: "the LORD" for YHWH, "Lord" for Adonai, "the Lord GOD" for Adonai YHWH, and "the LORD of hosts" for YHWH tseva'ot. The WABV text does not decide on questions of the growth of the book, and the footnotes record only the language.*

## Part One: Chapter 1, the day of the LORD against Judah and Jerusalem

### Chapter 1

**1** The word of the LORD that came to Zephaniah son of Cushi, son of Gedaliah, son of Amariah, son of Hizkiah, in the days of Josiah son of Amon, king of Judah.

**2** I will utterly sweep away everything from the face of the earth, declares the LORD.

**3** I will sweep away humans and animals. I will sweep away the birds of the heavens and the fish of the sea, and the rubble with the wicked. I will cut off humankind from the face of the earth, declares the LORD.

**4** I will stretch out my hand against Judah and against all the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and I will cut off from this place the remnant of Baal and the name of the idolatrous priests along with the priests,

**5** those who bow down on the roofs to the host of heaven, those who bow down and swear to the LORD and yet swear by Milcom,

**6** those who have turned away from following the LORD, who do not seek the LORD or inquire of him.

**7** Be silent before the Lord GOD, for the day of the LORD is near. The LORD has prepared a sacrifice and consecrated \[H-QADOSH\] his guests.

**8** And on the day of the LORD's sacrifice I will punish the officials and the king's sons and all who clothe themselves in foreign garments.

**9** On that day I will punish everyone who leaps over the threshold, and those who fill their master's house with violence and fraud.

**10** On that day, declares the LORD, a cry will go up from the Fish Gate, a wail from the Second Quarter, a loud crash from the hills.

**11** Wail, you inhabitants of the Mortar. For all the traders are no more, all who weigh out silver are cut off.

**12** At that time I will search Jerusalem with lamps, and I will punish the people who are thickened on their dregs, who say in their hearts, "The LORD will not do good, nor will he do harm."

**13** Their wealth will be plundered, and their houses laid waste. Though they build houses, they will not live in them. Though they plant vineyards, they will not drink their wine.

**14** The great day of the LORD is near, near and hastening fast. The sound of the day of the LORD is bitter. The warrior cries aloud there.

**15** A day of wrath is that day, a day of distress and anguish, a day of ruin and devastation, a day of darkness and gloom, a day of clouds and thick darkness,

**16** a day of trumpet blast and battle cry against the fortified cities and against the lofty corner towers.

**17** I will bring distress on humankind, so that they will walk like the blind, because they have sinned against the LORD. Their blood will be poured out like dust, and their flesh like dung.

**18** Neither their silver nor their gold will be able to deliver them on the day of the LORD's wrath. In the fire of his jealousy the whole earth will be consumed, for he will make a full and sudden end of all the inhabitants of the earth.

*Review note: Zephaniah 1 was drafted in WABV wording. It is the heading and the line of Zephaniah (1:1), the sweeping away of everything (1:2-3), the judgement on Judah and Jerusalem and on the worship of Baal, the host of heaven and Milcom (1:4-6), the silence before the Lord GOD and the sacrifice of the day of the LORD (1:7-13), and the great day of the LORD and the end of the earth (1:14-18). The numbering is the same as the Hebrew. The whole chapter is in poetry, apart from the heading in 1:1 and parts of 1:4-6 and 1:8-9, and the verses are set in lines. The date in 1:1 is by Josiah, and the footnote records the matter. In 1:7 "consecrated" translates hiqdish and is tagged H-QADOSH. In 1:6 "turned away" translates hannasogim, from nasog, a different verb from shuv, and is not tagged. The words of 1:7 ("be silent before the Lord GOD") are the same in the Hebrew as part of Habakkuk 2:20 ("let all the earth be silent before him"), and the words "be silent before" are given the same wording. The words of 1:7 and 1:14 ("the day of the LORD is near") are the same in the Hebrew as Joel 1:15 and 3:14 (and close to Joel 2:1), and the words of 1:15 ("a day of darkness and gloom, a day of clouds and thick darkness") are the same in the Hebrew as Joel 2:2, and are given the same wording as the WABV text of Joel. They are also close to Amos 5:18-20. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapter 1. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 1:4 (*chemarim*, "the idolatrous priests"), 1:5, 1:8-11 (the places of Jerusalem), 1:12 (*ha-qofe'im*) and 1:17 (*lechumam*). The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the sweeping away of humankind and the animals (1:2-3), the sacrifice of the guests (1:7) and the pouring out of blood like dust (1:17) are rendered as written, with no comment. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Two: Chapters 2 and 3, the nations, the city and the promise

### Chapter 2

**1** Gather together, gather, nation without shame,

**2** before the decree takes effect (the day passes like chaff), before the burning anger of the LORD comes on you, before the day of the LORD's anger comes on you.

**3** Seek the LORD, all you humble of the land, who do his justice. Seek righteousness \[H-TSEDAQAH\], seek humility. Perhaps you may be hidden on the day of the LORD's anger.

**4** For Gaza will be forsaken, and Ashkelon a desolation. The people of Ashdod will be driven out at noon, and Ekron uprooted.

**5** Woe to the inhabitants of the seacoast, the nation of the Cherethites. The word of the LORD is against you, Canaan, land of the Philistines: I will destroy you until no inhabitant is left.

**6** And you, seacoast, will be pastures, meadows for shepherds and folds for flocks.

**7** The coast will become the possession of the remnant of the house of Judah, on which they will graze. In the houses of Ashkelon they will lie down at evening, for the LORD their God will care for them and restore \[H-SHUV\] their fortunes.

**8** I have heard the taunts of Moab and the revilings of the Ammonites, how they taunted my people and made boasts against their territory.

**9** Therefore, as I live, declares the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel, Moab will become like Sodom and the Ammonites like Gomorrah, a land possessed by weeds and salt pits, and a waste for ever. The remnant of my people will plunder them, and the survivors of my nation will possess them.

**10** This will be their lot in return for their pride, because they taunted and boasted against the people of the LORD of hosts.

**11** The LORD will be awesome against them, for he will famish all the gods of the earth, and to him will bow down, each in its place, all the coasts of the nations.

**12** You also, Cushites, will be slain by my sword.

**13** And he will stretch out his hand against the north and destroy Assyria, and he will make Nineveh a desolation, a dry waste like the desert.

**14** Herds will lie down in her midst, every wild animal of the nations. The desert owl and the screech owl will lodge on her capitals. A voice will sing in the window, rubble will be on the threshold, for he has laid bare the cedar work.

**15** This is the exultant city that lived securely, that said in her heart, "I am, and there is no one else." What a desolation she has become, a lair for wild animals. Everyone who passes by her hisses and shakes his fist.

### Chapter 3

**1** Woe to her who is rebellious and defiled, the oppressing city.

**2** She listens to no voice. She accepts no correction. She does not trust in the LORD. She does not draw near to her God.

**3** Her officials within her are roaring lions. Her judges are wolves of the evening that leave nothing for the morning.

**4** Her prophets are reckless, treacherous people. Her priests profane what is holy \[H-QADOSH\], they do violence to the teaching \[H-TORAH\].

**5** The LORD within her is righteous \[H-TSEDAQAH\]. He does no injustice. Every morning he brings his justice to light. He does not fail. But the unjust knows no shame.

**6** I have cut off nations. Their corner towers are in ruins. I have laid waste their streets so that no one passes by. Their cities are made desolate, without people, without inhabitant.

**7** I said, Surely you will fear \[H-YIRAH\] me, you will accept correction. Then her dwelling would not be cut off according to all that I appointed against her. But all the more they were eager to make all their deeds corrupt.

**8** Therefore wait for me, declares the LORD, for the day when I rise up as a witness. For my decision is to gather nations, to assemble kingdoms, to pour out on them my indignation, all my burning anger. For in the fire of my jealousy the whole earth will be consumed.

**9** For at that time I will change the speech of the peoples to a pure speech, that all of them may call on the name of the LORD and serve him with one accord.

**10** From beyond the rivers of Cush my worshippers, my scattered ones, will bring my offering.

**11** On that day you will not be put to shame because of all the deeds by which you have rebelled against me. For then I will remove from your midst your proudly exultant ones, and you will no more be haughty in my holy \[H-QADOSH\] mountain.

**12** But I will leave in your midst a people humble and lowly. They will seek refuge in the name of the LORD,

**13** those who are left in Israel. They will do no injustice and speak no lies, nor will a deceitful tongue be found in their mouths. For they will graze and lie down, and none will make them afraid.

**14** Sing aloud, daughter of Zion. Shout, Israel. Be glad and exult with all your heart, daughter of Jerusalem.

**15** The LORD has taken away the judgements against you. He has cleared away your enemy. The King of Israel, the LORD, is in your midst. You will never again fear \[H-YIRAH\] evil.

**16** On that day it will be said to Jerusalem: Do not fear, Zion. Do not let your hands grow weak.

**17** The LORD your God is in your midst, a warrior who saves. He will rejoice over you with gladness. He will quiet you by his love \[H-AHAV\], he will exult over you with loud singing.

**18** I will gather those of you who mourn for the festival, so that you will no longer bear reproach on account of it.

**19** See, at that time I will deal with all your oppressors. And I will save the lame and gather the outcast, and I will change their shame into praise and renown in all the earth.

**20** At that time I will bring you in, at the time when I gather you. For I will make you renowned and praised among all the peoples of the earth, when I restore \[H-SHUV\] your fortunes before your eyes, says the LORD.

*Review note: Zephaniah 2 and 3 were drafted in WABV wording. They are the call to seek the LORD (2:1-3), the oracles against Philistia, Moab and Ammon, Cush and Assyria (2:4-15), the woe on the city and the charge against its rulers (3:1-7), the gathering of the nations and the pure speech (3:8-10), the humble remnant (3:11-13) and the song of joy and the promise of the return (3:14-20). The numbering is the same as the Hebrew. The whole of the part is in poetry, and the verses are set in lines. In 2:3 "righteousness" translates tsedeq and in 3:5 "righteous" translates tsaddiq, and both are tagged H-TSEDAQAH. In 2:7 and 3:20 "restore their fortunes" translates shav shevutam and shuv et-shevutekhem and is tagged H-SHUV, and the words are the same in the Hebrew as Amos 9:14 and Joel 3:1, and are given the same wording ("restore the fortunes"). In 3:4 and 3:11 "holy" translates qodesh and qodshi and is tagged H-QADOSH. In 3:4 "the teaching" translates torah and is tagged H-TORAH. In 3:7 and 3:15 "fear" translates tira and is tagged H-YIRAH. In 3:17 "love" translates be-ahavato and is tagged H-AHAV. The words of 3:8 ("in the fire of my jealousy the whole earth will be consumed") are the same as 1:18, and are given the same wording. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapters 2 and 3, and none in the whole book. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 2:1-2, 2:14 (*qa'at* and *qippod*, the two owls), 3:3, 3:8 (*le-ad*, "as a witness"), 3:10, 3:17 (*yacharish be-ahavato*) and 3:18. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the destruction of the cities of the nations (2:4-15), the plundering of Moab and Ammon (2:9) and the gathering of the nations for judgement (3:8) are rendered as written, with no comment. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Footnotes

- **1:1** "Zephaniah" translates *Tsefanyah*, "the LORD has hidden" or "treasured", and "Cushi" translates *Kushi*, "a Cushite". "Hizkiah" translates *Chizqiyyah*, and he may be King Hezekiah. "Josiah son of Amon" reigned about 640 to 609 before Christ.
- **1:2** "I will utterly sweep away" translates *asoph asef*, in which the infinitive adds force.
- **1:3** "Humans and animals" translates *adam u-vehemah*, and "the rubble with the wicked" translates *ha-makhshelot et-ha-resha'im*, in which *makhshelot* may also be read "the stumbling blocks".
- **1:4** "I will stretch out my hand" translates *natiti yadi*, "the remnant of Baal" translates *she'ar ha-Baal*, and "the idolatrous priests" translates *chemarim*, a word for priests of other gods, which is rare and hard.
- **1:5** "The host of heaven" translates *tseva ha-shamayim*. "Milcom" translates *malkam*, which may also be read "their king", as in Amos 1:15, and Milcom was the god of the Ammonites (1 Kings 11:5).
- **1:6** "Turned away" translates *ha-nasogim*, from *nasog*, a different verb from *shuv*, and it is not tagged.
- **1:7** "Be silent" translates *has*, and the words "be silent before" are the same as Habakkuk 2:20. "The Lord GOD" translates *Adonai YHWH*. "Is near" translates *qarov*, as in Joel 1:15 and 3:14. "A sacrifice" translates *zevach*, "consecrated" translates *hiqdish*, and "his guests" translates *qeru'av*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **1:8** "The officials" translates *ha-sarim*, and "foreign garments" translates *malbush nokhri*.
- **1:9** "Leaps over the threshold" translates *ha-dolegh al-ha-miftan*, as in 1 Samuel 5:5, and "violence and fraud" translates *chamas u-mirmah*.
- **1:10** "The Fish Gate" translates *sha'ar ha-dagim*, and "the Second Quarter" translates *ha-mishneh*, a district of Jerusalem (see 2 Kings 22:14). "A loud crash" translates *shever gadol*.
- **1:11** "The Mortar" translates *ha-maktesh*, a low district of Jerusalem.
- **1:12** "I will search Jerusalem with lamps" translates *acha-ppes et-Yerushalayim ba-nerot*, and "thickened on their dregs" translates *ha-qofe'im el-shimreihem*, a picture of wine left to stand on its dregs, and then of people who are complacent. "Do good" and "do harm" translate *yetiv* and *yera*.
- **1:13** "Plundered" translates *limeshissah*, and "laid waste" translates *li-shemamah*. Compare Deuteronomy 28:30, Amos 5:11 and Micah 6:15.
- **1:14** "Near and hastening fast" translates *qarov u-mahër me'od*, and the words "the day of the LORD is near" are the same as Joel 1:15 and 3:14, and close to Joel 2:1. "The warrior cries aloud" translates *tsorech sham gibbor*.
- **1:15** "Wrath" translates *evrah*, and "distress and anguish" translates *tsarah u-metsuqah*. "Ruin and devastation" translates *sho'ah u-meshoah*. "A day of darkness and gloom, a day of clouds and thick darkness" translates *yom choshekh va-afelah yom anan va-arafel*, and the words are the same as Joel 2:2, and are given the same wording as the WABV text of Joel. See also Amos 5:18-20.
- **1:16** "A trumpet blast" translates *shofar*, "a battle cry" translates *teru'ah*, and "the corner towers" translates *pinnot*.
- **1:17** "Walk like the blind" translates *ve-halkhu ka-ivrim*, and "their flesh" translates *lechumam*, which is hard. "Dung" translates *ge'alim*.
- **1:18** "His jealousy" translates *qin'ato*, and "a full and sudden end" translates *kalah ach-nivhalah*. The same words are in 3:8.
- **2:1** "Gather together, gather" translates *hitqosheshu va-qoshu*, from a rare root that may mean "gather straw", and "nation without shame" translates *goy lo nikhsaf*, which is hard and may also be read "nation not desired".
- **2:2** "The decree" translates *choq*, "passes like chaff" translates *ke-mots avar yom*, and "burning anger" translates *charon af-YHWH*. The verse is hard.
- **2:3** "The humble" translates *anvei*, "justice" translates *mishpato*, "righteousness" translates *tsedeq*, and "humility" translates *anavah*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. "You may be hidden" translates *tissaterv*.
- **2:4** The words "Gaza" (*Azzah*) and "forsaken" (*azuvah*), and "Ekron" (*Eqron*) and "uprooted" (*te'aqer*), are word-plays. "At noon" translates *ba-tsohorayim*.
- **2:5** "The seacoast" translates *chevel ha-yam*, "the Cherethites" translates *Keretim*, and "the Philistines" translates *Pelishtim*.
- **2:6** "Pastures" translates *nevot*, and "meadows" translates *keroth*, which is rare and hard, and may also be read "caves" or "wells".
- **2:7** "Restore their fortunes" translates *ve-shav shevutam*, and the words are the same as Amos 9:14 and Joel 3:1, and are given the same wording. See entry H-SHUV. "Will care for them" translates *yifqedem*.
- **2:8** "The taunts" translates *cherpat*, "the revilings" translates *giddufe*, and "made boasts" translates *va-yagdilu*.
- **2:9** "As I live" translates *chai ani*, and "weeds" and "salt pits" translate *mimshaq charul* and *mikhreh-melach*. For Sodom and Gomorrah, see Genesis 19:24-25.
- **2:11** "Awesome" translates *nora*, "he will famish" translates *razah*, and "the coasts" translates *iyyei*.
- **2:12** "The Cushites" translates *Kushiyyim*.
- **2:13** "The north" translates *tsafon*, and "a dry waste" translates *tsiyyah*.
- **2:14** "The desert owl" and "the screech owl" translate *qa'at* and *qippod*, which are birds or animals of uncertain kind. "Her capitals" translates *kaftoreha*, "rubble" translates *choreb*, and "the cedar work" translates *arzah*.
- **2:15** "Exultant" translates *ha-alizah*, and "I am, and there is no one else" translates *ani ve-ephsi od*, as in Isaiah 47:8. "Hisses" translates *yishrok*, and "shakes his fist" translates *yaniya yado*.
- **3:1** "Rebellious" translates *mora*, "defiled" translates *nig'alah*, and "the oppressing city" translates *ha-ir ha-yonah*, in which *yonah* may also be read "dove".
- **3:2** "Correction" translates *musar*, and "draw near" translates *qarevah*.
- **3:3** "Her officials" translates *sareha*, and "wolves of the evening" translates *ze'evei erev*. "Leave nothing" translates *lo garemu*, which is hard.
- **3:4** "Reckless" translates *pochazim*, and "treacherous people" translates *anshei bogedot*. "Profane what is holy" translates *chillelu-qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH. "Do violence to the teaching" translates *chamesu torah*. See entry H-TORAH.
- **3:5** "Righteous" translates *tsaddiq*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. "Every morning" translates *ba-boqer ba-boqer*, and "the unjust" translates *avval*.
- **3:6** "Their corner towers" translates *pinnotam*, and "I have laid waste" translates *hecheravti*.
- **3:7** "You will fear me" translates *tira otti*. See entry H-YIRAH. "I appointed" translates *paqadeti*, and "they were eager" translates *hishkimu*.
- **3:8** "As a witness" translates *le-ad*. The *same letters, with different vowels, may also be read "for plunder"*. "In the fire of my jealousy the whole earth will be consumed" is the same as 1:18, and is given the same wording.
- **3:9** "A pure speech" translates *safah berurah*, and "with one accord" translates *shekhem echad*, literally "one shoulder".
- **3:10** "My worshippers" translates *atarai*, which is hard, and "my scattered ones" translates *bat-putsai*, literally "the daughter of my dispersed".
- **3:11** "Put to shame" translates *tevoshi*, and "my holy mountain" translates *har qodshi*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **3:12** "Humble and lowly" translates *ani va-dal*, and "seek refuge" translates *chasu*.
- **3:14** "Sing aloud" translates *roni*, and "shout" translates *hari'u*.
- **3:15** "The judgements" translates *mishpatayikh*, and "has cleared away" translates *pinnah*. "You will never again fear evil" translates *lo-tira ra od*. The Greek reads "you will not see evil any more" (*tir'i*). See entry H-YIRAH.
- **3:17** "A warrior who saves" translates *gibbor yoshia*, and "he will rejoice" translates *yasis*. "He will quiet you by his love" translates *yacharish be-ahavato*, which is hard. The Greek reads "he will renew you in his love" (*yechaddesh*). See entry H-AHAV.
- **3:18** "Those of you who mourn for the festival" translates *nugei mi-mo'ed*, which is very hard.
- **3:19** "The lame" and "the outcast" translate *ha-tsol'ah* and *ha-niddachah*, as in Micah 4:6-7.
- **3:20** "When I restore your fortunes" translates *be-shuvi et-shevuteikhem*, and the words are the same as Joel 3:1 and Amos 9:14, and are given the same wording. See entry H-SHUV.
