# WABV: Song of Songs

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-NEPHESH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. The Song of Songs is a poem in several voices, a woman, a man and a group of friends (the daughters of Jerusalem), and the Hebrew does not mark the speakers. The WABV text adds a short label in italics (*She*, *He*, *Friends*) at the start of each speech, taken from the gender of the Hebrew words, and the footnote on 1:2 records the matter and the verses where the speaker is open. The chapters are numbered in the English way, and English 6:13 is Hebrew 7:1, so that English 7:1-13 is Hebrew 7:2-14, and the footnote on 6:13 gives the matter. The title "the Song of Songs" means the best of songs. The WABV text does not decide between the readings of the Song as a poem of human love, as an allegory or as both, and the footnotes record only the language.*

## Part One: The Beginning of the Song

### Chapter 1

**1** The Song of Songs, which is Solomon's.

**2** *She:* Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth, for your love is better than wine.

**3** Your anointing oils are fragrant. Your name is oil poured out. Therefore the young women love you.

**4** Draw me after you. Let us run. The king has brought me into his chambers.

*Friends:* We will exult and rejoice in you. We will praise your love more than wine. Rightly do they love you.

**5** *She:* I am dark, but lovely, daughters of Jerusalem, like the tents of Kedar, like the curtains of Solomon.

**6** Do not stare at me because I am dark, because the sun has looked on me. My mother's sons were angry with me. They made me keeper of the vineyards, but my own vineyard I have not kept.

**7** Tell me, you whom my soul \[H-NEPHESH\] loves, where you pasture your flock, where you make it lie down at noon. For why should I be like one who veils herself beside the flocks of your companions?

**8** *He:* If you do not know, most beautiful among women, follow the tracks of the flock and pasture your young goats beside the shepherds' tents.

**9** I compare you, my love, to a mare among Pharaoh's chariots.

**10** Your cheeks are lovely with ornaments, your neck with strings of jewels.

**11** We will make for you ornaments of gold, studded with silver.

**12** *She:* While the king was on his couch, my nard gave forth its fragrance.

**13** My beloved is to me a sachet of myrrh that lies between my breasts.

**14** My beloved is to me a cluster of henna blossoms in the vineyards of Engedi.

**15** *He:* See, you are beautiful, my love. See, you are beautiful. Your eyes are doves.

**16** *She:* See, you are beautiful, my beloved, truly delightful. Our couch is green.

**17** The beams of our house are cedar. Our rafters are pine.

### Chapter 2:1-7

**1** *She:* I am a meadow flower of Sharon, a lily of the valleys.

**2** *He:* Like a lily among brambles, so is my love among the young women.

**3** *She:* Like an apple tree among the trees of the forest, so is my beloved among the young men. With great delight I sat in his shadow, and his fruit was sweet to my taste.

**4** He brought me to the banqueting house, and his banner over me was love.

**5** Sustain me with raisins. Refresh me with apples, for I am sick with love.

**6** His left hand is under my head, and his right hand embraces me.

**7** I charge you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the gazelles or the does of the field, that you not stir up or awaken love until it pleases.

*Review note: Song of Songs 1:1 to 2:7 were drafted in WABV wording. The section has the title (1:1), the first speeches of the woman, the friends and the man (1:2-2:6) and the first adjuration to the daughters of Jerusalem (2:7). The speaker labels are not in the Hebrew, and they are given in italics from the gender of the Hebrew words, as the introduction records. In 1:2 the shift from "him" to "you" is as in the Hebrew. In 1:4 "Draw me after you. Let us run" is hard, and the footnote records the readings. In 1:5-6 the woman's words on her colour ("dark") and her brothers are rendered as written, and the footnote records the words. In 1:7 "you whom my soul loves" translates *she-ahavah nafshi*, with the tag, and the same phrase is used in 3:1-4. In 1:15 and 1:16 the man and the woman use the same words ("you are beautiful"). In 2:1 the flower is not named with certainty, and the WABV text gives "meadow flower", and the footnote records the other readings ("rose", "crocus", "lotus"). In 2:7 "love" (*ha-ahavah*) has the article, and the footnote records the readings ("love itself", "the beloved"). The adjuration in 2:7 is repeated in 3:5 and 8:4, and the wording is kept the same. The Hebrew is very hard in some places, including 1:4, 1:5, 1:7, 1:8-11, 1:12, 1:14, 2:1, 2:4, 2:5 and 2:7. There are no departures from the Hebrew in the section. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Two: The Spring, the Night and the Garden

### Chapter 2:8-17

**8** *She:* The voice of my beloved. See, he comes, leaping over the mountains, bounding over the hills.

**9** My beloved is like a gazelle or a young stag. See, there he stands behind our wall, gazing through the windows, looking through the lattice.

**10** My beloved speaks and says to me:

*He:* "Arise, my love, my beautiful one, and come away,

**11** for see, the winter is past. The rain is over and gone.

**12** The flowers appear on the earth. The time of singing has come, and the voice of the turtledove is heard in our land.

**13** The fig tree ripens its figs, and the vines are in blossom. They give forth fragrance. Arise, my love, my beautiful one, and come away.

**14** My dove, in the clefts of the rock, in the hiding places on the cliff, let me see your face. Let me hear your voice, for your voice is sweet, and your face is lovely.

**15** Catch the foxes for us, the little foxes that spoil the vineyards, for our vineyards are in blossom."

**16** *She:* My beloved is mine, and I am his. He grazes among the lilies.

**17** Until the day breathes and the shadows flee, turn, my beloved, be like a gazelle or a young stag on the cleft mountains.

### Chapter 3

**1** *She:* On my bed by night I sought him whom my soul \[H-NEPHESH\] loves. I sought him, but I did not find him.

**2** "I will rise now and go about the city, in the streets and in the squares. I will seek him whom my soul loves." I sought him, but I did not find him.

**3** The watchmen found me as they went about in the city. "Have you seen him whom my soul loves?"

**4** Scarcely had I passed them when I found him whom my soul loves. I held him, and would not let him go until I had brought him into my mother's house, and into the chamber of her who conceived me.

**5** I charge you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the gazelles or the does of the field, that you not stir up or awaken love until it pleases.

**6** *Friends:* Who is this coming up from the wilderness like columns of smoke, perfumed with myrrh and frankincense, with all the fragrant powders of a merchant?

**7** See, it is the litter of Solomon. Around it are sixty mighty men, some of the mighty men of Israel,

**8** all of them wearing swords and expert in war, each with his sword at his thigh against terror by night.

**9** King Solomon made himself a carriage from the wood of Lebanon.

**10** He made its posts of silver, its back of gold, its seat of purple. Its interior was inlaid with love by the daughters of Jerusalem.

**11** Go out, daughters of Zion, and look on King Solomon, with the crown with which his mother crowned him on the day of his wedding, on the day of the gladness of his heart.

### Chapter 4

**1** *He:* See, you are beautiful, my love. See, you are beautiful. Your eyes are doves behind your veil. Your hair is like a flock of goats leaping down the slopes of Gilead.

**2** Your teeth are like a flock of shorn ewes that have come up from the washing, all of which bear twins, and not one among them has lost its young.

**3** Your lips are like a scarlet thread, and your mouth is lovely. Your cheeks are like halves of a pomegranate behind your veil.

**4** Your neck is like the tower of David, built in rows of stone. On it hang a thousand shields, all of them shields of warriors.

**5** Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle, that graze among the lilies.

**6** Until the day breathes and the shadows flee, I will go away to the mountain of myrrh and the hill of frankincense.

**7** You are altogether beautiful, my love. There is no flaw in you.

**8** Come with me from Lebanon, my bride. Come with me from Lebanon. Depart from the peak of Amana, from the peak of Senir and Hermon, from the dens of lions, from the mountains of leopards.

**9** You have captured my heart, my sister, my bride. You have captured my heart with one glance of your eyes, with one jewel of your necklace.

**10** How beautiful is your love, my sister, my bride. How much better is your love than wine, and the fragrance of your oils than any spice.

**11** Your lips drip nectar, my bride. Honey and milk are under your tongue. The fragrance of your garments is like the fragrance of Lebanon.

**12** A garden locked is my sister, my bride, a spring locked, a fountain sealed.

**13** Your shoots are an orchard of pomegranates with all choicest fruits, henna with nard,

**14** nard and saffron, calamus and cinnamon, with all trees of frankincense, myrrh and aloes, with all choice spices,

**15** a garden fountain, a well of living water, and flowing streams from Lebanon.

**16** *She:* Awake, north wind, and come, south wind. Blow on my garden, let its spices flow. Let my beloved come to his garden and eat its choicest fruits.

*Review note: Song of Songs 2:8 to 4:16 were drafted in WABV wording. The section has the woman's account of her beloved coming in the spring and the man's invitation (2:8-17), the woman's search for him by night and the finding (3:1-5), the friends' description of the litter of Solomon and the wedding (3:6-11), and the man's long praise of the woman and the garden, with the woman's answer (4:1-16). In 2:15 "Catch the foxes for us" is hard, and the speaker is not named in the Hebrew (the WABV text gives it to the man as part of his speech, and the footnote records that it may be the woman or the friends). In 2:17 and 4:6 "until the day breathes and the shadows flee" is the same phrase, and the footnote records the readings ("until the day cools" and "until the day breaks"). In 3:1-4 "him whom my soul loves" is tagged as in 1:7. In 3:5 the adjuration is the same as 2:7, and the wording is matched. In 3:6-11 the "litter", the "carriage" and the "love" with which it is inlaid are hard words, and the footnotes record the readings. In 4:1-5 the images of the woman (the doves, the goats, the ewes, the scarlet thread, the pomegranate, the tower and the fawns) are rendered as written, and the footnote on 4:4 records the readings of the tower. In 4:8 "my bride" translates *kallah*, and in 4:9 and 4:10 "my sister, my bride" is given as the Hebrew has it. In 4:12-15 the garden and the spring are rendered as written, and the footnote records the meaning of the figure. In 4:16 "his garden" may be read as the man's, and the woman speaks. The Hebrew is very hard in some places, including 2:9, 2:15, 2:17, 3:6, 3:9, 3:10, 4:1, 4:4, 4:6, 4:9, 4:13 and 4:16. There are no departures from the Hebrew in the section. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Three: The Dream and the Praise

### Chapter 5

**1** *He:* I came to my garden, my sister, my bride. I gathered my myrrh with my spice. I ate my honeycomb with my honey. I drank my wine with my milk.

*Friends:* Eat, friends. Drink, and be drunk with love.

**2** *She:* I slept, but my heart was awake. The voice of my beloved. He is knocking. "Open to me, my sister, my love, my dove, my perfect one, for my head is wet with dew, my locks with the drops of the night."

**3** I had taken off my garment. How could I put it on? I had bathed my feet. How could I soil them?

**4** My beloved put his hand through the opening, and my heart was stirred for him.

**5** I arose to open to my beloved, and my hands dripped with myrrh, my fingers with liquid myrrh, on the handles of the bolt.

**6** I opened to my beloved, but my beloved had turned and gone. My soul \[H-NEPHESH\] failed when he spoke. I sought him, but I did not find him. I called him, but he gave no answer.

**7** The watchmen found me as they went about in the city. They beat me, they bruised me. They took away my veil, those watchmen of the walls.

**8** I charge you, daughters of Jerusalem, if you find my beloved, what will you tell him? That I am sick with love.

**9** *Friends:* What is your beloved more than another beloved, most beautiful among women? What is your beloved more than another beloved, that you charge us like this?

**10** *She:* My beloved is radiant and ruddy, distinguished among ten thousand.

**11** His head is the finest gold. His locks are wavy, black as a raven.

**12** His eyes are like doves beside streams of water, bathed in milk, sitting beside a full pool.

**13** His cheeks are like beds of spices, mounds of sweet-smelling herbs. His lips are lilies, dripping liquid myrrh.

**14** His arms are rods of gold, set with jewels. His body is polished ivory, covered with sapphires.

**15** His legs are alabaster columns, set on bases of gold. His appearance is like Lebanon, choice as the cedars.

**16** His mouth is most sweet, and he is altogether desirable. This is my beloved and this is my friend, daughters of Jerusalem.

### Chapter 6

**1** *Friends:* Where has your beloved gone, most beautiful among women? Where has your beloved turned, that we may seek him with you?

**2** *She:* My beloved has gone down to his garden, to the beds of spices, to graze in the gardens and to gather lilies.

**3** I am my beloved's, and my beloved is mine. He grazes among the lilies.

**4** *He:* You are beautiful as Tirzah, my love, lovely as Jerusalem, awesome as an army with banners.

**5** Turn your eyes away from me, for they overwhelm me. Your hair is like a flock of goats leaping down the slopes of Gilead.

**6** Your teeth are like a flock of ewes that have come up from the washing. All of them bear twins, and not one among them has lost its young.

**7** Your cheeks are like halves of a pomegranate behind your veil.

**8** There are sixty queens and eighty concubines, and young women without number.

**9** My dove, my perfect one, is the only one, the only daughter of her mother, pure to her who bore her. The young women saw her and called her blessed. The queens and concubines also, and they praised her:

**10** "Who is this who looks down like the dawn, beautiful as the moon, bright as the sun, awesome as an army with banners?"

**11** I went down to the nut orchard to look at the blossoms of the valley, to see whether the vines had budded, whether the pomegranates were in bloom.

**12** Before I was aware, my desire \[H-NEPHESH\] set me among the chariots of my noble people.

**13** *Friends:* Return, return, Shulammite. Return, return, that we may look at you.

*He:* Why should you look at the Shulammite, as at a dance before two armies?

### Chapter 7:1-9

**1** How beautiful are your feet in sandals, noble daughter. Your rounded thighs are like jewels, the work of a master hand.

**2** Your navel is a rounded bowl that never lacks mixed wine. Your belly is a heap of wheat, encircled with lilies.

**3** Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle.

**4** Your neck is like an ivory tower. Your eyes are pools in Heshbon, by the gate of Bath-rabbim. Your nose is like a tower of Lebanon, which looks toward Damascus.

**5** Your head crowns you like Carmel, and your flowing locks are like purple. A king is held captive in the tresses.

**6** How beautiful and pleasant you are, loved one, with all your delights.

**7** Your stature is like a palm tree, and your breasts are like its clusters.

**8** I say I will climb the palm tree and lay hold of its fruit. May your breasts be like clusters of the vine, and the scent of your breath like apples,

**9** and your mouth like the best wine.

*She:* It goes down smoothly for my beloved, gliding over lips and teeth.

In 5:1 the speaker of the first lines is the man, and the call to the friends is the friends or the man, and the footnote records the matter. In 5:2-8 the dream of the knocking, the removed garment and the beating by the watchmen are rendered as written, with the same watchmen as in 3:3. In 5:6 "my soul failed when he spoke" translates *nafshi yatse'ah be-dabbero*, with the tag, and the footnote records the other readings ("my heart went out when he turned"). In 5:10-16 the images of the man (gold, doves, spices, ivory, alabaster columns) are rendered as written, with the footnote on 5:14 recording the stones. ## Part Four: Love Is Strong as Death

### Chapter 7:10-13

**10** *She:* I am my beloved's, and his desire is for me.

**11** Come, my beloved, let us go out into the fields and lodge in the villages.

**12** Let us go out early to the vineyards and see whether the vines have budded, whether the grape blossoms have opened and the pomegranates are in bloom. There I will give you my love.

**13** The mandrakes give forth fragrance, and beside our doors are all choice fruits, new as well as old, which I have laid up for you, my beloved.

### Chapter 8

**1** *She:* Oh that you were like a brother to me, who nursed at my mother's breasts. If I found you outside, I would kiss you, and none would despise me.

**2** I would lead you and bring you into the house of my mother, and she would teach me. I would give you spiced wine to drink, the juice of my pomegranate.

**3** His left hand is under my head, and his right hand embraces me.

**4** I charge you, daughters of Jerusalem, that you not stir up or awaken love until it pleases.

**5** *Friends:* Who is this coming up from the wilderness, leaning on her beloved?

*She:* Under the apple tree I awakened you. There your mother was in labour with you. There she who bore you was in labour.

**6** Set me as a seal on your heart, as a seal on your arm, for love is strong as death, jealousy is fierce as Sheol \[H-SHEOL\]. Its flashes are flashes of fire, the very flame of Yah.

**7** Many waters cannot quench love, neither can floods drown it. If a person offered for love all the wealth of his house, he would be utterly despised.

**8** *Brothers:* We have a little sister, and she has no breasts. What shall we do for our sister on the day when she is spoken for?

**9** If she is a wall, we will build on her a battlement of silver. But if she is a door, we will enclose her with boards of cedar.

**10** *She:* I was a wall, and my breasts were like towers. Then I was in his eyes as one who finds peace \[H-SHALOM\].

**11** Solomon had a vineyard at Baal-hamon. He entrusted the vineyard to keepers. Each one was to bring for its fruit a thousand pieces of silver.

**12** My vineyard, my own, is for myself. You, Solomon, may have the thousand, and the keepers of the fruit two hundred.

**13** *He:* You who dwell in the gardens, with companions listening for your voice, let me hear it.

**14** *She:* Make haste, my beloved, and be like a gazelle or a young stag on the mountains of spices.

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

- **1:1** "The Song of Songs" translates *shir ha-shirim*, a superlative form, "the best of songs". "Which is Solomon's" translates *asher li-Shelomoh*, in which the preposition may mean "by", "for", "about" or "belonging to" Solomon.
- **1:2** The speaker labels (*She*, *He*, *Friends*, and *Brothers* in 8:8-9) are not in the Hebrew. They are given from the gender of the Hebrew verbs and endings and from the sense, and the verses where the speaker is open are 2:15, 4:16, 5:1, 8:5 and 8:8-9. "The friends" are the daughters of Jerusalem. "Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth" translates *yishqeni mi-neshiqot pihu*, in which the speech changes from "him" to "you" within the verse. "Your love" translates *dodekha*, in which *dodim* means "love" or "lovemaking", and is a word that is used throughout the Song.
- **1:3** "Your anointing oils" translates *shemanekha*, and "your name is oil poured out" translates *shemen turaq shemekha*, which has a play on *shem* and *shemen*. "The young women" translates *alamot*.
- **1:4** "Draw me after you. Let us run" translates *moshkheni acharekha narutsah*, in which the verb "draw me" is singular and "let us run" is plural, and the line is hard. "Chambers" translates *chadarav*. "Rightly" translates *meisharim*, "uprightly".
- **1:5** "Dark, but lovely" translates *shechorah ani ve-na'vah*, in which the Hebrew word *ve* may be read "and" or "but". "The curtains of Solomon" translates *yeri'ot Shelomoh*.
- **1:6** "The sun has looked on me" translates *she-shezafatni ha-shamesh*. "My mother's sons" translates *benei immi*. "My own vineyard" translates *karmi shelli*, and is a figure of herself.
- **1:7** "You whom my soul loves" translates *she-ahavah nafshi*. See entry H-NEPHESH. The phrase is used in 3:1-4. "One who veils herself" translates *ke-oteyah*, which follows the Hebrew. The Greek and Syriac have "one who wanders" (*ke-tsoyah* or similar).
- **1:8** "Most beautiful among women" translates *ha-yafah ba-nashim*, as in 5:9 and 6:1. "Your young goats" translates *gediyyotayikh*.
- **1:9** "A mare among Pharaoh's chariots" translates *le-susati be-rikhvei Par'oh*, which is hard. "My love" translates *ra'yati*, which means "my companion" or "my friend".
- **1:10** "Ornaments" translates *torim*, and "strings of jewels" translates *charuzim*.
- **1:12** "On his couch" translates *bi-msibbo*, which may also mean "at his table" or "in his circle". "Nard" translates *nirdi*, a perfume.
- **1:13** "A sachet of myrrh" translates *tserror ha-mor*.
- **1:14** "A cluster of henna blossoms" translates *eshkol ha-kofer*.
- **1:16** "Truly delightful" translates *af na'im*. "Our couch is green" translates *af arsenu ra'ananah*.
- **1:17** "Rafters" translates *rahitenu*, a rare word, and "pine" translates *berotim*, which may also be "juniper" or "cypress".
- **2:1** "A meadow flower" translates *chavatselet*, a rare word that may mean "rose", "crocus" or "lotus". "A lily" translates *shoshannah*, which may be any flower of the type. "Sharon" is the coastal plain.
- **2:2** "Brambles" translates *chochim*.
- **2:3** "An apple tree" translates *tappuach*, which may be the apple, the apricot or the citron. "I sat in his shadow" translates *chimmadti ve-yashavti*, "I delighted and sat".
- **2:4** "The banqueting house" translates *beit ha-yayin*, "the house of wine". "His banner over me was love" translates *ve-diglo alai ahavah*, which is hard and may be read "his intention toward me was love".
- **2:5** "Raisins" translates *ashishot*, "cakes of raisins". "I am sick with love" translates *cholat ahavah ani*.
- **2:6** The verse is the same as 8:3.
- **2:7** "I charge you" translates *hishba'ti etkhem*, "I make you swear". "By the gazelles or the does of the field" translates *bi-tsvaot o be-ayelot ha-sadeh*, which has a sound like the divine titles *tseva'ot* and *El Shaddai*. "Love" translates *ha-ahavah*, with the article, which may be read "love itself" or "the beloved". "Until it pleases" translates *ad she-techpats*. The verse is repeated in 3:5 and, in a shorter form, in 8:4.
- **2:8** "Leaping" translates *medalleg*, and "bounding" translates *meqapets*.
- **2:9** "The lattice" translates *charakkim*, a rare word.
- **2:11** "The winter" translates *ha-setav*, a rare word.
- **2:12** "The flowers" translates *ha-nitsanim*. "The time of singing" translates *et ha-zamir*, in which *zamir* may mean "singing" or "pruning". "The turtledove" translates *tor*.
- **2:13** "Ripens" translates *chanteta*, and "the vines in blossom" translates *ha-gefanim semadar*.
- **2:14** "The clefts of the rock" translates *chagvei ha-sela*, and "the cliff" translates *madregah*.
- **2:15** "Catch the foxes for us" translates *echezu-lanu shu'alim*. The speaker is not named, and the line may be the woman's or the friends'. The word *shu'alim* may mean "foxes" or "jackals".
- **2:17** "Until the day breathes and the shadows flee" translates *ad she-yafuach ha-yom ve-nasu ha-tselalim*, which may be read "until the day cools" or "until the day breaks". "Turn" translates *sov*. "The cleft mountains" translates *harei veter*, in which *veter* may mean "cleft" or may be a name (Bether).
- **3:1** "Him whom my soul loves" is as in 1:7. See entry H-NEPHESH.
- **3:3** "The watchmen" translates *ha-shomerim*, and "as they went about" translates *ha-sovevim*.
- **3:4** "The chamber of her who conceived me" translates *cheder horati*.
- **3:5** The verse is the same as 2:7, and the wording is matched.
- **3:6** "Like columns of smoke" translates *ke-timrot ashan*, in which *timrot* is a rare word that may mean "pillars" or "palm-like columns". "Fragrant powders" translates *avqat rokhel*.
- **3:7** "The litter" translates *mittato*, "his couch" or "his palanquin". "Mighty men" translates *gibborim*.
- **3:9** "A carriage" translates *appiryon*, a rare word that may be a Greek loanword and may mean "litter" or "palanquin".
- **3:10** "Inlaid with love by the daughters of Jerusalem" translates *tokho ratsuf ahavah mi-benot Yerushalaim*, which is hard and may be read "its interior was inlaid with leather by the daughters of Jerusalem".
- **3:11** "The crown" translates *atarah*, and "his wedding" translates *chatunnato*.
- **4:1** "Your veil" translates *tsammatekh*. "Leaping down" translates *shegalshu*, which is a rare verb.
- **4:2** "Shorn ewes" translates *ha-qetsuvot*, and "bear twins" translates *mat'imot*.
- **4:3** "Your mouth" translates *midbarekh*, which may mean "your mouth" or "your speech". "A scarlet thread" translates *chut ha-shani*. "Halves of a pomegranate" translates *pelach ha-rimmon*.
- **4:4** "Built in rows of stone" translates *banui le-talpiyyot*, in which *talpiyyot* is a rare word that may mean "rows", "armoury" or "terraces".
- **4:5** "Fawns" translates *ofarim*.
- **4:6** "The mountain of myrrh and the hill of frankincense" is a figure of the woman, as 4:12-15 and 5:1 suggest.
- **4:8** "My bride" translates *kallah*, which means "bride" or "daughter-in-law", and is used in 4:8-12 and 5:1.
- **4:9** "You have captured my heart" translates *libbavtini*, a rare verb. "My sister, my bride" translates *achoti kallah*, in which "sister" is a term of love and not of kinship. "One jewel of your necklace" translates *be-achad me-anaqayikh*.
- **4:10** "Your love" translates *dodayikh*.
- **4:11** "Nectar" translates *nofet*, "the drippings of the honeycomb".
- **4:12** "A garden locked" translates *gan na'ul*, and "a spring locked" translates *gal na'ul*, in which *gal* is a rare word for "spring" or "heap". The meaning of *gal* is uncertain. "Sealed" translates *chatum*.
- **4:13** "Your shoots" translates *shelachayikh*, which may mean "your shoots" or "your channels". "An orchard" translates *pardes*, a Persian loanword, and "henna" translates *kefarim*.
- **4:14** "Saffron" translates *karkom*, "calamus" translates *qaneh*, "cinnamon" translates *qinnamon*, and "aloes" translates *ahalot*.
- **4:15** "A well of living water" translates *be'er mayim chayyim*.
- **4:16** "Awake" translates *uri*. "His garden" translates *gani*, the woman's garden, which she gives to him.
- **5:1** "I gathered my myrrh with my spice" translates *ariti moori im-bisami*. "Honeycomb" translates *ya'ri*, and "be drunk with love" translates *shikhru dodim*. The speaker of the last line ("Eat, friends") is the friends, or the poet, or the man, and the footnote does not decide the matter.
- **5:2** "My heart was awake" translates *ve-libbi er*. "Knocking" translates *dofeq*. "My perfect one" translates *tammati*. "My locks" translates *qevutsotai*.
- **5:3** "My garment" translates *kuttonti*, and "soil" translates *atannefem*.
- **5:4** "Put his hand through the opening" translates *shalach yado min-ha-chor*, and "my heart was stirred for him" translates *u-me'ai hamu alav*, literally "my inward parts were stirred".
- **5:5** "The handles of the bolt" translates *kappot ha-man'ul*.
- **5:6** "My soul failed when he spoke" translates *nafshi yatse'ah be-dabbero*, "my soul went out when he spoke". See entry H-NEPHESH. The line may also be read "my heart went out at his turning away".
- **5:7** "Veil" translates *redid*, a rare word that may mean "cloak".
- **5:10** "Radiant and ruddy" translates *tsach ve-adom*, and "distinguished" translates *dagul*, "marked out".
- **5:11** "The finest gold" translates *ketem paz*, and "wavy" translates *taltallim*, a rare word.
- **5:12** "Bathed in milk, sitting beside a full pool" translates *rochatsot be-chalav yoshevot al-milet*, which is hard.
- **5:13** "Beds of spices" translates *arugat ha-bosem*, and "mounds" translates *migdelot*.
- **5:14** "Rods of gold" translates *gelilei zahav*. "Jewels" translates *tarshish*, a precious stone. "Body" translates *me'av*, which may mean "belly" or "torso". "Covered with sapphires" translates *me'ulefet sappirim*.
- **5:15** "Alabaster columns" translates *ammudei shesh*, and "bases" translates *adnei*.
- **5:16** "His mouth" translates *chikko*, and "desirable" translates *machamaddim*. "My friend" translates *re'i*.
- **6:4** "Tirzah" is a city of the northern kingdom (see 1 Kings 14:17). "Awesome" translates *ayummah*, and "an army with banners" translates *nidgalot*, a rare word.
- **6:5** "They overwhelm me" translates *hem hirhivuni*, which is a rare verb that may mean "overwhelm" or "stir me".
- **6:8** "Queens" translates *melakhot*, "concubines" translates *pilagshim*, and "young women" translates *alamot*.
- **6:9** "The only one" translates *achat hi*, and "pure" translates *barah*. "Called her blessed" translates *va-ye'ashruha*.
- **6:11** "The nut orchard" translates *ginnat egoz*.
- **6:12** "My desire set me among the chariots of my noble people" translates *lo yadati nafshi samatni markevot Ammi-nadiv*, which is very hard. See entry H-NEPHESH. *Ammi-nadiv* may be a name (Amminadib), or may mean "my noble people" or "my people is noble". The Greek has "the chariots of Aminadab".
- **6:13** In the Hebrew the verse is 7:1, and English 7:1-9 is Hebrew 7:2-10. English 7:10-13 is Hebrew 7:11-14. "Shulammite" translates *ha-Shulammit*, which may mean a woman of Shulem (Shunem), or may be a feminine form of Solomon, or may mean "the peaceful one". "A dance before two armies" translates *kimcholat ha-machanayim*, in which *machanayim* may be the name Mahanaim, or the word for "two camps", and the line is hard.
- **7:1** "Noble daughter" translates *bat-nadiv*. "Rounded thighs" translates *chammuqei yerekhayikh*, and "the work of a master hand" translates *ma'aseh yedei amman*.
- **7:2** "Your navel" translates *shorrekh*, which may also mean "your vulva" or "your waist". "A rounded bowl" translates *aggan ha-sahar*, and "mixed wine" translates *ha-mezeg*. "Your belly" translates *bitnekh*.
- **7:4** "Pools in Heshbon" translates *berekhot be-Cheshbon*, and "the gate of Bath-rabbim" translates *sha'ar bat-rabbim*. "Your nose" translates *appekh*.
- **7:5** "Flowing locks" translates *dallat roshekh*, in which *dallah* may mean "hair" or "threads". "Purple" translates *argaman*, and "the tresses" translates *ha-rehatim*. "A king is held captive" translates *melekh asur*.
- **7:6** "Loved one" translates *ahavah*, and "delights" translates *ta'anugim*.
- **7:7** "Your stature" translates *qomatekh*, and "clusters" translates *eshkolot*.
- **7:8** "Lay hold of its fruit" translates *echezah be-sansinnav*, in which *sansinnav* is a rare word. "The scent of your breath" translates *reyach appekh*, "the scent of your nose".
- **7:9** "Gliding over lips and teeth" follows the Greek and Syriac (*siftei shinnayim*). The Hebrew has *dovev siftei yeshenim*, "moving the lips of sleepers". The WABV text departs from the Hebrew here, and is flagged for the scholar. "It goes down smoothly for my beloved" translates *holekh le-dodi le-meisharim*. The speaker changes to the woman in this verse.
- **7:10** "His desire" translates *teshuqato*, the word used in Genesis 3:16.
- **7:11** "Lodge in the villages" translates *nalinah ba-kefarim*, in which *kefarim* may mean "villages" or "henna bushes" (see 1:14).
- **7:12** "The grape blossoms" translates *ha-semadar*.
- **7:13** "The mandrakes" translates *ha-dudaim*, which are in Genesis 30:14-16. "Doors" translates *petachenu*. "New as well as old" translates *chadashim gam-yeshanim*.
- **8:1** "Like a brother to me" translates *ke-ach li*. "None would despise me" translates *gam lo-yavuzu li*.
- **8:2** "I would lead you" translates *enhagekha*. "She would teach me" translates *telammedeni*, which the Greek reads "and into the chamber of her who bore me". "Spiced wine" translates *yayin ha-reqach*, and "the juice of my pomegranate" translates *asis rimmoni*.
- **8:3** The verse is the same as 2:6, and the wording is matched.
- **8:4** The verse is nearly the same as 2:7 and 3:5, and in the Hebrew it does not have the words "by the gazelles or the does of the field", which the WABV text follows.
- **8:5** "Leaning" translates *mitrappeqet*, a rare word. "I awakened you" translates *orartikha*, in which the form is masculine, so that the woman speaks to the man. "Was in labour with you" translates *chibbelatkha*, in which the verb is the verb of birth pains.
- **8:6** "A seal" translates *chotam*. "Love is strong as death" translates *azzah ka-mavet ahavah*, and "jealousy is fierce as Sheol" translates *qasha kish'ol qin'ah*. See entry H-SHEOL. "Flashes" translates *reshafeha*, in which *reshef* is also the name of a god of plague in other texts. "The very flame of Yah" translates *shalhevetyah*, in which the ending *-yah* may be the short form of the divine name, or may be an ending that gives the sense of "a most vehement flame". It is the only place in the Song where the divine name may be used, and the footnote does not decide the matter.
- **8:7** "Many waters" translates *mayim rabbim*, and "drown" translates *yishtefuha*. "The wealth of his house" translates *kol-hon beito*, and "utterly despised" translates *bo z yavuzu lo*.
- **8:8** "We have a little sister" translates *achot lanu qetannah*. The speakers are not named in the Hebrew, and they may be her brothers (as in 1:6) or the friends. "Is spoken for" translates *she-yedubbar-bah*.
- **8:9** "A wall" translates *chomah*, "a battlement" translates *tirat*, "a door" translates *delet*, and "enclose" translates *natsur*.
- **8:10** "One who finds peace" translates *ke-motset shalom*. See entry H-SHALOM. The word *shalom* has a sound like the name Solomon (*Shelomoh*) in 8:11-12.
- **8:11** "Baal-hamon" is a place that is not known. "Keepers" translates *notrim*, and "a thousand pieces of silver" translates *elef kasef*.
- **8:12** "My vineyard, my own" translates *karmi shelli lefanai*, as in 1:6.
- **8:13** "You who dwell in the gardens" translates *ha-yoshevet ba-gannim*, and "companions" translates *chaverim*.
- **8:14** "Make haste" translates *berach*, which may mean "flee" or "hurry". The line is like 2:17, in which the Hebrew has *sov* ("turn"), so that the wording is not the same.
