# WABV: Colossians

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[G-EKKLESIA\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Colossians is the twelfth book of the New Testament in the order that the WABV text follows, and the seventh of the letters. It is written in Greek, and the WABV text is drafted from a critical Greek text, with the Byzantine tradition noted in the footnotes where it differs, as the Charter requires. It is a letter from Paul and Timothy to the holy ones and faithful brothers in Colossae, a town of the valley of the Lycus, near Laodicea and Hierapolis, written from prison (4:3, 4:10, 4:18) and carried by Tychicus and Onesimus (4:7-9), the same two who are named in Ephesians 6:21 and Philemon 10-12. Paul had not met the Colossians (1:4, 2:1), and the assembly had been founded by Epaphras (1:7, 4:12). The letter answers a teaching that is not described in full, but that joins rules about food and festivals (2:16), a worship of angels (2:18) and a severe treatment of the body (2:23) to a claim to special knowledge and visions, and it answers it with the greatness of Christ: the hymn of 1:15-20 and the statement of 2:9-10. Two matters are debated, and the WABV text does not decide either. The first is its authorship, which some scholars hold to be Paul and some a close follower of Paul, and the letter shares much of its wording with Ephesians. The second is the nature of the teaching that it opposes. The chapter and verse numbering is the usual numbering. The WABV text of Colossians is drafted with the voice standard (Part 46 of the review package), and it sets the hymn of 1:15-20 in lines. It makes a number of fixed choices that follow from its Greek, and they are written down here so that they are used throughout. *Hagioi* is rendered "holy ones" and is tagged G-HAGIOS. *Ekklesia* is rendered "assembly" and is tagged G-EKKLESIA, and the team should decide between "assembly" and "church". *Eikon* (1:15) is rendered "image", and *prototokos* (1:15, 1:18) "firstborn", and *pleroma* (1:19, 2:9) "fullness". *Apolutrosis* is rendered "redemption" and is tagged G-LYTRON. *Musterion* is rendered "mystery". *Stoicheia* (2:8, 2:20) is rendered "elemental forces", as in Galatians. *Eirene* is rendered "peace" and is tagged G-EIRENE. *Basileia* is rendered "kingdom" and is tagged G-BASILEIA. *Doxa* is rendered "glory" and is tagged G-DOXA. *Pistis* is rendered "faith" and *pisteuo* "believe", and both are tagged G-PISTIS. *Agape* is rendered "love" and is tagged G-AGAPE. *Charis* is rendered "grace" and is tagged G-CHARIS. *Euangelion* is rendered "good news" and is tagged G-EUANGELION. *Zoe* is rendered "life" and is tagged G-ZOE. *Sabbaton* is rendered "sabbath" and is tagged G-SABBATON. *Doulos* is rendered "slave". *Adelphoi* is rendered "brothers", and the footnote records that in many places it may include the sisters, and the team should decide. *Christos* is rendered "Christ" and is tagged G-CHRISTOS. The headings of the parts are not in the Greek. The markers beginning with G- (for Greek words) are defined in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. The wording of the WABV text is drafted from the Greek and is not taken from any other English translation.*

## Chapters 1 to 4, the greatness of Christ, the new self and the household

### Chapter 1

**1** Paul, an apostle of Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] Jesus by the will of God, and Timothy our brother,

**2** to the holy ones \[G-HAGIOS\] and faithful brothers in Christ at Colossae: grace \[G-CHARIS\] to you and peace \[G-EIRENE\] from God our Father.

**3** We always thank God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, when we pray for you,

**4** since we heard of your faith \[G-PISTIS\] in Christ Jesus and of the love \[G-AGAPE\] that you have for all the holy ones,

**5** because of the hope laid up for you in heaven. Of this you have heard before in the word of the truth \[G-ALETHEIA\], the good news \[G-EUANGELION\],

**6** which has come to you, as indeed in the whole world it is bearing fruit and growing, as it also does among you, since the day you heard it and understood the grace \[G-CHARIS\] of God in truth,

**7** just as you learned it from Epaphras our beloved fellow slave. He is a faithful minister of Christ on our behalf

**8** and has made known to us your love \[G-AGAPE\] in the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\].

**9** For this reason, since the day we heard, we have not ceased to pray for you, asking that you may be filled with the knowledge of his will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding,

**10** so as to walk in a manner worthy of the Lord, fully pleasing to him, bearing fruit in every good work and growing in the knowledge of God,

**11** being strengthened with all power, according to his glorious \[G-DOXA\] might, for all endurance and patience with joy,

**12** giving thanks to the Father, who has qualified you to share in the inheritance of the holy ones \[G-HAGIOS\] in the light.

**13** He has delivered us from the domain of darkness and transferred us to the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] of his beloved Son,

**14** in whom we have redemption \[G-LYTRON\], the forgiveness of sins.

**15** He is the image of the invisible God,\
the firstborn of all creation.

**16** For in him all things were created,\
in heaven and on earth,\
visible and invisible,\
whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities.\
All things were created through him and for him.

**17** He is before all things,\
and in him all things hold together.

**18** He is the head of the body, the assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\].\
He is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead,\
that in everything he might be first.

**19** For in him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell,

**20** and through him to reconcile to himself all things,\
whether on earth or in heaven,\
making peace \[G-EIRENE\] by the blood of his cross.

**21** And you, who once were alienated and hostile in mind, doing evil deeds,

**22** he has now reconciled in his body of flesh by his death, to present you holy \[G-HAGIOS\] and blameless and above reproach before him,

**23** if indeed you continue in the faith \[G-PISTIS\], stable and steadfast, not shifting from the hope of the good news \[G-EUANGELION\] that you heard, which has been proclaimed in all creation under heaven, and of which I, Paul, became a minister.

**24** Now I rejoice in my sufferings for your sake, and in my flesh I am filling up what is lacking in Christ's afflictions for the sake of his body, that is, the assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\],

**25** of which I became a minister according to the stewardship from God that was given to me for you, to make the word of God fully known,

**26** the mystery hidden for ages and generations but now revealed to his holy ones \[G-HAGIOS\].

**27** To them God chose to make known how great among the nations are the riches of the glory \[G-DOXA\] of this mystery, which is Christ in you, the hope of glory.

**28** Him we proclaim, warning everyone and teaching everyone in all wisdom, that we may present everyone mature in Christ.

**29** For this I toil, struggling with all his energy that he powerfully works within me.

### Chapter 2

**1** For I want you to know how great a struggle I have for you and for those at Laodicea and for all who have not seen me face to face,

**2** that their hearts may be encouraged, being knit together in love \[G-AGAPE\], to reach all the riches of full assurance of understanding and the knowledge of God's mystery, which is Christ,

**3** in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.

**4** I say this in order that no one may delude you with plausible arguments.

**5** For though I am absent in body, yet I am with you in spirit \[G-PNEUMA\], rejoicing to see your good order and the firmness of your faith \[G-PISTIS\] in Christ.

**6** Therefore, as you received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in him,

**7** rooted and built up in him and established in the faith \[G-PISTIS\], just as you were taught, abounding in thanksgiving.

**8** See to it that no one takes you captive by philosophy and empty deceit, according to human tradition, according to the elemental forces of the world, and not according to Christ.

**9** For in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily,

**10** and you have been filled in him, who is the head of all rule and authority.

**11** In him also you were circumcised with a circumcision made without hands, by putting off the body of the flesh, by the circumcision of Christ,

**12** having been buried with him in baptism, in which you were also raised with him through faith \[G-PISTIS\] in the powerful working of God, who raised him from the dead.

**13** And you, who were dead in your trespasses and the uncircumcision of your flesh, God made alive together with him, having forgiven us all our trespasses,

**14** by cancelling the record of debt that stood against us with its legal demands. This he set aside, nailing it to the cross.

**15** He disarmed the rulers and authorities and put them to open shame, triumphing over them in him.

**16** Therefore let no one pass judgement on you in questions of food and drink, or with regard to a festival or a new moon or a sabbath \[G-SABBATON\].

**17** These are a shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ.

**18** Let no one disqualify you, insisting on asceticism and worship of angels, going on in detail about visions, puffed up without reason by his sensuous mind,

**19** and not holding fast to the head, from whom the whole body, nourished and knit together through its joints and ligaments, grows with a growth that is from God.

**20** If with Christ you died to the elemental forces of the world, why, as if you were still alive in the world, do you submit to regulations,

**21** "Do not handle, do not taste, do not touch"

**22** (referring to things that all perish as they are used), according to human precepts and teachings?

**23** These have indeed an appearance of wisdom in promoting self-made religion and asceticism and severity to the body, but they are of no value in stopping the indulgence of the flesh.

### Chapter 3

**1** If then you have been raised with Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\], seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God.

**2** Set your minds on things that are above, not on things that are on earth.

**3** For you have died, and your life \[G-ZOE\] is hidden with Christ in God.

**4** When Christ who is your life \[G-ZOE\] appears, then you also will appear with him in glory \[G-DOXA\].

**5** Put to death therefore what is earthly in you: sexual immorality, impurity, passion, evil desire, and greed, which is idolatry.

**6** On account of these the wrath of God is coming.

**7** In these you too once walked, when you were living in them.

**8** But now you must put them all away: anger, wrath, malice, slander, and obscene talk from your mouth.

**9** Do not lie to one another, seeing that you have put off the old self with its practices

**10** and have put on the new self, which is being renewed in knowledge after the image of its creator.

**11** Here there is not Greek and Jew, circumcised and uncircumcised, foreigner, Scythian, slave, free, but Christ is all, and in all.

**12** Put on then, as God's chosen ones, holy \[G-HAGIOS\] and loved \[G-AGAPE\], compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience,

**13** bearing with one another and, if one has a complaint against another, forgiving each other. As the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive.

**14** And above all these put on love \[G-AGAPE\], which binds everything together in perfect harmony.

**15** And let the peace \[G-EIRENE\] of Christ rule in your hearts, to which indeed you were called in one body. And be thankful.

**16** Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly, teaching and admonishing one another in all wisdom, singing psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, with thankfulness in your hearts to God.

**17** And whatever you do, in word or deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him.

**18** Wives, submit to your husbands, as is fitting in the Lord.

**19** Husbands, love \[G-AGAPE\] your wives, and do not be harsh with them.

**20** Children, obey your parents in everything, for this pleases the Lord.

**21** Fathers, do not provoke your children, lest they become discouraged.

**22** Slaves, obey in everything those who are your earthly masters, not with eye-service, as people-pleasers, but with sincerity of heart, fearing the Lord.

**23** Whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for people,

**24** knowing that from the Lord you will receive the inheritance as your reward. You are serving the Lord Christ.

**25** For the wrongdoer will be paid back for the wrong he has done, and there is no partiality.

### Chapter 4

**1** Masters, treat your slaves justly \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\] and fairly, knowing that you also have a Master in heaven.

**2** Continue steadfastly in prayer, being watchful in it with thanksgiving.

**3** At the same time pray also for us, that God may open to us a door for the word, to declare the mystery of Christ, on account of which I am in prison,

**4** that I may make it plain, as I ought to speak.

**5** Walk in wisdom toward outsiders, making the best use of the time.

**6** Let your speech always be gracious \[G-CHARIS\], seasoned with salt, so that you may know how you ought to answer each person.

**7** Tychicus will tell you all about my activities. He is a beloved brother and faithful minister and fellow slave in the Lord.

**8** I have sent him to you for this very purpose, that you may know how we are and that he may encourage your hearts,

**9** and with him Onesimus, our faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you. They will tell you of everything that has taken place here.

**10** Aristarchus my fellow prisoner greets you, and Mark the cousin of Barnabas (concerning whom you have received instructions; if he comes to you, welcome him),

**11** and Jesus who is called Justus. These are the only men of the circumcision among my fellow workers for the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] of God, and they have been a comfort to me.

**12** Epaphras, who is one of you, a servant of Christ Jesus, greets you, always struggling on your behalf in his prayers, that you may stand mature and fully assured in all the will of God.

**13** For I bear him witness that he has worked hard for you and for those in Laodicea and in Hierapolis.

**14** Luke the beloved physician greets you, as does Demas.

**15** Give my greetings to the brothers at Laodicea, and to Nympha and the assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\] in her house.

**16** And when this letter has been read among you, have it also read in the assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\] of the Laodiceans, and see that you also read the letter from Laodicea.

**17** And say to Archippus, "See that you fulfil the ministry that you have received in the Lord."

**18** I, Paul, write this greeting with my own hand. Remember my chains. Grace \[G-CHARIS\] be with you.

*Review note: Colossians 1 to 4 were drafted in WABV wording, from the Greek, using the fixed choices set out in the introduction to this book, and with the WABV voice standard (Part 46 of the review package) applied, and the hymn of 1:15-20 is set in lines. They are the greeting (1:1-2), the thanksgiving and the report of Epaphras (1:3-8), the prayer for the Colossians (1:9-14), the hymn of Christ (1:15-20), the reconciliation of the Colossians (1:21-23), the ministry of Paul and the mystery (1:24-29), the struggle of Paul for the Colossians and Laodicea (2:1-5), the warning against the philosophy of the elemental forces (2:6-15), the warning against rules about food, festivals and angels (2:16-23), seeking the things above (3:1-4), putting off the old self and putting on the new (3:5-17), the household code (3:18-4:1), prayer and conduct toward outsiders (4:2-6) and the messengers, the greetings and the closing (4:7-18). The numbering is the usual numbering. No new G- marker is used, and the markers are G-CHRISTOS (1:1-3:1), G-HAGIOS (1:2-3:12), G-CHARIS (1:2-4:18), G-EIRENE (1:2-3:15), G-PISTIS (1:4-2:12), G-AGAPE (1:4-3:19), G-ALETHEIA (1:5), G-EUANGELION (1:5-23), G-PNEUMA (1:8, 2:5), G-DOXA (1:11-3:4), G-BASILEIA (1:13, 4:11), G-LYTRON (1:14), G-EKKLESIA (1:18-4:16), G-SABBATON (2:16), G-ZOE (3:3-4) and G-DIKAIOSUNE (4:1). In 1:7 "on our behalf" is the reading of the critical text (*huper hemon*), and some manuscripts read "on your behalf" (*huper humon*), and the footnote records the matter. In 1:12 "who has qualified you" is the reading of the critical text (*humas*), and some manuscripts read "us" (*hemas*), and the footnote records the matter. In 1:16 "thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities" is rendered as written. In 1:17 "in him all things hold together" is rendered as written. In 1:19 "all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell" is rendered as written, and the subject of "was pleased" is not stated, and may be read "all the fullness" or "God", and the footnote records the matter. In 1:20 "to reconcile to himself all things" is rendered as written, and "making peace by the blood of his cross" is rendered as written, and the footnote records that "through him" is repeated in some manuscripts after "whether". In 1:22 "in his body of flesh" translates *en to somati tes sarkos autou*. In 1:23 "in all creation under heaven" is rendered as written. In 1:26-27 "the mystery hidden for ages and generations but now revealed to his holy ones" and "Christ in you, the hope of glory" are rendered as written. In 1:27 "the nations" translates *ethnesin*. In 2:1 "Laodicea" is a city about eleven miles from Colossae. In 2:9 "the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily" is rendered as written. In 2:14 "the record of debt that stood against us with its legal demands" translates *to kath' hemon cheirographon tois dogmasin*, and the footnote records that the *cheirographon* is a handwritten note of debt. In 2:16 "a sabbath" translates *sabbaton*, and it is tagged G-SABBATON. In 2:17 "a shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ" is rendered as written. In 2:18 "asceticism" translates *tapeinophrosune*, literally "humility". In 3:11 "foreigner" translates *barbaros*, and "Scythian" is the word for a people who were thought the most uncivilised, and the footnote records the matter. In 3:12 "holy and loved" is rendered as written, and "holy" translates *hagioi*. In 3:13 "as the Lord has forgiven you" is the reading of the critical text (*ho kurios*), and some manuscripts read "Christ" or "God", and the footnote records the matter. In 3:15 "the peace of Christ" is the reading of the critical text, and some manuscripts read "the peace of God", and the footnote records the matter. In 3:22-25 "slaves" translates *douloi*, and "masters" translates *kurioi*, and the passage is rendered as written, and the footnote records that it presupposes the institution of slavery and does not address it, and that it is connected with the letter to Philemon. In 3:22 "people-pleasers" translates *anthropareskoi*. In 4:1 "justly and fairly" translates *to dikaion kai ten isoteta*, and *dikaion* is tagged G-DIKAIOSUNE because it is from the same root, and the footnote records that *isotes* may also be read "equality". In 4:3 "a door for the word" is rendered as written. In 4:6 "gracious, seasoned with salt" is rendered as written, and "gracious" translates *en chariti*. In 4:9 "Onesimus" is the slave of Philemon (Philemon 10-12). In 4:10 "Aristarchus my fellow prisoner" is named in Acts 19:29, 20:4 and 27:2 and Philemon 24, and "Mark the cousin of Barnabas" is the John Mark of Acts 12:12 and 15:37-39. In 4:11 "Jesus who is called Justus" is named only here, and "the men of the circumcision" is rendered as written. In 4:14 "Luke the beloved physician" is rendered as written. In 4:15 "Nympha" is the reading of the critical text (the accusative *Numphan*, which may be a woman's name, Nympha, or a man's name, Nymphas), and the manuscripts differ on "her house" (*autes*), "his house" (*autou*) and "their house" (*auton*), and the footnote records the matter, and the team should decide. In 4:16 "the letter from Laodicea" is rendered as written, and the footnote records that it may be a lost letter or the letter that is called Ephesians. In 4:17 "Archippus" is also named in Philemon 2. In 4:18 "with my own hand" is the Greek of the text, and "remember my chains" is rendered as written. In 1:2, 4:15 "brothers" translates *adelphoi*, and the team should decide whether it is to include the sisters. In 3:11 "Greek and Jew" is rendered as written. There are no departures from the critical Greek text in chapters 1 to 4. The Greek is hard in some places, including 1:15-20, 1:24, 2:2, 2:8-23, 3:11, and 4:15. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The section needs a check against the Greek by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked. With this the book of Colossians is complete in first draft.*

- **2:12** "Having been buried with him in baptism" is rendered as written. See entry G-PISTIS.
- **2:14** "The record of debt that stood against us with its legal demands" translates *to kath' hemon cheirographon tois dogmasin*, and the *cheirographon* is a handwritten note of debt.
- **2:15** "He disarmed the rulers and authorities" is rendered as written, and the subject (God or Christ) is not stated, and "in him" may be read "in it" (the cross).
- **2:16** "A sabbath" translates *sabbaton*. See entry G-SABBATON.
- **2:17** "A shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ" is rendered as written.
- **2:18** "Worship of angels" may be read "worship that angels perform" or "worship offered to angels". "Going on in detail about visions" translates *ha heoraken embateuon*, which is one of the hardest phrases in the New Testament, and is also read "taking his stand on what he has seen" or "entering into what he has seen". The WABV text does not decide. "Asceticism" translates *tapeinophrosune*, literally "humility".
- **2:21** "Do not handle, do not taste, do not touch" is a set of slogans of the teachers.
- **2:23** "Self-made religion" translates *ethelothreskia*, and "they are of no value in stopping the indulgence of the flesh" is rendered as written, and the Greek is hard.
- **3:1** "Seated at the right hand of God" is rendered as written. See entry G-CHRISTOS.
- **3:3** "Life" translates *zoe*. See entry G-ZOE.
- **3:5** "Greed, which is idolatry" is rendered as written.
- **3:6** "On account of these the wrath of God is coming" is the reading of the critical text, and some manuscripts add "on the sons of disobedience", and Papyrus 46 and Vaticanus omit it.
- **3:11** "Foreigner" translates *barbaros*, and "Scythian" is the word for a people who were thought the most uncivilised.
- **3:12** "Holy" translates *hagioi*, and "loved" translates *egapemenoi*. See entries G-HAGIOS and G-AGAPE.
- **3:13** "As the Lord has forgiven you" is the reading of the critical text (*ho kurios*), and some manuscripts read "Christ" or "God".
- **3:15** "The peace of Christ" is the reading of the critical text, and some manuscripts read "the peace of God".
- **3:16** "The word of Christ" is the reading of the critical text, and some manuscripts read "the word of God" or "of the Lord". "Psalms and hymns and spiritual songs" is as in Ephesians 5:19.
- **3:18** The household code in 3:18 to 4:1 is rendered as written, and it is shorter than that of Ephesians 5:22 to 6:9.
- **3:22** "Slaves" translates *douloi*, "masters" translates *kurioi*, and "people-pleasers" translates *anthropareskoi*. The passage on slaves and masters presupposes the institution of slavery and does not address it, and it is connected with the letter to Philemon.
- **4:1** "Justly and fairly" translates *to dikaion kai ten isoteta*, and *dikaion* is tagged G-DIKAIOSUNE because it is from the same root, and *isotes* may also be read "equality".
- **4:3** "A door for the word" is rendered as written.
- **4:6** "Gracious, seasoned with salt" is rendered as written, and "gracious" translates *en chariti*. See entry G-CHARIS.
- **4:9** "Onesimus" is the slave of Philemon (Philemon 10-12).
- **4:10** "Aristarchus my fellow prisoner" is named in Acts 19:29, 20:4 and 27:2, and Philemon 24, and "Mark the cousin of Barnabas" is the John Mark of Acts 12:12 and 15:37-39.
- **4:11** "Jesus who is called Justus" is named only here, and "the men of the circumcision" is rendered as written. See entry G-BASILEIA.
- **4:14** "Luke the beloved physician" is rendered as written.
- **4:15** "Nympha" is the reading of the critical text (the accusative *Numphan*, which may be a woman's name, Nympha, or a man's name, Nymphas), and the manuscripts differ on "her house" (*autes*), "his house" (*autou*) and "their house" (*auton*). The team should decide. See entry G-EKKLESIA.
- **4:16** "The letter from Laodicea" is rendered as written, and it may be a lost letter or the letter that is called Ephesians.
- **4:17** "Archippus" is also named in Philemon 2.
- **4:18** "With my own hand" is the Greek of the text, and "remember my chains" is rendered as written. See entry G-CHARIS.
