# WABV: Habakkuk

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-RUACH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Habakkuk is the eighth of the twelve books of the Minor Prophets in the Protestant order that the WABV text follows. It is in Hebrew and in poetry, in the form of a dialogue between the prophet and the LORD, with a psalm-like prayer in chapter 3. The book is called an oracle that Habakkuk the prophet saw (1:1), and it has no date of its own. It speaks of the rise of the Chaldeans (1:6), and the WABV text does not decide more than that. The chapter and verse numbering is the English numbering, and in Habakkuk it is the same as the Hebrew numbering. The book falls into three parts in the WABV text: the complaint of the prophet and the answer of the LORD (chapter 1), the watchtower, the vision and the five woes (chapter 2), and the prayer of Habakkuk (chapter 3). The headings of the parts are not in the Hebrew. The prayer has a title (3:1) and a closing note for the choirmaster (3:19), and the word *Selah* in 3:3, 3:9 and 3:13, and the footnotes record the matter. Two lines are the same in the Hebrew as other books, and the wording of the lines that are the same is the same in the WABV text: 2:14, which is close to Isaiah 11:9, and 3:19, which is the same in part as Psalm 18:33 (and 2 Samuel 22:34). The Hebrew of Habakkuk is mostly clear in chapters 1 and 2 and very hard in places in chapter 3, and the footnotes record the other readings. The Dead Sea Scrolls have a commentary on chapters 1 and 2, and the footnotes record the places where its text differs from the Hebrew. The names of God are given as in the earlier books: "the LORD" for YHWH, "Lord" for Adonai, and "the Lord GOD" for Adonai YHWH. The WABV text does not decide on questions of the growth of the book, and the footnotes record only the language.*

## Part One: Chapter 1, the complaint and the answer

### Chapter 1

**1** The oracle that Habakkuk the prophet saw.

**2** How long, LORD, must I cry for help, and you do not listen? I cry out to you, "Violence," and you do not save.

**3** Why do you make me see iniquity and look on trouble? Destruction and violence are before me. Strife arises, and contention lifts its head.

**4** So the law \[H-TORAH\] is paralysed, and justice never goes forth. For the wicked surround the righteous \[H-TSEDAQAH\], so justice goes out crooked.

**5** Look among the nations and see. Be astonished and astounded. For I am doing a work in your days that you would not believe if it were told.

**6** For see, I am raising up the Chaldeans, that bitter and impetuous nation, who march through the breadth of the earth to seize dwellings that are not theirs.

**7** They are dreaded and fearsome. Their justice and their dignity go forth from themselves.

**8** Their horses are swifter than leopards, fiercer than wolves at dusk. Their horsemen press on. Their horsemen come from afar. They fly like an eagle swooping to devour.

**9** All of them come for violence. Their faces are set forward. They gather captives like sand.

**10** At kings they scoff, and rulers are a laughing matter to them. They laugh at every fortress, for they pile up earth and take it.

**11** Then they sweep on like the wind \[H-RUACH\] and go on, guilty people, whose own might is their god.

**12** Are you not from of old, LORD my God, my Holy One \[H-QADOSH\]? We shall not die. LORD, you have appointed them as a judgement, and you, Rock, have established them for correction.

**13** You whose eyes are too pure to see evil and who cannot look on wrong, why do you look on the treacherous and are silent when the wicked swallows up one more righteous \[H-TSEDAQAH\] than he?

**14** You make human beings like the fish of the sea, like crawling things that have no ruler.

**15** He brings all of them up with a hook, he drags them out with his net, he gathers them in his dragnet. So he rejoices and is glad.

**16** Therefore he sacrifices to his net and makes offerings to his dragnet, for by them his portion is rich and his food plentiful.

**17** Is he then to keep on emptying his net and killing nations without mercy for ever?

*Review note: Habakkuk 1 was drafted in WABV wording. It is the heading (1:1), the first complaint of the prophet (1:2-4), the answer of the LORD that he is raising up the Chaldeans (1:5-11) and the second complaint (1:12-17). The numbering is the same as the Hebrew. The whole chapter is in poetry, apart from the heading in 1:1, and the verses are set in lines. In 1:4 "the law" translates torah and is tagged H-TORAH. In 1:4 and 1:13 "the righteous" and "one more righteous" translate tsaddiq and tsaddiq mimmennu and are tagged H-TSEDAQAH. In 1:11 "the wind" translates ruach and is tagged H-RUACH. In 1:12 "my Holy One" translates qedoshi and is tagged H-QADOSH, and "Rock" translates tsur, a name of God, and is not tagged. In 1:12 the Hebrew has "we shall not die" (*lo namut*), and a scribal tradition records that the original was "you do not die" (*lo tamut*), and the footnote records the matter. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapter 1. The Dead Sea commentary on Habakkuk reads the text of chapter 1 with a few differences, and the footnotes record the main ones. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 1:7, 1:9 (*megammat*), 1:11 and 1:12. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the Chaldeans (1:6-11) and on the net that kills nations (1:15-17) are rendered as written, with no comment. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Two: Chapter 2, the vision and the five woes

### Chapter 2

**1** I will take my stand at my watchpost and station myself on the tower, and look out to see what he will say to me, and what I will answer concerning my complaint.

**2** And the LORD answered me: Write down the vision, and make it clear on tablets, so that the one who reads it may run.

**3** For the vision still awaits its appointed time. It hurries toward the end and does not lie. If it seems slow, wait for it. It will surely come. It will not delay.

**4** See, the proud one, his soul \[H-NEPHESH\] is not upright within him, but the righteous \[H-TSEDAQAH\] will live by his faithfulness.

**5** Moreover, wine is treacherous. The arrogant man does not abide. His greed \[H-NEPHESH\] is as wide as Sheol \[H-SHEOL\], and like death he is never satisfied. He gathers to himself all nations and collects all peoples as his own.

**6** Will not all these take up a taunt against him, in scoffing and riddles against him, and say: Woe to the one who piles up what is not his own (for how long?) and loads himself with pledges.

**7** Will not your creditors suddenly arise, and those awake who will make you tremble? Then you will be plunder for them.

**8** Because you have plundered many nations, all that are left of the peoples will plunder you, for the blood of human beings and violence to the earth, to cities and all who live in them.

**9** Woe to the one who gains evil gain for his house, to set his nest on high, to be safe from the reach of harm.

**10** You have devised shame for your house by cutting off many peoples. You have forfeited your life \[H-NEPHESH\].

**11** For the stone cries out from the wall, and the beam from the woodwork answers.

**12** Woe to the one who builds a town with blood and founds a city on iniquity.

**13** Is it not from the LORD of hosts that peoples labour for fire, and nations weary themselves for nothing?

**14** For the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD as the waters cover the sea.

**15** Woe to the one who makes his neighbours drink, pouring out your wrath and making them drunk, in order to gaze at their nakedness.

**16** You will have your fill of shame instead of glory. Drink, you yourself, and show your uncircumcision. The cup in the LORD's right hand will come around to you, and shame will come on your glory.

**17** The violence done to Lebanon will overwhelm you, as will the destruction of the animals that terrified them, for the blood of human beings and violence to the earth, to cities and all who live in them.

**18** What profit is an idol when its maker has shaped it, a metal image, a teacher of lies? For its maker trusts in his own creation when he makes speechless idols.

**19** Woe to the one who says to a wooden thing, "Awake," to a silent stone, "Arise." Can this teach? See, it is overlaid with gold and silver, and there is no breath \[H-RUACH\] at all in it.

**20** But the LORD is in his holy \[H-QADOSH\] temple. Let all the earth be silent before him.

*Review note: Habakkuk 2 was drafted in WABV wording. It is the watchpost and the answer of the LORD, the vision that awaits its time (2:1-3), the proud one and the righteous who lives by faithfulness (2:4-5), and the five woes against the plunderer, the one who builds on evil gain, the one who builds with blood, the one who makes his neighbours drink and the one who trusts in idols (2:6-19), and the call to silence before the LORD in his holy temple (2:20). The numbering is the same as the Hebrew. The whole chapter is in poetry, and the verses are set in lines. In 2:4 "his soul" translates nafsho and is tagged H-NEPHESH, in 2:5 "his greed" translates nafsho, literally "his appetite", and in 2:10 "your life" translates nafshekha, and each is tagged H-NEPHESH. In 2:4 "the righteous" translates tsaddiq and is tagged H-TSEDAQAH. In 2:5 "Sheol" is tagged H-SHEOL. In 2:19 "breath" translates ruach and is tagged H-RUACH. In 2:20 "holy" is tagged H-QADOSH. The words of 2:14 ("as the waters cover the sea") are the same in the Hebrew as Isaiah 11:9, and are given the same wording as the WABV text of Isaiah, and the footnote records that Habakkuk adds "the glory of" and has "the earth will be filled" where Isaiah has "the earth will be full". In 2:4 "his faithfulness" translates emunato, and the Greek has "my faithfulness" and the Dead Sea commentary has "their faithfulness", and the footnote records the matter, because the line is quoted in Romans 1:17, Galatians 3:11 and Hebrews 10:38. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapter 2. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 2:4-5, 2:6 (*chitevot*, "pledges"), 2:16 (*ve-hei'arel*, "show your uncircumcision") and 2:17. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the blood of the cities (2:8, 2:12, 2:17) and the nakedness of the neighbours (2:15) are rendered as written, with no comment. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Three: Chapter 3, the prayer of Habakkuk

### Chapter 3

**1** A prayer of Habakkuk the prophet, according to Shigionoth.

**2** LORD, I have heard the report of you, and your work, LORD, I fear \[H-YIRAH\]. In the midst of the years revive it, in the midst of the years make it known. In wrath remember mercy.

**3** God came from Teman, and the Holy One \[H-QADOSH\] from Mount Paran. *Selah* His glory covered the heavens, and the earth was full of his praise.

**4** His brightness was like the light, rays flashed from his hand, and there his power was hidden.

**5** Before him went pestilence, and plague followed at his heels.

**6** He stood and measured the earth. He looked and made the nations tremble. The ancient mountains were shattered, the everlasting hills sank low. His ways are everlasting.

**7** I saw the tents of Cushan under affliction. The tent curtains of the land of Midian trembled.

**8** Was your wrath against the rivers, LORD? Was your anger against the rivers, or your indignation against the sea, when you rode on your horses, on your chariot of salvation?

**9** You stripped the sheath from your bow, sworn were the rods of the tribes. *Selah* You split the earth with rivers.

**10** The mountains saw you and writhed. The raging waters swept on. The deep gave its voice, it lifted its hands on high.

**11** Sun and moon stood still in their place, at the light of your arrows as they sped, at the flash of your glittering spear.

**12** In fury you marched through the earth, in anger you trampled the nations.

**13** You went out for the salvation of your people, for the salvation of your anointed one \[H-MASHIACH\]. You crushed the head of the house of the wicked, laying him bare from foundation to neck. *Selah*

**14** You pierced with his own arrows the heads of his warriors, who came like a whirlwind to scatter me, rejoicing as if to devour the poor in secret.

**15** You trampled the sea with your horses, churning the great waters.

**16** I heard, and my body trembled. My lips quivered at the sound. Rottenness entered my bones, and my legs trembled beneath me. Yet I will quietly wait for the day of trouble to come on the people who invade us.

**17** Though the fig tree does not blossom, and no fruit is on the vines, the produce of the olive fails, and the fields yield no food, the flock is cut off from the fold, and there is no herd in the stalls,

**18** yet I will rejoice in the LORD. I will exult in the God of my salvation.

**19** God, the Lord, is my strength. He makes my feet like the feet of a deer and makes me walk on my heights. For the choirmaster: with stringed instruments.

*Review note: Habakkuk 3 was drafted in WABV wording. It is the title and the prayer for the work of the LORD to be made known (3:1-2), the coming of God from Teman and the storm (3:3-7), the questions on the rivers and the sea and the war of the LORD (3:8-15), the trembling of the prophet (3:16) and the confession of joy (3:17-19). The numbering is the same as the Hebrew. The whole chapter is a prayer in poetry, with a title (3:1), the word *Selah* in 3:3, 3:9 and 3:13, and a closing note to the choirmaster (3:19), and the verses are set in lines. In 3:2 "I fear" translates yare'ti and is tagged H-YIRAH. In 3:3 "the Holy One" translates qadosh and is tagged H-QADOSH. In 3:13 "your anointed one" translates meshichekha and is tagged H-MASHIACH, and the rendering is open to the team's decision with Isaiah 45:1, Lamentations 4:20 and Daniel 9:25-26 ("anointed" or "messiah"). The words of 3:19 ("he makes my feet like the feet of a deer") are the same in the Hebrew as Psalm 18:33 (and 2 Samuel 22:34), and are given the same wording as the WABV text of Psalm 18:33, including "heights" for *bamot*. The words of 3:3 ("God came from Teman, and the Holy One from Mount Paran") are close to Deuteronomy 33:2, and the footnotes record the match. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapter 3, and none in the whole book. The Hebrew is very hard in places, including 3:2 (*be-qerev shanim*), 3:4 (*qarnayim*), 3:5 (*resheph*), 3:7, 3:9-10, 3:13-14 and 3:16. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the war of the LORD against the nations (3:12-15) and the crushing of the head of the house of the wicked (3:13) are rendered as written, with no comment. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Footnotes

- **1:1** "The oracle" translates *massa*, which may also be "the burden", and it is part of the open decision on the word in the WABV text. "Habakkuk" translates *Chavaqquq*, and "saw" translates *chazah*.
- **1:2** "How long" translates *ad-anah*, "cry for help" translates *shiwwa'ti*, "violence" translates *chamas*, and "save" translates *toshia*.
- **1:3** "Iniquity" translates *aven*, "trouble" translates *amal*, "destruction" translates *shod*, and "strife" and "contention" translate *riv* and *madon*.
- **1:4** "Is paralysed" translates *tafug*, "the law" translates *torah*, and "justice" translates *mishpat*. See entry H-TORAH. "The righteous" translates *ha-tsaddiq*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. "Crooked" translates *me'uqqal*.
- **1:5** "Look among the nations" follows the Hebrew (*re'u va-goyim*). The Dead Sea scrolls and the Greek read "look, you scoffers" (*bogedim*), which Acts 13:41 follows. "A work" translates *po'al*.
- **1:6** "The Chaldeans" translates *ha-Kasdim*, "bitter" translates *ha-mar*, and "impetuous" translates *ha-nimhar*.
- **1:7** "Their dignity" translates *se'eto*.
- **1:8** "Wolves at dusk" translates *ze'evei erev*, "press on" translates *u-fashu*, and "swooping" translates *chush*.
- **1:9** "Their faces are set forward" translates *megammat penehem qadimah*, which is very hard (*megammat* is rare, and *qadimah* may mean "forward" or "eastward"). "Captives" translates *shevi*.
- **1:10** "Scoff" translates *yitqallasu*, "laugh" translates *yischaq*, and "pile up earth" translates *ve-yitsbor afar*.
- **1:11** "Sweep on like the wind" translates *chalaf ruach*. See entry H-RUACH. "Guilty people" translates *ve-asham*, which is hard, and "whose own might is their god" translates *zu kocho le-eloho*.
- **1:12** "From of old" translates *miqqedem*, and "my Holy One" translates *qedoshi*. See entry H-QADOSH. "We shall not die" translates *lo namut*. A scribal tradition records that the original reading was "you do not die" (*lo tamut*). "Rock" translates *tsur*, a name of God, as in Deuteronomy 32:4 and the Psalms, and it is not tagged. "A judgement" translates *le-mishpat*, and "correction" translates *le-hokhiach*.
- **1:13** "Eyes too pure" translates *tehor einayim*, "the treacherous" translates *bogedim*, and "swallows up" translates *tevalla*. "One more righteous" translates *tsaddiq mimmennu*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH.
- **1:14** "You make human beings like the fish of the sea" translates *va-ta'aseh adam kidgei ha-yam*.
- **1:15** "A hook" translates *chakkah*, "his net" translates *chermo*, in which *cherem* means "a net" and is not the ban, so it is not tagged H-CHEREM (as in Micah 7:2), and "his dragnet" translates *mikhmarto*.
- **1:16** "He sacrifices to his net" translates *yezabbe'ach le-chermo*.
- **1:17** "Emptying" translates *yariq*, and "killing" translates *laharog*.
- **2:1** "My watchpost" translates *mishmarti*, "the tower" translates *matsor*, and "my complaint" translates *tokhachti*, which may also be read "my being reproved". "What he will say to me" translates *mah-yedabber bi*, and the Greek has "what he will say in me".
- **2:2** "Tablets" translates *ha-luchot*, and "the one who reads it may run" translates *lema'an yarutz qore bo*, which may also be read "so that the one who reads it may run with it".
- **2:3** "Its appointed time" translates *la-mo'ed*, "hurries" translates *yafe'ach*, "does not lie" translates *lo yekhazzev*, and "will not delay" translates *lo ye'acher*. The Hebrews 10:37 quotation follows the Greek.
- **2:4** "The proud one" translates *ufelah*, and "his soul is not upright within him" translates *lo-yashrah nafsho vo*. See entry H-NEPHESH. The text of this half is hard, and the Dead Sea commentary reads it with differences. "The righteous" translates *tsaddiq*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. "By his faithfulness" translates *be-emunato*. The Greek reads "by my faithfulness" (*ek pisteos mou*), and the Dead Sea commentary reads "by their faithfulness". The line is quoted in Romans 1:17, Galatians 3:11 and Hebrews 10:38.
- **2:5** "Wine is treacherous" translates *ha-yayin bogéd*, and the Dead Sea scrolls read "wealth" (*hon*). "The arrogant man" translates *gever yahir*. "His greed is as wide as Sheol" translates *hirchiv ki-She'ol nafsho*. See entries H-NEPHESH and H-SHEOL.
- **2:6** "A taunt" translates *mashal*, "riddles" translates *chidot*, and "pledges" translates *avtit*, which is rare and hard, and may also be read "heavy debts".
- **2:7** "Your creditors" translates *noshekha*, literally "those who bite you", and "those awake who will make you tremble" translates *ve-yaqitsu mezi'ekha*.
- **2:8** "Violence" translates *chamas*.
- **2:9** "Evil gain" translates *betsa ra*, and "his nest" translates *qinno*.
- **2:10** "You have forfeited your life" translates *chotè nafshekha*. See entry H-NEPHESH.
- **2:11** "The beam" translates *kafis*, which is rare.
- **2:12** "Builds a town with blood" translates *boneh ir be-damim*.
- **2:13** "Labour for fire" translates *yigge'u ammim be-dei esh*, and "for nothing" translates *be-dei-riq*.
- **2:14** "The earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD as the waters cover the sea" translates *ki timmale ha-arets la-da'at et-kevod YHWH ka-mayim mekhassim al-yam*. The words "as the waters cover the sea" are the same as Isaiah 11:9, and are given the same wording as the WABV text of Isaiah. Habakkuk adds "the glory of" and has "will be filled" where Isaiah has "will be full". See also Numbers 14:21.
- **2:15** "Pouring out your wrath" translates *mosephakh chamatekha*, which is hard, and the Dead Sea scrolls differ. "In order to gaze at their nakedness" translates *lema'an habbit al-me'oreihem*.
- **2:16** "Show your uncircumcision" translates *ve-hei'arel*. The Dead Sea scrolls and the Greek read "and stagger" (*ve-hera'el*). "Shame will come on your glory" translates *qiqalon al-kevodekha*.
- **2:17** "The violence done to Lebanon" translates *chamas Levanon*, and "the destruction of the animals" translates *shod behemot*. The verse is hard.
- **2:18** "Profit" translates *mah-hoshia*, "a metal image" translates *massekhah*, "a teacher of lies" translates *moreh sheqer*, and "speechless idols" translates *elilim illemim*.
- **2:19** "A wooden thing" translates *la-etz*, and "breath" translates *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH.
- **2:20** "His holy temple" translates *heikhal qodsho*. See entry H-QADOSH. "Be silent" translates *has*, as in Zephaniah 1:7.
- **3:1** "A prayer" translates *tefillah*, and "Shigionoth" translates *shigyonot*, a word of unknown meaning, perhaps a musical or literary term (see the title of Psalm 7).
- **3:2** "The report of you" translates *shim'akha*, and "I fear" translates *yare'ti*. See entry H-YIRAH. "Revive it" translates *chayyehu*, and "in the midst of the years" translates *be-qerev shanim*, which may also be read "in the midst of the years" as a time that is near. "Remember mercy" translates *rachem tizkor*.
- **3:3** "God" translates *Eloah*, "Teman" and "Mount Paran" are places in the south, as in Deuteronomy 33:2 and Judges 5:4, and "the Holy One" translates *qadosh*. See entry H-QADOSH. *Selah* is a word of unknown meaning, as in the Psalms, and it is not translated. "His praise" translates *tehillato*.
- **3:4** "His brightness" translates *nogah*, "rays" translates *qarnayim*, literally "horns", and "his power was hidden" translates *chevyon uzzoh*.
- **3:5** "Pestilence" translates *dever*, and "plague" translates *resheph*, which is also the name of a god of plague.
- **3:6** "He stood and measured" translates *amad va-yomeded*, and "made the nations tremble" translates *va-yatter goyim*, which is hard and may also be read "made the nations leap". "The ancient mountains" translates *harerei ad*, and "his ways" translates *halikhot*.
- **3:7** "Cushan" translates *Kushan*, and "the tent curtains" translates *yeri'ot*.
- **3:8** "Your chariot of salvation" translates *markevotekha yeshu'ah*.
- **3:9** "Sworn were the rods of the tribes" translates *shevu'ot mattot omer*, which is very hard.
- **3:10** "Writhed" translates *yacholu*, "the raging waters" translates *zerem mayim*, and "it lifted its hands on high" translates *nasa rom yadeha*.
- **3:11** "Stood still in their place" translates *amad zevulah*, "your arrows" translates *chitsatsekha*, and "your glittering spear" translates *nogah chanitekha*.
- **3:12** "Marched" translates *tits'ad*, and "trampled" translates *tadush*.
- **3:13** "Your anointed one" translates *meshichekha*. See entry H-MASHIACH. The Hebrew has the singular, which may refer to the king or to the people, and the Greek has "anointed ones".
