# WABV: Philippians

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[G-CHRISTOS\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Philippians is the eleventh book of the New Testament in the order that the WABV text follows, and the sixth of the letters. It is written in Greek, and the WABV text is drafted from a critical Greek text, with the Byzantine tradition noted in the footnotes where it differs, as the Charter requires. It is a letter from Paul and Timothy to the holy ones in Philippi, with their overseers and servants, written from prison (1:7, 1:13-14, 1:17), and it is a letter of thanks for a gift that Epaphroditus brought (2:25, 4:10-18) and of joy: the word for joy and the verb for rejoicing are used about sixteen times in four chapters. The place of writing (Rome, Ephesus or Caesarea) and its unity are debated, since many scholars hold that 3:2 to 4:1 or 4:10-20 are separate pieces, and the WABV text keeps the order of the manuscripts and does not decide. The chapter and verse numbering is the usual numbering. The WABV text of Philippians is drafted with the voice standard (Part 46 of the review package), and it sets the hymn of 2:6-11 in lines, as the standard requires for poetry and quotations. It makes a number of fixed choices that follow from its Greek, and they are written down here so that they are used throughout. *Episkopoi* and *diakonoi* (1:1) are rendered "overseers" and "servants", and the footnote records "bishops" and "deacons". *Hagioi* is rendered "holy ones" and is tagged G-HAGIOS. *Ekklesia* is rendered "assembly" and is tagged G-EKKLESIA, and the team should decide between "assembly" and "church". *Morphe* (2:6-7) is rendered "form", and *harpagmos* (2:6) "something to be seized", and *ekenosen* (2:7) "emptied himself", and the footnote records the alternatives. *Politeuma* (3:20) is rendered "citizenship". *Skubala* (3:8) is rendered "rubbish". *Dikaiosune* is rendered "righteousness" and is tagged G-DIKAIOSUNE, and *pistis Christou* at 3:9 is rendered "faith in Christ", and the footnote records "the faithfulness of Christ", and the team should decide. *Pistis* is rendered "faith" and *pisteuo* "believe", and both are tagged G-PISTIS. *Euangelion* is rendered "good news" and is tagged G-EUANGELION. *Agape* is rendered "love" and is tagged G-AGAPE. *Charis* is rendered "grace" and is tagged G-CHARIS. *Eirene* is rendered "peace" and is tagged G-EIRENE. *Doxa* is rendered "glory" and is tagged G-DOXA. *Sozo* and *soteria* are rendered "save" and "salvation" or "deliverance", and are tagged G-SOZO. *Adelphoi* is rendered "brothers", and the footnote records that in many places it may include the sisters, and the team should decide. *Christos* is rendered "Christ" and is tagged G-CHRISTOS. The headings of the parts are not in the Greek. The markers beginning with G- (for Greek words) are defined in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. The wording of the WABV text is drafted from the Greek and is not taken from any other English translation.*

## Chapters 1 to 4, joy, the hymn of Christ and the peace of God

### Chapter 1

**1** Paul and Timothy, slaves of Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] Jesus, to all the holy ones \[G-HAGIOS\] in Christ Jesus who are in Philippi, with the overseers and servants:

**2** grace \[G-CHARIS\] to you and peace \[G-EIRENE\] from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**3** I thank my God every time I remember you,

**4** always in every prayer of mine for all of you making my prayer with joy,

**5** because of your partnership in the good news \[G-EUANGELION\] from the first day until now.

**6** I am sure of this, that he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ.

**7** It is right for me to think this about all of you, because I hold you in my heart, since you all share in grace \[G-CHARIS\] with me, both in my imprisonment and in the defence and confirmation of the good news.

**8** For God is my witness, how I long for all of you with the affection of Christ Jesus.

**9** And it is my prayer that your love \[G-AGAPE\] may abound still more and more, with knowledge and all discernment,

**10** so that you may approve what is excellent, and so be pure and blameless for the day of Christ,

**11** filled with the fruit of righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\] that comes through Jesus Christ, to the glory \[G-DOXA\] and praise of God.

**12** I want you to know, brothers, that what has happened to me has actually served to advance the good news,

**13** so that it has become known throughout the whole imperial guard and to everyone else that my imprisonment is for Christ.

**14** And most of the brothers, having become confident in the Lord because of my imprisonment, are much more bold to speak the word without fear.

**15** Some, to be sure, are preaching Christ from envy and rivalry, but others from good will.

**16** The latter do it out of love \[G-AGAPE\], knowing that I am put here for the defence of the good news.

**17** The former proclaim Christ out of selfish ambition, not sincerely, thinking to afflict me in my imprisonment.

**18** What then? Only that in every way, whether in pretence or in truth \[G-ALETHEIA\], Christ is proclaimed, and in that I rejoice. Yes, and I will rejoice,

**19** for I know that through your prayers and the help of the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] of Jesus Christ this will turn out for my deliverance \[G-SOZO\],

**20** as it is my eager expectation and hope that I will not be at all ashamed, but that with full courage now as always Christ will be honoured in my body, whether by life \[G-ZOE\] or by death.

**21** For to me, to live is Christ, and to die is gain.

**22** If I am to live in the flesh, that means fruitful labour for me. Yet which I shall choose I cannot tell.

**23** I am hard pressed between the two. My desire is to depart and be with Christ, for that is far better.

**24** But to remain in the flesh is more necessary on your account.

**25** Convinced of this, I know that I will remain and continue with you all, for your progress and joy in the faith \[G-PISTIS\],

**26** so that in me you may have ample cause to glory in Christ Jesus, because of my coming to you again.

**27** Only let your manner of life be worthy of the good news \[G-EUANGELION\] of Christ, so that whether I come and see you or am absent, I may hear of you that you are standing firm in one spirit \[G-PNEUMA\], with one mind striving side by side for the faith of the good news,

**28** and not frightened in anything by your opponents. This is a clear sign to them of their destruction, but of your salvation \[G-SOZO\], and that from God.

**29** For it has been granted to you that for the sake of Christ you should not only believe \[G-PISTIS\] in him but also suffer for his sake,

**30** engaged in the same conflict that you saw I had and now hear that I still have.

### Chapter 2

**1** So if there is any encouragement in Christ, any comfort from love \[G-AGAPE\], any sharing in the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\], any affection and sympathy,

**2** complete my joy by being of the same mind, having the same love \[G-AGAPE\], being in full accord and of one mind.

**3** Do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit, but in humility count others more significant than yourselves.

**4** Let each of you look not only to his own interests, but also to the interests of others.

**5** Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] Jesus,

**6** who, though he was in the form of God,\
did not count equality with God\
as something to be seized,

**7** but emptied himself,\
taking the form of a slave,\
being born in the likeness of human beings.

**8** And being found in human form,\
he humbled himself\
by becoming obedient to the point of death,\
even death on a cross.

**9** Therefore God has highly exalted him\
and gave him the name\
that is above every name,

**10** so that at the name of Jesus\
every knee should bow,\
in heaven and on earth and under the earth,

**11** and every tongue confess\
that Jesus Christ is Lord,\
to the glory \[G-DOXA\] of God the Father.

**12** Therefore, my loved ones \[G-AGAPE\], as you have always obeyed, so now, not only as in my presence but much more in my absence, work out your own salvation \[G-SOZO\] with fear and trembling,

**13** for it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure.

**14** Do all things without grumbling or disputing,

**15** that you may be blameless and innocent, children of God without blemish in the midst of a crooked and twisted generation, among whom you shine as lights in the world,

**16** holding fast to the word of life \[G-ZOE\], so that in the day of Christ I may be proud that I did not run in vain or labour in vain.

**17** Even if I am to be poured out as a drink offering upon the sacrificial offering of your faith \[G-PISTIS\], I am glad and rejoice with you all.

**18** Likewise you also should be glad and rejoice with me.

**19** I hope in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you soon, so that I too may be cheered by news of you.

**20** For I have no one like him, who will be genuinely concerned for your welfare.

**21** For they all seek their own interests, not those of Jesus Christ.

**22** But you know Timothy's proven worth, how as a son with a father he has served with me in the work of the good news \[G-EUANGELION\].

**23** I hope therefore to send him just as soon as I see how things go with me,

**24** and I trust in the Lord that shortly I myself will come also.

**25** I have thought it necessary to send to you Epaphroditus my brother and fellow worker and fellow soldier, and your messenger and minister to my need,

**26** for he has been longing for you all and has been distressed because you heard that he was ill.

**27** Indeed he was ill, near to death. But God had mercy \[G-ELEOS\] on him, and not only on him but on me also, lest I should have sorrow upon sorrow.

**28** I am the more eager to send him, therefore, that you may rejoice at seeing him again, and that I may be less anxious.

**29** So receive him in the Lord with all joy, and honour such men,

**30** for he nearly died for the work of Christ, risking his life to complete what was lacking in your service to me.

### Chapter 3

**1** Finally, my brothers, rejoice in the Lord. To write the same things to you is no trouble to me and is a safeguard for you.

**2** Look out for the dogs, look out for the evil workers, look out for those who mutilate the flesh.

**3** For we are the circumcision, who worship by the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] of God and glory in Christ Jesus and put no confidence in the flesh,

**4** though I myself have reason for confidence in the flesh also. If anyone else thinks he has reason for confidence in the flesh, I have more:

**5** circumcised on the eighth day, of the people of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of Hebrews; as to the law \[G-NOMOS\], a Pharisee;

**6** as to zeal, a persecutor of the assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\]; as to righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\] under the law, blameless.

**7** But whatever gain I had, I counted as loss for the sake of Christ.

**8** Indeed, I count everything as loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord. For his sake I have suffered the loss of all things and count them as rubbish, in order that I may gain Christ

**9** and be found in him, not having a righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\] of my own that comes from the law \[G-NOMOS\], but that which comes through faith in Christ \[G-PISTIS\], the righteousness from God that depends on faith,

**10** that I may know him and the power of his resurrection, and may share his sufferings, becoming like him in his death,

**11** that by any means possible I may attain the resurrection from the dead.

**12** Not that I have already obtained this or am already perfect, but I press on to make it my own, because Christ Jesus has made me his own.

**13** Brothers, I do not consider that I have made it my own. But one thing I do: forgetting what lies behind and straining forward to what lies ahead,

**14** I press on toward the goal for the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus.

**15** Let those of us who are mature think this way, and if in anything you think otherwise, God will reveal that also to you.

**16** Only let us hold true to what we have attained.

**17** Brothers, join in imitating me, and keep your eyes on those who walk according to the example you have in us.

**18** For many, of whom I have often told you and now tell you even with tears, walk as enemies of the cross of Christ.

**19** Their end is destruction, their god is their belly, and they glory in their shame, with minds set on earthly things.

**20** But our citizenship is in heaven, and from it we await a Saviour, the Lord Jesus Christ,

**21** who will transform our lowly body to be like his glorious \[G-DOXA\] body, by the power that enables him even to subject all things to himself.

### Chapter 4

**1** Therefore, my brothers, whom I love \[G-AGAPE\] and long for, my joy and crown, stand firm thus in the Lord, my loved ones.

**2** I entreat Euodia and I entreat Syntyche to agree in the Lord.

**3** Yes, I ask you also, true companion, help these women, who have laboured side by side with me in the good news \[G-EUANGELION\], together with Clement and the rest of my fellow workers, whose names are in the book of life \[G-ZOE\].

**4** Rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice.

**5** Let your reasonableness be known to everyone. The Lord is near.

**6** Do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God.

**7** And the peace \[G-EIRENE\] of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.

**8** Finally, brothers, whatever is true \[G-ALETHEIA\], whatever is honourable, whatever is right, whatever is pure, whatever is lovely, whatever is commendable, if there is any excellence, if there is anything worthy of praise, think about these things.

**9** What you have learned and received and heard and seen in me, practise these things, and the God of peace \[G-EIRENE\] will be with you.

**10** I rejoiced in the Lord greatly that now at length you have revived your concern for me. You were indeed concerned for me, but you had no opportunity.

**11** Not that I am speaking of being in need, for I have learned in whatever situation I am to be content.

**12** I know how to be brought low, and I know how to abound. In any and every circumstance, I have learned the secret of facing plenty and hunger, abundance and need.

**13** I can do all things through him who strengthens me.

**14** Yet it was kind of you to share my trouble.

**15** And you Philippians yourselves know that in the beginning of the good news \[G-EUANGELION\], when I left Macedonia, no assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\] entered into partnership with me in giving and receiving, except you only.

**16** Even in Thessalonica you sent me help for my needs once and again.

**17** Not that I seek the gift, but I seek the fruit that increases to your credit.

**18** I have received full payment, and more. I am well supplied, having received from Epaphroditus the gifts you sent, a fragrant offering, a sacrifice acceptable and pleasing to God.

**19** And my God will supply every need of yours according to his riches in glory \[G-DOXA\] in Christ Jesus.

**20** To our God and Father be glory \[G-DOXA\] for ever and ever. Amen.

**21** Greet every holy one \[G-HAGIOS\] in Christ Jesus. The brothers who are with me greet you.

**22** All the holy ones \[G-HAGIOS\] greet you, especially those of Caesar's household.

**23** The grace \[G-CHARIS\] of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit \[G-PNEUMA\].

*Review note: Philippians 1 to 4 were drafted in WABV wording, from the Greek, using the fixed choices set out in the introduction to this book, and with the WABV voice standard (Part 46 of the review package) applied, and the hymn of 2:6-11 is set in lines. They are the greeting (1:1-2), the thanksgiving and the prayer for the Philippians (1:3-11), the imprisonment of Paul and the advance of the good news (1:12-18), the hope of Paul between life and death (1:19-26), the call to stand firm and to suffer (1:27-30), the call to unity and humility (2:1-4), the hymn of Christ (2:5-11), working out salvation and shining as lights (2:12-18), Timothy and Epaphroditus (2:19-30), the warning against those who mutilate the flesh and the loss of all things for Christ (3:1-11), pressing on toward the goal (3:12-16), the enemies of the cross and the citizenship in heaven (3:17-21), the call to stand firm and the entreaty to Euodia and Syntyche (4:1-3), joy, the peace of God and what to think about (4:4-9), the thanks for the gift (4:10-20) and the greetings (4:21-23). The numbering is the usual numbering. No new G- marker is used, and the markers are G-CHRISTOS (1:1, 2:5, 2:11), G-HAGIOS (1:1, 4:21-22), G-CHARIS (1:2, 1:7, 4:23), G-EIRENE (1:2, 4:7-9), G-EUANGELION (1:5-27, 2:22, 4:3-15), G-AGAPE (1:9-16, 2:1-12, 4:1), G-DIKAIOSUNE (1:11, 3:6-9), G-DOXA (1:11, 2:11, 3:21, 4:19-20), G-ALETHEIA (1:18, 4:8), G-PNEUMA (1:19-27, 2:1, 3:3, 4:23), G-SOZO (1:19, 1:28, 2:12), G-ZOE (1:20, 2:16, 4:3), G-PISTIS (1:25-29, 2:17, 3:9), G-ELEOS (2:27), G-NOMOS (3:5-9) and G-EKKLESIA (3:6, 4:15). In 1:1 "overseers and servants" translates *episkopois kai diakonois*, and the footnote records that they may be read "bishops and deacons", and that the offices are not yet fixed, and the order of the greeting (the overseers and servants after the holy ones) is not found elsewhere. In 1:5 "your partnership in the good news" translates *koinonia*, and the footnote records that it may be read "sharing" or "fellowship" and that it includes the gifts of the Philippians. In 1:7 "you all share in grace with me" is rendered as written. In 1:12-18 "the whole imperial guard" translates *en holo to praitorio*, which may also be read "the whole palace" or "the whole governor's residence", and the footnote records the matter, and the place of writing is debated. In 1:18 "in pretence or in truth" is rendered as written. In 1:19 "this will turn out for my deliverance" is the quotation of Job 13:16 in the Greek, and "deliverance" translates *soterian*, and the footnote records that it may be read "salvation" or "release". In 1:19 "the Spirit of Jesus Christ" is rendered as written. In 1:21 "to live is Christ, and to die is gain" is rendered as written, with no comment on how it is read. In 1:23 "to depart and be with Christ" is rendered as written. In 1:27 "let your manner of life be worthy" translates *politeuesthe*, and the footnote records that the verb is from the same family as *politeuma* (3:20). In 1:27 "one spirit" may be read of the Spirit of God or of the human spirit. In 1:28 "a clear sign to them of their destruction" is rendered as written, and the Greek is hard. In 1:29 "it has been granted to you" translates *echaristhe*, from the family of *charis*. In 2:1 "any sharing in the Spirit" translates *koinonia pneumatos*, and it may be read "sharing in the Spirit" or "fellowship of the Spirit". In 2:3 "count others more significant than yourselves" is rendered as written. In 2:5-11 the hymn is set in lines, and the footnote records that it may be a hymn of the early assemblies that Paul quotes or composes, and that the structure and the origin are debated. In 2:5 "have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus" is the Greek of the text, and the footnote records that the sentence has no verb and may be read in several ways, and the WABV text does not decide. In 2:6 "the form of God" translates *morphe theou*, and "as something to be seized" translates *harpagmon*, which may also be read "as something to be grasped", "as something to exploit" or "as a prize to hold on to", and the footnote records the matter, and the WABV text does not decide. In 2:7 "emptied himself" translates *heauton ekenosen*, and the footnote records that it may be read "made himself nothing" or "poured himself out". In 2:7 "the form of a slave" is rendered as written. In 2:8 "even death on a cross" is rendered as written. In 2:9 "gave him the name that is above every name" is rendered as written, and the footnote records that the name is "Lord" (verse 11) or "Jesus". In 2:10-11 "every knee should bow ... and every tongue confess" echoes Isaiah 45:23, as in Romans 14:11. In 2:11 "Jesus Christ is Lord" is rendered as written. In 2:12 "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling" is rendered as written, with no comment on how it is read, and the footnote records the matter. In 2:13 "it is God who works in you, both to will and to work" is rendered as written. In 2:15 "among whom you shine as lights in the world" may be read as a statement or as an imperative, and the footnote records that the allusion is to Deuteronomy 32:5 and Daniel 12:3. In 2:16 "holding fast to the word of life" translates *logon zoes epechontes*, which may also be read "holding out the word of life", and the footnote records the matter. In 2:17 "poured out as a drink offering" is rendered as written. In 2:25 "my brother and fellow worker and fellow soldier" is rendered as written. In 2:27 "God had mercy on him" translates *eleesen auton*, and it is tagged G-ELEOS. In 2:30 "risking his life" is the reading of the critical text (*paraboleusamenos te psyche*), and some manuscripts read *parabouleusamenos*, and the footnote records the matter. In 3:1 "rejoice in the Lord" may also be read "farewell in the Lord". In 3:2 "the dogs" and "those who mutilate the flesh" are rendered as written, and the footnote records that the word for the last is *katatome*, a pun on *peritome* (circumcision). In 3:3 "who worship by the Spirit of God" is the reading of the critical text (*pneumati theou*), and some manuscripts read "worship God in the Spirit" (*theo pneumati*), and the footnote records the matter. In 3:5-6 the list of the privileges of Paul is rendered as written. In 3:8 "rubbish" translates *skubala*, a coarse word that may also be read "dung" or "refuse", and the footnote records the matter. In 3:9 "through faith in Christ" translates *dia pisteos Christou*, which may also be read "through the faithfulness of Christ", and the team should decide, and the footnote records the matter. In 3:9 "the righteousness from God that depends on faith" is rendered as written. In 3:10 "the power of his resurrection" and "share his sufferings" are rendered as written. In 3:11 "attain the resurrection from the dead" translates *ten exanastasin ten ek nekron*, and it is the only place in the New Testament where the word *exanastasis* is used. In 3:12 "already perfect" translates *teteleiomai*. In 3:12-14 "I press on to make it my own" is rendered as written. In 3:13 "I do not consider that I have made it my own" is the reading of the critical text (*emauton ou logizomai kateilephenai*), and some manuscripts omit "myself" (*emauton*), and the footnote records the matter. In 3:14 "the upward call of God" is rendered as written. In 3:16 "hold true to what we have attained" is the reading of the critical text, and the Byzantine tradition adds "let us walk by the same rule, let us think the same", and the footnote records the matter. In 3:17 "keep your eyes on" translates *skopeite*. In 3:19 "their god is their belly" is rendered as written. In 3:20 "our citizenship is in heaven" translates *politeuma*, and the footnote records that it may also be read "our commonwealth" or "the colony of heaven", and "Saviour" translates *soter*. In 3:21 "our lowly body" translates *to soma tes tapeinoseos hemon*, and "like his glorious body" is rendered as written. In 4:1 "my joy and crown" is rendered as written. In 4:2 "Euodia" and "Syntyche" are named only here. In 4:3 "true companion" translates *gnesie suzuge*, and the footnote records that it may be a name (Syzygus), and that who is meant is unknown, and "Clement" is named only here. In 4:3 "the book of life" is rendered as written. In 4:5 "your reasonableness" translates *to epieikes humon*, and the footnote records that it may be read "your gentleness" or "your forbearance". In 4:6-7 "do not be anxious about anything" and "the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding" are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. In 4:8 the list of virtues is rendered as written. In 4:11 "to be content" translates *autarkes*. In 4:13 "I can do all things through him who strengthens me" is rendered as written, and the footnote records that "him" is Christ in the critical text, and that the Byzantine tradition adds "Christ". In 4:15 "no assembly entered into partnership with me" is rendered as written. In 4:17 "the fruit that increases to your credit" is rendered as written, and the footnote records the commercial language of the passage (*logon doseos kai lempseos*, "giving and receiving"). In 4:18 "a fragrant offering, a sacrifice acceptable and pleasing to God" is rendered as written. In 4:19 "my God will supply every need of yours" is rendered as written. In 4:22 "Caesar's household" is rendered as written. In 4:23 "be with your spirit" is the reading of the critical text, and some manuscripts add "Amen". In 1:12, 3:1, 3:13, 3:17, 4:1, 4:8, 4:21 "brothers" translates *adelphoi*, and the team should decide whether it is to include the sisters. There are no departures from the critical Greek text in chapters 1 to 4. The Greek is hard in some places, including 1:7, 1:13, 1:19-26, 1:28, 2:5-11, 2:15-16, 3:2-11, 3:12-16, 4:3 and 4:5. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the good work that God began (1:6), "to live is Christ, and to die is gain" (1:21), the hymn of Christ (2:5-11), "work out your own salvation" (2:12-13), the loss of all things for Christ (3:7-11), pressing on toward the goal (3:12-14), our citizenship in heaven (3:20-21), "do not be anxious about anything" (4:6-7), the list of virtues (4:8) and "I can do all things through him who strengthens me" (4:13) are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. The section needs a check against the Greek by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked. With this the book of Philippians is complete in first draft.*

The hymn in verses 6 to 11 is set in lines, and it may be a hymn of the early assemblies that Paul quotes or composes, and its structure and origin are debated.
- **2:6** "The form of God" translates *morphe theou*, and "as something to be seized" translates *harpagmon*, which may also be read "as something to be grasped", "as something to exploit" or "as a prize to hold on to". The WABV text does not decide.
- **2:7** "Emptied himself" translates *heauton ekenosen*, and may be read "made himself nothing" or "poured himself out". "The form of a slave" is rendered as written.
- **2:8** "Even death on a cross" is rendered as written.
- **2:9** "The name that is above every name" is rendered as written, and the name is "Lord" (verse 11) or "Jesus".
- **2:10** "Every knee should bow ... and every tongue confess" echoes Isaiah 45:23, as in Romans 14:11.
- **2:11** "Jesus Christ is Lord" is rendered as written. See entry G-DOXA.
- **2:12** "Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling" is rendered as written.
- **2:13** "It is God who works in you, both to will and to work" is rendered as written.
- **2:15** "Among whom you shine as lights in the world" may be read as a statement or as an imperative, and the allusion is to Deuteronomy 32:5 and Daniel 12:3.
- **2:16** "Holding fast to the word of life" translates *logon zoes epechontes*, which may also be read "holding out the word of life". See entry G-ZOE.
- **2:17** "Poured out as a drink offering" is rendered as written. See entry G-PISTIS.
- **2:25** "My brother and fellow worker and fellow soldier" is rendered as written.
- **2:27** "God had mercy on him" translates *eleesen auton*. See entry G-ELEOS.
- **2:30** "Risking his life" is the reading of the critical text (*paraboleusamenos te psyche*), and some manuscripts read *parabouleusamenos*.
- **3:1** "Rejoice in the Lord" may also be read "farewell in the Lord".
- **3:2** "The dogs" and "those who mutilate the flesh" are rendered as written, and the word for the last is *katatome*, a pun on *peritome* (circumcision).
- **3:3** "Who worship by the Spirit of God" is the reading of the critical text (*pneumati theou*), and some manuscripts read "worship God in the Spirit" (*theo pneumati*). See entry G-PNEUMA.
- **3:5** The list of the privileges of Paul in verses 5 and 6 is rendered as written. See entries G-NOMOS, G-EKKLESIA and G-DIKAIOSUNE.
- **3:8** "Rubbish" translates *skubala*, a coarse word that may also be read "dung" or "refuse".
- **3:9** "Through faith in Christ" translates *dia pisteos Christou*, which may also be read "through the faithfulness of Christ", and the team should decide. "The righteousness from God that depends on faith" is rendered as written. See entries G-DIKAIOSUNE and G-PISTIS.
- **3:10** "The power of his resurrection" and "share his sufferings" are rendered as written.
- **3:11** "Attain the resurrection from the dead" translates *ten exanastasin ten ek nekron*, and *exanastasis* is used only here in the New Testament.
- **3:12** "Already perfect" translates *teteleiomai*, and "I press on to make it my own" is rendered as written.
- **3:13** "I do not consider that I have made it my own" is the reading of the critical text (*emauton ou logizomai kateilephenai*), and some manuscripts omit *emauton*.
- **3:14** "The upward call of God" is rendered as written.
- **3:16** "Hold true to what we have attained" is the reading of the critical text, and the Byzantine tradition adds "let us walk by the same rule, let us think the same".
- **3:17** "Keep your eyes on" translates *skopeite*.
- **3:19** "Their god is their belly" is rendered as written.
- **3:20** "Our citizenship" translates *politeuma*, which may also be read "our commonwealth" or "the colony of heaven", and "Saviour" translates *soter*.
- **3:21** "Our lowly body" translates *to soma tes tapeinoseos hemon*, and "like his glorious body" is rendered as written. See entry G-DOXA.
- **4:1** "My joy and crown" is rendered as written. See entry G-AGAPE.
- **4:2** "Euodia" and "Syntyche" are named only here.
- **4:3** "True companion" translates *gnesie suzuge*, and may be a name (Syzygus), and who is meant is unknown. "Clement" is named only here. "The book of life" is rendered as written. See entry G-EUANGELION.
- **4:5** "Your reasonableness" translates *to epieikes humon*, which may be read "your gentleness" or "your forbearance".
- **4:6** "Do not be anxious about anything" is rendered as written.
- **4:7** "The peace of God, which surpasses all understanding" is rendered as written. See entry G-EIRENE.
- **4:8** The list of virtues is rendered as written. See entry G-ALETHEIA.
- **4:11** "To be content" translates *autarkes*.
- **4:13** "I can do all things through him who strengthens me" is rendered as written, and "him" is Christ, and the Byzantine tradition adds "Christ".
- **4:15** "No assembly entered into partnership with me" is rendered as written. See entry G-EKKLESIA.
- **4:17** "The fruit that increases to your credit" is rendered as written, and the language of the passage is commercial (*logon doseos kai lempseos*, "giving and receiving").
- **4:18** "A fragrant offering, a sacrifice acceptable and pleasing to God" is rendered as written.
- **4:19** "My God will supply every need of yours" is rendered as written.
- **4:22** "Caesar's household" is rendered as written.
- **4:23** "Be with your spirit" is the reading of the critical text, and some manuscripts add "Amen".
