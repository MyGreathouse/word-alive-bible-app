# WABV: Matthew

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[G-PNEUMA\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Matthew is the first book of the New Testament in the order that the WABV text follows. It is written in Greek, and the WABV text is drafted from a critical Greek text, with the Byzantine tradition noted in the footnotes where it differs, as the Charter requires. The book is a gospel, an account of the life, teaching, death and resurrection of Jesus, and it gathers much of his teaching into five long discourses (chapters 5 to 7, 10, 13, 18 and 24 to 25), each of which ends with a closing formula such as "when Jesus had finished these words". It quotes the Hebrew scriptures often, and says of many events that they happened "to fulfil" what was spoken by the prophets, and the centre references link each quotation to the place it comes from. The book is anonymous in the Greek, and the name "Matthew" comes from the title and from early tradition. The WABV text does not decide on the questions of authorship, date or sources, and the footnotes record only the language. The chapter and verse numbering is the usual numbering. Some verses that the Byzantine tradition has are not in the critical Greek text (for example 17:21, 18:11 and 23:14), and the verse number is kept, with a footnote. The words "Son of Man" and "brothers" and the word "Christ" are open decisions in the Charter or in the project, and they are rendered literally in the draft and flagged in the footnotes for the team. The book falls into seven parts in the WABV text: the birth and the beginning of the ministry (chapters 1 to 4), the Sermon on the Mount (5 to 7), healings, the mission and John's question (8 to 11), conflict, the parables and the feeding of the crowds (12 to 15), the confession at Caesarea Philippi, the community and the road to Jerusalem (16 to 20), Jerusalem, the woes and the teaching on the end (21 to 25), and the passion, the resurrection and the commission (26 to 28). The headings of the parts are not in the Greek. The markers beginning with G- (for Greek words) are used here for the first time in the WABV text, and each needs a full entry in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. The names of God and of Jesus are given as the Greek has them: "God" for *theos*, "the Lord" for *kyrios*, "Jesus" and "Christ". The wording of the WABV text is drafted from the Greek and is not taken from any other English translation.*

## Part One: Chapters 1 to 4, the birth and the beginning of the ministry

### Chapter 1

**1** The record of the origin of Jesus Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\], son of David, son of Abraham.

**2** Abraham fathered Isaac, Isaac fathered Jacob, and Jacob fathered Judah and his brothers.

**3** Judah fathered Perez and Zerah, whose mother was Tamar. Perez fathered Hezron, and Hezron fathered Ram.

**4** Ram fathered Amminadab, Amminadab fathered Nahshon, and Nahshon fathered Salmon.

**5** Salmon fathered Boaz, whose mother was Rahab. Boaz fathered Obed, whose mother was Ruth. Obed fathered Jesse,

**6** and Jesse fathered King David. David fathered Solomon, whose mother had been the wife of Uriah.

**7** Solomon fathered Rehoboam, Rehoboam fathered Abijah, and Abijah fathered Asa.

**8** Asa fathered Jehoshaphat, Jehoshaphat fathered Joram, and Joram fathered Uzziah.

**9** Uzziah fathered Jotham, Jotham fathered Ahaz, and Ahaz fathered Hezekiah.

**10** Hezekiah fathered Manasseh, Manasseh fathered Amon, and Amon fathered Josiah.

**11** Josiah fathered Jechoniah and his brothers, at the time of the deportation to Babylon.

**12** After the deportation to Babylon, Jechoniah fathered Shealtiel, and Shealtiel fathered Zerubbabel.

**13** Zerubbabel fathered Abiud, Abiud fathered Eliakim, and Eliakim fathered Azor.

**14** Azor fathered Zadok, Zadok fathered Achim, and Achim fathered Eliud.

**15** Eliud fathered Eleazar, Eleazar fathered Matthan, and Matthan fathered Jacob.

**16** Jacob fathered Joseph, the husband of Mary, and from Mary was born Jesus, who is called Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\].

**17** So the generations from Abraham to David total fourteen, from David to the deportation to Babylon fourteen, and from the deportation to Babylon to the Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] fourteen.

**18** This is how the birth of Jesus Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] came about. His mother Mary was engaged to Joseph, but before they lived together she was found to be pregnant by the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\].

**19** Joseph, her husband-to-be, was a righteous \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\] man and did not want to shame her in public, so he planned to end the engagement quietly.

**20** While he was considering this, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream: "Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to take Mary home as your wife. The child conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\].

**21** She will give birth to a son, and you are to name him Jesus, because he will save \[G-SOZO\] his people from their sins."

**22** All of this happened so that what the Lord said through the prophet would come true:

**23** "See, the virgin will become pregnant and give birth to a son, and they will name him Emmanuel" (which means "God is with us").

**24** When Joseph woke up, he did what the angel of the Lord had told him and took Mary home as his wife.

**25** But he did not have intercourse with her until she gave birth to a son, and he named him Jesus.

### Chapter 2

**1** Jesus was born in Bethlehem in Judea, in the days of King Herod. Soon afterwards, magi from the east came to Jerusalem

**2** asking, "Where is the newborn king of the Jews? We saw his star rising, and we have come to bow before him."

**3** King Herod heard this and was shaken, and all Jerusalem with him.

**4** He called together all the chief priests and the scribes of the people, and asked where the Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] was to be born.

**5** "In Bethlehem in Judea," they told him. "This is what the prophet wrote:

**6** 'And you, Bethlehem in the land of Judah, are far from the least among Judah's leaders, for from you will come a leader who will shepherd my people Israel.'"

**7** Then Herod called the magi aside in secret and found out from them exactly when the star had appeared.

**8** He sent them to Bethlehem. "Go and make a careful search for the child," he said. "When you have found him, bring me word, so that I too can go and bow before him."

**9** They heard the king and set out. And the star they had seen rising went ahead of them until it stopped above the place where the child was.

**10** When they saw the star, their joy was enormous.

**11** They entered the house, saw the child with his mother Mary, fell to the ground and bowed before him. They opened their treasure boxes and presented him with gold, frankincense and myrrh.

**12** Warned in a dream not to return to Herod, they went back to their own country by a different road.

**13** After they had left, an angel of the Lord appeared to Joseph in a dream. "Get up," he said. "Take the child and his mother and escape to Egypt. Stay there until I tell you, because Herod is about to hunt for the child in order to kill him."

**14** Joseph got up, took the child and his mother in the night, and left for Egypt.

**15** He stayed there until Herod's death. This fulfilled what the Lord had said through the prophet: "I called my son out of Egypt."

**16** When Herod realised that the magi had outwitted him, he was enraged. He sent and had every boy killed in Bethlehem and the whole district who was two years old or younger, going by the time he had learned from the magi.

**17** Then the words spoken through the prophet Jeremiah came true:

**18** "A voice was heard in Ramah, weeping and bitter grief. Rachel was weeping for her children, and would not be consoled, because they were gone."

**19** After Herod died, an angel of the Lord appeared in a dream to Joseph in Egypt

**20** and said, "Get up, take the child and his mother, and go to the land of Israel, for those who wanted the child's life \[G-PSYCHE\] are dead."

**21** He got up, took the child and his mother, and went into the land of Israel.

**22** But when he heard that Archelaus was king in Judea in place of his father Herod, he was afraid to go there. Warned in a dream, he left for the region of Galilee

**23** and settled in a town called Nazareth. So what was said through the prophets came true: "He will be called a Nazarene."

### Chapter 3

**1** In those days John the Baptist appeared, announcing in the Judean wilderness,

**2** "Repent \[G-METANOIA\], for the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] of heaven has come near."

**3** This is the one the prophet Isaiah spoke of: "A voice shouting in the wilderness: Prepare the way of the Lord, make his paths straight."

**4** John wore clothing of camel hair with a leather belt round his waist, and his food was locusts and wild honey.

**5** People went out to him from Jerusalem, from all Judea, and from the whole region around the Jordan,

**6** and in the Jordan river they were baptised by him as they confessed their sins.

**7** When he saw many Pharisees and Sadducees coming for baptism, he said to them, "You brood of snakes, who warned you to run from the coming wrath?

**8** Produce fruit that matches repentance \[G-METANOIA\].

**9** And do not suppose you can say to yourselves, 'We have Abraham as our father.' I tell you, God can raise up children for Abraham from these stones.

**10** The axe already lies at the root of the trees. Every tree that does not produce good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire.

**11** I baptise you in water for repentance \[G-METANOIA\], but the one coming after me is stronger than I am, and I am not fit to carry his sandals. He will baptise you in the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] and fire.

**12** His winnowing fork is in his hand, and he will clear his threshing floor. He will gather his wheat into the barn, but the chaff he will burn with a fire that cannot be put out."

**13** Then Jesus came from Galilee to the Jordan, to John, to be baptised by him.

**14** John tried to stop him. "I need to be baptised by you," he said, "and you come to me?"

**15** Jesus answered him, "Let it be so for now, for this is the fitting way for us to fulfil all righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\]." Then John agreed.

**16** After Jesus was baptised he came straight up out of the water. And suddenly the heavens opened, and he saw the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] of God coming down like a dove and landing on him.

**17** And a voice from heaven said, "This is my Son, the beloved \[G-AGAPE\], in whom I take delight."

### Chapter 4

**1** Then the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] led Jesus up into the wilderness to be tested by the devil.

**2** After fasting for forty days and forty nights, he was hungry.

**3** The tester came and said to him, "If you are the Son of God, tell these stones to become loaves of bread."

**4** He answered, "It is written: 'A person will not live on bread alone, but on every word that comes out of God's mouth.'"

**5** Then the devil took him to the holy city, stood him on the highest point of the temple,

**6** and said to him, "If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down. For it is written: 'He will give his angels orders about you, and they will carry you in their hands, so that you do not strike your foot against a stone.'"

**7** Jesus said to him, "It is also written: 'You are not to test the Lord your God.'"

**8** Again the devil took him to a very high mountain and showed him all the kingdoms \[G-BASILEIA\] of the world and their splendour,

**9** and said to him, "All this I will give you if you fall down and bow to me."

**10** Then Jesus said to him, "Go away, Satan. For it is written: 'You are to bow to the Lord your God and serve him alone.'"

**11** Then the devil left him, and angels came and looked after him.

**12** When Jesus heard that John had been arrested, he withdrew to Galilee.

**13** He left Nazareth and went to live in Capernaum by the sea, in the territory of Zebulun and Naphtali,

**14** so that what was said through the prophet Isaiah would come true:

**15** "Land of Zebulun and land of Naphtali, the way to the sea, across the Jordan, Galilee of the nations,

**16** the people living in darkness have seen a great light, and for those living in the region of the shadow of death, light has dawned."

**17** From then on Jesus began to announce, "Repent \[G-METANOIA\], for the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] of heaven has come near."

**18** As he was walking beside the Sea of Galilee, he saw two brothers, Simon called Peter and his brother Andrew, throwing a net into the sea, for they were fishermen.

**19** He said to them, "Follow me, and I will make you fishers of people."

**20** At once they left their nets and followed him.

**21** Going on from there he saw two other brothers, James son of Zebedee and his brother John, in the boat with their father Zebedee, mending their nets. He called them,

**22** and at once they left the boat and their father and followed him.

**23** Jesus went all through Galilee, teaching in their synagogues, announcing the good news \[G-EUANGELION\] of the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\], and healing every disease and every sickness among the people.

**24** News about him spread through all Syria, and people brought him all who were ill, suffering from various diseases and pains, those possessed by demons, epileptics and paralysed people, and he healed them.

**25** Large crowds followed him from Galilee, the Decapolis, Jerusalem, Judea and beyond the Jordan.

*Review note: Matthew 1 to 4 were drafted in WABV wording, from the Greek. They are the genealogy of Jesus Christ (1:1-17), the birth and the dream of Joseph (1:18-25), the magi, the flight to Egypt and the return to Nazareth (2:1-23), John the Baptist and the baptism of Jesus (3:1-17), the testing in the wilderness (4:1-11) and the beginning of the ministry in Galilee, with the call of the first four disciples (4:12-25). The numbering is the usual numbering. The chapters are narrative prose, with a few quotations from the Hebrew scriptures that are introduced by "to fulfil" or "it is written" (1:23; 2:6, 2:15, 2:18, 2:23; 3:3; 4:4, 4:6, 4:7, 4:10, 4:15-16). The markers beginning with G- are new in the WABV text, and each needs a full entry in the Biblical Concepts and Language System: G-CHRISTOS (Christ), G-PNEUMA (Spirit, 1:18, 1:20, 3:11, 3:16, 4:1), G-DIKAIOSUNE (righteousness, 1:19, 3:15), G-SOZO (save, 1:21), G-PSYCHE (life, 2:20), G-METANOIA (repentance, 3:2, 3:8, 3:11, 4:17), G-BASILEIA (kingdom, 3:2, 4:8, 4:17, 4:23), G-AGAPE (beloved, 3:17) and G-EUANGELION (good news, 4:23). "Christ" translates christos and is tagged G-CHRISTOS, and the rendering is open to the team's decision ("Christ", "Messiah" or "the Anointed One"), together with the open decision on H-MASHIACH in the Old Testament. In 1:23 "the virgin" follows the Greek (parthenos) of Isaiah 7:14, where the Hebrew has almah, and the footnote records the matter without comment. The words "brothers" in 1:2, 1:11, 4:18 and 4:21 are literal, and refer to men in a family. In the genealogy the names are given as in the WABV text of the Old Testament, and the Greek spellings are in the footnote. There are no departures from the critical Greek text in chapters 1 to 4. The Greek is hard in some places, including 1:16, 1:18 (*en gastri echousa*), 1:25 (*heos hou*), 2:2 (*en te anatole*, "rising" or "in the east"), 4:1 and 4:24 (*seleniazomenous*, "epileptics"). The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the killing of the boys in Bethlehem (2:16-18) are rendered as written, with no comment. The section needs a check against the Greek by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Two: Chapters 5 to 7, the Sermon on the Mount

### Chapter 5

**1** Seeing the crowds, Jesus went up the mountain. When he had sat down, his disciples came to him,

**2** and he opened his mouth and taught them:

**3** "Blessed \[G-MAKARIOS\] are the destitute in spirit \[G-PNEUMA\], for theirs is the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] of heaven.

**4** Blessed are the mourners, for they will be comforted.

**5** Blessed are the gentle, for they will inherit the land.

**6** Blessed are those hungering and thirsting for righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\], for they will be fed to the full.

**7** Blessed are the merciful, for mercy \[G-ELEOS\] will be shown to them.

**8** Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God.

**9** Blessed are those who make peace \[G-EIRENE\], for they will be called God's sons.

**10** Blessed are those hounded because of righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\], for theirs is the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] of heaven.

**11** Blessed are you whenever people reproach you, hound you, and lie about you, saying every kind of evil because of me.

**12** Rejoice and leap for joy, for your reward in heaven is large. They hounded the prophets before you in just this way.

**13** You are the salt of the earth. But if the salt goes flat, what will make it salty? It has no more use, except to be thrown outside and trampled underfoot.

**14** You are the light of the world. A city lying on a hilltop cannot be hidden.

**15** People do not light a lamp and set it under a measuring bowl. They set it on its stand, and it shines for everyone in the house.

**16** So let your light shine in front of people, so that they will see the good you do and honour your Father in heaven.

**17** Do not suppose that I have come to dismantle the Law \[G-NOMOS\] or the Prophets. I have not come to dismantle but to fulfil \[G-PLEROO\].

**18** Amen, I tell you: until heaven and earth pass away, not one letter, not one pen-stroke, will pass from the Law until it has all taken place.

**19** So whoever loosens one of the smallest of these commands, and teaches people to do likewise, will be called smallest in the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] of heaven. But whoever keeps and teaches them will be called great in the kingdom of heaven.

**20** For I tell you that unless your righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\] is far greater than the scribes' and Pharisees', you will never enter the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] of heaven.

**21** You have heard that it was said to those of old: 'You will not commit murder,' and 'Whoever commits murder will be answerable to the court.'

**22** But I tell you that everyone who is angry with his brother will be answerable to the court, whoever says to his brother 'Empty-head' will be answerable to the council, and whoever says 'Fool' will be answerable to the fire of Gehenna.

**23** So if you are offering your gift at the altar and there remember that your brother has something against you,

**24** leave your gift there in front of the altar and go. First make peace with your brother, and then come and offer your gift.

**25** Come to terms with your accuser quickly, while you are still with him on the road, or he will hand you to the judge, and the judge to the guard, and you will be thrown into prison.

**26** Amen, I tell you: you will not get out of there until you have paid the last copper coin.

**27** You have heard that it was said, 'You will not commit adultery.'

**28** But I tell you that everyone who looks at a woman in order to desire her has already committed adultery with her in his heart.

**29** If your right eye trips you up, pull it out and throw it away. It is better for you that one of your limbs be lost than that your whole body be thrown into Gehenna.

**30** And if your right hand trips you up, cut it off and throw it away. It is better for you that one of your limbs be lost than that your whole body go into Gehenna.

**31** It was also said, 'Whoever sends his wife away must give her a divorce certificate.'

**32** But I tell you that everyone who sends his wife away, except for a matter of sexual immorality, makes her commit adultery, and whoever marries a woman who has been sent away commits adultery.

**33** Again you have heard that it was said to those of old, 'You will not break an oath, but you will give the Lord what you have sworn.'

**34** But I tell you: do not swear at all, not by heaven, for it is God's throne,

**35** nor by the earth, for it is the footstool under his feet, nor by Jerusalem, for it is the city of the great King.

**36** Do not swear by your head either, because you cannot turn one hair white or black.

**37** Let your 'yes' be 'yes' and your 'no' be 'no.' Whatever goes beyond these comes from the evil one.

**38** You have heard that it was said, 'Eye for eye and tooth for tooth.'

**39** But I tell you: do not stand against the one who does evil. If someone hits you on the right cheek, offer him the other as well.

**40** If someone wants to take you to court and take your tunic, let him have your cloak too.

**41** If someone conscripts you for one mile, go with him for two.

**42** Give to anyone who asks you, and do not turn away from anyone who wants to borrow from you.

**43** You have heard that it was said, 'You will love \[G-AGAPE\] your neighbour and hate your enemy.'

**44** But I tell you: love \[G-AGAPE\] your enemies, and pray for those who hound you,

**45** so that you may become sons of your Father in heaven. He makes his sun rise on the wicked and the good, and sends rain on the righteous and the unrighteous.

**46** If you love \[G-AGAPE\] only those who love you, what reward is there in that? Do the tax collectors not do the same?

**47** And if you greet only your brothers, what are you doing that is out of the ordinary? Do the Gentiles not do the same?

**48** You, then, are to be complete, just as your heavenly Father is complete."

### Chapter 6

**1** Be careful not to do your righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\] in front of people so that they will watch you. Otherwise there is no reward for you with your Father in heaven.

**2** So when you give to the poor, do not have a trumpet blown ahead of you, as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets, to be praised by people. Amen, I tell you: they have been paid in full.

**3** When you give to the poor, do not let your left hand find out what your right hand is doing,

**4** so that your giving stays hidden. Your Father, who sees what is hidden, will pay you back.

**5** And when you pray, do not be like the hypocrites. They love to stand in the synagogues and on the corners of the squares and pray, so that people will see them. Amen, I tell you: they have been paid in full.

**6** But when you pray, go into your storeroom, shut your door, and pray to your Father in the hidden place. Your Father, who sees what is hidden, will pay you back.

**7** When you pray, do not babble on as the Gentiles do. They imagine they will be heard because of their many words.

**8** Do not imitate them. Your Father knows what you need before you ask him.

**9** This, then, is how you are to pray: 'Our Father in the heavens, let your name be treated as holy \[G-HAGIOS\],

**10** let your kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] come, let your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven.

**11** Give us today our bread for the coming day.

**12** Release us from our debts, as we also have released those who owe us.

**13** Do not lead us into testing, but rescue us from the evil one.'

**14** If you forgive people their failures, your heavenly Father will forgive you too.

**15** But if you do not forgive people, your Father will not forgive your failures either.

**16** When you fast, do not put on a gloomy face as the hypocrites do. They make their faces unrecognisable so that people will see that they are fasting. Amen, I tell you: they have been paid in full.

**17** But when you fast, oil your head and wash your face,

**18** so that it is not people who see you fasting but your Father in the hidden place. Your Father, who sees what is hidden, will pay you back.

**19** Do not pile up treasures for yourselves on the earth, where moths and corrosion eat them away and thieves dig through and steal.

**20** Pile up treasures for yourselves in heaven, where neither moths nor corrosion eat them away and thieves do not dig through and steal.

**21** For your heart will be wherever your treasure is.

**22** The eye is the lamp of the body. So if your eye is generous, your whole body will be bright.

**23** But if your eye is stingy, your whole body will be dark. And if the light inside you is darkness, how deep that darkness is.

**24** No one can be a slave to two masters. Either he will hate one and love the other, or he will stick to one and look down on the other. You cannot be slaves to God and to Mammon.

**25** That is why I tell you: do not be anxious about your life \[G-PSYCHE\], what you will eat, or about your body, what you will put on. Is life not more than food, and the body more than clothes?

**26** Look at the wild birds. They do not sow, they do not harvest, they do not store food in barns, yet your heavenly Father feeds them. Are you not worth far more than they are?

**27** Which of you, by being anxious, can add one cubit to his span of life?

**28** And why be anxious about clothes? Learn from the wildflowers, how they grow. They do not labour or spin,

**29** yet I tell you that even Solomon in all his splendour was never dressed like one of these.

**30** If God dresses the grass in the field like this, grass that is alive today and thrown into the oven tomorrow, will he not do much more for you, you of little faith \[G-PISTIS\]?

**31** So do not be anxious and ask, 'What will we eat?' or 'What will we drink?' or 'What will we put on?'

**32** The nations chase after all of this, and your heavenly Father knows that you need all of it.

**33** Seek first his kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] and his righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\], and all of this will be given to you as well.

**34** So do not be anxious about tomorrow. Tomorrow will be anxious about itself. Each day has trouble enough."

### Chapter 7

**1** Do not pass judgement, so that judgement will not be passed on you.

**2** The verdict you give will be the verdict you receive, and the measure you use will be the measure used on you.

**3** Why do you look at the splinter in your brother's eye and not notice the log in your own?

**4** Or how can you say to your brother, 'Let me pull the splinter out of your eye,' when the log is in your own eye?

**5** You hypocrite, first pull the log out of your own eye, and then you will see clearly enough to pull the splinter out of your brother's eye.

**6** Do not give what is holy to the dogs, and do not throw your pearls in front of the pigs, or they will trample them and turn round and tear you to pieces.

**7** Keep on asking and it will be given to you. Keep on searching and you will find. Keep on knocking and it will be opened for you.

**8** For everyone who asks receives, and the one who searches finds, and for the one who knocks it will be opened.

**9** Which of you, when his son asks for bread, will hand him a stone?

**10** Or when he asks for a fish, will hand him a snake?

**11** If you, evil as you are, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father in the heavens give good things to those who ask him.

**12** So whatever you want people to do for you, do the same for them. This is the Law \[G-NOMOS\] and the Prophets.

**13** Go in through the narrow gate. The gate is wide and the road easy that leads to destruction, and many are going in through it.

**14** But the gate is narrow and the road squeezed that leads to life \[G-ZOE\], and few are finding it.

**15** Watch out for the false prophets. They come to you dressed as sheep, but inside they are ravenous wolves.

**16** You will recognise them by their fruit. Are grapes gathered from thornbushes, or figs from thistles?

**17** So every good tree bears good fruit, but the rotten tree bears bad fruit.

**18** A good tree cannot bear bad fruit, and a rotten tree cannot bear good fruit.

**19** Every tree that does not bear good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire.

**20** So then, you will recognise them by their fruit.

**21** Not everyone who says to me, 'Lord, Lord,' will enter the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] of heaven, but the one who does the will of my Father in the heavens.

**22** Many will say to me on that day, 'Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name, drive out demons in your name, and perform many mighty acts in your name?'

**23** Then I will tell them openly, 'I never knew you. Leave me, you workers of lawlessness.'

**24** So everyone who hears these words of mine and puts them into practice will be like a sensible man who built his house on the rock.

**25** The rain came down, the rivers rose, the winds blew and hit that house, and it did not fall, because it had its foundation on the rock.

**26** And everyone who hears these words of mine and does not put them into practice will be like a foolish man who built his house on the sand.

**27** The rain came down, the rivers rose, the winds blew and struck that house, and it fell, and its fall was great.

**28** When Jesus finished these words, the crowds were amazed at his teaching,

**29** for he taught them like someone with authority, and not like their scribes.

*Review note: Matthew 5 to 7 were drafted in WABV wording, from the Greek, and chapters 5 and 6 were re-voiced once after the first draft followed familiar translations too closely. They are the first of the five discourses, the Sermon on the Mount: the beatitudes (5:3-12), salt and light (5:13-16), the Law and the Prophets and the six sayings "you have heard ... but I tell you" (5:17-48), giving, praying, fasting and the prayer that Jesus gave (6:1-18), treasure, the eye, the two masters and anxiety (6:19-34), judging, asking, the two gates, the two trees and the two houses (7:1-27), and the closing formula (7:28-29). The numbering is the usual numbering. The chapters are speech, with some lines set apart (the prayer, 6:9-13). The G- markers used are G-MAKARIOS (blessed, 5:3-11), G-PNEUMA (5:3), G-BASILEIA (5:3, 5:10, 5:19, 5:20, 6:10, 6:33, 7:21), G-DIKAIOSUNE (5:6, 5:10, 5:20, 6:1, 6:33), G-ELEOS (5:7), G-EIRENE (5:9), G-NOMOS (5:17, 7:12), G-PLEROO (5:17), G-AGAPE (5:43-46), G-HAGIOS (6:9), G-PSYCHE (6:25), G-PISTIS (6:30) and G-ZOE (7:14). All are new and each needs a full entry. The Greek of 6:13 ends at "the evil one", and the Byzantine tradition adds the words "for yours is the kingdom and the power and the glory for ever, amen", which are not in the critical Greek text and are given in the footnote. The words "sons of God" (5:9) and "sons of your Father" (5:45) are literal, and the team should decide them with the Charter's open decision on "brothers". In 5:22 "Empty-head" translates raka, which is an Aramaic word of abuse. In 5:37 and 6:13 "the evil one" translates tou ponerou, which may also be "evil", and in 5:39 "the one who does evil" translates to poneroi, which may also be "evil" or "the evil one", and the footnotes record the matter. In 6:24 "Mammon" is left as the Greek word (mamonas), which means wealth or money. There are no departures from the critical Greek text in chapters 5 to 7. The Greek is hard in some places, including 5:48 (*teleioi*, "complete"), 6:11 (*epiousios*, "for the coming day"), 6:22-23 (*haplous*, "generous") and 7:6. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The sayings on anger, lust, divorce and oaths (5:21-37), the sayings on the second cheek and the second mile (5:38-42), and the words on the two roads (7:13-14) and on those who say "Lord, Lord" (7:21-23) are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are applied. The section needs a check against the Greek by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

The section needs a check against the Greek by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked. With this part the book of Matthew is complete in first draft.*

- **27:63** "Deceiver" translates *planos*.

- **28:6** "Has been raised" translates *egerthe*.
- **28:9** "Bowed" translates *prosekunesan*, which may also be read "worshipped".
- **28:10** "My brothers" translates *adelphois mou*, and it is literal, of the disciples.
- **28:12** "Consulting together" translates *sumboulion labontes*.
- **28:17** "Bowed down" translates *prosekunesan*, and "some doubted" translates *hoi de edistasan*.
- **28:18** "Authority" translates *exousia*.
- **28:19** "Make disciples" translates *matheteusate*, "the nations" translates *panta ta ethne*, and "name" is singular in the Greek (*to onoma*). "The Holy Spirit" translates *tou hagiou pneumatos*. See entry G-PNEUMA.
- **28:20** "The close of the age" translates *sunteleias tou aionos*.
