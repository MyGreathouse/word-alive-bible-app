# WABV: Malachi

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-RUACH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Malachi is the twelfth and last of the books of the Minor Prophets in the Protestant order that the WABV text follows, and the last book of the Old Testament. It is in Hebrew, in prose, with some lines in poetry, and it is set out as a series of disputes in which the LORD makes a claim, the people ask "how?", and the LORD answers. The book is called an oracle by Malachi (1:1), and the name means "my messenger", which is also the word in 3:1. The WABV text does not decide whether the word is a name or a title, or when the book was written, except that it is set after the rebuilding of the temple, and the footnotes record only the language. The chapter and verse numbering is the English numbering. English 4:1-6 is Hebrew 3:19-24, so the Hebrew book has three chapters and the English book has four. The other verses are numbered alike. The book falls into two parts in the WABV text: the love of the LORD, the offerings and the priests (chapters 1 and 2), and the messenger, the call to return and the day of the LORD (3 and 4). The headings of the parts are not in the Hebrew. One line is the same in the Hebrew as another book, and the wording is the same in the WABV text: 3:7, which is the same as Zechariah 1:3. The Hebrew of Malachi is mostly clear, with a few very hard verses (for example 2:12, 2:15 and 2:16), and the footnotes record the other readings. The names of God are given as in the earlier books: "the LORD" for YHWH, "Lord" for Adonai, and "the LORD of hosts" for YHWH tseva'ot. The wording of the WABV text is drafted from the Hebrew and is not taken from any other English translation.*

## Part One: Chapters 1 and 2, the LORD's love and the priests

### Chapter 1

**1** An oracle. The word of the LORD to Israel by the hand of Malachi.

**2** "I have loved \[H-AHAV\] you," says the LORD. But you ask, "How have you loved us?" Was Esau not Jacob's brother? declares the LORD. Yet I loved \[H-AHAV\] Jacob,

**3** and Esau I hated. I made his hill country a wasteland and left his inheritance to the desert jackals.

**4** If Edom says, "We are shattered, but we will rebuild the ruins," this is what the LORD of hosts says: Let them build, and I will tear down. They will be called the Land of Wickedness, the people the LORD has been angry with for ever.

**5** Your own eyes will see it, and you will say, "The LORD is great, far beyond the border of Israel."

**6** A son honours his father, and a servant his master. So if I am a father, where is the honour due to me? If I am a master, where is the fear \[H-YIRAH\] due to me? says the LORD of hosts to you, priests, who despise my name. You say, "How have we despised your name?"

**7** You offer polluted food on my altar. You say, "How have we polluted you?" By saying, "The LORD's table can be treated with contempt."

**8** When you offer a blind animal for sacrifice, is nothing wrong? When you offer one that is lame or sick, is nothing wrong? Try giving it to your governor. Will he be pleased with you? Will he welcome you? says the LORD of hosts.

**9** So now, try to soften the face of God, that he may be gracious to us. With gifts like these in your hands, will he welcome any of you? says the LORD of hosts.

**10** If only one of you would shut the temple doors, so that you would stop lighting my altar fire for nothing. I take no pleasure in you, says the LORD of hosts, and I will accept no offering from your hands.

**11** From the rising of the sun to its setting, my name is great among the nations. Everywhere incense is burned to my name, with a pure offering, for my name is great among the nations, says the LORD of hosts.

**12** But you profane it when you say, "The LORD's table is polluted, and its food can be treated with contempt."

**13** You say, "What a burden," and you sniff at it, says the LORD of hosts. You bring what was seized, the lame and the sick, and present these as your offering. Should I accept them from your hands? says the LORD.

**14** A curse on the cheat who has a healthy male in his flock, vows to give it, and then sacrifices a damaged animal to the Lord. For I am a great king, says the LORD of hosts, and my name is feared \[H-YIRAH\] among the nations.

### Chapter 2

**1** And now, this command is for you, priests.

**2** If you will not listen, and will not take it to heart to give honour to my name, says the LORD of hosts, I will send the curse on you and turn your blessings into curses. Indeed I have already cursed them, because you have not taken it to heart.

**3** See, I will rebuke your offspring, and spread dung on your faces, the dung of your festival offerings, and you will be carried off with it.

**4** Then you will know that I sent you this command, so that my covenant \[H-BERIT\] with Levi may continue, says the LORD of hosts.

**5** My covenant \[H-BERIT\] with him was life and peace \[H-SHALOM\], and I gave them to him, with fear \[H-YIRAH\], and he feared \[H-YIRAH\] me and stood in awe of my name.

**6** The teaching \[H-TORAH\] of truth \[H-EMET\] was in his mouth, and nothing false was found on his lips. He walked with me in peace \[H-SHALOM\] and uprightness, and he turned \[H-SHUV\] many away from iniquity.

**7** For a priest's lips should guard knowledge, and people should seek teaching \[H-TORAH\] from his mouth, because he is the messenger of the LORD of hosts.

**8** But you have turned aside from the way. You have made many stumble by your teaching \[H-TORAH\]. You have ruined the covenant \[H-BERIT\] of Levi, says the LORD of hosts.

**9** So I in turn have made you despised and low before all the people, because you do not keep my ways but show favouritism in your teaching \[H-TORAH\].

**10** Do we not all have one father? Did one God not create us? Why then do we break faith with one another, profaning the covenant \[H-BERIT\] of our fathers?

**11** Judah has broken faith, and an abomination has been committed in Israel and in Jerusalem. For Judah has profaned the holy place \[H-QADOSH\] of the LORD, which he loves \[H-AHAV\], by marrying the daughter of a foreign god.

**12** May the LORD cut off from the tents of Jacob anyone who does this, the one who is awake and the one who answers, even one who brings an offering to the LORD of hosts.

**13** And this is something else you do: you cover the LORD's altar with tears, with weeping and groaning, because he no longer looks at the offering or accepts it with favour from your hand.

**14** You ask, "Why?" Because the LORD stood as witness between you and the wife of your youth, to whom you have been faithless, though she is your companion and your wife by covenant \[H-BERIT\].

**15** Did he not make one, with a remnant of the spirit \[H-RUACH\] in her? And what was the one seeking? Offspring from God. So guard your spirit \[H-RUACH\], and let no one be faithless to the wife of his youth.

**16** For he hates sending away, says the LORD, the God of Israel, and he covers his garment with violence, says the LORD of hosts. So guard your spirit \[H-RUACH\], and do not be faithless.

**17** You have wearied the LORD with your words. You ask, "How have we wearied him?" By saying, "Everyone who does evil is good in the LORD's eyes, and he delights in them," or, "Where is the God of justice?"

*Review note: Malachi 1 and 2 were drafted in WABV wording, from the Hebrew. They are the heading (1:1), the love of the LORD for Jacob and the word about Edom (1:2-5), the charge against the priests for the polluted offerings (1:6-14), the command to the priests and the covenant of Levi (2:1-9), the faithlessness of Judah in marriage and in the covenant (2:10-16) and the weariness of the LORD with the words of the people (2:17). The numbering is the same as the Hebrew. The part is in prose, with lines where the Hebrew is poetry or near poetry. The form is a dispute: the LORD makes a claim, and the people ask "how?" (1:2, 1:6, 1:7, 2:14, 2:17). In 1:2 and 1:3 and 2:11 "loved" and "loves" translate ahavti and ahev and are tagged H-AHAV, and the footnote records that "hated" (sanethi) in 1:3 is the language of choice and rejection. In 1:6, 1:14, 2:5 "fear" and "feared" translate mora and yare and are tagged H-YIRAH. In 2:4, 2:5, 2:8, 2:10 and 2:14 "covenant" translates berit and is tagged H-BERIT. In 2:6, 2:7, 2:8 and 2:9 "teaching" translates torah and is tagged H-TORAH, and in 2:6 "truth" is tagged H-EMET. In 2:5 and 2:6 "peace" translates shalom and is tagged H-SHALOM. In 2:6 "turned many away" translates heshiv and is tagged H-SHUV. In 2:11 "the holy place" translates qodesh and is tagged H-QADOSH. In 2:15 and 2:16 "spirit" translates ruach and is tagged H-RUACH, and it is written with a small letter, because the word is here not clearly the spirit of the LORD, and the team should decide it with the other places. In 2:7 "the messenger of the LORD of hosts" translates mal'akh YHWH tseva'ot, the same word as the name Malachi and as 3:1. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapters 1 and 2. The Hebrew is very hard in some places, including 1:4 (*ritsatsnu*), 1:9, 2:3 (*zera*, "offspring", which the Greek reads as "shoulder" (zero'a)), 2:12 (*er ve-oneh*), 2:15 and 2:16 (*ki-sana shalach*). The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on Esau and Edom (1:2-5), the dung on the faces of the priests (2:3) and the marriage to the daughter of a foreign god (2:11) are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Two: Chapters 3 and 4, the messenger and the day of the LORD

### Chapter 3

**1** See, I am sending my messenger, and he will clear the way before me. And suddenly the Lord whom you seek will come to his temple, the messenger of the covenant \[H-BERIT\] in whom you delight. See, he is coming, says the LORD of hosts.

**2** But who can endure the day of his coming? Who can stand when he appears? For he is like a refiner's fire and like the soap of launderers.

**3** He will sit as a refiner and purifier of silver. He will purify the sons of Levi and refine them like gold and silver, until they bring offerings to the LORD in righteousness \[H-TSEDAQAH\].

**4** Then the offerings of Judah and Jerusalem will please the LORD as in days of old, as in former years.

**5** I will come near to you for judgement. I will be a swift witness against sorcerers, against adulterers, against those who swear falsely, against those who cheat workers of their wages, who oppress the widow and the orphan, who push the foreigner aside, and who do not fear \[H-YIRAH\] me, says the LORD of hosts.

**6** I, the LORD, do not change, and so you, descendants of Jacob, have not been destroyed.

**7** Since the days of your fathers you have turned aside from my statutes and have not kept them. Return \[H-SHUV\] to me, and I will return \[H-SHUV\] to you, says the LORD of hosts. But you ask, "How are we to return \[H-SHUV\]?"

**8** Will a human being rob God? Yet you rob me. You ask, "How have we robbed you?" In tithes and contributions.

**9** You are under a curse, the whole nation of you, for you are robbing me.

**10** Bring the whole tithe into the storehouse, so that there may be food in my house. Test me in this, says the LORD of hosts, and see whether I will not open the windows of heaven for you and pour out blessing until there is no more need.

**11** I will rebuke the devourer for you, so that it will not destroy the produce of your soil, and your vine in the field will not lose its fruit, says the LORD of hosts.

**12** Then all the nations will call you blessed, for you will be a land of delight, says the LORD of hosts.

**13** You have spoken harsh words against me, says the LORD. Yet you ask, "What have we said against you?"

**14** You have said, "It is pointless to serve God. What do we gain by keeping his charge, or by walking about in mourning before the LORD of hosts?

**15** So now we call the arrogant blessed. Evildoers are built up, and those who test God escape."

**16** Then those who feared \[H-YIRAH\] the LORD spoke with one another. The LORD listened and heard, and a book of remembrance was written before him for those who fear \[H-YIRAH\] the LORD and honour his name.

**17** They will be mine, says the LORD of hosts, my own treasured possession on the day I act. I will have compassion on them as a man has compassion on his son who serves him.

**18** Then you will again see the difference between the righteous \[H-TSEDAQAH\] and the wicked, between one who serves God and one who does not.

### Chapter 4

**1** See, the day is coming, burning like a furnace. All the arrogant and all evildoers will be stubble, and the day that is coming will set them ablaze, says the LORD of hosts, leaving them neither root nor branch.

**2** But for you who fear \[H-YIRAH\] my name, the sun of righteousness \[H-TSEDAQAH\] will rise, and healing will be in its wings. You will go out and leap like calves from the stall.

**3** You will trample the wicked, for they will be ashes under the soles of your feet on the day I act, says the LORD of hosts.

**4** Remember the teaching \[H-TORAH\] of my servant Moses, the statutes and judgements I commanded him at Horeb for all Israel.

**5** See, I am sending you the prophet Elijah before the great and terrible day of the LORD comes.

**6** He will turn \[H-SHUV\] the hearts of fathers to their children and the hearts of children to their fathers, so that I will not come and strike the land with a ban of destruction \[H-CHEREM\].

*Review note: Malachi 3 and 4 were drafted in WABV wording, from the Hebrew. They are the messenger and the messenger of the covenant, the refiner and the judgement (3:1-5), the call to return and the dispute about the tithes (3:6-12), the complaint that serving God is pointless and the book of remembrance (3:13-18), and the day like a furnace, the sun of righteousness, the teaching of Moses and the coming of Elijah (4:1-6). The numbering is the English numbering: English 4:1-6 is Hebrew 3:19-24. The part is in prose, with lines where the Hebrew is poetry or near poetry, and it keeps the form of the dispute (3:7, 3:8, 3:13). In 3:1 "the messenger of the covenant" translates mal'akh ha-berit and is tagged H-BERIT, and "my messenger" translates mal'akhi, the same word as the name Malachi in 1:1 and as 2:7. In 3:3 and 4:2 "righteousness" translates tsedaqah and in 3:18 "the righteous" translates tsaddiq, and all are tagged H-TSEDAQAH. In 3:5, 3:16 and 4:2 "fear" and "feared" translate yare and are tagged H-YIRAH. In 3:7 "return" translates shuvu and ashuva and "are we to return" translates nashuv, and in 4:6 "he will turn" translates heshiv, and all are tagged H-SHUV. The words "return to me, and I will return to you" in 3:7 are the same in the Hebrew as part of Zechariah 1:3, and are given the same wording as the WABV text of Zechariah. In 3:18 "you will again see" translates ve-shavtem ve-re'item, in which shuv is used as an adverb, and it is not tagged. In 4:4 "the teaching" translates torah and is tagged H-TORAH. In 4:6 "a ban of destruction" translates cherem and is tagged H-CHEREM, as in Zechariah 14:11. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapters 3 and 4. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 3:1 (*ha-adon*, "the Lord"), 3:2 (*borit*, "soap"), 3:5, 3:10 (*ad-beli-dai*, "until there is no more need"), 3:14 and 4:2. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the day of the LORD (3:2, 4:1-3) and on Elijah and the turning of hearts (4:5-6) are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. The book ends with the word "ban" (*cherem*) in the Hebrew of 4:6, and in the public reading of the Hebrew the verse before it (4:5) is repeated aloud so that the reading does not end on that word, and the footnote records the matter. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Footnotes

- **1:1** "An oracle" translates *massa*, and it is part of the open decision on the word. "Malachi" translates *Mal'akhi*, "my messenger", the word that is used in 3:1.
- **1:2** "I have loved" translates *ahavti*. See entry H-AHAV. "How have you loved us" translates *ba-mah ahavtanu*.
- **1:3** "Hated" translates *sanethi*, which is the language of choice and rejection (see Genesis 29:31, 33 and Romans 9:13). "A wasteland" translates *shemamah*, and "desert jackals" translates *tannot midbar*.
- **1:4** "We are shattered" translates *rutsatsnu*, and "we will rebuild" translates *nashuv ve-nivneh*, literally "we will return and build", in which *shuv* is used as an adverb and is not tagged. "The Land of Wickedness" translates *gevul rish'ah*, and "has been angry" translates *za'am*.
- **1:5** "Great, far beyond the border" translates *yigdal me-al le-gevul*.
- **1:6** "Honours" translates *yekhabbed*, "master" translates *adonim*, and "the fear due to me" translates *mora'i*. See entry H-YIRAH. "Who despise" translates *bozei*.
- **1:7** "Polluted" translates *me'gu'al*, "the table" translates *shulchan*, and "treated with contempt" translates *nivzeh*.
- **1:8** "Blind", "lame" and "sick" translate *ivver*, *pissei'ach* and *choleh*, "governor" translates *pechah*, and "welcome" translates *yissa fanekha*, literally "lift your face".
- **1:9** "Soften the face" translates *challu-na penei El*, and "gifts like these" translates *mi-yedkhem hayetah zot*.
- **1:10** "Shut the temple doors" translates *ve-yisgor delatayim*, "for nothing" translates *chinnam*, and "pleasure" translates *chefets*.
- **1:11** "Incense is burned" translates *muqtar muggash*, and "a pure offering" translates *minchah tehorah*. The verse is rendered as written, with no comment on how it is read.
- **1:12** "Profane" translates *mechallelim*.
- **1:13** "A burden" translates *matela'ah*, "you sniff at it" translates *hippachtem*, and "what was seized" translates *gazul*.
- **1:14** "The cheat" translates *nokhel*, "a healthy male" translates *zakhar*, "a damaged animal" translates *mashchat*, and "is feared" translates *nora*. See entry H-YIRAH.
- **2:1** "Command" translates *mitsvah*.
- **2:2** "Take it to heart" translates *tasimu al-lev*, "the curse" translates *ha-me'erah*, and "your blessings" translates *birkoteikhem*.
- **2:3** "I will rebuke" translates *go'er*, and "your offspring" translates *zera*, which the Greek reads as "shoulder" (*zero'a*). "Dung" translates *peresh*, and "your festival offerings" translates *chaggeikhem*. It is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **2:4** "My covenant" translates *beriti*. See entry H-BERIT.
- **2:5** "Life and peace" translates *ha-chayyim ve-ha-shalom*. See entry H-SHALOM. "Fear" translates *mora*, and "stood in awe" translates *nichat*. See entry H-YIRAH.
- **2:6** "The teaching of truth" translates *torat emet*. See entries H-TORAH and H-EMET. "False" translates *avlah*, "uprightness" translates *mishor*, and "turned many away from iniquity" translates *heshiv me-avon*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **2:7** "Should guard knowledge" translates *yishmeru-da'at*, and "the messenger" translates *mal'akh*, as in 3:1.
- **2:8** "Have made many stumble" translates *hikhshaltem*.
- **2:9** "Despised" translates *nivzim*, and "show favouritism" translates *nosim panim*.
- **2:10** "One father" translates *av echad*, and "break faith" translates *nivgad*. See Isaiah 63:16.
- **2:11** "Has broken faith" translates *bagedah*, "an abomination" translates *to'evah*, "the holy place" translates *qodesh*, and "loves" translates *ahev*. See entries H-QADOSH and H-AHAV. "The daughter of a foreign god" translates *bat-el nekhar*, and it is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **2:12** "The one who is awake and the one who answers" translates *er ve-oneh*, which is very hard, and the Greek reads "the teacher and the student".
- **2:13** "Tears" translates *dim'ah*, and "looks at" translates *panah el*.

- **2:15** The verse is very hard in the Hebrew (*ve-lo echad asah u-she'ar ruach lo*, literally "and not one he made, and a remnant of spirit was his"), and the readings differ. "The spirit" translates *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH. It is written with a small letter. "Offspring from God" translates *zera Elohim*.
- **2:16** "For he hates sending away" translates *ki-sane shalach*, in which the speaker is not clear, and some read "I hate divorce" and the Greek differs. "Violence" translates *chamas*.
- **2:17** "You have wearied" translates *hoga'tem*, and "the God of justice" translates *Elohei ha-mishpat*.

- **3:8** "Rob" translates *yiqba*, a rare verb, and "tithes and contributions" translates *ha-ma'aser ve-ha-terumah*.
- **3:9** "A curse" translates *ha-me'erah*.
- **3:10** "The whole tithe" translates *kol-ha-ma'aser*, "the storehouse" translates *ha-otsar*, and "food" translates *teref*. "The windows of heaven" translates *arubbot ha-shamayim*, as in Genesis 7:11. "Until there is no more need" translates *ad-beli-dai*, which is very hard and may also be read "until there is no end".
- **3:11** "The devourer" translates *ha-okhel*, and "will lose its fruit" translates *teshakkel*.
