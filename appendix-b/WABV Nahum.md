# WABV: Nahum

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-RUACH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Nahum is the seventh of the twelve books of the Minor Prophets in the Protestant order that the WABV text follows. It is in Hebrew and in poetry. The book is called an oracle concerning Nineveh, the book of the vision of Nahum of Elkosh (1:1), and it has no date of its own. It speaks of the fall of Thebes as past (3:8, about 663 before Christ) and of the fall of Nineveh as coming (612), and the WABV text does not decide more than that. The chapter and verse numbering is the English numbering. English 1:15 is Hebrew 2:1, so English 2:1-13 is Hebrew 2:2-14. Chapter 3 is numbered alike in English and Hebrew. The book falls into two parts in the WABV text: the LORD who avenges and is a stronghold, and the fall of Nineveh (chapters 1 and 2), and the woe on the city of blood (chapter 3). The headings of the parts are not in the Hebrew. The opening poem (1:2-8) is in part an alphabetic acrostic in the Hebrew, and the footnotes record the matter. The words of 1:3 are the same in the Hebrew as part of Exodus 34:7, and the words of 1:15 are the same as part of Isaiah 52:7, and the wording of the lines that are the same is the same in the WABV text, as the footnotes record. The Hebrew of Nahum is vivid and in places very hard (for example 2:3-10 and 3:8-19), and the footnotes record the other readings and the uncertain terms. The names of God are given as in the earlier books: "the LORD" for YHWH and "the LORD of hosts" for YHWH tseva'ot. The WABV text does not decide on questions of the growth of the book, and the footnotes record only the language.*

## Part One: Chapters 1 and 2, the LORD who avenges and the fall of Nineveh

### Chapter 1

**1** An oracle concerning Nineveh. The book of the vision of Nahum of Elkosh.

**2** A jealous and avenging God is the LORD. The LORD avenges and is full of wrath. The LORD takes vengeance on his adversaries and keeps wrath for his enemies.

**3** The LORD is slow to anger and great in power, and the LORD will not leave the guilty unpunished. His way is in whirlwind and storm, and the clouds are the dust of his feet.

**4** He rebukes the sea and makes it dry, and he dries up all the rivers. Bashan and Carmel wither, and the bloom of Lebanon withers.

**5** The mountains quake before him, and the hills melt. The earth heaves before him, the world and all who live in it.

**6** Who can stand before his indignation? Who can endure the heat of his anger? His wrath is poured out like fire, and the rocks are broken apart by him.

**7** The LORD is good, a stronghold in the day of trouble. He knows those who take refuge in him.

**8** But with an overflowing flood he will make a full end of her place, and will pursue his enemies into darkness.

**9** What do you plot against the LORD? He will make a full end. Trouble will not rise a second time.

**10** For they are like tangled thorns, and like drunkards drunk, they are consumed like dry straw.

**11** From you came one who plotted evil against the LORD, a wicked counsellor.

**12** Thus says the LORD: Though they are at full strength and many, they will be cut down and pass away. Though I have afflicted you, I will afflict you no more.

**13** And now I will break his yoke from off you and tear your bonds apart.

**14** The LORD has given a command about you: No more of your name will be sown. From the house of your gods I will cut off the carved image and the cast image. I will make your grave, for you are worthless.

**15** See, on the mountains the feet of the one who brings good news, who publishes peace \[H-SHALOM\]. Keep your feasts, Judah, fulfil your vows, for never again will the worthless pass through you. He is utterly cut off.

### Chapter 2

**1** The scatterer has come up against you. Guard the fortress. Watch the road. Brace yourselves. Summon all your strength.

**2** For the LORD is restoring \[H-SHUV\] the majesty of Jacob as the majesty of Israel, for plunderers have plundered them and ruined their branches.

**3** The shield of his warriors is red, his soldiers are clothed in scarlet. The chariots flash with steel on the day of their muster, and the cypress spears are brandished.

**4** The chariots race madly through the streets. They rush to and fro through the squares. They gleam like torches, they dart like lightning.

**5** He summons his officers. They stumble as they march. They hasten to the wall, and the covering is set up.

**6** The gates of the rivers are opened, and the palace melts away.

**7** It is decreed: the queen is stripped, she is carried off, her slave women moan like doves, beating their breasts.

**8** Nineveh is like a pool whose waters run away. "Halt, halt," they cry, but none turns back.

**9** Plunder the silver, plunder the gold. There is no end to the treasure, an abundance of every precious thing.

**10** Emptiness, desolation and waste. Hearts melt and knees tremble, anguish is in all loins, and all faces grow pale.

**11** Where is the lions' den, the feeding place of the young lions, where the lion and lioness went, and the lion's cub, with none to disturb them?

**12** The lion tore enough for his cubs and strangled prey for his lionesses. He filled his caves with prey and his dens with torn flesh.

**13** See, I am against you, declares the LORD of hosts, and I will burn your chariots in smoke, and the sword will devour your young lions. I will cut off your prey from the earth, and the voice of your messengers will be heard no more.

*Review note: Nahum 1 and 2 were drafted in WABV wording. They are the heading (1:1), the poem of the LORD who is jealous and avenging and also a stronghold (1:2-8), the word against Nineveh and the word of rescue for Judah (1:9-15), the assault on the city (2:1-10) and the end of the lions' den (2:11-13). The numbering is the English numbering: English 1:15 is Hebrew 2:1, so English 2:1-13 is Hebrew 2:2-14. The whole of the part is in poetry, apart from the heading in 1:1, and the verses are set in lines. In 1:15 "peace" translates shalom and is tagged H-SHALOM. In 2:2 "is restoring" translates shav and is tagged H-SHUV. In 2:8 "turns back" translates poneh, from panah, and is not tagged. The words of 1:3 ("slow to anger ... and the LORD will not leave the guilty unpunished") are the same in the Hebrew as part of Exodus 34:6-7, and are given the same wording as the WABV text of Exodus. The words of 1:15 ("the feet of the one who brings good news, who publishes peace") are the same in the Hebrew as part of Isaiah 52:7, and are given the same wording as the WABV text of Isaiah. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapters 1 and 2. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 1:8 (the word for her place), 1:10, 1:12, 2:3-10 and 2:7 (*hutstsav*, "it is decreed"). The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the vengeance of the LORD (1:2-3), the burning of the chariots and the cutting off of the prey (2:13) and the stripping of the queen (2:7) are rendered as written, with no comment. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Two: Chapter 3, woe to the city of blood

### Chapter 3

**1** Woe to the city of blood, all of it lies and plunder, with no end to the prey.

**2** The crack of the whip, the rumble of the wheel, galloping horse and bounding chariot.

**3** Horsemen charging, flashing sword and glittering spear, a multitude of slain, a heap of corpses, no end to the bodies. They stumble over the bodies.

**4** Because of the many whorings of the prostitute, graceful and charming, mistress of sorceries, who sells nations by her whorings and peoples by her sorceries,

**5** see, I am against you, declares the LORD of hosts, and I will lift your skirts over your face, and I will show the nations your nakedness and the kingdoms your shame.

**6** I will throw filth at you and treat you with contempt and make you a spectacle.

**7** And all who look on you will shrink from you and say, "Nineveh is laid waste. Who will grieve for her?" Where will I seek comforters \[H-NACHAM\] for you?

**8** Are you better than Thebes on the Nile, who sat by the streams, with water around her, her rampart a sea, and her wall water?

**9** Cush was her strength, and Egypt, and it was boundless. Put and the Libyans were her helpers.

**10** Yet she became an exile, she went into captivity. Her infants were dashed in pieces at the head of every street. For her honoured men lots were cast, and all her great men were bound in chains.

**11** You also will be drunken, you will go into hiding. You also will seek a refuge from the enemy.

**12** All your fortresses are like fig trees with first-ripe figs. If shaken, they fall into the mouth of the eater.

**13** See, your troops are women in your midst. The gates of your land are wide open to your enemies. Fire has devoured your bars.

**14** Draw water for the siege. Strengthen your forts. Go into the clay, tread the mortar, take hold of the brick mould.

**15** There the fire will devour you, the sword will cut you off. It will devour you like the hopping locust. Multiply yourselves like the hopping locust, multiply like the swarming locust.

**16** You increased your merchants more than the stars of the heavens. The hopping locust spreads its wings and flies away.

**17** Your princes are like swarming locusts, and your scribes like clouds of locusts settling on the walls on a cold day. When the sun rises, they fly away, and no one knows where they are.

**18** Your shepherds are asleep, king of Assyria. Your nobles slumber. Your people are scattered on the mountains with none to gather them.

**19** There is no easing your hurt. Your wound is grievous. All who hear the news of you clap their hands over you. For who has not felt your endless cruelty?

*Review note: Nahum 3 was drafted in WABV wording. It is the woe on the city of blood and the whips and the chariots (3:1-3), the prostitute and her sorceries and her shame (3:4-7), the comparison with Thebes (3:8-11), the fortresses like fig trees and the forts and the locusts (3:12-17) and the end of the king of Assyria (3:18-19). The numbering is the same as the Hebrew. The whole chapter is in poetry, and the verses are set in lines. In 3:7 "comforters" translates menachamim and is tagged H-NACHAM. The words of 3:5 ("see, I am against you, declares the LORD of hosts") are the same as 2:13 and are given the same wording. The words on the lifting of the skirts and the showing of the nakedness (3:5) are the same in the Hebrew as Jeremiah 13:26, Ezekiel 16:37 and Hosea 2:10, and the wording should be compared with those places in a final pass. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapter 3, and none in the whole book. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 3:8 (*No-Amon*, "Thebes"), 3:16-17 (the locusts) and 3:19. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the whorings and the sorceries of Nineveh (3:4), the lifting of the skirts and the showing of the nakedness (3:5-6), the dashing of the infants of Thebes (3:10) and the endless cruelty (3:19) are rendered as written, with no comment. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Footnotes

- **1:1** "An oracle" translates *massa*, which may also be "a burden", and it is part of the open decision on the word in the WABV text. "Nahum" translates *Nachum*, "comfort", and "Elkosh" translates *Elqosh*, which is a place that is not known. "The vision" translates *chazon*.
- **1:2** "Jealous" translates *qanno*, "avenging" translates *noqem*, and "full of wrath" translates *ba'al chemah*, literally "a master of wrath". "Keeps wrath" translates *notter hu*. The poem in verses 2 to 8 is in part an alphabetic acrostic in the Hebrew (the first letters of the lines follow the alphabet from *aleph* to *kaph*), and the acrostic is not shown in English.
- **1:3** "Slow to anger" translates *erekh appayim*, and "great in power" translates *gedol-koach*. "The LORD will not leave the guilty unpunished" translates *ve-naqeh lo yenaqqeh YHWH*, and the words are the same as part of Exodus 34:7, and are given the same wording as the WABV text of Exodus. "Whirlwind" and "storm" translate *suphah* and *se'arah*.
- **1:4** "Rebukes" translates *go'er*. "Bashan", "Carmel" and "Lebanon" are fertile lands.
- **1:5** "Heaves" translates *tissa*.
- **1:6** "His indignation" translates *zaamo*, and "the heat of his anger" translates *charon appo*.
- **1:7** "Good" translates *tov*, "a stronghold" translates *ma'oz*, "he knows" translates *yode'a*, and "those who take refuge" translates *chosei*.
- **1:8** "With an overflowing flood" translates *u-v-shetef over*, and "her place" translates *meqomah*, which is hard. Some read "its place".
- **1:9** "What do you plot" translates *mah techashevun*, and "trouble will not rise a second time" translates *lo-takum pa'amayim tsarah*.
- **1:10** "Tangled thorns" translates *sirim sevukhim*, and "drunkards drunk" translates *kesov'am sevu'im*. The verse is very hard.
- **1:11** "A wicked counsellor" translates *yo'ets Belial*, in which *Belial* means "worthless".
- **1:12** "At full strength" translates *shelemim*, and "they will be cut down" translates *nogozzu*. The verse speaks to Judah in its second half ("I will afflict you no more").
- **1:13** "His yoke" translates *motato*, and "your bonds" translates *moserotayikh*.
- **1:14** "A command" translates *tsivvah*, "carved image" and "cast image" translate *pesel* and *massekhah*, "your grave" translates *qivrekha*, and "worthless" translates *qallota*.
- **1:15** The verse is Hebrew 2:1. "The feet of the one who brings good news, who publishes peace" translates *raglei mevasser mashmia shalom*, and the words are the same as part of Isaiah 52:7, and are given the same wording as the WABV text of Isaiah. See entry H-SHALOM. "Your feasts" translates *chaggayikh*, and "your vows" translates *nedarayikh*. "The worthless" translates *Belial*, and "he is utterly cut off" translates *kullo nikhrat*.
- **2:1** The verses 1 to 13 are Hebrew 2:2-14. "The scatterer" translates *mefits*, and "brace yourselves" translates *chazzeq motnayim*, literally "strengthen the loins".
- **2:2** "Is restoring" translates *shav*, and it may also be read as the past ("has restored"). See entry H-SHUV. "The majesty" translates *ge'on*, "plunderers" translates *bokeqim*, and "their branches" translates *zemoroteihem*.
- **2:3** "Red" translates *me'addam*, "scarlet" translates *metulla'im*, and "flash with steel" translates *be-esh peladot*, which is very hard. "The cypress spears" translates *ha-berushim*, which may also be read "the cypress".
- **2:4** "Race madly" translates *yit-holelu*, and "torches" translates *lappidim*.
- **2:5** "His officers" translates *adirav*, and "the covering" translates *ha-sokhekh*.
- **2:6** "The gates of the rivers" translates *sha'arei ha-neharot*, and "melts away" translates *namog*.
- **2:7** "It is decreed" translates *hutstsav*, a word that may be a name (*Huzzab*, "the queen") or a verb, and the verse is very hard. "The queen" translates *ha-hutstsav*, and "moan" translates *menahagot*. It is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **2:8** "A pool" translates *berekhat-mayim*, and "none turns back" translates *ve-ein poneh*, from *panah*, and is not tagged.
- **2:10** "Emptiness, desolation and waste" translates *buqah u-mevuqah u-mevullaqah*, a word-play on three similar words, and "anguish" translates *chalchalah*.
- **2:11** "The lions' den" translates *me'on aryot*.
- **2:13** "Your chariots" translates *richbah*, and "your messengers" translates *mal'akhekhi*.
- **3:1** "The city of blood" translates *ir damim*, "lies" translates *kachash*, "plunder" translates *pereq*, and "prey" translates *taref*.
- **3:2** "The whip" translates *shot*, "the rumble" translates *ra'ash*, and "galloping" and "bounding" translate *dohereh* and *merqavah*.
- **3:3** "Flashing sword" and "glittering spear" translate *lahav cherev u-varaq chanit*, and "corpses" translates *peger*. The verse is hard.
- **3:4** "Whorings" translates *zenunei*, "graceful and charming" translates *tovat chen*, and "sorceries" translates *keshafim*. It is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **3:5** "See, I am against you" translates *hineni elayikh*, as in 2:13. "I will lift your skirts over your face" translates *ve-giliti shulayikh al-panayikh*, and it is close to Jeremiah 13:26. "Your nakedness" translates *ma'reikh*, and "your shame" translates *qalonekh*. See also Ezekiel 16:37 and Hosea 2:10.
- **3:6** "Filth" translates *shiqqutsim*, and "a spectacle" translates *ke-rosi*.
- **3:7** "Is laid waste" translates *shoddedah*, and "comforters" translates *menachamim*. See entry H-NACHAM.
- **3:8** "Thebes" translates *No-Amon*, the Egyptian city that fell to Assyria in about 663 before Christ, and "her rampart" translates *chel*.
- **3:9** "Cush", "Put" and "the Libyans" translate *Kush*, *Put* and *Lubim*, and "boundless" translates *ein qetsev*.
- **3:10** "She became an exile" translates *la-golah halakhah*, "her infants were dashed in pieces" translates *olaleha yerutstsu*, and "for her honoured men lots were cast" translates *ve-al-nikhbaddeha yadu goral*. It is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **3:11** "Will be drunken" translates *tishkeri*, and "you will go into hiding" translates *te'alemi*.
- **3:12** "First-ripe figs" translates *bikkurim*.
- **3:13** "Your troops" translates *amekh*, literally "your people", and "your bars" translates *berichayikh*.
- **3:14** "Forts" translates *mivtsarayikh*, "clay" translates *tit*, "mortar" translates *chomer*, and "brick mould" translates *malben*.
- **3:15** "The hopping locust" and "the swarming locust" translate *yeleq* and *arbeh*, as in Joel 1:4 and 2:25.
- **3:16** "Your merchants" translates *rokhelayikh*.
- **3:17** "Your princes" and "your scribes" translate *minnazerayikh* and *taphserayikh*, which are loan words of uncertain sense, and "clouds of locusts" translates *gov govai*.
- **3:18** "Your shepherds" translates *ro'ekha*, "your nobles" translates *addirekha*, and "are scattered" translates *nafoshu*.
- **3:19** "No easing" translates *ein kehah*, "your hurt" translates *shivrekha*, "your wound is grievous" translates *nachlah makkatekha*, and "endless cruelty" translates *ra'atekha tamid*. The verse is rendered as written, with no comment.
