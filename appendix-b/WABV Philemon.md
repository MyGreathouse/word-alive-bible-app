# WABV: Philemon

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[G-AGAPE\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Philemon is the eighteenth book of the New Testament in the order that the WABV text follows, and the thirteenth of the letters of Paul. It is written in Greek, and the WABV text is drafted from a critical Greek text, with the Byzantine tradition noted in the footnotes where it differs, as the Charter requires. It is a short personal letter from Paul and Timothy to Philemon, a believer of the Lycus valley (probably at Colossae), and to the assembly that meets in his house, and it is written from prison (verses 1, 9, 10, 13) and carried back by Onesimus (verse 12), the slave who had left Philemon and who is named with Tychicus in Colossians 4:7-9. Paul does not command, but appeals (verses 8-9), and asks Philemon to receive Onesimus "no longer as a slave but more than a slave" (verse 16). What Paul asks is debated, and the WABV text does not decide: that Onesimus be received back and forgiven, that he be freed, or that he be sent back to serve Paul. The letter has no verse numbering in chapters, and the usual numbering is the verse numbering. The WABV text of Philemon is drafted with the voice standard (Part 46 of the review package). Where the text of a critical edition is not certain, the draft says so in the footnote and asks the team to confirm the reading. It makes a number of fixed choices that follow from its Greek, and they are written down here so that they are used throughout. *Doulos* is rendered "slave", and the word is used of Onesimus in verse 16. *Onesimos* means "useful" (verses 10-11, 20), and Paul plays on the name, and the footnote records the wordplay. *Splanchna* (verses 7, 12, 20) is rendered "heart", and the footnote records "inner being" and the link with *splanchnizomai*. *Koinonia* (verse 6) is rendered "partnership". *Hagioi* is rendered "holy ones" and is tagged G-HAGIOS. *Pistis* is rendered "faith" and is tagged G-PISTIS. *Agape* is rendered "love" and is tagged G-AGAPE. *Charis* is rendered "grace" and is tagged G-CHARIS. *Eirene* is rendered "peace" and is tagged G-EIRENE. *Euangelion* is rendered "good news" and is tagged G-EUANGELION. *Ekklesia* is rendered "assembly" and is tagged G-EKKLESIA, and the team should decide between "assembly" and "church". *Pneuma* is rendered "spirit" and is tagged G-PNEUMA. *Adelphos* is rendered "brother", and the footnote records that in verse 2 "sister" is used of Apphia. *Christos* is rendered "Christ" and is tagged G-CHRISTOS. The markers beginning with G- (for Greek words) are defined in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. The wording of the WABV text is drafted from the Greek and is not taken from any other English translation.*

## The letter, verses 1 to 25

**1** Paul, a prisoner for Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] Jesus, and Timothy our brother, to Philemon our beloved \[G-AGAPE\] fellow worker

**2** and Apphia our sister and Archippus our fellow soldier, and the assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\] in your house:

**3** grace \[G-CHARIS\] to you and peace \[G-EIRENE\] from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**4** I thank my God always when I remember you in my prayers,

**5** because I hear of your love \[G-AGAPE\] and of the faith \[G-PISTIS\] that you have toward the Lord Jesus and for all the holy ones \[G-HAGIOS\],

**6** that the partnership of your faith \[G-PISTIS\] may become effective for the full knowledge of every good thing that is in us for the sake of Christ.

**7** For I have derived much joy and comfort from your love \[G-AGAPE\], because the hearts of the holy ones \[G-HAGIOS\] have been refreshed through you, my brother.

**8** Accordingly, though I am bold enough in Christ to command you to do what is required,

**9** yet for love's \[G-AGAPE\] sake I prefer to appeal to you, I, Paul, an old man and now a prisoner also for Christ Jesus.

**10** I appeal to you for my child, Onesimus, whose father I have become in my imprisonment.

**11** (Formerly he was useless to you, but now he is indeed useful to you and to me.)

**12** I am sending him back to you, sending my very heart.

**13** I would have been glad to keep him with me, in order that he might serve me on your behalf during my imprisonment for the good news \[G-EUANGELION\],

**14** but I preferred to do nothing without your consent, in order that your goodness might not be by compulsion but of your own accord.

**15** For perhaps this is why he was parted from you for a while, that you might have him back for ever,

**16** no longer as a slave but more than a slave, as a beloved \[G-AGAPE\] brother, especially to me but how much more to you, both in the flesh and in the Lord.

**17** So if you consider me your partner, receive him as you would receive me.

**18** If he has wronged you at all, or owes you anything, charge that to my account.

**19** I, Paul, write this with my own hand: I will repay it (to say nothing of your owing me even your own self).

**20** Yes, brother, I want some benefit from you in the Lord. Refresh my heart in Christ.

**21** Confident of your obedience, I write to you, knowing that you will do even more than I say.

**22** At the same time, prepare a guest room for me, for I am hoping that through your prayers I will be graciously given to you.

**23** Epaphras, my fellow prisoner in Christ Jesus, sends greetings to you,

**24** and so do Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke, my fellow workers.

**25** The grace \[G-CHARIS\] of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit \[G-PNEUMA\].

*Review note: Philemon was drafted in WABV wording, from the Greek, using the fixed choices set out in the introduction to this book, and with the WABV voice standard (Part 46 of the review package) applied. It has the greeting (verses 1-3), the thanksgiving for the love and faith of Philemon (4-7), the appeal for Onesimus (8-16), the request to receive him and Paul's promise to repay (17-20), the confidence and the hope of a visit (21-22) and the greetings and the closing (23-25). The numbering is the usual numbering. No new G- marker is used, and the markers are G-CHRISTOS (1-25), G-AGAPE (1, 5, 7, 9, 16), G-EKKLESIA (2), G-CHARIS (3, 25), G-EIRENE (3), G-PISTIS (5-6), G-HAGIOS (5, 7), G-EUANGELION (13) and G-PNEUMA (25). In verse 2 "Apphia our sister" translates *Apphia te adelphe*, and the Greek may also be read "the sister" (the sister of the assembly), and the footnote records that she may be the wife of Philemon, and "Archippus" is also named in Colossians 4:17, and "the assembly in your house" is rendered as written. In verse 6 "the partnership of your faith may become effective for the full knowledge of every good thing that is in us for the sake of Christ" translates *he koinonia tes pisteos sou energes genetai en epignosei pantos agathou tou en hemin eis Christon*, and the Greek is among the hardest in the letter, and the footnote records that "partnership" (*koinonia*) may be read "sharing" or "fellowship", and that "in us" is the reading of the critical text, and some manuscripts read "in you" (*en humin*), and the WABV text does not decide, and the footnote records the matter. In verse 7 "joy" is the reading of the critical text (*charan*), and some manuscripts read "grace" or "thanks" (*charin*), and the footnote records the matter, and "hearts" translates *splanchna*, and the footnote records that it may be read "inner being" and that it is from the same family as *splanchnizomai* ("be moved with compassion"), and it is not tagged G-SPLANCHNIZOMAI, because the Greek word is the noun, and the team should decide. In verse 9 "an old man" translates *presbutes*, which may also be read "an ambassador" (*presbeutes*), and the footnote records the matter. In verse 10 "my child, Onesimus, whose father I have become in my imprisonment" is rendered as written, and the name *Onesimos* means "useful", and Paul plays on it in verses 11 and 20, and the footnote records the wordplay. In verse 11 "useless" translates *achrestos*, and "useful" translates *euchrestos*, and the word play is with *Chrestos*, and the footnote records the matter. In verse 12 "sending him back to you, sending my very heart" is the reading of the critical text (*hon anepempsa soi, auton, tout' estin ta ema splanchna*), and the Byzantine tradition adds "receive him" (*prosλabou*) after "heart", and the footnote records the matter. In verse 14 "your goodness might not be by compulsion but of your own accord" is rendered as written. In verse 15 "he was parted from you for a while" is rendered as written, and "that you might have him back for ever" translates *aionion auton apechēs*, which may be read "for ever" or "for the age", and the footnote records the matter. In verse 16 "no longer as a slave but more than a slave, as a beloved brother" is rendered as written, with no comment on how it is read, and the footnote records that Paul does not ask outright that Onesimus be freed, and that scholars read the verse as a request to receive him as a brother, or to free him, or to send him back to serve Paul, and that the WABV text does not decide. In verse 16 "both in the flesh and in the Lord" is rendered as written. In verse 17 "your partner" translates *koinonon*. In verse 18 "charge that to my account" is rendered as written, and the word is *ellogei*, and the footnote records the matter. In verse 19 "I, Paul, write this with my own hand" is as in 1 Corinthians 16:21 and Galatians 6:11. In verse 19 "to say nothing of your owing me even your own self" is rendered as written. In verse 20 "I want some benefit from you" translates *egO sou onaimen*, which is a play on the name Onesimus, and the footnote records the matter. In verse 22 "I will be graciously given to you" translates *charisthesomai humin*, from the family of *charis*. In verse 23 "Epaphras, my fellow prisoner" is named also in Colossians 1:7 and 4:12. In verse 24 "Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke" are named also in Colossians 4:10-14. In verse 25 "with your spirit" is the reading of the critical text, and the "your" is plural in the Greek ("with your (plural) spirit"), and some manuscripts add "Amen", and the footnote records the matter. In verse 7 and 20 "brother" translates *adelphe*. There are no departures from the critical Greek text in Philemon. The Greek is hard in some places, including verses 6, 9, 15 and 16. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the appeal (8-9), Onesimus (10-12), "no longer as a slave but more than a slave" (16), "charge that to my account" (18) and the request to refresh my heart (20) are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. The section needs a check against the Greek by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked. With this the book of Philemon is complete in first draft.*
