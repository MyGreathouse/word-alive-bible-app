# WABV: Obadiah

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-RUACH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Obadiah is the fourth of the twelve books of the Minor Prophets in the Protestant order that the WABV text follows, and it is the shortest book of the Hebrew Bible, with one chapter of twenty-one verses. It is in Hebrew and in poetry. The book has no date, and the WABV text does not decide when it was written. The event it recalls is a day on which Jerusalem was taken, and Edom stood by and shared in the plunder (verses 10-14). The chapter and verse numbering is the English numbering, and it is the same as the Hebrew numbering. Verses 1b-9 are close to Jeremiah 49:7-22, and the wording of the lines that are the same in the Hebrew is the same in the WABV text, as the footnotes record. The book falls into one part in the WABV text, in three movements: the judgement on Edom (verses 1-9), the reason for it (verses 10-14), and the day of the LORD and the kingdom of the LORD (verses 15-21). The headings are not in the Hebrew. The Hebrew of Obadiah is clear in most verses, but a few are hard (for example verses 7, 20 and 21), and the footnotes record the other readings. The names of God are given as in the earlier books: "the LORD" for YHWH and "the Lord GOD" for Adonai YHWH. Edom and Esau are given as written, and the footnotes mark where the two names are used of the same people.*

## Part One: Obadiah 1 to 21, the vision against Edom

### Obadiah

**1** The vision of Obadiah. Thus says the Lord GOD concerning Edom: We have heard a message from the LORD, and an envoy has been sent among the nations: "Rise up, let us rise against her for battle."

**2** See, I will make you small among the nations. You are utterly despised.

**3** The pride of your heart has deceived you, you who live in the clefts of the rock, whose dwelling is high, who say in your heart, "Who will bring me down to the ground?"

**4** Though you make your nest as high as the eagle's, though you set it among the stars, from there I will bring you down, declares the LORD.

**5** If thieves came to you, if robbers by night, how you have been destroyed, would they not steal only enough for themselves? If grape gatherers came to you, would they not leave gleanings?

**6** How Esau has been searched out, his hidden treasures sought out.

**7** All your allies have driven you to the border. The people of your covenant \[H-BERIT\] have deceived you. Your friends have overpowered you. Those who eat your bread set a trap beneath you. There is no understanding in him.

**8** On that day, declares the LORD, will I not destroy the wise out of Edom, and understanding out of the mount of Esau?

**9** Your warriors will be dismayed, Teman, so that everyone from the mount of Esau will be cut off by slaughter.

**10** Because of the violence done to your brother Jacob, shame will cover you, and you will be cut off for ever.

**11** On the day that you stood aloof, on the day that strangers carried off his wealth, and foreigners entered his gates and cast lots for Jerusalem, you too were like one of them.

**12** Do not gloat over your brother in the day of his misfortune. Do not rejoice over the people of Judah in the day of their destruction. Do not boast in the day of distress.

**13** Do not enter the gate of my people in the day of their calamity. Do not gloat over his disaster in the day of his calamity. Do not seize his wealth in the day of his calamity.

**14** Do not stand at the crossroads to cut off his fugitives. Do not hand over his survivors in the day of distress.

**15** For the day of the LORD is near against all the nations. As you have done, it will be done to you. Your payment will return \[H-SHUV\] on your own head.

**16** For as you drank on my holy \[H-QADOSH\] mountain, so all the nations will drink continually. They will drink and swallow, and be as though they had never been.

**17** But on Mount Zion there will be those who escape, and it will be holy \[H-QADOSH\], and the house of Jacob will take possession of those who dispossessed them.

**18** The house of Jacob will be a fire, and the house of Joseph a flame, and the house of Esau stubble. They will burn them and consume them, and there will be no survivor of the house of Esau, for the LORD has spoken.

**19** Those of the Negeb will possess the mount of Esau, and those of the lowland the Philistine plain. They will possess the territory of Ephraim and the territory of Samaria, and Benjamin will possess Gilead.

**20** The exiles of this host of the people of Israel will possess what belongs to the Canaanites as far as Zarephath, and the exiles of Jerusalem who are in Sepharad will possess the cities of the Negeb.

**21** Saviours will go up on Mount Zion to rule the mount of Esau, and the kingdom will be the LORD's.

*Review note: Obadiah was drafted in WABV wording. It is the heading and the message among the nations (1), the judgement on Edom and the loss of its wisdom and its allies (2-9), the reasons for the judgement in the day Jerusalem was taken (10-14), and the day of the LORD, the end of the house of Esau and the kingdom of the LORD (15-21). The whole book is in poetry, and the verses are set in lines. The numbering is the same as the Hebrew. In 7 "the people of your covenant" translates anshei vritekha and is tagged H-BERIT. In 15 "return" translates yashuv and is tagged H-SHUV. In 16 and 17 "my holy mountain" and "holy" are tagged H-QADOSH. The lines of verses 1b, 2, 3, 4 and 5 that are the same in the Hebrew as Jeremiah 49:14, 49:15, 49:16 and 49:9 are given the same wording as the WABV text of Jeremiah 49 ("a message from the LORD, and an envoy has been sent among the nations", "I will make you small among the nations", "the pride of your heart ... you who live in the clefts of the rock", "though you make your nest as high as the eagle's, from there I will bring you down" and "grape gatherers came to you, would they not leave gleanings"). The words of 15 ("your payment will return on your own head") are matched with Joel 3:4 and 3:7. The two books also share the words on the day of the LORD (15 with Joel 1:15 and 2:1) and on Mount Zion (17 with Joel 2:32). There are no departures from the Hebrew in Obadiah. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 7 (the last line, "there is no understanding in him"), 20 (the exiles of this host, and Sepharad) and 21 ("saviours"). The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the end of the house of Esau (18) are rendered as written, with no comment. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Footnotes

- **1** "Obadiah" translates *Ovadyah*, "servant of the LORD". "Vision" translates *chazon*, "a message" translates *shemu'ah*, and "an envoy" translates *tsir*. The words "a message from the LORD, and an envoy has been sent among the nations" are the same in the Hebrew as Jeremiah 49:14, and they are given the same wording as the WABV text of Jeremiah. The last line ("rise up, let us rise against her for battle") is not the same as Jeremiah 49:14.
- **2** "I will make you small" translates *qatan netattikha*, and "utterly despised" translates *bazuy attah me'od*. The first words are the same as Jeremiah 49:15.
- **3** "The pride of your heart" translates *zadon libbekha*, and "you who live in the clefts of the rock" translates *shokhni be-chagvei sela*. The words are the same as Jeremiah 49:16, and the word *sela*, "rock", may also be the name of a city (Sela, later Petra). "Whose dwelling is high" translates *merom shivto*.
- **4** "Though you make your nest as high as the eagle's" and "from there I will bring you down, declares the LORD" are the same as Jeremiah 49:16. "The stars" translates *kokhavim*.
- **5** "Thieves" translates *gannavim*, "robbers by night" translates *shoddedei laylah*, and "how you have been destroyed" translates *eikh nidmeita*, which is hard. "Would they not leave gleanings" translates *halo yash'iru ollot*, and the words "if grape gatherers came to you" are the same as Jeremiah 49:9, in a different order.
- **6** "Has been searched out" translates *nechpesu*, and "his hidden treasures" translates *matspunav*. See Jeremiah 49:10.
- **7** "Your allies" translates *kol anshei verit*, and "the people of your covenant" translates *anshei vritekha*. See entry H-BERIT. "Your friends" translates *anshei shelomekha*, literally "the men of your peace". "Set a trap beneath you" translates *yasimu mazor tachtekha*, in which *mazor* is rare. "There is no understanding in him" translates *ein tevunah bo*, which is hard.
- **8** "The wise" translates *chakhamim*, and "understanding" translates *tevunah*. See Jeremiah 49:7.
- **9** "Teman" translates *Teman*, a region of Edom.
- **10** "The violence done" translates *chamas*, and "shame" translates *bushah*. Edom and Jacob are brothers (Genesis 25:19-34).
- **11** "You stood aloof" translates *be-yom amadkha mi-neged*, and "cast lots for Jerusalem" translates *yadu goral al-Yerushalayim*.
- **12** "Gloat" translates *tere*, "misfortune" translates *nokhro*, and "boast" translates *tagdel pikha*, literally "make your mouth great".
- **13** "Calamity" translates *eido*, and "disaster" translates *ra'ato*.
- **14** "The crossroads" translates *perek*, and "hand over" translates *tasgir*.
- **15** "The day of the LORD" translates *yom YHWH*. "Your payment will return" translates *gemulkha yashuv*, and the words "return on your own head" are the same as Joel 3:4 and 3:7. See entry H-SHUV.
- **16** "My holy mountain" translates *har qodshi*. See entry H-QADOSH. "They will drink and swallow" translates *ve-shatu ve-la'u*, in which *la'u* is rare and hard.
- **17** "Those who escape" translates *pelitah*, "holy" translates *qodesh*, and "those who dispossessed them" translates *morasheihem*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **18** "Stubble" translates *qash*, and "survivor" translates *saridd*. It is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **19** The verse is very hard in its structure, and the verbs are supplied from the first clause. "The Negeb" and "the lowland" translate *ha-Negev* and *ha-Shefelah*.
- **20** "The exiles of this host" translates *galut ha-chel ha-zeh*, which is hard. "Zarephath" translates *Tsarefat*, a town near Sidon, and "Sepharad" translates *Sefarad*, which is not certainly known (it may be Sardis, in Asia Minor).
- **21** "Saviours" translates *moshi'im*, and the Greek has "those who are saved". "The kingdom will be the LORD's" translates *ve-hayetah la-YHWH ha-melukhah*.
