# WABV: 3 John

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[G-AGAPE\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Third John is the twenty-fifth book of the New Testament in the order that the WABV text follows, and the sixth of the seven general letters. It is written in Greek, and the WABV text is drafted from a critical Greek text, with the Byzantine tradition noted in the footnotes where it differs, as the Charter requires. It is the shortest book of the New Testament by the count of its Greek words, and it is a personal letter from "the elder" (verse 1) to Gaius, a believer who has shown hospitality to travelling brothers, and it speaks of Diotrephes, who refuses them and puts people out of the assembly (verses 9-10), and of Demetrius, who is well spoken of (verse 12). It has the vocabulary of 1 and 2 John (truth, love, to walk in the truth) and no teaching about doctrine. Who the elder is is not stated, and the tradition names John, and the WABV text does not decide. The verse numbering is the usual numbering of fourteen verses, and some editions divide the last verse in two, so that there are fifteen, and the footnote records the matter. The WABV text of 3 John is drafted with the voice standard (Part 46 of the review package). Where the text of a critical edition is not certain, the draft says so in the footnote and asks the team to confirm the reading. It makes the same fixed choices as 1 and 2 John for the words that the letters share. *Agape* is rendered "love" and is tagged G-AGAPE. *Aletheia* is rendered "truth" and is tagged G-ALETHEIA. *Pistis* is rendered "faith" and is tagged G-PISTIS. *Ekklesia* is rendered "assembly" and is tagged G-EKKLESIA, and the team should decide between "assembly" and "church". *Psyche* is rendered "soul" and is tagged G-PSYCHE. *Eirene* is rendered "peace" and is tagged G-EIRENE. *Philoi* (verse 14) is rendered "friends". The markers beginning with G- (for Greek words) are defined in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. The wording of the WABV text is drafted from the Greek and is not taken from any other English translation.*

## The letter, verses 1 to 14

**1** The elder to the beloved Gaius, whom I love \[G-AGAPE\] in truth \[G-ALETHEIA\].

**2** Beloved, I pray that all may go well with you and that you may be in good health, as it goes well with your soul \[G-PSYCHE\].

**3** For I rejoiced greatly when the brothers came and testified to your truth \[G-ALETHEIA\], as indeed you are walking in the truth.

**4** I have no greater joy than to hear that my children are walking in the truth \[G-ALETHEIA\].

**5** Beloved, it is a faithful \[G-PISTIS\] thing you do in all your efforts for these brothers, strangers as they are,

**6** who testified to your love \[G-AGAPE\] before the assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\]. You will do well to send them on their journey in a manner worthy of God.

**7** For they have gone out for the sake of the name, accepting nothing from the nations.

**8** Therefore we ought to support people like these, that we may be fellow workers for the truth \[G-ALETHEIA\].

**9** I have written something to the assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\], but Diotrephes, who likes to put himself first among them, does not acknowledge our authority.

**10** So if I come, I will bring up what he is doing, accusing us with wicked words. And not content with that, he refuses to welcome the brothers, and also stops those who want to and puts them out of the assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\].

**11** Beloved, do not imitate evil but imitate good. Whoever does good is from God. Whoever does evil has not seen God.

**12** Demetrius has received a good testimony from everyone, and from the truth \[G-ALETHEIA\] itself. We also add our testimony, and you know that our testimony is true.

**13** I had much to write to you, but I would rather not write with pen and ink.

**14** I hope to see you soon, and we will talk face to face. Peace \[G-EIRENE\] be to you. The friends greet you. Greet the friends, each by name.

*Review note: 3 John was drafted in WABV wording, from the Greek, using the fixed choices set out in the introduction to this book, and with the WABV voice standard (Part 46 of the review package) applied. It has the greeting and the prayer for Gaius (verses 1-4), the faithful help for the travelling brothers (5-8), Diotrephes (9-10), the call to imitate good and Demetrius (11-12) and the closing (13-14). The numbering is the usual numbering of fourteen verses, and some editions divide verse 14 in two, so that there are fifteen. No new G- marker is used, and the markers are G-AGAPE (1, 6), G-ALETHEIA (1-12), G-PSYCHE (2), G-PISTIS (5), G-EKKLESIA (6-10) and G-EIRENE (14). Several readings are drafted with some uncertainty and are to be confirmed against the critical edition in use: verse 3 ("your truth" is rendered as written), verse 4 ("joy" or "grace", and "my children" or "my own children"), verse 7 ("the name" or "his name"), verse 8 ("support" or "receive") and verse 11 (the Byzantine "but he who does evil"). The main points are these, and the footnotes carry the Greek: verse 2 "as it goes well with your soul" is rendered as written, and the "soul" is tagged G-PSYCHE; verse 5 "a faithful thing" translates *piston poieis*, and may be read "you act faithfully"; verse 9 "Diotrephes, who likes to put himself first" translates *ho philoproteuon*, and is rendered as written, and the "assembly" is tagged G-EKKLESIA; verse 10 "puts them out of the assembly" is rendered as written, and the footnote records that it may be a formal act of the assembly or an exercise of one man's power, and the WABV text does not decide; verse 12 "Demetrius" is named only here; verse 14 "the friends" translates *hoi philoi*, and is rendered as written. There are no departures from the critical Greek text in 3 John. The Greek is hard in some places, including verses 7, 9, 10 and 12. The words on Gaius and the truth (1-4), the travelling brothers (5-8), Diotrephes (9-10), imitating good (11) and Demetrius (12) are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. The section needs a check against the Greek by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked. With this the book of 3 John is complete in first draft.*

## Appendix A: Footnotes

- **1** "The elder" translates *ho presbuteros*, and who he is is not stated, and the tradition names John. "Gaius" is a common name and is also the name of several people in Acts and the letters of Paul (Acts 19:29, 20:4, Romans 16:23, 1 Corinthians 1:14), and the footnote records that who this Gaius is is not known. See entries G-AGAPE and G-ALETHEIA.
- **2** "As it goes well with your soul" is rendered as written. See entry G-PSYCHE.
- **3** "Testified to your truth" is rendered as written, and may be read "testified to your faithfulness to the truth".
- **4** "Joy" is the reading that the draft follows (*charan*), and some manuscripts read "grace" (*charin*), and "my children" is the reading that the draft follows, and some manuscripts read "my own children", and the team should confirm the readings against the critical edition in use.
- **5** "A faithful thing you do" translates *piston poieis*, and may be read "you act faithfully". See entry G-PISTIS.
- **6** "Testified to your love before the assembly" is rendered as written, and "send them on their journey" translates *propempsas*, and means to help on the way with supplies. See entry G-EKKLESIA.
- **7** "For the sake of the name" is the reading that the draft follows, and some manuscripts read "his name", and "accepting nothing from the nations" is rendered as written, and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use.
- **8** "We ought to support people like these" is the reading that the draft follows (*hupolambanein*), and some manuscripts read "receive" (*apolambanein*), and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use.
- **9** "I have written something to the assembly" is rendered as written, and the letter is not known. "Diotrephes, who likes to put himself first" translates *ho philoproteuon autōn Diotrephes*, and is rendered as written.
- **10** "Puts them out of the assembly" is rendered as written, and may be a formal act of the assembly or an exercise of one man's power, and the WABV text does not decide.
- **11** "Do not imitate evil but imitate good. Whoever does good is from God" is rendered as written, and some manuscripts add "but he who does evil" before the second half, and the footnote records the matter.
- **12** "Demetrius" is named only here, and "a good testimony from everyone, and from the truth itself" is rendered as written, and "we also add our testimony" is as in John 19:35 and 21:24.
- **13** "With pen and ink" is rendered as written, and see 2 John 12.
- **14** "The friends greet you. Greet the friends, each by name" is rendered as written, and "the friends" translates *hoi philoi*, and the verse is divided in two in some editions (verses 14 and 15). See entry G-EIRENE.
