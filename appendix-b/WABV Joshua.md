# WABV: Joshua

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-QADOSH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end.*

## Joshua 1-5: Crossing into the Land

### Chapter 1

**1** After the death of Moses the servant of the LORD, the LORD spoke to Joshua son of Nun, the assistant of Moses:

**2** "Moses my servant is dead. Now get up and cross this Jordan, you and all this people, into the land that I am giving to them, to the Israelites.

**3** Every place where the sole of your foot treads I have given to you, as I promised Moses.

**4** Your territory will reach from the wilderness and this Lebanon as far as the great river, the Euphrates, all the land of the Hittites, and to the Great Sea in the west.

**5** No one will be able to stand against you all the days of your life. As I was with Moses, so I will be with you. I will not fail you or abandon you.

**6** Be strong and courageous, for you will lead this people to inherit the land that I swore to their ancestors to give them.

**7** Only be strong and very courageous, and take care to do all the teaching \[H-TORAH\] that Moses my servant commanded you. Do not turn from it to the right or to the left, so that you may succeed wherever you go.

**8** This book of the teaching is not to leave your mouth. You are to recite it day and night, so that you take care to do everything written in it. Then your way will prosper, and then you will succeed.

**9** Have I not commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid or dismayed, for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go."

**10** Then Joshua commanded the officers of the people:

**11** "Go through the camp and tell the people, 'Get food ready for yourselves, for in three days you are to cross this Jordan, to go in and take possession of the land that the LORD your God is giving you to possess.'"

**12** To the Reubenites, the Gadites and the half-tribe of Manasseh Joshua said:

**13** "Remember the word that Moses the servant of the LORD commanded you: 'The LORD your God is giving you rest and has given you this land.'

**14** Your wives, your little ones and your livestock may stay in the land that Moses gave you across the Jordan, but all your fighting men are to cross over armed ahead of your kinsmen, and you are to help them,

**15** until the LORD gives rest to your kinsmen as he has to you, and they also take possession of the land that the LORD your God is giving them. Then you may return to the land of your own possession and take possession of it, the land that Moses the servant of the LORD gave you across the Jordan, towards the sunrise."

**16** They answered Joshua: "Everything you have commanded us we will do, and wherever you send us we will go.

**17** Just as we listened to Moses in everything, so we will listen to you. Only may the LORD your God be with you, as he was with Moses.

**18** Anyone who rebels against your command and does not listen to your words, in whatever you command him, will be put to death. Only be strong and courageous."

### Chapter 2

**1** Joshua son of Nun secretly sent two men from Shittim as scouts, saying, "Go and look over the land, especially Jericho." They went and came to the house of a prostitute whose name was Rahab, and stayed there.

**2** The king of Jericho was told, "Some Israelites have come here tonight to search out the land."

**3** So the king of Jericho sent word to Rahab: "Bring out the men who have come to you, who came into your house, for they have come to search out the whole land."

**4** But the woman had taken the two men and hidden them. She said, "Yes, the men did come to me, but I did not know where they were from.

**5** At dusk, when it was time to shut the gate, the men went out. I do not know where they went. Chase after them quickly, and you may catch them."

**6** But she had taken them up to the roof and hidden them under the stalks of flax that she had laid out on the roof.

**7** So the men chased after them on the road to the Jordan, to the fords, and as soon as the pursuers had gone out, the gate was shut.

**8** Before the men lay down, she came up to them on the roof

**9** and said to them, "I know that the LORD has given you the land, and that terror of you has fallen on us, and that all who live in the land are melting in fear before you.

**10** For we have heard how the LORD dried up the water of the Red Sea before you when you came out of Egypt, and what you did to the two kings of the Amorites across the Jordan, to Sihon and Og, whom you devoted to destruction \[H-CHEREM\].

**11** When we heard it, our hearts melted, and no one had any courage left because of you, for the LORD your God is God in the heavens above and on the earth below.

**12** Now, please swear to me by the LORD that, since I have shown you faithful love \[H-HESED\], you will also show faithful love to my father's household, and give me a sure sign

**13** that you will let my father and mother, my brothers and sisters, and all who belong to them live, and save our lives from death."

**14** The men said to her, "Our lives for yours! If you do not tell this business of ours, then when the LORD gives us the land we will deal with you in faithful love and truth."

**15** Then she let them down by a rope through the window, for her house was built into the city wall, and she lived on the wall.

**16** She said to them, "Go to the hill country, so that the pursuers do not meet you. Hide there for three days, until the pursuers return. Then go on your way."

**17** The men said to her, "We will be released from this oath you have made us swear

**18** unless, when we come into the land, you tie this cord of scarlet thread in the window through which you let us down, and gather into your house your father and mother, your brothers and all your father's household.

**19** If anyone goes out of the doors of your house into the street, his blood will be on his own head, and we will be free of guilt. But for anyone who is with you in the house, his blood will be on our heads if a hand is laid on him.

**20** And if you tell this business of ours, we will be released from the oath you made us swear."

**21** She said, "Let it be as you say." She sent them away, and they left. Then she tied the scarlet cord in the window.

**22** They went and came to the hill country and stayed there three days, until the pursuers returned. The pursuers had searched all along the road and had not found them.

**23** Then the two men came back down from the hill country, crossed over and came to Joshua son of Nun, and told him everything that had happened to them.

**24** They said to Joshua, "The LORD has given all the land into our hands. All who live in the land are melting in fear before us."

### Chapter 3

**1** Joshua got up early in the morning, and he and all the Israelites set out from Shittim and came to the Jordan. They stayed there before they crossed over.

**2** After three days the officers went through the camp

**3** and commanded the people: "When you see the ark of the covenant of the LORD your God, and the Levitical priests carrying it, you are to set out from your place and follow it.

**4** But keep a distance of about two thousand cubits between you and it. Do not come near it, so that you may know the way you are to go, for you have not been this way before."

**5** Joshua said to the people, "Make yourselves holy \[H-QADOSH\], for tomorrow the LORD will do wonders among you."

**6** Joshua said to the priests, "Take up the ark of the covenant and cross over ahead of the people." So they took up the ark of the covenant and went ahead of the people.

**7** The LORD said to Joshua, "Today I will begin to make you great in the eyes of all Israel, so that they may know that, as I was with Moses, I will be with you.

**8** You are to command the priests who carry the ark of the covenant: 'When you come to the edge of the waters of the Jordan, stand still in the Jordan.'"

**9** Joshua said to the Israelites, "Come here and listen to the words of the LORD your God."

**10** Joshua said, "This is how you will know that the living God is among you, and that he will certainly drive out from before you the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Hivites, the Perizzites, the Girgashites, the Amorites and the Jebusites:

**11** the ark of the covenant of the Lord of all the earth is about to cross over ahead of you into the Jordan.

**12** Now choose twelve men from the tribes of Israel, one man for each tribe.

**13** When the soles of the feet of the priests who carry the ark of the LORD, the Lord of all the earth, come to rest in the waters of the Jordan, the waters of the Jordan will be cut off, the waters flowing down from above, and they will stand in a heap."

**14** So when the people set out from their tents to cross the Jordan, with the priests carrying the ark of the covenant ahead of the people,

**15** and when those carrying the ark reached the Jordan and the feet of the priests carrying the ark touched the edge of the water (the Jordan overflows all its banks throughout the harvest),

**16** the waters flowing down from above stood still and rose up in a heap, far off at Adam, the town beside Zarethan, and the waters flowing down to the Sea of the Arabah, the Salt Sea, were completely cut off. The people crossed over opposite Jericho.

**17** The priests who carried the ark of the covenant of the LORD stood firm on dry ground in the middle of the Jordan, while all Israel crossed over on dry ground, until the whole nation had finished crossing the Jordan.

### Chapter 4

**1** When the whole nation had finished crossing the Jordan, the LORD said to Joshua:

**2** "Choose twelve men from the people, one man for each tribe,

**3** and command them: 'Take twelve stones from here, out of the middle of the Jordan, from the place where the priests' feet stood firm, and carry them over with you and put them down in the place where you will spend the night.'"

**4** So Joshua called the twelve men whom he had appointed from the Israelites, one man for each tribe,

**5** and said to them, "Go over ahead of the ark of the LORD your God into the middle of the Jordan, and each of you is to lift a stone on his shoulder, one for each of the tribes of the Israelites,

**6** so that this may be a sign among you. When your children ask you in time to come, 'What do these stones mean to you?'

**7** you are to tell them that the waters of the Jordan were cut off before the ark of the covenant of the LORD. When it crossed the Jordan, the waters of the Jordan were cut off. So these stones will be a memorial for the Israelites for ever."

**8** The Israelites did as Joshua commanded. They took up twelve stones out of the middle of the Jordan, one for each of the tribes of the Israelites, as the LORD had told Joshua, and carried them over with them to the place where they camped, and put them down there.

**9** Joshua also set up twelve stones in the middle of the Jordan, in the place where the feet of the priests who carried the ark of the covenant had stood. They are there to this day.

**10** The priests who carried the ark stood in the middle of the Jordan until everything was done that the LORD had commanded Joshua to tell the people, according to all that Moses had commanded Joshua. The people hurried across.

**11** When all the people had finished crossing, the ark of the LORD and the priests crossed over in front of the people.

**12** The Reubenites, the Gadites and the half-tribe of Manasseh crossed over armed ahead of the Israelites, as Moses had told them.

**13** About forty thousand men ready for war crossed over before the LORD, to the plains of Jericho, for battle.

**14** On that day the LORD made Joshua great in the eyes of all Israel, and they stood in awe of him all the days of his life, as they had of Moses.

**15** The LORD said to Joshua:

**16** "Command the priests who carry the ark of the testimony to come up out of the Jordan."

**17** So Joshua commanded the priests, "Come up out of the Jordan."

**18** When the priests who carried the ark of the covenant of the LORD came up out of the middle of the Jordan, and the soles of their feet were lifted onto dry ground, the waters of the Jordan returned to their place and flowed over all its banks as before.

**19** The people came up out of the Jordan on the tenth day of the first month and camped at Gilgal, on the eastern edge of Jericho.

**20** Those twelve stones that they had taken out of the Jordan Joshua set up at Gilgal.

**21** He said to the Israelites, "When your children ask their parents in time to come, 'What do these stones mean?'

**22** you are to tell your children, 'Israel crossed over this Jordan on dry ground.'

**23** For the LORD your God dried up the waters of the Jordan before you until you had crossed over, as the LORD your God did to the Red Sea, which he dried up before us until we had crossed over,

**24** so that all the peoples of the earth may know that the hand of the LORD is strong, and so that you may fear the LORD your God for ever."

### Chapter 5

**1** When all the kings of the Amorites on the west side of the Jordan and all the kings of the Canaanites by the sea heard how the LORD had dried up the waters of the Jordan before the Israelites until they had crossed over, their hearts melted, and there was no courage left in them because of the Israelites.

**2** At that time the LORD said to Joshua, "Make flint knives and circumcise the Israelites again, a second time."

**3** So Joshua made flint knives and circumcised the Israelites at Gibeath-haaraloth.

**4** This is why Joshua circumcised them: all the people who came out of Egypt, the males, all the men of war, died in the wilderness on the road after they came out of Egypt.

**5** All the people who came out had been circumcised, but all the people born in the wilderness on the road after they came out of Egypt had not been circumcised.

**6** For the Israelites walked in the wilderness forty years, until all the nation, the men of war who came out of Egypt, had died, because they did not listen to the voice of the LORD. The LORD had sworn to them that he would not let them see the land that he had sworn to their ancestors to give us, a land flowing with milk and honey.

**7** It was their sons, whom he raised up in their place, that Joshua circumcised. They were uncircumcised, because they had not been circumcised on the road.

**8** When the whole nation had been circumcised, they stayed where they were in the camp until they were healed.

**9** The LORD said to Joshua, "Today I have rolled away the disgrace of Egypt from you." So the place is called Gilgal to this day.

**10** While the Israelites were camped at Gilgal, they kept the Passover on the evening of the fourteenth day of the month, on the plains of Jericho.

**11** On the day after the Passover, on that very day, they ate some of the produce of the land, unleavened bread and roasted grain.

**12** The manna stopped on the day after they ate the produce of the land. The Israelites no longer had manna, but ate the crops of the land of Canaan that year.

**13** When Joshua was near Jericho, he lifted up his eyes and looked, and there was a man standing in front of him with his sword drawn in his hand. Joshua went to him and said, "Are you for us, or for our enemies?"

**14** He said, "Neither. I am the commander of the army of the LORD. I have come now." Joshua fell on his face to the ground and bowed down and said to him, "What does my lord say to his servant?"

**15** The commander of the army of the LORD said to Joshua, "Take off your sandals from your feet, for the place where you are standing is holy." And Joshua did so.

*Review note: Joshua 1 to 5 was drafted in WABV wording, with the same terms as in Numbers and Deuteronomy for the events and phrases that the books share (for example "scouts", "devoted to destruction" and "the Ten Words"). The Jordan crossing and the Rahab story are very familiar, and some wording may sit close to existing translations. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Joshua 6-8: Jericho, Achan and Ai

### Chapter 6

**1** Now Jericho was shut up tight because of the Israelites. No one went out and no one came in.

**2** The LORD said to Joshua, "See, I have handed Jericho over to you, with its king and its fighting men.

**3** You are to march round the city, all the men of war, going round it once. Do this for six days.

**4** Seven priests are to carry seven ram's-horn trumpets in front of the ark. On the seventh day you are to march round the city seven times, with the priests blowing the trumpets.

**5** When they sound a long blast on the ram's horn, and you hear the sound of the trumpet, all the people are to shout a great shout. Then the wall of the city will fall down flat, and the people are to go up, each man straight ahead."

**6** So Joshua son of Nun called the priests and said to them, "Take up the ark of the covenant, and let seven priests carry seven ram's-horn trumpets in front of the ark of the LORD."

**7** He said to the people, "Go forward and march round the city, and let the armed men go ahead of the ark of the LORD."

**8** As Joshua had told the people, the seven priests carrying the seven ram's-horn trumpets before the LORD went forward and blew the trumpets, with the ark of the covenant of the LORD following them.

**9** The armed men went in front of the priests who blew the trumpets, and the rear guard came after the ark, while the trumpets sounded all the time.

**10** But Joshua had commanded the people, "You are not to shout or let your voice be heard, and no word is to come out of your mouth, until the day I say to you, 'Shout.' Then you are to shout."

**11** So he had the ark of the LORD go round the city, circling it once. Then they came back to the camp and spent the night in the camp.

**12** Joshua got up early in the morning, and the priests took up the ark of the LORD.

**13** The seven priests carrying the seven ram's-horn trumpets before the ark of the LORD went on, blowing the trumpets all the time. The armed men went in front of them, and the rear guard came after the ark of the LORD, while the trumpets sounded all the time.

**14** On the second day they marched round the city once and returned to the camp. They did this for six days.

**15** On the seventh day they got up early, at daybreak, and marched round the city in the same way seven times. That was the only day they marched round the city seven times.

**16** At the seventh time, when the priests blew the trumpets, Joshua said to the people, "Shout, for the LORD has given you the city.

**17** The city and everything in it is to be devoted to the LORD for destruction \[H-CHEREM\]. Only Rahab the prostitute and all who are with her in her house are to live, because she hid the messengers we sent.

**18** But you are to keep yourselves from what is devoted, so that you do not covet it and take some of what is devoted, and so make the camp of Israel a thing devoted to destruction and bring trouble on it.

**19** All the silver and gold, and the vessels of bronze and iron, are holy to the LORD. They are to go into the treasury of the LORD."

**20** So the people shouted, and the trumpets were blown. When the people heard the sound of the trumpet, they shouted a great shout, and the wall fell down flat, and the people went up into the city, each man straight ahead, and they captured the city.

**21** They devoted to destruction everything in the city, men and women, young and old, ox, sheep and donkey, with the edge of the sword.

**22** Joshua said to the two men who had scouted the land, "Go into the prostitute's house and bring the woman out of it, with all who belong to her, as you swore to her."

**23** So the young men who had been the scouts went in and brought out Rahab, her father, her mother, her brothers and all who belonged to her. They brought out all her family and put them outside the camp of Israel.

**24** They burned the city with fire, and everything in it. Only the silver and gold, and the vessels of bronze and iron, they put into the treasury of the house of the LORD.

**25** But Rahab the prostitute, with her father's household and all who belonged to her, Joshua let live. She has lived in the midst of Israel to this day, because she hid the messengers whom Joshua sent to spy out Jericho.

**26** At that time Joshua swore an oath: "Cursed before the LORD is the man who gets up and builds this city, Jericho. At the cost of his firstborn he will lay its foundation, and at the cost of his youngest he will set up its gates."

**27** So the LORD was with Joshua, and his fame spread through all the land.

### Chapter 7

**1** But the Israelites broke faith over what was devoted. Achan son of Carmi, son of Zabdi, son of Zerah, of the tribe of Judah, took some of what was devoted, and the anger of the LORD blazed against the Israelites.

**2** Joshua sent men from Jericho to Ai, which is near Beth-aven, east of Bethel, and told them, "Go up and spy out the land." So the men went up and spied out Ai.

**3** They returned to Joshua and said to him, "Do not send all the people up. Let about two or three thousand men go up and strike Ai. Do not make all the people toil up there, for they are few."

**4** So about three thousand men went up there from the people, but they fled before the men of Ai.

**5** The men of Ai struck down about thirty-six of them and chased them from the gate as far as Shebarim, and struck them down on the slope. The hearts of the people melted and became like water.

**6** Then Joshua tore his clothes and fell on his face to the ground before the ark of the LORD until evening, he and the elders of Israel. They put dust on their heads.

**7** Joshua said, "Alas, Lord GOD, why did you bring this people across the Jordan at all, only to hand us over to the Amorites to destroy us? If only we had been content to stay on the other side of the Jordan.

**8** What can I say, Lord, now that Israel has turned its back before its enemies?

**9** The Canaanites and all who live in the land will hear of it, and they will surround us and wipe out our name from the earth. And what will you do for your great name?"

**10** The LORD said to Joshua, "Get up. Why are you lying on your face?

**11** Israel has sinned. They have broken my covenant that I commanded them. They have taken some of what was devoted. They have stolen, they have lied, and they have put it among their own things.

**12** That is why the Israelites cannot stand before their enemies. They turn their backs before their enemies, because they have become devoted to destruction. I will not be with you any more unless you destroy what is devoted from among you.

**13** Get up. Make the people holy, and say, 'Make yourselves holy for tomorrow, for this is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: There is something devoted among you, Israel. You cannot stand before your enemies until you remove what is devoted from among you.

**14** In the morning you are to come forward tribe by tribe. The tribe that the LORD takes by lot is to come forward clan by clan. The clan that the LORD takes is to come forward household by household. The household that the LORD takes is to come forward man by man.

**15** The one who is taken with what is devoted is to be burned with fire, he and everything he has, because he has broken the covenant of the LORD and done a disgraceful thing in Israel.'"

**16** Joshua got up early in the morning and brought Israel forward tribe by tribe, and the tribe of Judah was taken.

**17** He brought forward the clans of Judah, and the clan of the Zerahites was taken. He brought forward the clan of the Zerahites man by man, and Zabdi was taken.

**18** He brought forward his household man by man, and Achan son of Carmi, son of Zabdi, son of Zerah, of the tribe of Judah, was taken.

**19** Then Joshua said to Achan, "My son, give glory to the LORD, the God of Israel, and make your confession to him. Tell me what you have done. Do not hide it from me."

**20** Achan answered Joshua, "It is true. I have sinned against the LORD, the God of Israel. This is what I did:

**21** when I saw among the plunder a fine cloak from Shinar, two hundred shekels of silver and a bar of gold weighing fifty shekels, I wanted them and took them. They are hidden in the ground inside my tent, with the silver underneath."

**22** Joshua sent messengers, and they ran to the tent, and there it was, hidden in his tent, with the silver underneath.

**23** They took them out of the tent and brought them to Joshua and all the Israelites, and laid them out before the LORD.

**24** Then Joshua and all Israel with him took Achan son of Zerah, with the silver, the cloak and the bar of gold, his sons and daughters, his ox, donkey and sheep, his tent and everything he had, and brought them up to the Valley of Achor.

**25** Joshua said, "Why have you brought trouble on us? The LORD brings trouble on you today." And all Israel stoned him with stones. They burned them with fire and stoned them with stones.

**26** They raised over him a great heap of stones, which is there to this day. Then the LORD turned from his burning anger. So that place has been called the Valley of Achor to this day.

### Chapter 8

**1** The LORD said to Joshua, "Do not be afraid or dismayed. Take all the fighting men with you, and get up and go up to Ai. See, I have given into your hand the king of Ai, his people, his city and his land.

**2** You are to do to Ai and its king as you did to Jericho and its king, except that you may take its plunder and its livestock as spoil for yourselves. Set an ambush against the city, behind it."

**3** So Joshua and all the fighting men set out to go up to Ai. Joshua chose thirty thousand mighty warriors and sent them out by night.

**4** He commanded them: "Listen, you are to lie in ambush against the city, behind it. Do not go very far from the city, but all of you be ready.

**5** I and all the people with me will approach the city. When they come out against us as they did the first time, we will run away from them.

**6** They will come out after us until we have drawn them away from the city, for they will say, 'They are running away from us as they did the first time.' So we will run away from them.

**7** Then you are to rise from the ambush and take the city, for the LORD your God will hand it over to you.

**8** When you have taken the city, set it on fire. Do it according to the word of the LORD. See, I have commanded you."

**9** So Joshua sent them off, and they went to the place of the ambush and lay in wait between Bethel and Ai, to the west of Ai. But Joshua spent that night among the people.

**10** Joshua got up early in the morning and mustered the people, and he went up, he and the elders of Israel, ahead of the people to Ai.

**11** All the fighting men who were with him went up and came near and arrived in front of the city and camped on the north side of Ai, with a valley between them and Ai.

**12** He took about five thousand men and set them in ambush between Bethel and Ai, to the west of the city.

**13** So they took up their positions, the main camp north of the city and its rear guard west of the city. But Joshua spent that night in the valley.

**14** When the king of Ai saw this, he and all his people, the men of the city, hurried and got up early and went out to meet Israel in battle at the appointed place facing the Arabah. But he did not know that there was an ambush against him behind the city.

**15** Joshua and all Israel pretended to be beaten by them and ran away in the direction of the wilderness.

**16** So all the people in the city were called together to chase after them, and as they chased Joshua they were drawn away from the city.

**17** Not a man was left in Ai or Bethel who did not go out after Israel. They left the city open and chased Israel.

**18** Then the LORD said to Joshua, "Hold out the javelin that is in your hand towards Ai, for I will give it into your hand." So Joshua held out the javelin in his hand towards the city.

**19** As soon as he stretched out his hand, the men in the ambush rose quickly from their place and ran, and entered the city and captured it. They quickly set the city on fire.

**20** When the men of Ai looked back, they saw the smoke of the city going up to the sky. They had no strength to run this way or that, for the people who had been running to the wilderness turned back on their pursuers.

**21** When Joshua and all Israel saw that the ambush had captured the city and that the smoke of the city was going up, they turned back and struck down the men of Ai.

**22** The others came out of the city to meet them, so that they were in the middle of Israel, some on this side and some on that. Israel struck them down until none were left who survived or escaped.

**23** But the king of Ai they took alive, and brought him to Joshua.

**24** When Israel had finished killing all the people of Ai in the open country, in the wilderness where they chased them, and all of them had fallen by the edge of the sword to the very last man, all Israel returned to Ai and struck it down with the edge of the sword.

**25** All who fell that day, men and women, were twelve thousand, all the people of Ai.

**26** For Joshua did not draw back his hand, with which he had held out the javelin, until he had devoted to destruction all who lived in Ai.

**27** Only the livestock and the plunder of that city Israel took as spoil for themselves, according to the word of the LORD that he had commanded Joshua.

**28** So Joshua burned Ai and made it a heap of ruins for ever, a desolation to this day.

**29** The king of Ai he hanged on a tree until evening. At sunset Joshua commanded, and they took his body down from the tree and threw it down at the entrance of the city gate, and raised over it a great heap of stones, which is there to this day.

**30** Then Joshua built an altar to the LORD, the God of Israel, on Mount Ebal,

**31** as Moses the servant of the LORD had commanded the Israelites, as it is written in the book of the teaching of Moses: an altar of uncut stones, on which no iron had been used. They offered burnt offerings on it to the LORD and sacrificed peace offerings.

**32** There, in the presence of the Israelites, Joshua wrote on the stones a copy of the teaching of Moses, which Moses had written.

**33** All Israel, foreigner and native alike, with their elders, officers and judges, stood on both sides of the ark in front of the Levitical priests who carried the ark of the covenant of the LORD, half of them in front of Mount Gerizim and half of them in front of Mount Ebal, as Moses the servant of the LORD had commanded at the first, to bless the people of Israel.

**34** After that he read aloud all the words of the teaching, the blessing and the curse, according to all that is written in the book of the teaching.

**35** There was not a word of all that Moses had commanded that Joshua did not read aloud in front of the whole assembly of Israel, with the women, the little ones and the foreigners who lived among them.

**16** "This is what the whole community of the LORD says: What is this breach of faith that you have committed against the God of Israel, turning away today from following the LORD by building yourselves an altar, in rebellion against the LORD today?

**18** and now you are turning away today from following the LORD! If you rebel against the LORD today, tomorrow he will be angry with the whole community of Israel.

**20** Did not Achan son of Zerah break faith over what was devoted, and wrath fell on the whole community of Israel? He was not the only one to die for his sin."

**22** "The Mighty One, God, the LORD! The Mighty One, God, the LORD! He knows, and may Israel know too. If it was in rebellion or in breach of faith against the LORD, do not spare us today

**23** for building an altar to turn away from following the LORD. If we built it to offer burnt offerings or grain offerings on it, or to make peace offerings on it, may the LORD himself call us to account.

**24** No, we did it out of fear of what might be said. In time to come your children might say to our children, 'What have you to do with the LORD, the God of Israel?

**3** You have seen everything the LORD your God has done to all these nations for your sake, for it is the LORD your God who has fought for you.

**4** See, I have allotted to you as an inheritance for your tribes the nations that remain, as well as all the nations I have cut off, from the Jordan to the Great Sea in the west.

**5** The LORD your God himself will push them back before you and drive them out ahead of you, and you will take possession of their land, as the LORD your God promised you.

**6** So be very strong, to keep and to do all that is written in the book of the teaching of Moses, without turning aside from it to the right or to the left,

**7** so that you do not mix with these nations that remain among you, or mention the names of their gods, or swear by them, or serve them or bow down to them,

**8** but hold fast to the LORD your God, as you have done to this day.

**9** For the LORD has driven out great and strong nations before you, and to this day no one has been able to stand against you.

**10** One of you chases a thousand, because it is the LORD your God who fights for you, as he promised you.

**11** So take great care, for your own lives, to love the LORD your God.

**12** For if you turn back and attach yourselves to the survivors of these nations that remain among you, and intermarry with them, and mix with them and they with you,

**13** know for certain that the LORD your God will not go on driving out these nations before you. They will become a snare and a trap for you, a whip on your sides and thorns in your eyes, until you perish from this good land that the LORD your God has given you.

**14** Now I am about to go the way of all the earth. You know in your hearts and souls, all of you, that not one word has failed of all the good words that the LORD your God spoke about you. All have come to pass for you. Not one of them has failed.

**15** But just as all the good words that the LORD your God spoke to you have come to pass, so the LORD will bring on you every evil word, until he has destroyed you from this good land that the LORD your God has given you,

**16** if you break the covenant \[H-BERIT\] of the LORD your God, which he commanded you, and go and serve other gods and bow down to them. Then the anger of the LORD will blaze against you, and you will quickly perish from the good land that he has given you."

### Chapter 24

**1** Then Joshua gathered all the tribes of Israel at Shechem. He called the elders, the heads, the judges and the officers of Israel, and they presented themselves before God.

**2** Joshua said to all the people, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: 'Long ago your ancestors lived beyond the River, Terah, the father of Abraham and of Nahor, and they served other gods.

**3** Then I took your father Abraham from beyond the River and led him through all the land of Canaan, and I made his descendants many. I gave him Isaac.

**4** To Isaac I gave Jacob and Esau. To Esau I gave the hill country of Seir as a possession, but Jacob and his sons went down to Egypt.

**5** I sent Moses and Aaron, and I struck Egypt with what I did in the middle of it, and afterwards I brought you out.

**6** I brought your ancestors out of Egypt, and you came to the sea. The Egyptians chased your ancestors with chariots and horsemen as far as the Red Sea.

**7** When they cried out to the LORD, he put darkness between you and the Egyptians, and made the sea come over them and cover them. Your own eyes saw what I did in Egypt. Then you lived in the wilderness for a long time.

**8** I brought you to the land of the Amorites who lived across the Jordan. They fought against you, and I handed them over to you, and you took possession of their land. I destroyed them before you.

**9** Then Balak son of Zippor, king of Moab, got up and fought against Israel. He sent and called Balaam son of Beor to curse you,

**10** but I would not listen to Balaam, so he blessed you instead. So I saved you from his hand.

**11** You crossed the Jordan and came to Jericho, and the citizens of Jericho fought against you, and also the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Girgashites, the Hivites and the Jebusites. I handed them over to you.

**12** I sent the hornet ahead of you, and it drove them out before you, the two kings of the Amorites. It was not by your sword or your bow.

**13** I gave you a land on which you had not worked, and towns that you had not built, and you live in them. You eat from vineyards and olive groves that you did not plant.'

**14** "So now fear the LORD and serve him completely and faithfully. Put away the gods that your ancestors served beyond the River and in Egypt, and serve the LORD.

**15** If it seems wrong to you to serve the LORD, choose today whom you will serve, whether the gods your ancestors served beyond the River, or the gods of the Amorites in whose land you are living. But as for me and my household, we will serve the LORD."

**16** The people answered, "Far be it from us to abandon the LORD and serve other gods.

**17** For it is the LORD our God who brought us and our ancestors up out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery, and who did those great signs before our eyes, and protected us all along the way we went and among all the peoples we passed through.

**18** The LORD drove out before us all the peoples, the Amorites who lived in the land. We too will serve the LORD, for he is our God."

**19** But Joshua said to the people, "You cannot serve the LORD, for he is a holy \[H-QADOSH\] God. He is a jealous \[H-QANNA\] God. He will not forgive your rebellion or your sins.

**20** If you abandon the LORD and serve foreign gods, he will turn and bring harm on you, and finish you off, after having done you good."

**21** The people said to Joshua, "No, we will serve the LORD."

**22** Joshua said to the people, "You are witnesses against yourselves that you have chosen the LORD for yourselves, to serve him." They said, "We are witnesses."

**23** "Then put away the foreign gods that are among you, and turn your hearts to the LORD, the God of Israel."

**24** The people said to Joshua, "We will serve the LORD our God, and we will listen to his voice."

**25** So Joshua made a covenant \[H-BERIT\] with the people that day, and set down a statute and a ruling for them at Shechem.

**26** Joshua wrote these words in the book of the teaching of God. He took a large stone and set it up there under the oak that was by the sanctuary of the LORD.

**27** Joshua said to all the people, "See, this stone will be a witness against us, for it has heard all the words of the LORD that he spoke to us. It will be a witness against you, so that you do not deny your God."

**28** Then Joshua sent the people away, each to his own inheritance.

**29** After these things Joshua son of Nun, the servant of the LORD, died at the age of a hundred and ten.

**30** They buried him in the land of his own inheritance at Timnath-serah, which is in the hill country of Ephraim, north of Mount Gaash.

**31** Israel served the LORD all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua and who knew all that the LORD had done for Israel.

**32** The bones of Joseph, which the Israelites had brought up from Egypt, they buried at Shechem, in the plot of ground that Jacob had bought from the sons of Hamor, the father of Shechem, for a hundred pieces of silver. It became the inheritance of the descendants of Joseph.

**33** And Eleazar son of Aaron died, and they buried him at Gibeah, which belonged to his son Phinehas, which had been given to him in the hill country of Ephraim.

*End of the book of Joshua. Review note: Joshua 22 to 24 was drafted in WABV wording. Chapter 22 has the altar of the eastern tribes, resolved by the offer of a witness and without bloodshed. Chapter 24:15 and 24:19 are very familiar, and some wording may sit close to existing translations. Joshua 24 also shows the Greek and Hebrew texts differing in several places, and the footnotes record the main ones. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **1:1** "Servant of the LORD" is the title given to Moses at his death (Deuteronomy 34:5). "Assistant" is Hebrew *meshareth*, one who serves a leader. Joshua is called by it from Exodus 24:13.
- **1:4** The borders repeat those of Deuteronomy 11:24. "The land of the Hittites" is a general name for the territory north of Canaan.
- **1:5** "I will not fail you or abandon you" repeats the promise to Joshua in Deuteronomy 31:6-8. It is quoted in Hebrews 13:5.
- **1:6** "Be strong and courageous" is a refrain that runs through the chapter (verses 6, 7, 9 and 18).
- **1:7** "Teaching" is Hebrew *torah*. See entry H-TORAH. "Succeed" is Hebrew *taskil*, which can also mean "act wisely".
- **1:8** "Recite" is Hebrew *hagah*, to murmur or speak quietly, as a person does when reading aloud or meditating. See Psalm 1:2.
- **1:11** "In three days" is the time given for the crossing. Chapter 2:16-22 has the scouts hiding for three days, and 3:2 has another three days. How the three periods fit together is debated.
- **1:12** The tribes east of the Jordan are reminded of their promise in Numbers 32.
- **1:18** The vow to put to death anyone who rebels shows the people's loyalty to Joshua.
- **2:1** "Scouts" is used for the two men, as in Numbers 13 and Deuteronomy 1. "Prostitute" is Hebrew *zonah*. A tradition reads it as "innkeeper", but the Hebrew word is the same, and the text is rendered as written. Rahab is named in Matthew 1:5, Hebrews 11:31 and James 2:25.
- **2:6** "Stalks of flax" were laid on the roof to dry.
- **2:9** "Melting in fear" repeats the language of Exodus 15:15. It is used again in verse 11 and in 5:1.
- **2:10** "Devoted to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM.
- **2:11** Rahab's confession that "the LORD your God is God in the heavens above and on the earth below" repeats Deuteronomy 4:39.
- **2:12** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. Rahab asks for the same faithful love that she has shown to them.
- **2:15** Her house was built into the city wall, so a rope from the window reached the ground outside.
- **2:18** The scarlet cord is a sign that marks her house, as the blood marked the houses at the Passover (Exodus 12:7, 13).
- **2:19** "His blood will be on his own head" means he will be responsible for his own death.
- **3:3** The ark is carried by the Levitical priests, as in Numbers 4 and Deuteronomy 31:9.
- **3:4** Two thousand cubits is about 900 metres. The people keep a distance from the ark.
- **3:5** "Make yourselves holy" is Hebrew *qadash*. See entry H-QADOSH. It means to prepare for the LORD's coming, by washing and keeping from impurity (compare Exodus 19:10-15).
- **3:10** The list of seven nations repeats Deuteronomy 7:1.
- **3:13** "The Lord of all the earth" is a title used here and in verse 11.
- **3:15** "The Jordan overflows all its banks throughout the harvest" means the crossing is in the spring flood.
- **3:16** "Adam" is a town on the Jordan, north of Jericho. The place named Zarethan is uncertain. The waters were held back far upstream.
- **4:3** The twelve stones are taken from the river and set up at the camp (verse 8). In verse 9 a second set of stones is set up in the middle of the river. The text may describe two sets, or one set.
- **4:6** The children's question is the same as in Exodus 12:26 and Deuteronomy 6:20.
- **4:13** "About forty thousand" is a round number. The number of fighting men in the tribes was higher in Numbers 26. The Hebrew *eleph* can mean a thousand or a unit of troops.
- **4:16** "The ark of the testimony" is another name for the ark of the covenant (see Exodus 25:22).
- **4:19** The tenth day of the first month is the date in Exodus 12:3 when the Passover lamb was chosen.
- **4:24** "The hand of the LORD is strong" and "fear the LORD" give two purposes of the memorial: that the nations know, and that Israel fear.
- **5:2** "Flint knives" are stone knives. The circumcision is called "a second time" because the whole people were circumcised in the covenant of Abraham, and this generation had not been.
- **5:3** "Gibeath-haaraloth" means "hill of foreskins".
- **5:6** The old generation died in the wilderness because they did not listen (see Numbers 14:20-35).
- **5:9** "I have rolled away the disgrace of Egypt" explains the name Gilgal, from the Hebrew *galal*, "to roll". The meaning of "disgrace" is debated. It may refer to the shame of being uncircumcised or of slavery.
- **5:10** The Passover is held at Gilgal. This is the first Passover in the land.
- **5:12** The manna stops on the day after the people eat the food of the land.
- **5:13** The man with the drawn sword is like the angel in Numbers 22:23 and the man who wrestled with Jacob.
- **5:14** "Neither" is Hebrew *lo*, which can mean "no" or, in another spelling, "to him". The commander is on neither side, but on the LORD's.
- **5:15** "The place ... is holy" repeats Exodus 3:5, as the same words are said to Moses at the burning bush.
- **6:1** "Shut up tight" means the city gates were closed and barred.
- **6:2** "I have handed Jericho over to you" uses a past tense for something not yet done. It states the outcome as already certain.
- **6:4** "Ram's-horn trumpets" is Hebrew *shofarot yovelim*. The word *yovel* is the same as in "Jubilee" (see entry H-YOVEL), and here means a ram's horn.
- **6:5** "Fall down flat" is literally "fall under itself". The wall collapsed in its place.
- **6:17** "Devoted to the LORD for destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. The whole city is set apart for the LORD and given up. Rahab's household is the only exception.
- **6:18** "Bring trouble" is Hebrew *akar*. The word anticipates the name Achan (also spelled Achar in 1 Chronicles 2:7) and the Valley of Achor in 7:24-26.
- **6:19** What is not destroyed, the silver, gold, bronze and iron, is holy and goes into the treasury of the LORD.
- **6:21** "Men and women, young and old" is rendered as written. The text records the destruction of everyone in the city. Excavations at Jericho have been interpreted in different ways, and the account may use the language of ancient war reports.
- **6:24** "The house of the LORD" is the tent of meeting, not the temple.
- **6:25** "To this day" shows that the story was written down when Rahab's descendants were still known in Israel.
- **6:26** Joshua's curse is fulfilled in 1 Kings 16:34.
- **7:1** "Broke faith" is Hebrew *ma'al*, a word for a breach of trust, especially over what belongs to God. The account first tells that the people are guilty, and then names the man.
- **7:2** The location of Ai is uncertain. It is a town near Bethel. The word *ai* means "ruin".
- **7:5** "Became like water" is a figure for fear. The thirty-six dead are few, but the defeat is a sign.
- **7:6** Tearing clothes and putting dust on the head are signs of grief.
- **7:9** "What will you do for your great name?" appeals to God's honour, as Moses did in Numbers 14:13-16.
- **7:11** "They have sinned" and "they have taken" are plural. The whole people share the responsibility, though one man acted.
- **7:14** The lot is cast by tribe, clan, household and man. The text does not say how, but it may use the Urim and Thummim.
- **7:15** Burning for breaking the ban follows the law in Leviticus 20:14 (for a certain sexual offence) and the ban on devoted things in Deuteronomy 7:25-26.
- **7:19** "Give glory to the LORD" means to confess the truth. It is a formal phrase.
- **7:21** "A fine cloak from Shinar" is a garment from Babylonia. The silver and gold are about 2.3 kilograms and 570 grams.
- **7:24** Achan's household is taken with him. Deuteronomy 24:16 says that each is to be put to death for his own sin. Some read the household as taking part, since the goods were hidden in the tent. Verse 25 names only Achan as stoned, and "they" burned. The text is rendered as written.
- **7:25** "Trouble" is Hebrew *akar*, and a play on the name Achor (and on Achan). "The LORD brings trouble on you today" answers Achan's act.
- **7:26** The Valley of Achor later becomes a place of hope. See Hosea 2:15 and Isaiah 65:10.
- **8:2** Here the plunder and livestock may be taken, unlike at Jericho.
- **8:3** "Thirty thousand" and the "about five thousand" in verse 12 are two ambush numbers. They may be two groups, or the text has an error in one.
- **8:18** "The javelin" is Hebrew *kidon*, a short spear or sickle sword, held out as a signal.
- **8:25** "Twelve thousand" is the number killed in Ai.
- **8:26** "Devoted to destruction" is *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM.
- **8:28** "A heap of ruins" plays on the name Ai, which means "ruin".
- **8:29** The king of Ai is hanged on a tree after death. His body is taken down at sunset, as in Deuteronomy 21:22-23.
- **8:30** The altar at Mount Ebal carries out the command of Deuteronomy 27:4-8. Mount Ebal is near Shechem, a long way from Ai. The text does not describe the journey.
- **8:31** "The book of the teaching of Moses" is the written teaching. The uncut stones follow Exodus 20:25 and Deuteronomy 27:5.
- **8:33** "Foreigner and native alike" shows the covenant is for all who live among Israel.
- **8:34** "The blessing and the curse" is the ceremony commanded in Deuteronomy 27 and 28.
- **8:35** The reading includes the women, the children and the foreigners.
- **9:4** "Acted with cunning" is Hebrew *ormah*, a word for shrewdness that can be good or bad. "Disguised themselves as envoys" is uncertain, because the Hebrew word for "envoys" is rare, and some ancient texts read it differently.
- **9:7** The Gibeonites are called Hivites here and in 11:19. Their story is of a people of the land who avoid destruction by a trick.
- **9:14** "They did not ask for the LORD's decision" is literally "the mouth of the LORD". The leaders took the food as evidence and did not consult him.
- **9:15** "A treaty" is Hebrew *berit*, a covenant sealed by oath. Treaties with the peoples of the land were forbidden (Exodus 23:32; Deuteronomy 7:2).
- **9:17** The four towns are a league of Gibeonite towns. Kiriath-jearim is where the ark is later kept (1 Samuel 7:1).
- **9:18** The leaders keep their oath, which had been sworn by the LORD, even though it was obtained by deceit. The oath binds them.
- **9:21** "Woodcutters and water-carriers" is a description of menial service. See also Deuteronomy 29:11, where foreign labourers are among those in the covenant.
- **9:23** "The house of my God" means the sanctuary. The Gibeonites serve it as workers.
- **9:24** The Gibeonites give the reason for their trick: they knew Moses had commanded the destruction of the people of the land.
- **9:27** The treaty is later broken by Saul, with consequences in 2 Samuel 21:1-9.
- **10:1** "Adoni-zedek" means "my lord is righteousness". This is the first mention of the king of Jerusalem in Joshua. See Melchizedek in Genesis 14:18.
- **10:5** The five kings form an alliance of the southern towns against Gibeon, for making peace with Israel.
- **10:10** "Threw them into confusion" is Hebrew *hamam*, the same word as for the Egyptians at the sea (Exodus 14:24).
- **10:11** "Large stones from the sky" are hailstones. The storm kills more than the sword.
- **10:12** The poem in verses 12 to 13 is a quotation from the Book of Jashar, a lost collection of songs (see also 2 Samuel 1:18). "Stand still" is Hebrew *dom*, which can mean "be silent" or "stop". It may be a command to the sun to cease shining and not to stop moving.
- **10:13** Whether the sun and moon actually stopped, or the day was darkened by the storm or lengthened in some other way, is debated. The text itself says that the sun "did not hurry to set for about a whole day". The WABV text renders the poem and the comment on it without choosing between the readings.
- **10:14** "The LORD listened to the voice of a man" means that the LORD answered Joshua's prayer.
- **10:24** "Put your feet on the necks" is a sign of complete victory. See also Psalm 110:1.
- **10:26** Hanging the kings on trees after death repeats 8:29. The bodies are taken down at sunset, as in Deuteronomy 21:22-23.
- **10:28** "Devoted to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. The reports of Makkedah, Libnah, Lachish, Eglon, Hebron and Debir (verses 28 to 39) repeat the same formula. This is rendered as written.
- **10:33** Horam king of Gezer is killed, but Gezer itself is not taken (see 16:10).
- **10:36** Hebron and Debir are said to be taken here. Judges 1:10-15 gives a different account of their capture by Caleb and Othniel. The two accounts are recorded, and no way of harmonising them is chosen.
- **10:40** "Everything that breathed" is a sweeping statement, which is limited by other texts (see 11:22 and Judges 1:19, 27-36).
- **10:41** "The land of Goshen" here is a district in the south of Judah. It is not the Goshen of Egypt.
- **11:1** Jabin king of Hazor is the leader of the northern alliance. Hazor was a large city. Another Jabin of Hazor appears in Judges 4.
- **11:6** "Cripple their horses" means to hamstring them, so that they cannot be used in war. See also 2 Samuel 8:4.
- **11:8** "Greater Sidon" is the main city of Sidon.
- **11:10** Hazor had been "the head of all those kingdoms", the chief city of the north.
- **11:13** Towns "on their mounds" were left standing. Only Hazor is burned.
- **11:15** The chain of command, from the LORD to Moses to Joshua, is stated as a statement of faithful obedience.
- **11:18** "A long time" means that the war lasted for years. See 14:10, which implies about seven years.
- **11:19** Only the Gibeonites made peace. The other towns were taken in battle.
- **11:20** "To harden their hearts" repeats the language used of Pharaoh (Exodus 4:21) and of Sihon (Deuteronomy 2:30). "Mercy" is Hebrew *tehinnah*, favour or a plea for grace.
- **11:21** The Anakites are the tall people of Numbers 13:33. Some of them survive in Gaza, Gath and Ashdod.
- **11:23** "The land had rest from war" sums up the campaign. Chapter 13:1 says that very much land remains to be taken, and the footnote on that verse will record the tension.
- **12:1** The list of defeated kings begins east of the Jordan, with the kings of Numbers 21 and Deuteronomy 2 and 3.
- **12:4** "The last of the Rephaim" describes Og, who is also described as a giant in Deuteronomy 3:11.
- **12:9** The list of thirty-one kings west of the Jordan has some towns that are not mentioned elsewhere in Joshua. Several names are uncertain.
- **12:23** "The king of Goiim in Gilgal" is uncertain. The Greek text reads "Goiim in Galilee". The name means "nations".
- **12:24** The total of thirty-one kings is the number given in the Hebrew text, though the list may vary by one or two names.
- **13:1** "A very great deal of the land still remains to be taken" sits in tension with 11:23, which says the whole land was taken and had rest. Both statements are kept. The first speaks of the main campaigns, and the second of land that was still not occupied.
- **13:2** The land that remains is the Philistine coast, the Geshurites, and the Sidonian and Lebanon regions in the north.
- **13:3** "The Shihor" is a branch or stream on the eastern edge of Egypt. "The five rulers of the Philistines" is Hebrew *seranim*, a title for the leaders of the five Philistine cities.
- **13:6** "I myself will drive them out" puts the responsibility on the LORD. Israel's part is to divide the land by lot.
- **13:8** The east-bank inheritance of Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh is described again here, following Numbers 32 and Deuteronomy 3.
- **13:13** Geshur and Maacath are not driven out. This is the first of several notes of land not taken (see also 15:63, 16:10 and 17:12).
- **13:14** "The offerings made by fire" are the Levites' share, as in Numbers 18:20 and Deuteronomy 18:1.
- **13:21** The Midianite chiefs are called "princes of Sihon", which may mean they were allies or vassals of Sihon. Numbers 31:8 calls them kings of Midian.
- **13:22** Balaam's death is told in Numbers 31:8. "The diviner" is Hebrew *qosem*.
- **13:30** "The villages of Jair" are the sixty towns of Bashan (Deuteronomy 3:14).
- **14:1** Eleazar and Joshua, with the heads of the ancestral houses, supervise the lot, as commanded in Numbers 34:17-18.
- **14:4** The sons of Joseph form two tribes, which brings the number of tribes with land to twelve, since Levi has none.
- **14:6** "Caleb the Kenizzite" and "Moses the man of God" are titles used with care. See the notes on Numbers 32:12 and Deuteronomy 33:1.
- **14:10** Caleb's age of eighty-five, forty-five years after Kadesh, implies that the campaign lasted about seven years (forty-five minus the thirty-eight years in the wilderness).
- **14:12** Caleb asks for the hill country where the Anakites live, which the scouts had called impossible (Numbers 13:28-33).
- **14:13** Joshua blessing Caleb is an act of confirming the gift.
- **14:15** "Kiriath-arba" means "city of Arba". "And the land had rest from war" repeats 11:23.
- **15:1** Judah's lot is first and the longest in the book. Its borders run on the south, east, north and west.
- **15:2** The southern border follows the same line as the land's southern border in Numbers 34:3-5.
- **15:8** The northern border passes along "the Valley of Ben-hinnom", south of Jerusalem. The valley is later called Gehenna.
- **15:13** The account of Caleb at Hebron is placed in Judah's list. It parallels Judges 1:10-15, 20, with some differences in detail.
- **15:16** Offering a daughter as the prize for taking a town was a custom of the time.
- **15:18** The Hebrew of this verse is ambiguous. It can be read "she urged him" or "he urged her". "Got down" (*tsanach*) is uncertain, and may mean "clapped" or "sighed".
- **15:19** "Give me a blessing" is Hebrew *berakhah*, here meaning a gift. The springs were a necessity in dry land.
- **15:21** The list of Judah's southern towns is given as twenty-nine in verse 32, but the names in verses 21 to 32 number more. The count and the names are kept as they stand.
- **15:36** The list is called "fourteen towns", but there are fifteen names. "Gederothaim" may be a variant of "Gederah".
- **15:59** Some ancient Greek manuscripts add a further group of eleven towns after this verse, including Tekoa and Ephrathah (Bethlehem). The Hebrew text does not have them.
- **15:62** "The City of Salt" is near the Dead Sea.
- **15:63** The Jebusites remain in Jerusalem. Judges 1:21 says this of the Benjamites, and David takes the city in 2 Samuel 5:6-9.
- **16:1** Joseph's border runs from the Jordan at Jericho up to Bethel and across to Gezer and the sea. It gives the combined territory of Ephraim and Manasseh.
- **16:9** Some Ephraimite towns lie inside the land of Manasseh.
- **16:10** The Canaanites at Gezer are put to forced labour. In 1 Kings 9:16 Gezer is later given to Solomon by Pharaoh.
- **17:1** Machir, the firstborn of Manasseh, receives Gilead and Bashan east of the Jordan, because he was a man of war.
- **17:3** The daughters of Zelophehad receive their inheritance as promised in Numbers 27:1-11, and as the case is settled in Numbers 36.
- **17:5** "Ten shares" are the five clans west of the Jordan and the five daughters.
- **17:11** "The third is Naphath" is obscure. It may be a name for the group of three, or a place name.
- **17:12** "Determined to stay" is Hebrew *ho'il*, to decide to do something. The Canaanites would not leave.
- **17:16** "Iron chariots" are a sign of military power. The people of Joseph fear them.
- **17:18** Joshua tells the sons of Joseph that they must clear the forest and drive out the Canaanites, in spite of the iron chariots.
- **18:1** The tent of meeting is set up at Shiloh, which becomes the central sanctuary until the time of Samuel. "Subdued" means the land was under Israel's control.
- **18:3** "How long will you put off" is Joshua's rebuke to the seven tribes, who had not yet taken their land.
- **18:4** Three men from each of the seven tribes make twenty-one surveyors. They write a description of the land in seven parts.
- **18:7** The Levites' inheritance is "the priesthood of the LORD", as in 13:14 and Numbers 18:20.
- **18:9** "In a book" is Hebrew *sefer*, a written record. The survey was recorded, and the lots were then cast on it.
- **18:11** The territory of Benjamin lies between Judah and the descendants of Joseph, as the first and the last of the lots to be drawn suggest.
- **18:12** The north and south borders of Benjamin follow the same lines as the southern border of Joseph in 16:1-3 and the northern border of Judah in 15:5-9.
- **18:21** The Benjaminite towns are in two groups, twelve towns (verses 21 to 24) and fourteen towns (verses 25 to 28). The counts match the names.
- **18:28** "Jebus (that is, Jerusalem)" is given to Benjamin here. In 15:8 and 15:63 the Jebusite city is on the border of Judah. See Judges 1:8 and 1:21.
- **19:1** Simeon's inheritance lies inside Judah's, so the tribe is scattered, as Jacob had said (Genesis 49:7).
- **19:2** The list of Simeon's towns is given as thirteen in verse 6, but the names are fourteen. "Beersheba, Sheba" may be one town. The list in 1 Chronicles 4:28-32 differs in some names.
- **19:9** The share of Judah was too large, so Simeon is given a part of it.
- **19:15** Bethlehem here is a town in the territory of Zebulun, in Galilee, and not the Bethlehem of Judah.
- **19:22** The Issacharite list names sixteen places, counting the border towns Tabor, Shahazumah and Beth-shemesh.
- **19:28** "Greater Sidon" is the main city of Sidon. The reference to Tyre in verse 29 is the first in the Bible to a fortified town of Tyre.
- **19:38** The list of Naphtali's towns is given as nineteen.
- **19:47** The Danites, who could not hold their coast, go north and capture Leshem (called Laish in Judges 18:29) and rename it Dan.
- **19:49** Joshua's own share is given last, after the tribes have their land.
- **19:50** Timnath-serah is in the hill country of Ephraim, and it is where Joshua is later buried (24:30).
- **19:51** "At the entrance of the tent of meeting" repeats the setting of the whole lot in Shiloh.
- **20:2** The command to set apart cities of refuge follows Numbers 35:9-34 and Deuteronomy 19:1-13.
- **20:3** "Unintentionally" is Hebrew *bishgagah*. "The avenger of blood" is the *go'el ha-dam*. See entry H-GAAL.
- **20:4** The one who flees states his case at the city gate, in front of the elders. This is the first stage of the process. The trial before the assembly comes after.
- **20:6** The stay until "the death of the high priest" repeats Numbers 35:25, 28.
- **20:7** The three western cities are Kedesh, Shechem and Hebron. The three eastern cities, named in Deuteronomy 4:41-43, are Bezer, Ramoth and Golan.
- **20:9** The foreigner who lives among Israel is included.
- **21:1** The Levites come to the leaders at Shiloh with the request that Moses had commanded to be granted (Numbers 35:1-8).
- **21:4** The first lot goes to the descendants of Aaron, the priests, and gives them thirteen towns from Judah, Simeon and Benjamin. These are the towns nearest to Jerusalem.
- **21:8** The total of forty-eight towns is made up of thirteen, ten, thirteen and twelve.
- **21:12** The fields and villages of Hebron stay with Caleb, as in 14:13 and 15:13.
- **21:13** The six cities of refuge are among the Levite towns. Hebron, Shechem, Golan, Kedesh and Ramoth are named here, and Bezer is in verse 36.
- **21:21** Gezer is listed here as a Levite town, though 16:10 says that the Canaanites of Gezer were not driven out.
- **21:25** Gath-rimmon is named in verse 24 for Dan and again here for Manasseh. The parallel list in 1 Chronicles 6:70 has Ibleam in the second place.
- **21:36** Verses 36 and 37, the towns of Reuben, are missing in some Hebrew manuscripts but present in others and in 1 Chronicles 6:78-79. They are included here.
- **21:41** "Forty-eight" repeats Numbers 35:7.
- **21:43** Verses 43 to 45 are a summary: the LORD gave Israel all the land, gave them rest, and every promise was kept. This is in tension with 13:1 and with the notes of land not taken (13:13; 15:63; 16:10; 17:12). Judges 1:1-3:6 describes the same land as unfinished. Both views are kept.
- **22:1** Joshua dismisses the eastern tribes with praise, because they kept their promise to fight (see 1:12-18 and Numbers 32).
- **22:7** The note that Moses gave one half of Manasseh land in Bashan, and Joshua the other half in the west, repeats the division in 13:29-31 and 17:1-6.
- **22:8** "Share the plunder with your kinsmen" means the eastern tribes are to share their wealth with those who stayed behind, as in Numbers 31:27.
- **22:10** "In the region of the Jordan" translates a Hebrew word (*gelilot*) whose exact meaning is uncertain. The site of the altar is not known.
- **22:12** The community gathers at Shiloh to go to war against its own kinsmen. This follows the logic of Deuteronomy 13:12-18, where a town that turns to other gods is to be struck down. The people first send a delegation.
- **22:13** Phinehas is sent with one chief from each of the ten tribes west of the Jordan. He is the priest who acted at Peor (Numbers 25:7-8).
- **22:16** "Breach of faith" is Hebrew *ma'al*, as in 7:1.
- **22:17** "The sin at Peor" is the event of Numbers 25.
- **22:19** "The land of your possession is unclean" suggests that land outside Canaan might be unfit for worship, and so the delegation offers the eastern tribes land in the west. "The land that belongs to the LORD" is the land where the tabernacle stands.
- **22:20** Achan's case (chapter 7) is the precedent: one man's sin brought wrath on the whole community.
- **22:22** "The Mighty One, God, the LORD" repeats three names for God, *El*, *Elohim* and *YHWH*, twice. It is an emphatic oath.
- **22:25** The eastern tribes fear that the Jordan will be seen as a boundary that cuts them off from the LORD.
- **22:27** The altar is a witness, not for sacrifice, and a copy of the altar at the tabernacle (verse 28).
- **22:31** "You have saved the Israelites from the hand of the LORD" means they have kept the people from his punishment.
- **22:34** The Hebrew text of this verse does not give the name of the altar. It says "they called the altar ...", and the name is missing. Some manuscripts and the Greek add "Witness", which is supplied here in line with the explanation that follows.
- **23:1** "A long time after" is the period of Israel's rest. Joshua is old, near the end of his life (see 24:29).
- **23:4** The nations that remain are said to be allotted as an inheritance, as Joshua promised in 13:6.
- **23:7** Not mentioning the names of other gods is part of a ban on swearing by them.
- **23:10** "One of you chases a thousand" repeats Deuteronomy 32:30 and Leviticus 26:8.
- **23:12** The ban on intermarriage repeats Deuteronomy 7:3-4.
- **23:13** "A snare and a trap ... a whip on your sides and thorns in your eyes" echoes Numbers 33:55 and Judges 2:3.
- **23:14** "The way of all the earth" is a figure for death. "Not one word has failed" repeats 21:45. The speech goes on to warn that the evil words will be fulfilled as well as the good (verse 15).
- **23:16** "Covenant" is Hebrew *berit*. See entry H-BERIT.
- **24:1** The Hebrew text names Shechem as the place of assembly. Some Greek manuscripts name Shiloh. Shechem is where Abraham first built an altar (Genesis 12:6-7) and where the people were blessed at Gerizim and Ebal (8:30-35).
- **24:2** "Beyond the River" means east of the Euphrates. That Terah served other gods is mentioned only here.
- **24:5** "I struck Egypt" refers to the plagues.
- **24:7** "He put darkness between you and the Egyptians" recalls the pillar of cloud at the sea (Exodus 14:19-20).
- **24:9** Balak "fought" against Israel by sending for Balaam to curse. He did not take up arms in the account in Numbers 22 to 24.
- **24:11** "The citizens of Jericho" is Hebrew *ba'alei yeriho*, literally "the lords of Jericho".
- **24:12** "The two kings of the Amorites" is the Hebrew text, referring to Sihon and Og. The Greek has "twelve". "The hornet" is also in Exodus 23:28 and Deuteronomy 7:20.
- **24:14** "Serve him completely and faithfully" uses two words, *tamim* and *emet*. The word *tamim* is also in Deuteronomy 18:13.
- **24:15** "If it seems wrong to you" is literally "if it is evil in your eyes". Joshua gives the people a real choice. His household's decision does not depend on theirs.
- **24:19** "You cannot serve the LORD" is striking. He is "holy" (*qadosh*, see entry H-QADOSH) and "jealous" (*qanna*, see entry H-QANNA), and he will not overlook rebellion. Compare Exodus 34:7, which speaks of forgiveness, and Exodus 23:21.
- **24:23** The people still have foreign gods among them. Joshua tells them to put them away.
- **24:25** "A statute and a ruling" repeats Exodus 15:25. "Covenant" is *berit* (see entry H-BERIT).
- **24:26** "The oak" may also be a terebinth. The oak at Shechem is mentioned in Genesis 35:4 and Judges 9:6. "The sanctuary of the LORD" at Shechem is a place of worship near the oak.
- **24:27** The stone is a witness because "it has heard" the words. This is a figure of speech, as with heaven and earth in Deuteronomy 4:26.
- **24:29** Joshua dies at a hundred and ten, as Joseph did (Genesis 50:26). He is called "the servant of the LORD", the title given to Moses.
- **24:30** Timnath-serah is called Timnath-heres in Judges 2:9. The name means "portion of the sun" in the second form.
- **24:31** "The elders who outlived Joshua" is the last generation to know the LORD's work, and Judges 2:10 says that the next did not.
- **24:32** Joseph's bones were brought up from Egypt as he had asked (Genesis 50:25 and Exodus 13:19). "A hundred pieces of silver" is Hebrew *qesitah*, a unit of money of uncertain value. See Genesis 33:19.
- **24:33** Eleazar is buried at Gibeah, which belongs to Phinehas. Some Greek manuscripts add a longer ending about the ark and Phinehas.
