# WABV: Titus

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[G-PNEUMA\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Titus is the seventeenth book of the New Testament in the order that the WABV text follows, and the twelfth of the letters, and the third of the pastoral letters. It is written in Greek, and the WABV text is drafted from a critical Greek text, with the Byzantine tradition noted in the footnotes where it differs, as the Charter requires. It is a letter from Paul to Titus, left in Crete (1:5) to put the assemblies in order and to appoint elders, and it shares with 1 Timothy the qualifications of the overseer (1:5-9), the rules for the households (2:1-10), the warning against false teaching and the trustworthy saying (3:8). It also holds two passages that are among the most important in the pastoral letters, the grace of God that has appeared (2:11-14) and the washing of rebirth (3:4-7). The authorship of the pastoral letters is debated, since some scholars hold them to be Paul and some a close follower of Paul, and the WABV text does not decide. The chapter and verse numbering is the usual numbering. The WABV text of Titus is drafted with the voice standard (Part 46 of the review package). Where the text of a critical edition is not certain, the draft says so in the footnote and asks the team to confirm the reading. It makes a number of fixed choices that follow from its Greek, and they are written down here so that they are used throughout, and they are the same as those of 1 and 2 Timothy for the words that the letters share. *Presbuteros* is rendered "elder" and *episkopos* "overseer", and the footnote records that the two words are used of the same office in 1:5-7. *Soter* is rendered "Saviour", and it is used of God (1:3, 2:10, 3:4) and of Christ (1:4, 2:13, 3:6). *Eusebeia* is rendered "godliness". *Pistos ho logos* is rendered "the saying is trustworthy". *Paliggenesia* (3:5) is rendered "rebirth". *Hagios* is rendered "holy" and is tagged G-HAGIOS. *Pistis* is rendered "faith" and *pisteuo* "believe", and both are tagged G-PISTIS. *Agape* is rendered "love" and is tagged G-AGAPE. *Charis* is rendered "grace" and is tagged G-CHARIS. *Eleos* is rendered "mercy" and is tagged G-ELEOS. *Eirene* is rendered "peace" and is tagged G-EIRENE. *Doxa* is rendered "glory" and is tagged G-DOXA. *Sozo* and *soteria* are rendered "save" and "salvation" and are tagged G-SOZO. *Zoe* is rendered "life" and is tagged G-ZOE. *Dikaiosune* and *dikaioo* are rendered "righteousness" and "declare righteous" and are tagged G-DIKAIOSUNE. *Lutroo* (2:14) is rendered "redeem" and is tagged G-LYTRON. *Aletheia* is rendered "truth" and is tagged G-ALETHEIA. *Nomos* is rendered "law" and is tagged G-NOMOS. *Pneuma* is rendered "Spirit" or "spirit" and is tagged G-PNEUMA. *Christos* is rendered "Christ" and is tagged G-CHRISTOS. The headings of the parts are not in the Greek. The markers beginning with G- (for Greek words) are defined in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. The wording of the WABV text is drafted from the Greek and is not taken from any other English translation.*

## Chapters 1 to 3, elders, sound teaching and the grace that has appeared

### Chapter 1

**1** Paul, a slave of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\], for the sake of the faith \[G-PISTIS\] of God's elect and their knowledge of the truth \[G-ALETHEIA\] that accords with godliness,

**2** in hope of eternal life \[G-ZOE\], which God, who never lies, promised before the ages began

**3** and at the proper time manifested in his word through the proclamation with which I have been entrusted by the command of God our Saviour;

**4** to Titus, my true child in a common faith \[G-PISTIS\]: grace \[G-CHARIS\] and peace \[G-EIRENE\] from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Saviour.

**5** This is why I left you in Crete, that you might put what remained into order, and appoint elders in every town as I directed you,

**6** if anyone is above reproach, the husband of one wife, and his children are believers and not open to the charge of debauchery or insubordination.

**7** For an overseer, as God's steward, must be above reproach. He must not be arrogant or quick-tempered or a drunkard or violent or greedy for gain,

**8** but hospitable, a lover of good, self-controlled, upright, holy, and disciplined.

**9** He must hold firm to the trustworthy word as taught, so that he may be able to give instruction in sound teaching and also to rebuke those who contradict it.

**10** For there are many who are insubordinate, empty talkers and deceivers, especially those of the circumcision party.

**11** They must be silenced, since they are upsetting whole families by teaching for shameful gain what they ought not to teach.

**12** One of themselves, a prophet of their own, said, "Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy gluttons."

**13** This testimony is true. Therefore rebuke them sharply, that they may be sound in the faith \[G-PISTIS\],

**14** not devoting themselves to Jewish myths and the commands of people who turn away from the truth \[G-ALETHEIA\].

**15** To the pure, all things are pure, but to the defiled and unbelieving, nothing is pure. Their very minds and consciences are defiled.

**16** They profess to know God, but they deny him by their works. They are detestable, disobedient, unfit for any good work.

### Chapter 2

**1** But as for you, teach what accords with sound teaching.

**2** Older men are to be sober-minded, dignified, self-controlled, sound in faith \[G-PISTIS\], in love \[G-AGAPE\], and in steadfastness.

**3** Older women likewise are to be reverent in behaviour, not slanderers or slaves to much wine. They are to teach what is good,

**4** and so train the young women to love their husbands and children,

**5** to be self-controlled, pure, working at home, kind, and submissive to their own husbands, that the word of God may not be reviled.

**6** Likewise urge the younger men to be self-controlled.

**7** Show yourself in all respects to be a model of good works, and in your teaching show integrity, dignity,

**8** and sound speech that cannot be condemned, so that an opponent may be put to shame, having nothing evil to say about us.

**9** Slaves are to be submissive to their own masters in everything, to be well-pleasing, not argumentative,

**10** not pilfering, but showing all good faith \[G-PISTIS\], so that in everything they may adorn the teaching of God our Saviour.

**11** For the grace \[G-CHARIS\] of God has appeared, bringing salvation \[G-SOZO\] for all people,

**12** training us to renounce ungodliness and worldly passions, and to live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives in the present age,

**13** waiting for our blessed hope, the appearing of the glory \[G-DOXA\] of our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ,

**14** who gave himself for us to redeem \[G-LYTRON\] us from all lawlessness and to purify for himself a people for his own possession who are zealous for good works.

**15** Declare these things. Exhort and rebuke with all authority. Let no one disregard you.

### Chapter 3

**1** Remind them to be submissive to rulers and authorities, to be obedient, to be ready for every good work,

**2** to speak evil of no one, to avoid quarrelling, to be gentle, and to show perfect courtesy toward all people.

**3** For we ourselves were once foolish, disobedient, led astray, slaves to various passions and pleasures, passing our days in malice and envy, hated by others and hating one another.

**4** But when the goodness and loving kindness of God our Saviour appeared,

**5** he saved \[G-SOZO\] us, not because of works done by us in righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\], but according to his own mercy \[G-ELEOS\], by the washing of rebirth and renewal by the Holy \[G-HAGIOS\] Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\],

**6** whom he poured out on us richly through Jesus Christ our Saviour,

**7** so that being declared righteous \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\] by his grace \[G-CHARIS\] we might become heirs according to the hope of eternal life \[G-ZOE\].

**8** The saying is trustworthy, and I want you to insist on these things, so that those who have believed \[G-PISTIS\] in God may be careful to devote themselves to good works. These things are excellent and profitable for people.

**9** But avoid foolish controversies, genealogies, dissensions, and quarrels about the law \[G-NOMOS\], for they are unprofitable and worthless.

**10** As for a person who stirs up division, after warning him once and then twice, have nothing more to do with him,

**11** knowing that such a person is warped and sinful. He is self-condemned.

**12** When I send Artemas or Tychicus to you, do your best to come to me at Nicopolis, for I have decided to spend the winter there.

**13** Do your best to speed Zenas the lawyer and Apollos on their way. See that they lack nothing.

**14** And let our people learn to devote themselves to good works, so as to help cases of urgent need, and not be unfruitful.

**15** All who are with me send greetings to you. Greet those who love \[G-AGAPE\] us in the faith \[G-PISTIS\]. Grace \[G-CHARIS\] be with you all.

*Review note: Titus 1 to 3 were drafted in WABV wording, from the Greek, using the fixed choices set out in the introduction to this book, and with the WABV voice standard (Part 46 of the review package) applied. They are the greeting, with its long statement of the apostleship of Paul (1:1-4), the appointment of elders in Crete (1:5-9), the false teachers and the Cretans (1:10-16), what accords with sound teaching, for older men, older women, younger women, younger men and slaves (2:1-10), the grace of God that has appeared (2:11-14), the exhortation to Titus (2:15), the duties to rulers and to all (3:1-2), the kindness of God and the washing of rebirth (3:3-7), the trustworthy saying and the avoiding of foolish controversies (3:8-11) and the personal requests and the closing (3:12-15). The numbering is the usual numbering. No new G- marker is used, and the markers are G-CHRISTOS (1:1, 1:4), G-PISTIS (1:1-3:15), G-ALETHEIA (1:1, 1:14), G-ZOE (1:2, 3:7), G-CHARIS (1:4, 2:11, 3:7, 3:15), G-EIRENE (1:4), G-HAGIOS (1:8, 3:5), G-AGAPE (2:2, 3:15), G-SOZO (2:11, 3:5), G-DOXA (2:13), G-LYTRON (2:14), G-DIKAIOSUNE (3:5-7), G-ELEOS (3:5), G-PNEUMA (3:5) and G-NOMOS (3:9). In 1:1-3 "the faith of God's elect and their knowledge of the truth that accords with godliness" is rendered as written. In 1:2 "God, who never lies" is rendered as written. In 1:3 "God our Saviour" is used of God, as in 1 Timothy 1:1. In 1:5 "elders" translates *presbuterous*, and "put what remained into order" is rendered as written. In 1:6-9 the qualifications of the elder and the overseer are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read, and the footnote records that "elder" (verse 5) and "overseer" (verse 7) are used of the same office, and that the list follows that of 1 Timothy 3:2-7, and that "the husband of one wife" is as in 1 Timothy 3:2. In 1:6 "his children are believers" translates *tekna echon pista*, and may be read "faithful children", and the footnote records the matter. In 1:9 "hold firm to the trustworthy word as taught" is rendered as written. In 1:10 "the circumcision party" translates *hoi ek peritomes*. In 1:12 the quotation is from the poet Epimenides of Crete, and it is also known as the saying of the "liar paradox", and the footnote records the matter. In 1:14 "Jewish myths" is rendered as written. In 1:15 "to the pure, all things are pure" is rendered as written. In 2:1-10 the passage on the duties of older men, older women, younger women, younger men and slaves is rendered as written, with no comment on how it is read, and the footnote records that the duties of slaves are as in 1 Timothy 6:1-2, and that the passage presupposes the institution of slavery and does not address it. In 2:3 "slaves to much wine" is rendered as written. In 2:4-5 "train the young women to love their husbands and children" and "working at home" are rendered as written, and the Greek has "working at home" (*oikourgous*) in the critical text, and some manuscripts read "keeping house" (*oikourous*), and the footnote records the matter. In 2:9-10 "slaves ... adorn the teaching of God our Saviour" is rendered as written. In 2:11 "has appeared" translates *epephane*, and "bringing salvation for all people" may be read "the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation for all people" or "has appeared to all people, bringing salvation", and the footnote records the matter. In 2:13 "our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ" translates *tou megalou theou kai soteros hemon Iesou Christou*, and the Greek has one article for "God" and "Saviour", and it may be read as one person ("our great God and Saviour, Jesus Christ") or as two ("the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ"), and the footnote records the matter, and the WABV text does not decide, and the rendering keeps the first reading. In 2:14 "to redeem us from all lawlessness" translates *hina lutrosetai hemas apo pases anomias*, and it is tagged G-LYTRON, because *lutroo* is from the same root as *lutron*, and "a people for his own possession" is as in Exodus 19:5 and Deuteronomy 14:2, and the footnote records the matter. In 3:1 "rulers and authorities" is rendered as written. In 3:4 "the goodness and loving kindness of God our Saviour appeared" is rendered as written. In 3:5 "he saved us, not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to his own mercy" is rendered as written, and the verb is tagged G-SOZO. In 3:5 "the washing of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit" translates *loutrou paliggenesias kai anakainoseos pneumatos hagiou*, and the footnote records that "washing" may refer to baptism, and that "rebirth" (*paliggenesia*) is used elsewhere in the New Testament only at Matthew 19:28, and that "by the Holy Spirit" may go with "renewal" or with "rebirth and renewal", and the WABV text does not decide. In 3:7 "being declared righteous by his grace" translates *dikaiothentes te ekeinou chariti*, and it is tagged G-DIKAIOSUNE. In 3:8 "the saying is trustworthy" is the fifth of the trustworthy sayings in the pastoral letters, and it may refer to verses 4 to 7 or to what follows, and the footnote records the matter. In 3:9 "quarrels about the law" is rendered as written. In 3:10 "a person who stirs up division" translates *hairetikon anthropon*, which may be read "a heretic" or "a factious person", and the footnote records the matter. In 3:12 "Artemas or Tychicus" and "Nicopolis" are rendered as written, and "Nicopolis" is the name of several towns. In 3:13 "Zenas the lawyer and Apollos" is rendered as written. In 3:15 "with you all" is the reading of the critical text, and some manuscripts add "Amen". There are no departures from the critical Greek text in chapters 1 to 3. The Greek is hard in some places, including 1:6, 1:9, 2:4-5, 2:11, 2:13, 3:5 and 3:8. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the elders and the overseer (1:5-9), the Cretans (1:12), the duties of the households (2:1-10), the grace of God that has appeared (2:11-14), our great God and Saviour (2:13), the washing of rebirth (3:5), being declared righteous by grace (3:7) and the person who stirs up division (3:10) are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. The section needs a check against the Greek by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked. With this the book of Titus is complete in first draft.*
