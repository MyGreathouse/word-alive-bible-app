# WABV: Micah

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-RUACH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Micah is the sixth of the twelve books of the Minor Prophets in the Protestant order that the WABV text follows. It is in Hebrew and in poetry, with prose in a few places. The book names Micah of Moresheth and dates his word to the days of Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, kings of Judah (1:1), and the footnotes give the dates in the modern count. The chapter and verse numbering is the English numbering. English 5:1 is Hebrew 4:14, so English 5:2-15 is Hebrew 5:1-14. The other chapters are numbered alike in English and Hebrew. The book falls into three parts in the WABV text, each of which begins with a call to hear (1:2; 3:1; 6:1): the judgement on Samaria and Jerusalem and on the rulers and the prophets (chapters 1 to 3), the mountain of the LORD and the ruler from Bethlehem (4 and 5), and the case of the LORD, the call to do justice and the lament and hope (6 and 7). The headings of the parts are not in the Hebrew. Two passages are the same in the Hebrew as other books, and the wording of the lines that are the same is the same in the WABV text: 4:1-3, which is nearly the same as Isaiah 2:2-4, and 3:12, which is quoted in Jeremiah 26:18. The Hebrew of Micah is mostly clear, with some very hard places (for example 1:10-16, with its word-plays on the names of towns, and 2:6-11), and the footnotes record the other readings. The names of God are given as in the earlier books: "the LORD" for YHWH, "Lord" for Adonai, "the Lord GOD" for Adonai YHWH, and "the LORD of hosts" for YHWH tseva'ot. The WABV text does not decide on questions of how the book grew, and the footnotes record only the language.*

## Part One: Chapters 1 to 3, the judgement on Samaria, Jerusalem and the rulers

### Chapter 1

**1** The word of the LORD that came to Micah of Moresheth in the days of Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, kings of Judah, which he saw concerning Samaria and Jerusalem.

**2** Hear, you peoples, all of you. Listen, earth, and all that fills it. And let the Lord GOD be a witness against you, the Lord from his holy \[H-QADOSH\] temple.

**3** For see, the LORD is coming out of his place, and he will come down and tread on the high places of the earth.

**4** And the mountains will melt beneath him, and the valleys will split open, like wax before the fire, like waters poured down a slope.

**5** All this is for the transgression of Jacob and for the sins of the house of Israel. What is the transgression of Jacob? Is it not Samaria? And what is the high place of Judah? Is it not Jerusalem?

**6** Therefore I will make Samaria a heap in the open country, a place for planting vineyards, and I will pour down her stones into the valley and lay bare her foundations.

**7** All her carved images will be beaten to pieces, all her wages burned with fire, and all her idols I will lay waste, for from the wages of a prostitute she gathered them, and to the wages of a prostitute they will return \[H-SHUV\].

**8** For this I will lament and wail. I will go stripped and naked. I will make lamentation like the jackals, and mourning like the ostriches.

**9** For her wound is incurable, and it has come to Judah. It has reached the gate of my people, to Jerusalem itself.

**10** Tell it not in Gath, weep not at all. In Beth-le-aphrah I roll myself in the dust.

**11** Pass on your way, inhabitants of Shaphir, in nakedness and shame. The inhabitants of Zaanan do not come out. Beth-ezel mourns, its standing place is taken from you.

**12** For the inhabitants of Maroth wait anxiously for good, because disaster has come down from the LORD to the gate of Jerusalem.

**13** Harness the steeds to the chariot, inhabitants of Lachish. It was the beginning of sin for the daughter of Zion, for in you were found the transgressions of Israel.

**14** Therefore you will give parting gifts to Moresheth-gath. The houses of Achzib will be a deception to the kings of Israel.

**15** I will again bring a conqueror to you, inhabitants of Mareshah. The glory of Israel will come to Adullam.

**16** Make yourselves bald and cut off your hair for the children of your delight. Make yourselves as bald as the vulture, for they have gone from you into exile.

### Chapter 2

**1** Woe to those who plan iniquity and work evil on their beds. When the morning dawns they do it, because it is in the power of their hand.

**2** They covet fields and seize them, and houses, and take them away. They oppress a man and his house, a person and his inheritance.

**3** Therefore thus says the LORD: See, against this family I am planning disaster, from which you will not remove your necks, and you will not walk haughtily, for it will be a time of disaster.

**4** On that day they will take up a taunt song against you and mourn with bitter lamentation, and say, "We are utterly ruined. He changes the portion of my people. How he removes it from me. To the one who turns away \[H-SHUV\] he allots our fields."

**5** Therefore you will have no one to cast the line by lot in the assembly of the LORD.

**6** "Do not preach," they preach. "One should not preach of such things. Disgrace will not overtake us."

**7** Should this be said, house of Jacob? Has the Spirit \[H-RUACH\] of the LORD grown impatient? Are these his deeds? Do not my words do good to the one who walks uprightly?

**8** But lately my people have risen up as an enemy. You strip the robe from those who pass by trustingly, from those who return \[H-SHUV\] from war.

**9** The women of my people you drive out from their pleasant houses. From their children you take away my splendour for ever.

**10** Arise and go, for this is no place of rest, because of uncleanness that destroys with a grievous destruction.

**11** If a man should go about and utter wind \[H-RUACH\] and lies, saying, "I will preach to you of wine and strong drink," he would be the preacher for this people.

**12** I will surely gather all of you, Jacob, I will gather the remnant of Israel. I will set them together like sheep in a fold, like a flock in its pasture, a noisy multitude of people.

**13** The one who opens the breach goes up before them. They break through and pass the gate, going out through it. Their king passes on before them, the LORD at their head.

### Chapter 3

**1** And I said: Hear, you heads of Jacob and rulers of the house of Israel. Is it not for you to know justice?

**2** You who hate the good and love \[H-AHAV\] evil, who tear the skin from off my people and their flesh from off their bones,

**3** who eat the flesh of my people, and flay their skin from off them, and break their bones in pieces and chop them up like meat in a pot, like flesh in a cauldron.

**4** Then they will cry to the LORD, but he will not answer them. He will hide his face from them at that time, because they have made their deeds evil.

**5** Thus says the LORD concerning the prophets who lead my people astray, who cry "Peace \[H-SHALOM\]" when they have something to eat, but consecrate \[H-QADOSH\] war against the one who puts nothing into their mouths.

**6** Therefore it will be night to you, without vision, and darkness to you, without divination. The sun will go down on the prophets, and the day will be black over them.

**7** The seers will be disgraced and the diviners put to shame. They will all cover their lips, for there is no answer from God.

**8** But as for me, I am filled with power, with the Spirit \[H-RUACH\] of the LORD, and with justice and might, to declare to Jacob his transgression and to Israel his sin.

**9** Hear this, you heads of the house of Jacob and rulers of the house of Israel, who detest justice and make crooked all that is straight,

**10** who build Zion with blood and Jerusalem with iniquity.

**11** Its heads give judgement for a bribe, its priests teach for a price, its prophets practise divination for money. Yet they lean on the LORD and say, "Is not the LORD in our midst? No disaster will come on us."

**12** Therefore because of you Zion will be ploughed as a field, Jerusalem will become a heap of ruins, and the mountain of the house a wooded height.

*Review note: Micah 1 to 3 were drafted in WABV wording. They are the heading and the coming of the LORD against Samaria and Jerusalem (1:1-9), the lament over the towns of Judah (1:10-16), the woe on those who plan iniquity and seize fields (2:1-5), the quarrel with the preachers and the promise of the remnant (2:6-13), the charge against the rulers and the prophets (3:1-8) and the end of Zion (3:9-12). The numbering is the same as the Hebrew. The whole of the part is in poetry, apart from the heading in 1:1, and the verses are set in lines. The date in 1:1 is by the kings of Judah, and the footnote records the matter. In 1:2 "holy" and in 3:5 "consecrate" (qiddeshu, as in Joel 3:9) are tagged H-QADOSH. In 1:7, 2:4 and 2:8 forms of shuv ("return", "the one who turns away", "those who return") are tagged H-SHUV. In 2:7 "the Spirit of the LORD", in 2:11 "wind" and in 3:8 "the Spirit of the LORD" translate ruach and are tagged H-RUACH, and the capital letter in 3:8 is the draft's choice, as in Isaiah 63:10-11 and Ezekiel 11:5. In 3:2 "love" translates ohavei and is tagged H-AHAV. In 3:5 "Peace" translates shalom and is tagged H-SHALOM. The words of 3:12 ("Zion will be ploughed as a field, Jerusalem will become a heap of ruins, and the mountain of the house a wooded height") are the same in the Hebrew as Jeremiah 26:18, where the verse is quoted, and they are given the same wording as the WABV text of Jeremiah. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapters 1 to 3. The Hebrew is very hard in some places, including 1:10-16 (the word-plays on the names of the towns), 2:4 (*le-shovev*), 2:6-11 and 3:3. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the eating of the flesh of the people (3:2-3), the stripping of those who pass by and the driving out of the women (2:8-9) and the building of Zion with blood (3:10) are rendered as written, with no comment. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Two: Chapters 4 and 5, the mountain of the LORD and the ruler from Bethlehem

### Chapter 4

**1** It will come to pass in the latter days that the mountain of the house of the LORD will be established as the highest of the mountains and will be lifted up above the hills, and peoples will flow to it,

**2** and many nations will come, and say: "Come, let us go up to the mountain of the LORD, to the house of the God of Jacob, that he may teach us his ways and that we may walk in his paths." For out of Zion will go the teaching \[H-TORAH\], and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem.

**3** He will judge between many peoples and will decide disputes for strong nations far away. They will beat their swords into ploughshares and their spears into pruning hooks. Nation will not lift up sword against nation, neither will they learn war any more.

**4** But they will sit, each one under his vine and under his fig tree, and no one will make them afraid, for the mouth of the LORD of hosts has spoken.

**5** For all the peoples walk, each in the name of its god, but we will walk in the name of the LORD our God for ever and ever.

**6** In that day, declares the LORD, I will assemble the lame and gather those who have been driven away and those whom I have afflicted,

**7** and the lame I will make the remnant, and those who were cast off a strong nation. And the LORD will reign over them on Mount Zion from this time forth and for ever.

**8** And you, tower of the flock, hill of the daughter of Zion, to you it will come, the former dominion will come, kingship for the daughter of Jerusalem.

**9** Now why do you cry aloud? Is there no king in you? Has your counsellor perished, that pain seizes you like a woman in labour?

**10** Writhe and groan, daughter of Zion, like a woman in labour, for now you will go out from the city and live in the open country. You will go to Babylon. There you will be rescued. There the LORD will redeem \[H-GAAL\] you from the hand of your enemies.

**11** Now many nations are assembled against you, saying, "Let her be defiled, and let our eyes gaze on Zion."

**12** But they do not know the thoughts of the LORD, they do not understand his plan, that he has gathered them like sheaves to the threshing floor.

**13** Arise and thresh, daughter of Zion, for I will make your horn iron, and I will make your hoofs bronze. You will beat in pieces many peoples, and devote \[H-CHEREM\] their gain to the LORD, their wealth to the Lord of the whole earth.

### Chapter 5

**1** Now muster yourselves in troops, daughter of troops. A siege is laid against us. With a rod they strike the judge of Israel on the cheek.

**2** But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, who are little to be among the clans of Judah, from you will come forth for me one who is to be ruler in Israel, whose coming forth is from of old, from ancient days.

**3** Therefore he will give them up until the time when she who is in labour has given birth. Then the rest of his brothers will return \[H-SHUV\] to the people of Israel.

**4** And he will stand and shepherd his flock in the strength of the LORD, in the majesty of the name of the LORD his God. And they will live secure, for now he will be great to the ends of the earth.

**5** And he will be their peace \[H-SHALOM\]. When the Assyrian comes into our land and treads in our palaces, then we will raise against him seven shepherds and eight princes of the people,

**6** and they will shepherd the land of Assyria with the sword, and the land of Nimrod at its entrances. And he will deliver us from the Assyrian when he comes into our land and treads within our border.

**7** Then the remnant of Jacob will be in the midst of many peoples like dew from the LORD, like showers on the grass, which do not wait for a man nor delay for human beings.

**8** And the remnant of Jacob will be among the nations, in the midst of many peoples, like a lion among the animals of the forest, like a young lion among the flocks of sheep, which, when it goes through, treads down and tears in pieces, and there is none to rescue.

**9** Your hand will be lifted up over your adversaries, and all your enemies will be cut off.

**10** And in that day, declares the LORD, I will cut off your horses from among you and will destroy your chariots,

**11** and I will cut off the cities of your land and throw down all your strongholds,

**12** and I will cut off sorceries from your hand, and you will have no more tellers of fortunes,

**13** and I will cut off your carved images and your pillars from among you, and you will bow down no more to the work of your hands,

**14** and I will root out your Asherah poles from among you and destroy your cities.

**15** And in anger and wrath I will execute vengeance on the nations that did not obey.

*Review note: Micah 4 and 5 were drafted in WABV wording. They are the mountain of the LORD and the peace of the nations (4:1-5), the gathering of the lame and the kingdom of the LORD (4:6-8), the pain of the daughter of Zion and the rescue in Babylon (4:9-10), the nations gathered like sheaves and the threshing of the daughter of Zion (4:11-13), the siege and the ruler from Bethlehem (5:1-5a), the deliverance from the Assyrian (5:5b-6), the remnant of Jacob like dew and like a lion (5:7-9) and the cutting off of the horses, the images and the Asherah poles (5:10-15). The numbering is the English numbering: English 5:1 is Hebrew 4:14, so English 5:2-15 is Hebrew 5:1-14. The whole of the part is in poetry, and the verses are set in lines. In 4:2 "the teaching" translates torah and is tagged H-TORAH. In 4:10 "redeem" translates yig'alekha, from ga'al, and is tagged H-GAAL. In 4:13 "devote" translates hacharamti and is tagged H-CHEREM. In 5:3 "return" translates yeshuvun and is tagged H-SHUV. In 5:5 "peace" translates shalom and is tagged H-SHALOM. The words of 4:1-3 are nearly the same in the Hebrew as Isaiah 2:2-4, and the lines that are the same are given the same wording as the WABV text of Isaiah 2:2-4 ("in the latter days", "the mountain of the house of the LORD will be established as the highest of the mountains and will be lifted up above the hills", "Come, let us go up to the mountain of the LORD, to the house of the God of Jacob, that he may teach us his ways and that we may walk in his paths", "for out of Zion will go the teaching, and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem", "they will beat their swords into ploughshares and their spears into pruning hooks" and "nation will not lift up sword against nation, neither will they learn war any more"). The places where the two books differ in the Hebrew ("peoples" and "nations" in the first lines, the order of "many peoples" and "strong nations far away" in the judging, and the added verse 4) are kept as the Hebrew has them, and the footnotes record the matter. The words of Joel 3:10 are the reverse of these lines. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapters 4 and 5. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 4:8 (*migdal-eder*), 4:13, 5:1 (*gedud*), 5:2, 5:5 (*petachehah*) and 5:6. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The ruler from Bethlehem (5:2-5) is rendered as written, with no comment on how it is read, and the vengeance on the nations (5:15) and the cutting off of the horses and the cities (5:10-14) are rendered as written, with no comment. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Three: Chapters 6 and 7, the case of the LORD and the hope

### Chapter 6

**1** Hear what the LORD says: Arise, plead your case before the mountains, and let the hills hear your voice.

**2** Hear, you mountains, the case of the LORD, and you enduring foundations of the earth, for the LORD has a case against his people, and he will argue with Israel.

**3** My people, what have I done to you? How have I wearied you? Answer me.

**4** For I brought you up from the land of Egypt and redeemed \[H-PADAH\] you from the house of slavery, and I sent before you Moses, Aaron and Miriam.

**5** My people, remember what Balak king of Moab devised, and what Balaam the son of Beor answered him, and what happened from Shittim to Gilgal, that you may know the righteous acts \[H-TSEDAQAH\] of the LORD.

**6** With what shall I come before the LORD, and bow myself before God on high? Shall I come before him with burnt offerings, with calves a year old?

**7** Will the LORD be pleased with thousands of rams, with ten thousands of rivers of oil? Shall I give my firstborn for my transgression, the fruit of my body for the sin of my soul \[H-NEPHESH\]?

**8** He has shown you, human being, what is good. And what does the LORD ask of you but to do justice, to love \[H-AHAV\] faithful love \[H-HESED\], and to walk humbly with your God?

**9** The voice of the LORD cries to the city, and it is sound wisdom to fear \[H-YIRAH\] your name: Hear the rod, and the one who appointed it.

**10** Can I forget the treasures of wickedness in the house of the wicked, and the short measure that is accursed?

**11** Shall I acquit the one with wicked scales and with a bag of deceitful weights?

**12** Your rich people are full of violence. Your inhabitants speak lies, and their tongue is deceitful in their mouth.

**13** Therefore I have begun to strike you down, making you desolate because of your sins.

**14** You will eat, but not be satisfied, and your emptiness will be in your midst. You will put away, but not preserve, and what you preserve I will give to the sword.

**15** You will sow, but not reap. You will tread olives, but not anoint yourselves with oil. You will tread grapes, but not drink wine.

**16** For you have kept the statutes of Omri, and all the works of the house of Ahab, and you have walked in their counsels, that I may make you a desolation, and your inhabitants a hissing, so you will bear the scorn of my people.

### Chapter 7

**1** Woe is me. For I have become like one who gathers summer fruit, like one who gleans the vintage, with no cluster to eat, no first-ripe fig that my soul \[H-NEPHESH\] desires.

**2** The faithful \[H-HESED\] have perished from the earth, and there is no one upright among humankind. They all lie in wait for blood, and each hunts his brother with a net.

**3** Their hands are skilled to do evil. The prince makes demands, and the judge goes along for a bribe, and the great man utters the evil desire of his soul \[H-NEPHESH\], and they weave it together.

**4** The best of them is like a brier, the most upright of them a thorn hedge. The day of your watchmen, of your punishment, has come. Now their confusion is at hand.

**5** Put no trust in a neighbour. Have no confidence in a friend. Guard the doors of your mouth from the one who lies in your arms.

**6** For the son treats the father with contempt, the daughter rises up against her mother, the daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law. A man's enemies are the people of his own house.

**7** But as for me, I will look to the LORD. I will wait for the God of my salvation. My God will hear me.

**8** Do not rejoice over me, my enemy. When I fall, I will rise. When I sit in darkness, the LORD will be a light to me.

**9** I will bear the indignation of the LORD because I have sinned against him, until he pleads my case and executes judgement for me. He will bring me out to the light. I will look on his vindication \[H-TSEDAQAH\].

**10** Then my enemy will see, and shame will cover the one who said to me, "Where is the LORD your God?" My eyes will look on her. Now she will be trampled down like mud in the streets.

**11** A day for the building of your walls, that day the boundary will be far away.

**12** In that day they will come to you from Assyria and the cities of Egypt, and from Egypt to the River, from sea to sea and from mountain to mountain.

**13** But the earth will be desolate because of its inhabitants, for the fruit of their deeds.

**14** Shepherd your people with your staff, the flock of your inheritance, who live alone in a forest in the midst of a garden land. Let them graze in Bashan and Gilead as in the days of old.

**15** As in the days when you came out of the land of Egypt, I will show them marvellous things.

**16** The nations will see and be ashamed of all their might. They will lay their hands on their mouths. Their ears will be deaf.

**17** They will lick the dust like a serpent, like the crawling things of the earth. They will come trembling out of their strongholds. They will come in dread to the LORD our God, and they will fear \[H-YIRAH\] you.

**18** Who is a God like you, pardoning iniquity and passing over transgression for the remnant of his inheritance? He does not retain his anger for ever, because he delights in faithful love \[H-HESED\].

**19** He will again have compassion on us. He will tread our iniquities underfoot. You will cast all our sins into the depths of the sea.

**20** You will give truth \[H-EMET\] to Jacob and faithful love \[H-HESED\] to Abraham, as you swore to our fathers from the days of old.

*Review note: Micah 6 and 7 were drafted in WABV wording. They are the case of the LORD before the mountains and the remembrance of the exodus (6:1-5), the question of what the LORD asks and the answer (6:6-8), the charge against the city and the sentence (6:9-16), the lament over the faithful who have perished (7:1-6), the confession and the hope of the one who waits (7:7-10), the promise of the building of the walls and the return of the nations (7:11-17) and the praise of the God who pardons (7:18-20). The numbering is the same as the Hebrew. The whole of the part is in poetry, and the verses are set in lines. In 6:4 "redeemed" translates peditikha, from padah, and is tagged H-PADAH. In 6:5, 7:9 "righteous acts" and "vindication" translate tsidqot and tsidqato and are tagged H-TSEDAQAH. In 6:7, 7:1 and 7:3 "soul" translates nafshi and nafsho and is tagged H-NEPHESH. In 6:8 "to love faithful love" translates ahavat chesed and is tagged H-AHAV and H-HESED. In 6:9 and 7:17 "to fear" and "they will fear" are tagged H-YIRAH. In 7:2, 7:18 and 7:20 "the faithful" and "faithful love" are tagged H-HESED. In 7:20 "truth" translates emet and is tagged H-EMET. In 7:19 "he will again have compassion" translates yashuv yerachamenu, in which shuv is used as an adverb, and it is not tagged. The words of 7:18-20 are matched with Exodus 34:6-7, Exodus 15:11 and Psalm 103:8-12 where the Hebrew words are the same. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapters 6 and 7, and none in the whole book. The Hebrew is very hard in some places, including 6:9-14, 7:4, 7:11-12 and 7:14. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the firstborn and the sin of the soul (6:7), the accusation of the house of Omri and Ahab (6:16) and the break-up of the family (7:6) are rendered as written, with no comment. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Footnotes

- **1:1** "Micah" translates *Mikhah*, a short form of *Mikhayahu*, "who is like the LORD". "Moresheth" translates *Moreshet*, which is *Moresheth-gath* in 1:14, near Gath. The kings Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah reigned between about 750 and 700 before Christ. "Which he saw" translates *asher chazah*.
- **1:2** "A witness" translates *le-ed*, and "his holy temple" translates *heikhal qodsho*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **1:3** "The high places of the earth" translates *bamotei arets*.
- **1:5** "Transgression" translates *pesha*, and "the high place of Judah" translates *bamot Yehudah*.
- **1:6** "A heap" translates *i*, and "a place for planting vineyards" translates *maffetot karem*.
- **1:7** "Carved images" translates *pesileha*, and "the wages of a prostitute" translates *etnan zonah*. "They will return" translates *yashuvu*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **1:8** "Jackals" translates *tannim*, and "ostriches" translates *benot ya'anah*.
- **1:10** The verses 10 to 16 are very hard, and they are full of word-plays on the names of the towns, which are partly kept and partly lost in English. "Tell it not in Gath" is as in 2 Samuel 1:20. "Beth-le-aphrah" means "the house of dust" (*afar*, "dust").
- **1:11** "Shaphir" means "pleasant", "Zaanan" sounds like "go out" (*yatsa*), and "Beth-ezel" is not certain. "Its standing place" translates *omdato*, which is hard.
- **1:12** "Maroth" sounds like "bitter" (*mar*).
- **1:13** "Steeds" translates *rekhesh*, a word-play on *Lakhish*, "Lachish".
- **1:14** "Parting gifts" translates *shilluchim*, and "Achzib" sounds like "deception" (*akhzav*).
- **1:15** "A conqueror" translates *ha-yoresh*, a word-play on *Mareshah*, and "Adullam" is a town of Judah.
- **1:16** "The vulture" translates *nesher*, which may also be "the eagle", and "the children of your delight" translates *benei ta'anugayikh*.
- **2:1** "Plan iniquity" translates *chosh'vei aven*, and "it is in the power of their hand" translates *yesh le-el yadam*.
- **2:2** "They covet" translates *chamdu*.
- **2:3** "Family" translates *mishpachah*, and "disaster" translates *ra'ah*, the same open decision as in Isaiah 45:7.
- **2:4** "A taunt song" translates *mashal*, and "lamentation" translates *nehi nihyah*. "To the one who turns away" translates *le-shovev*, which is hard, and may be read "to the apostate", "to the captor" or "to a restorer". See entry H-SHUV.
- **2:5** "To cast the line by lot" translates *mashlikh chevel be-goral*.
- **2:6** "Preach" translates *tittefu*, literally "drip", the word for prophetic speech. The verses 6 to 11 are very hard.
- **2:7** "Has the Spirit of the LORD grown impatient" translates *ha-qatsar ruach YHWH*, literally "is the spirit of the LORD short". See entry H-RUACH. The capital letter is the draft's choice, and the team should decide it with Isaiah 63:10-11.
- **2:8** "The robe" translates *addaret*, "trustingly" translates *betach*, and "those who return from war" translates *shuvei milchamah*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **2:10** "Uncleanness" translates *tum'ah*.
- **2:11** "Wind" translates *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH. "Utter wind and lies" translates *holekh ruach ve-shaqer kizzev*, which is hard and may be read "walks in the wind and deals falsely".
- **2:12** "The remnant" translates *she'erit*, "a fold" translates *botsrah*, and "a noisy multitude of people" translates *tehimennah me-adam*.
- **2:13** "The one who opens the breach" translates *ha-poretz*, and "their king" translates *malkam*.
- **3:1** "Heads" translates *rashim*, and "justice" translates *mishpat*.
- **3:2** "Love" translates *ohavei*. See entry H-AHAV.
- **3:3** "A cauldron" translates *qallachat*. The verses 2 and 3 are rendered as written, with no comment.
- **3:5** "Peace" translates *shalom*. See entry H-SHALOM. "Consecrate war" translates *qiddeshu alav milchamah*, as in Joel 3:9. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **3:6** "Vision" translates *chazon*, and "divination" translates *miqsam*.
- **3:7** "The seers" translates *chozim*, "the diviners" translates *qosemim*, and "cover their lips" translates *ataph saftam*.
- **3:8** "The Spirit of the LORD" translates *ruach YHWH*. See entry H-RUACH. The capital letter is the draft's choice. "Power" translates *koach*, and "justice" translates *mishpat*.
- **3:9** "Detest" translates *mete'avim*, and "make crooked" translates *ye'aqqeshu*.
- **3:10** "With blood" translates *be-damim*.
- **3:11** "A bribe" translates *shochad*, "for a price" translates *bi-mchir*, and "divination" translates *yiqsemu*. "Disaster" translates *ra'ah*.
- **3:12** "Zion will be ploughed as a field, Jerusalem will become a heap of ruins, and the mountain of the house a wooded height" translates *Tsiyyon sadeh techarash ve-Yerushalayim iyyim tihyeh ve-har ha-bayit le-vamot ya'ar*, and the words are the same as Jeremiah 26:18, where the verse is quoted. "Heap of ruins" translates *iyyim*, as in Jeremiah 26:18.
- **4:1** "In the latter days" translates *be-acharit ha-yamim*, and "will be established" translates *nakhon yihyeh*. The verses 1 to 3 are nearly the same as Isaiah 2:2-4, and the lines that are the same in the Hebrew are given the same wording as the WABV text of Isaiah. In Isaiah the first lines say "all the nations will flow to it", and in Micah the Hebrew has "peoples" (*ammim*).
- **4:2** "Many nations" translates *goyim rabbim*, where Isaiah has "many peoples". "The teaching" translates *torah*. See entry H-TORAH.
- **4:3** "He will judge between many peoples and will decide disputes for strong nations far away" translates *ve-shafat bein ammim rabbim ve-hokhiach le-goyim atsumim ad-rachoq*, and in Isaiah the order is "between the nations ... for many peoples" and the words "far away" are not there. "Swords" translates *charvotam*. The words of Joel 3:10 are the reverse of these lines.
- **4:4** The verse is not in Isaiah. "Each one under his vine and under his fig tree" is as in 1 Kings 4:25 and Zechariah 3:10, and "the mouth of the LORD of hosts has spoken" translates *ki-fi YHWH tseva'ot dibber*.
- **4:5** "In the name of its god" translates *be-shem eloheiv*.
- **4:6** "The lame" translates *tsol'ah*, and "those who have been driven away" translates *nidachah*.
- **4:7** "The remnant" translates *she'erit*, "those who were cast off" translates *nahala'ah*, and "a strong nation" translates *goy atsum*.
- **4:8** "Tower of the flock" translates *migdal-eder*, a place near Bethlehem (Genesis 35:21), and "hill" translates *Ofel*. "Dominion" translates *memshalah*. The verse is hard.
- **4:9** "Cry aloud" translates *tari'i re'a*, and "counsellor" translates *yo'ets*.
- **4:10** "Writhe" and "groan" translate *chuli* and *ve-gochi*. "Babylon" translates *Bavel*, and Babylon was not yet the power of Micah's day. "Redeem" translates *yig'alekha*, from *ga'al*. See entry H-GAAL.
- **4:11** "Let her be defiled" translates *techenaf*.
- **4:12** "His plan" translates *atsato*, and "sheaves" translates *amir*.
- **4:13** "Thresh" translates *dushi*, "devote" translates *hacharamti*, from *cherem*, and "their gain" translates *betsa'am*. See entry H-CHEREM. The verse is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **5:1** The verse is Hebrew 4:14, and the verses 2 to 15 are Hebrew 5:1-14. "Muster yourselves in troops, daughter of troops" translates *titgodedi bat-gedud*, which is hard. "A siege" translates *matsor*, and "the judge of Israel" translates *shofet Yisra'el*.
- **5:2** "Bethlehem Ephrathah" translates *Beit-Lechem Efratah*, "little" translates *tsa'ir*, and "clans" translates *alfei*, literally "thousands". "One who is to be ruler" translates *moshel*, and "whose coming forth is from of old, from ancient days" translates *motsa'otav miqqedem mi-yemei olam*. See Matthew 2:6. It is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **5:3** "He will give them up" translates *yittenem*, "she who is in labour" translates *yoledah*, and "will return" translates *yeshuvun*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **5:4** "Majesty" translates *ge'on*.
- **5:5** "He will be their peace" translates *ve-hayah zeh shalom*. See entry H-SHALOM. "Seven shepherds and eight princes" translates *shiv'ah ro'im ushmonah nesikhei adam*.
- **5:6** "The land of Nimrod at its entrances" translates *erets Nimrod bi-fetachehah*, which is hard and may be read "with its own sword" (*be-fitcheha*).
- **5:7** "Dew" translates *tal*.
- **5:8** "A young lion" translates *kefir*.
- **5:10** The verses 10 to 14 name what is cut off: "horses" and "chariots" (10), "cities" and "strongholds" (11), "sorceries" (*keshafim*) and "tellers of fortunes" (*me'onenim*) (12), "carved images" and "pillars" (13) and "Asherah poles" (*asherekha*) (14). They are rendered as written, with no comment.
- **5:15** "Vengeance" translates *naqam*, and the verse is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **6:1** "Plead your case" translates *riv*, in the language of a lawsuit, as in verse 2.
- **6:2** "The enduring foundations of the earth" translates *ha-eitanim mosdei arets*, and "he will argue" translates *yitvakach*.
- **6:3** "How have I wearied you" translates *ma hil'eitikha*.
- **6:4** "I brought you up" translates *he'elitikha*, "redeemed" translates *peditikha*, from *padah*, and "the house of slavery" translates *beit avadim*. See entry H-PADAH.
- **6:5** "Balak" and "Balaam" are as in Numbers 22 to 24, and "from Shittim to Gilgal" refers to Numbers 25 and Joshua 3 and 4. "The righteous acts of the LORD" translates *tsidqot YHWH*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH.
- **6:6** "God on high" translates *Elohei marom*, and "calves a year old" translates *agalim bnei shanah*.
- **6:7** "My firstborn" translates *bekhori*, "the fruit of my body" translates *peri vitni*, and "the sin of my soul" translates *chattat nafshi*. See entry H-NEPHESH. The verse is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **6:8** "He has shown you" translates *higgid lekha*, "human being" translates *adam*, "justice" translates *mishpat*, "to love faithful love" translates *ahavat chesed*, and "to walk humbly" translates *hatsne'a lekhet*. See entries H-AHAV and H-HESED.
- **6:9** "Sound wisdom" translates *tushiyyah*, and "to fear your name" translates *yir'eh shemekha*, which is very hard. It may also be read "your name sees", and the Greek has "he will save those who fear his name". See entry H-YIRAH. "The rod" translates *matteh*. The verses 9 to 14 are hard.
- **6:10** "The short measure" translates *efat razon*, and "accursed" translates *zeumah*.
- **6:11** "Shall I acquit" translates *ha-ezkeh*, and "wicked scales" translates *moznei resha*.
- **6:14** "Your emptiness" translates *yeshkha*, which is rare and hard, and "you will put away" translates *tasig*.
- **6:16** "The statutes of Omri" and "the works of the house of Ahab" refer to 1 Kings 16:25-33. "A hissing" translates *shereqah*, and "the scorn of my people" translates *cherpat ammi*, where the Greek has "the scorn of the peoples".
- **7:1** "Summer fruit" translates *qetsi*, "gleans" translates *olelot*, "the first-ripe fig" translates *bikkurah*, and "my soul desires" translates *avvtah nafshi*. See entry H-NEPHESH.
- **7:2** "The faithful" translates *chasid*. See entry H-HESED. "Hunts his brother with a net" translates *ish et-achihu yatsudu cherem*, in which *cherem* means "a net" and is not the ban, so it is not tagged H-CHEREM.
- **7:3** "The evil desire of his soul" translates *havvat nafsho*, and "they weave it together" translates *va-yavtuha*, which is hard. See entry H-NEPHESH. "A bribe" translates *shillum*.
- **7:4** "A brier" translates *chedeq*, "a thorn hedge" translates *mesukhah*, and "their confusion" translates *mevukhatam*.
- **7:5** "A neighbour" translates *re'a*, and "a friend" translates *alluf*.
- **7:6** "Treats with contempt" translates *menavel*. See Matthew 10:35-36.
- **7:7** "I will look" translates *atsapeh*, and "the God of my salvation" translates *Elohei yish'i*.
- **7:8** "My enemy" translates *oyavti*, which is feminine in form, as in verse 10.
- **7:9** "The indignation" translates *za'af*, "he pleads my case" translates *yarib rivi*, and "his vindication" translates *tsidqato*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH.
- **7:10** "Trampled down" translates *mirmas*.
- **7:11** The verses 11 and 12 are very hard in the Hebrew. "The boundary" translates *choq*.
- **7:12** "The cities of Egypt" translates *arei Matsor*, and "the River" translates *nahar*, the Euphrates.
- **7:14** "A garden land" translates *karmel*, which may also be the name Carmel, and the verse is hard.
- **7:15** "Marvellous things" translates *nifla'ot*.
- **7:17** "In dread" translates *yifchadu*, and "they will fear you" translates *ve-yir'u mimmekha*. See entry H-YIRAH.
- **7:18** "Who is a God like you" translates *mi El kamokha*, a play on the name *Mikhah* (*mi-kha-yah*, "who is like the LORD"). See also Exodus 15:11. "Pardoning iniquity and passing over transgression" translates *nosei avon ve-over al-pesha*, as in Exodus 34:7. "Delights in faithful love" translates *chafetz chesed hu*. See entry H-HESED.
- **7:19** "He will again have compassion on us" translates *yashuv yerachamenu*, in which *shuv* is used as an adverb and is not tagged. "Will tread underfoot" translates *yikhbosh*, and "the depths of the sea" translates *metsulot yam*.
- **7:20** "Truth" translates *emet*, and "faithful love" translates *chesed*. See entries H-EMET and H-HESED. See also Genesis 22:16-18.
