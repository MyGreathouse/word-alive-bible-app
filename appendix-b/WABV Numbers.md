## Numbers 1-4: Counting the People and the Camp

### Chapter 1

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai, in the tent of meeting, on the first day of the second month in the second year after they had come out of the land of Egypt:

**2** "Take a census of the whole community of the Israelites, by their clans and their families, counting every male by name, one by one.

**3** You and Aaron are to number all in Israel who are twenty years old or more and able to go out to war, company by company.

**4** One man from each tribe, the head of his family, is to be with you.

**5** These are the names of the men who are to stand with you: from Reuben, Elizur son of Shedeur;

**6** from Simeon, Shelumiel son of Zurishaddai;

**7** from Judah, Nahshon son of Amminadab;

**8** from Issachar, Nethanel son of Zuar;

**9** from Zebulun, Eliab son of Helon;

**10** from the sons of Joseph, from Ephraim, Elishama son of Ammihud, and from Manasseh, Gamaliel son of Pedahzur;

**11** from Benjamin, Abidan son of Gideoni;

**12** from Dan, Ahiezer son of Ammishaddai;

**13** from Asher, Pagiel son of Okran;

**14** from Gad, Eliasaph son of Deuel;

**15** from Naphtali, Ahira son of Enan."

**16** These were the ones called from the community, the leaders of their ancestral tribes. They were the heads of the clans of Israel.

**17** Moses and Aaron took these men who had been named by name,

**18** and they gathered the whole community together on the first day of the second month. The people declared their ancestry by clans and families, and the men twenty years old or more were counted by name, one by one,

**19** as the LORD had commanded Moses. So he counted them in the wilderness of Sinai.

**20** The descendants of Reuben, Israel's firstborn: all the men twenty years old or more who could go out to war were counted by name, one by one, by their clans and families.

**21** The number counted from the tribe of Reuben was 46,500.

**22** The descendants of Simeon: all the men twenty years old or more who could go out to war were counted by name, one by one, by their clans and families.

**23** The number counted from the tribe of Simeon was 59,300.

**24** The descendants of Gad: all the men twenty years old or more who could go out to war were counted by name, by their clans and families.

**25** The number counted from the tribe of Gad was 45,650.

**26** The descendants of Judah: all the men twenty years old or more who could go out to war were counted by name, by their clans and families.

**27** The number counted from the tribe of Judah was 74,600.

**28** The descendants of Issachar: all the men twenty years old or more who could go out to war were counted by name, by their clans and families.

**29** The number counted from the tribe of Issachar was 54,400.

**30** The descendants of Zebulun: all the men twenty years old or more who could go out to war were counted by name, by their clans and families.

**31** The number counted from the tribe of Zebulun was 57,400.

**32** The descendants of Joseph, first the descendants of Ephraim: all the men twenty years old or more who could go out to war were counted by name, by their clans and families.

**33** The number counted from the tribe of Ephraim was 40,500.

**34** The descendants of Manasseh: all the men twenty years old or more who could go out to war were counted by name, by their clans and families.

**35** The number counted from the tribe of Manasseh was 32,200.

**36** The descendants of Benjamin: all the men twenty years old or more who could go out to war were counted by name, by their clans and families.

**37** The number counted from the tribe of Benjamin was 35,400.

**38** The descendants of Dan: all the men twenty years old or more who could go out to war were counted by name, by their clans and families.

**39** The number counted from the tribe of Dan was 62,700.

**40** The descendants of Asher: all the men twenty years old or more who could go out to war were counted by name, by their clans and families.

**41** The number counted from the tribe of Asher was 41,500.

**42** The descendants of Naphtali: all the men twenty years old or more who could go out to war were counted by name, by their clans and families.

**43** The number counted from the tribe of Naphtali was 53,400.

**44** These were the ones counted by Moses and Aaron and the twelve leaders of Israel, each one standing for his family.

**45** All the Israelites twenty years old or more who could go out to war in Israel were counted by their families.

**46** The total number counted was 603,550.

**47** But the Levites were not counted among them by their ancestral tribe.

**48** For the LORD had spoken to Moses:

**49** "Only the tribe of Levi you are not to count. You are not to include them in the census of the Israelites.

**50** Instead, put the Levites in charge of the tabernacle of the Testimony, over all its furnishings and all that belongs to it. They are to carry the tabernacle and all its furnishings, and they are to tend it. They are to camp round the tabernacle.

**51** When the tabernacle is to move on, the Levites are to take it down, and when the tabernacle is to be set up, the Levites are to put it up. Any outsider who comes near it is to be put to death.

**52** The Israelites are to camp by divisions, each man in his own camp and each under his own standard.

**53** But the Levites are to camp round the tabernacle of the Testimony, so that no wrath will fall on the community of the Israelites. The Levites are to keep guard over the tabernacle of the Testimony."

**54** The Israelites did this. They did everything just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

### Chapter 2

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron:

**2** "The Israelites are to camp round the tent of meeting at some distance from it, each under his own standard, under the banners of their families.

**3** On the east side, towards the sunrise, are to camp the divisions of the camp of Judah under their standard. The leader of the people of Judah is Nahshon son of Amminadab.

**4** His division, those counted, numbers 74,600.

**5** Next to him the tribe of Issachar is to camp. The leader of the people of Issachar is Nethanel son of Zuar.

**6** His division, those counted, numbers 54,400.

**7** Then the tribe of Zebulun. The leader of the people of Zebulun is Eliab son of Helon.

**8** His division, those counted, numbers 57,400.

**9** All those counted in the camp of Judah, by their divisions, number 186,400. They are to set out first.

**10** On the south side are to be the divisions of the camp of Reuben under their standard. The leader of the people of Reuben is Elizur son of Shedeur.

**11** His division, those counted, numbers 46,500.

**12** Next to him the tribe of Simeon is to camp. The leader of the people of Simeon is Shelumiel son of Zurishaddai.

**13** His division, those counted, numbers 59,300.

**14** Then the tribe of Gad. The leader of the people of Gad is Eliasaph son of Deuel.

**15** His division, those counted, numbers 45,650.

**16** All those counted in the camp of Reuben, by their divisions, number 151,450. They are to set out second.

**17** Then the tent of meeting is to set out, with the camp of the Levites in the middle of the camps. As they camp, so they are to set out, each in his place, by his standard.

**18** On the west side are to be the divisions of the camp of Ephraim under their standard. The leader of the people of Ephraim is Elishama son of Ammihud.

**19** His division, those counted, numbers 40,500.

**20** Next to him the tribe of Manasseh. The leader of the people of Manasseh is Gamaliel son of Pedahzur.

**21** His division, those counted, numbers 32,200.

**22** Then the tribe of Benjamin. The leader of the people of Benjamin is Abidan son of Gideoni.

**23** His division, those counted, numbers 35,400.

**24** All those counted in the camp of Ephraim, by their divisions, number 108,100. They are to set out third.

**25** On the north side are to be the divisions of the camp of Dan under their standard. The leader of the people of Dan is Ahiezer son of Ammishaddai.

**26** His division, those counted, numbers 62,700.

**27** Next to him the tribe of Asher is to camp. The leader of the people of Asher is Pagiel son of Okran.

**28** His division, those counted, numbers 41,500.

**29** Then the tribe of Naphtali. The leader of the people of Naphtali is Ahira son of Enan.

**30** His division, those counted, numbers 53,400.

**31** All those counted in the camp of Dan number 157,600. They are to set out last, under their standards."

**32** These are the Israelites, counted by their families. All those counted in the camps, by their divisions, were 603,550.

**33** But the Levites were not counted among the Israelites, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**34** The Israelites did everything just as the LORD had commanded Moses. So they camped by their standards, and so they set out, each by his clan and his family.

### Chapter 3

**1** These are the generations of Aaron and Moses at the time when the LORD spoke with Moses on Mount Sinai.

**2** These are the names of Aaron's sons: Nadab the firstborn, then Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar.

**3** These are the names of Aaron's sons, the anointed priests, whom he installed to serve as priests.

**4** But Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD when they offered unauthorised fire before the LORD in the wilderness of Sinai, and they had no sons. So Eleazar and Ithamar served as priests during the lifetime of their father Aaron.

**5** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**6** "Bring the tribe of Levi near and present them to Aaron the priest, so that they may serve him.

**7** They are to carry out duties for him and for the whole community before the tent of meeting, doing the work of the tabernacle.

**8** They are to guard all the furnishings of the tent of meeting, and carry out duties for the Israelites, doing the work of the tabernacle.

**9** You are to give the Levites to Aaron and his sons. They are given to him, given from among the Israelites.

**10** You are to appoint Aaron and his sons to keep their priesthood. Any outsider who comes near is to be put to death."

**11** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**12** "I myself have taken the Levites from among the Israelites in place of every firstborn who opens the womb among the Israelites. The Levites are mine,

**13** for every firstborn is mine. On the day I struck down every firstborn in the land of Egypt, I set apart for myself every firstborn in Israel, of people and of animals. They are to be mine. I am the LORD."

**14** The LORD spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai:

**15** "Count the Levites by their families and clans. You are to count every male from a month old and upwards."

**16** So Moses counted them, as he was commanded by the word of the LORD.

**17** These were the sons of Levi by name: Gershon, Kohath and Merari.

**18** These are the names of the sons of Gershon by their clans: Libni and Shimei.

**19** The sons of Kohath by their clans: Amram, Izhar, Hebron and Uzziel.

**20** The sons of Merari by their clans: Mahli and Mushi. These are the clans of the Levites by their families.

**21** To Gershon belonged the clan of the Libnites and the clan of the Shimeites. These were the clans of the Gershonites.

**22** Those counted, every male from a month old and upwards, numbered 7,500.

**23** The clans of the Gershonites were to camp behind the tabernacle, on the west.

**24** The leader of the family of the Gershonites was Eliasaph son of Lael.

**25** At the tent of meeting the Gershonites were responsible for the tabernacle and the tent, its covering, the screen for the entrance of the tent of meeting,

**26** the hangings of the courtyard, the screen for the entrance of the courtyard that surrounds the tabernacle and the altar, and its ropes, with all the work connected with them.

**27** To Kohath belonged the clan of the Amramites, the clan of the Izharites, the clan of the Hebronites and the clan of the Uzzielites. These were the clans of the Kohathites.

**28** Counting every male from a month old and upwards, there were 8,600, responsible for the care of the sanctuary.

**29** The clans of the Kohathites were to camp on the south side of the tabernacle.

**30** The leader of the family of the clans of the Kohathites was Elizaphan son of Uzziel.

**31** They were responsible for the ark, the table, the lampstand, the altars, the utensils of the sanctuary with which the priests serve, the screen, and all the work connected with them.

**32** The chief of the leaders of the Levites was Eleazar son of Aaron the priest. He was appointed over those who were responsible for the care of the sanctuary.

**33** To Merari belonged the clan of the Mahlites and the clan of the Mushites. These were the clans of Merari.

**34** Those counted, every male from a month old and upwards, numbered 6,200.

**35** The leader of the family of the clans of Merari was Zuriel son of Abihail. They were to camp on the north side of the tabernacle.

**36** The appointed duty of the Merarites was the care of the frames of the tabernacle, its bars, its posts and its bases, all its equipment and all the work connected with them,

**37** and the posts of the courtyard round it with their bases, their pegs and their ropes.

**38** Moses and Aaron and his sons were to camp in front of the tabernacle on the east, in front of the tent of meeting, responsible for the care of the sanctuary on behalf of the Israelites. Any outsider who came near was to be put to death.

**39** All the Levites who were counted, whom Moses and Aaron counted at the LORD's command by their clans, every male from a month old and upwards, numbered 22,000.

**40** The LORD said to Moses, "Count all the firstborn males of the Israelites from a month old and upwards, and take the number of their names.

**41** Take the Levites for me in place of all the firstborn of the Israelites, and the livestock of the Levites in place of all the firstborn of the livestock of the Israelites. I am the LORD."

**42** So Moses counted all the firstborn of the Israelites, as the LORD had commanded him.

**43** The total of the firstborn males from a month old and upwards, counted by name, was 22,273.

**44** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**45** "Take the Levites in place of all the firstborn of the Israelites, and the livestock of the Levites in place of their livestock. The Levites are to be mine. I am the LORD.

**46** For the redemption \[H-PADAH\] of the 273 firstborn of the Israelites who are more than the number of the Levites,

**47** you are to take five shekels for each one, by the shekel of the sanctuary (a shekel is twenty gerahs).

**48** Give the money for the redemption of those who are in excess to Aaron and his sons."

**49** Moses took the redemption money from those who were in excess of those redeemed by the Levites.

**50** From the firstborn of the Israelites he took the silver, 1,365 shekels by the shekel of the sanctuary.

**51** Moses gave the redemption money to Aaron and his sons, at the word of the LORD, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

### Chapter 4

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron:

**2** "Take a census of the Kohathites among the Levites, by their clans and families,

**3** from thirty years old up to fifty, all who come to take part in the work of the tent of meeting.

**4** This is the work of the Kohathites in the tent of meeting: the care of the most holy things.

**5** When the camp sets out, Aaron and his sons are to go in and take down the screening curtain and cover the ark of the Testimony with it.

**6** They are to put over it a covering of fine leather, and spread a cloth of pure blue on top, and put its poles in place.

**7** Over the table of the Presence they are to spread a blue cloth and put on it the plates, the dishes, the bowls and the flagons for the drink offering. The regular bread is to remain on it.

**8** They are to spread over them a crimson cloth and cover it with a covering of fine leather, and put its poles in place.

**9** They are to take a blue cloth and cover the lampstand for the light, with its lamps, its tongs, its trays and all its oil vessels, with which they supply it.

**10** They are to put it with all its utensils in a covering of fine leather and place it on the carrying frame.

**11** Over the golden altar they are to spread a blue cloth and cover it with a covering of fine leather, and put its poles in place.

**12** They are to take all the utensils of the service with which they serve in the sanctuary and put them in a blue cloth, cover them with a covering of fine leather and place them on the carrying frame.

**13** They are to clear the ashes from the altar and spread a purple cloth over it.

**14** They are to put on it all the utensils with which they serve there: the fire pans, the flesh-forks, the shovels and the sprinkling bowls, all the utensils of the altar. They are to spread over it a covering of fine leather and put its poles in place.

**15** When Aaron and his sons have finished covering the sanctuary and all the furnishings of the sanctuary, when the camp is ready to set out, the Kohathites are to come to carry them. But they must not touch the holy things, or they will die. These are the things in the tent of meeting that the Kohathites are to carry.

**16** Eleazar son of Aaron the priest is to have charge of the oil for the light, the fragrant incense, the regular grain offering and the anointing oil, with the oversight of the whole tabernacle and all that is in it, the sanctuary and its furnishings."

**17** The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron:

**18** "Do not let the tribe of the clans of the Kohathites be cut off from among the Levites.

**19** So that they may live and not die when they approach the most holy things, do this for them: Aaron and his sons are to go in and assign each man his work and his load.

**20** But the Kohathites must not go in to look at the holy things, even for a moment, or they will die."

**21** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**22** "Take a census of the Gershonites also, by their families and clans.

**23** You are to count the men from thirty years old up to fifty, all who come to take part in the work of the tent of meeting.

**24** This is the work of the clans of the Gershonites, in serving and in carrying:

**25** they are to carry the curtains of the tabernacle, the tent of meeting, its covering and the covering of fine leather that is on top of it, and the screen for the entrance of the tent of meeting,

**26** and the hangings of the courtyard, the screen for the entrance of the gate of the courtyard that surrounds the tabernacle and the altar, and their ropes and all the equipment for their service. They are to do all that needs to be done with them.

**27** All the service of the Gershonites, in all their carrying and in all their work, is to be under the direction of Aaron and his sons. You are to assign to them as their responsibility all that they are to carry.

**28** This is the service of the clans of the Gershonites in the tent of meeting, and their duties are to be under the direction of Ithamar son of Aaron the priest.

**29** As for the Merarites, you are to count them by their clans and families.

**30** You are to count them from thirty years old up to fifty, all who come to take part in the work of the tent of meeting.

**31** This is what they are responsible for carrying, as the whole of their service in the tent of meeting: the frames of the tabernacle, its bars, its posts and its bases,

**32** and the posts of the surrounding courtyard with their bases, their pegs and their ropes, with all their equipment and all their service. You are to assign by name the things they are responsible for carrying.

**33** This is the service of the clans of the Merarites, for all their work in the tent of meeting, under the direction of Ithamar son of Aaron the priest."

**34** Moses, Aaron and the leaders of the community counted the Kohathites by their clans and families,

**35** from thirty years old up to fifty, all who came to take part in the work of the tent of meeting.

**36** Those counted by their clans numbered 2,750.

**37** This was the total of those counted from the clans of the Kohathites, all who served in the tent of meeting, whom Moses and Aaron counted at the LORD's command through Moses.

**38** Those of the Gershonites who were counted, by their clans and families,

**39** from thirty years old up to fifty, all who came to take part in the work of the tent of meeting,

**40** those counted by their clans and families numbered 2,630.

**41** This was the total of those counted from the clans of the Gershonites, all who served in the tent of meeting, whom Moses and Aaron counted at the LORD's command.

**42** Those of the clans of the Merarites who were counted, by their clans and families,

**43** from thirty years old up to fifty, all who came to take part in the work of the tent of meeting,

**44** those counted by their clans numbered 3,200.

**45** This was the total of those counted from the clans of the Merarites, whom Moses and Aaron counted at the LORD's command through Moses.

**46** All those of the Levites whom Moses, Aaron and the leaders of Israel counted, by their clans and families,

**47** from thirty years old up to fifty, all who came to do the work of service and the work of carrying in the tent of meeting,

**48** those counted numbered 8,580.

**49** At the LORD's command through Moses, each was assigned his service and his load. So they were counted, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

## Numbers 5-6: Purity, Vows and Blessing

### Chapter 5

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**2** "Command the Israelites to send out of the camp everyone who has a skin disease or a discharge, and everyone who is unclean because of a dead body.

**3** You are to send out both male and female. Send them outside the camp, so that they do not make the camp unclean, where I live in their midst."

**4** The Israelites did so, and sent them outside the camp. They did just as the LORD had said to Moses.

**5** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**6** "Tell the Israelites: When a man or a woman does any wrong to another person, by acting unfaithfully towards the LORD, that person is guilty.

**7** He is to confess the wrong he has done. He is to make full restitution for his guilt, adding a fifth to it, and give it to the one he has wronged.

**8** If the person has no close relative to whom restitution can be made for the guilt, the restitution for guilt belongs to the LORD, for the priest, apart from the ram of atonement, with which atonement is made for him.

**9** Every contribution from all the holy gifts of the Israelites, which they bring to the priest, will be his.

**10** Each person's holy gifts are his own, but whatever he gives to the priest will be the priest's."

**11** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**12** "Speak to the Israelites and tell them: If any man's wife goes astray and is unfaithful to him,

**13** and a man lies with her, and it is hidden from her husband's eyes and she is undetected, though she is unclean, and there is no witness against her, and she was not caught,

**14** and a spirit of jealousy comes over him and he is jealous of his wife, who is unclean, or if a spirit of jealousy comes over him and he is jealous of his wife, though she is not unclean,

**15** then the man is to bring his wife to the priest. He is to bring the offering required for her, a tenth of an ephah of barley flour. He is not to pour oil on it or put frankincense on it, for it is a grain offering of jealousy, a grain offering of remembrance, bringing guilt to remembrance.

**16** The priest is to bring her near and have her stand before the LORD.

**17** The priest is to take holy water in a clay vessel, and take some of the dust that is on the floor of the tabernacle and put it into the water.

**18** The priest is to have the woman stand before the LORD and uncover her head, and put into her hands the grain offering of remembrance, which is the grain offering of jealousy. The priest is to hold in his hand the bitter water that brings a curse.

**19** Then the priest is to put her under an oath and say to the woman, 'If no man has lain with you, and if you have not gone astray into uncleanness while you were under your husband's authority, be free from this bitter water that brings a curse.

**20** But if you have gone astray while under your husband's authority, and if you have made yourself unclean, and some man other than your husband has lain with you,'

**21** then the priest is to put the woman under the oath of the curse, and the priest is to say to the woman, 'May the LORD make you a curse and an oath among your people, by making your thigh fall away and your belly swell.

**22** May this water that brings a curse go into your body and make your belly swell and your thigh fall away.' And the woman is to say, 'Amen, amen.'

**23** The priest is to write these curses on a scroll and wash them off into the bitter water.

**24** He is to make the woman drink the bitter water that brings a curse, and the water that brings a curse will enter her and become bitter.

**25** The priest is to take the grain offering of jealousy from the woman's hand, lift it up before the LORD and bring it to the altar.

**26** The priest is to take a handful of the grain offering as its memorial portion and burn it on the altar, and afterwards he is to make the woman drink the water.

**27** When he has made her drink the water, if she has made herself unclean and has been unfaithful to her husband, the water that brings a curse will enter her and become bitter, and her belly will swell and her thigh will fall away, and the woman will become a curse among her people.

**28** But if the woman has not made herself unclean and is pure, then she will be free and will be able to conceive children.

**29** This is the teaching about jealousy, when a woman goes astray while under her husband's authority and makes herself unclean,

**30** or when a spirit of jealousy comes over a man and he is jealous of his wife. Then he is to have the woman stand before the LORD, and the priest is to carry out all this teaching on her.

**31** The man will be free from guilt, but that woman will bear the consequences of her guilt."

### Chapter 6

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**2** "Speak to the Israelites and tell them: When a man or a woman makes a special vow, the vow of a Nazirite \[H-NAZIR\], to set himself apart for the LORD,

**3** he is to keep away from wine and strong drink. He is not to drink vinegar made from wine or strong drink, and he is not to drink any grape juice or eat fresh or dried grapes.

**4** All the days of his separation he is not to eat anything that is produced by the grapevine, from seeds to skins.

**5** All the days of his vow of separation no razor is to pass over his head. Until the days are completed for which he separates himself to the LORD, he is to be holy \[H-QADOSH\]. He is to let the locks of the hair of his head grow long.

**6** All the days that he separates himself to the LORD he is not to go near a dead body.

**7** Even for his father or for his mother, for his brother or for his sister, he is not to make himself unclean if they die, because the sign of his separation to God is on his head.

**8** All the days of his separation he is holy to the LORD.

**9** If someone dies very suddenly beside him and he defiles his consecrated head, he is to shave his head on the day of his cleansing. He is to shave it on the seventh day.

**10** On the eighth day he is to bring two turtledoves or two young pigeons to the priest, to the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**11** The priest is to offer one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering, and make atonement for him because of the guilt he incurred through the dead body. That same day he is to consecrate his head again.

**12** He is to separate himself to the LORD for the days of his separation and bring a year-old male lamb as a guilt offering. The earlier days are lost, because his separation was defiled.

**13** This is the teaching for the Nazirite when the days of his separation are completed: he is to be brought to the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**14** He is to present his offering to the LORD, one year-old male lamb without blemish as a burnt offering, one year-old ewe lamb without blemish as a sin offering, one ram without blemish as a peace offering,

**15** and a basket of unleavened bread, loaves of fine flour mixed with oil and unleavened wafers spread with oil, with their grain offering and their drink offerings.

**16** The priest is to present them before the LORD and offer his sin offering and his burnt offering.

**17** He is to offer the ram as a sacrifice of peace offerings to the LORD, with the basket of unleavened bread. The priest is also to offer its grain offering and its drink offering.

**18** Then the Nazirite is to shave his consecrated head at the entrance of the tent of meeting, and take the hair from his consecrated head and put it on the fire that is under the sacrifice of the peace offerings.

**19** The priest is to take the boiled shoulder of the ram, and one unleavened loaf from the basket and one unleavened wafer, and put them in the hands of the Nazirite, after he has shaved his consecrated head.

**20** The priest is to lift them up as an elevation offering before the LORD. They are holy for the priest, together with the breast that is lifted up and the thigh that is contributed. After that the Nazirite may drink wine.

**12** Do not let her be like one born dead, whose flesh is half eaten away when it comes out of its mother's womb."

**13** Moses cried out to the LORD, "O God, please heal her."

**14** The LORD said to Moses, "If her father had spat in her face, would she not be shamed for seven days? Let her be shut outside the camp for seven days, and after that she may be brought in again."

**15** So Miriam was shut outside the camp for seven days, and the people did not set out until Miriam was brought in again.

**16** After that the people set out from Hazeroth and camped in the wilderness of Paran.

### Chapter 13

**2** "Send men to scout out the land of Canaan, which I am about to give to the Israelites. Send one man from each ancestral tribe, each of them a chief among his people."

**3** Moses sent them out from the wilderness of Paran as the LORD directed. All of them were heads of the Israelites.

**4** These were their names: from the tribe of Reuben, Shammua son of Zaccur;

**5** from the tribe of Simeon, Shaphat son of Hori;

**6** from the tribe of Judah, Caleb son of Jephunneh;

**7** from the tribe of Issachar, Igal son of Joseph;

**8** from the tribe of Ephraim, Hoshea son of Nun;

**9** from the tribe of Benjamin, Palti son of Raphu;

**10** from the tribe of Zebulun, Gaddiel son of Sodi;

**11** from the tribe of Joseph, that is, the tribe of Manasseh, Gaddi son of Susi;

**12** from the tribe of Dan, Ammiel son of Gemalli;

**13** from the tribe of Asher, Sethur son of Michael;

**14** from the tribe of Naphtali, Nahbi son of Vophsi;

**15** from the tribe of Gad, Geuel son of Machi.

**18** Look at the land: what is it like? And at the people living there: are they strong or weak, few or many?

**26** They came to Moses, Aaron and the whole community of Israel at Kadesh in the wilderness of Paran. They gave their report to them and to the whole assembly, and showed them the produce of the land.

**33** We even saw the Nephilim there (the descendants of Anak are part of the Nephilim). Next to them we looked like grasshoppers to ourselves, and that is how we looked to them."

### Chapter 14

They have heard that you, LORD, are in the midst of these people, that you, LORD, have shown yourself face to face, that your cloud stands over them, and that you go ahead of them in a pillar of cloud by day and a pillar of fire by night.

**23** will see the land I promised by oath to their ancestors. None who have spurned me will see it.

**24** But my servant Caleb, because he has a different spirit and has followed me wholeheartedly, I will bring into the land he entered, and his descendants will possess it.

**39** When Moses told these words to all the Israelites, the people mourned deeply.

**42** Do not go up, for the LORD is not in your midst, and you will be struck down by your enemies.

**43** The Amalekites and the Canaanites are there in front of you, and you will fall by the sword. Because you have turned away from the LORD, the LORD will not be with you."

**44** But in their arrogance they went up towards the heights of the hill country, though neither the ark of the LORD's covenant nor Moses left the camp.

**45** The Amalekites and the Canaanites who lived in that hill country came down, struck them down and chased them all the way to Hormah.

## Numbers 15-19: Laws, Rebellion and Priesthood

### Chapter 15

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**2** "Say to the Israelites: Once you have entered the land that I am giving you as your home,

**3** you will offer to the LORD an offering made by fire. It may be a burnt offering or some other sacrifice, to fulfil a special vow, or as a gift freely given, or at one of your festivals, to send up a pleasing aroma to the LORD from your herd or flock.

**4** Whoever presents his gift is to bring to the LORD, as a grain offering, a tenth of an ephah of fine flour blended with a quarter of a hin of oil,

**5** and a quarter of a hin of wine as a drink offering. Prepare this with each lamb, whether for the burnt offering or the sacrifice.

**6** With a ram, prepare as a grain offering two tenths of an ephah of fine flour blended with a third of a hin of oil,

**7** and a third of a hin of wine as a drink offering, to be offered as a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**8** If you prepare a young bull as a burnt offering or sacrifice, to fulfil a special vow or as a peace offering to the LORD,

**9** bring with the bull a grain offering of three tenths of an ephah of fine flour blended with half a hin of oil,

**10** and half a hin of wine as a drink offering. This is a food offering, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**11** Follow this for every ox, every ram, and every lamb or kid.

**12** Whatever the number you prepare, do it in this way for each one.

**13** Every native-born Israelite is to follow this pattern whenever he presents a food offering as a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**14** Should a foreigner be living among you, or anyone else settled in your midst in generations to come, and he wishes to present a food offering as a pleasing aroma to the LORD, he is to do what you do.

**15** There is to be one rule for the assembly, for you and for the foreigner living among you, a lasting rule through your generations. Before the LORD, you and the foreigner stand alike.

**16** One teaching and one ruling will hold for you and for the foreigner who lives with you."

**17** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**18** "Say to the Israelites: When you come into the land where I am taking you,

**19** and you eat the bread of that land, you are to set aside a contribution for the LORD.

**20** Of the first of your coarse meal, set aside a loaf as a contribution. Present it as you would present a contribution from the threshing floor.

**21** From the first of your coarse meal you are to give a contribution to the LORD, generation after generation.

**22** Now suppose you go astray without meaning to, and fail to keep any one of these commands that the LORD gave Moses,

**23** anything at all that the LORD commanded you through Moses, from the day the LORD gave the command and on through your generations.

**24** If this was done unintentionally, and the community did not know, then the whole community is to present one young bull as a burnt offering, a pleasing aroma to the LORD, with its grain offering and drink offering as the rule requires, and one male goat as a sin offering.

**25** The priest is to make atonement for the whole community of the Israelites, and they will be forgiven, since it was unintentional, and they brought their gift, a food offering to the LORD, and their sin offering before the LORD, for their mistake.

**26** The whole community of the Israelites will be forgiven, and the foreigner living among them as well, since the entire people were involved in the mistake.

**27** If it is one person who goes astray without meaning to, he is to bring a female goat in her first year as a sin offering.

**28** The priest is to make atonement before the LORD for the person who went astray, who erred unintentionally, so as to make atonement for him, and he will be forgiven.

**29** There is to be one teaching for the one who acts unintentionally, whether he is native-born among the Israelites or a foreigner living among them.

**30** But the person who acts defiantly, native-born or foreigner, insults the LORD. That person is to be cut off from his people.

**31** He has despised the LORD's word and broken his command. That person is to be cut off completely. His guilt remains on him."

**32** While the Israelites were in the wilderness, they found a man gathering sticks on the Sabbath day.

**33** Those who found him gathering sticks brought him to Moses, Aaron and the whole community.

**34** They held him in custody, because it had not been spelled out what was to be done to him.

**35** The LORD said to Moses, "The man is certainly to die. The whole community is to stone him outside the camp."

**36** The whole community led him outside the camp and stoned him, and he died, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**37** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**38** "Say to the Israelites: Through all your generations you are to make tassels for the corners of your garments, and attach to each corner tassel a cord of blue.

**39** It will be your tassel, and when you look at it you will remember all the commands of the LORD and do them, and you will not wander after the desires of your own hearts and eyes, which draw you into unfaithfulness.

**40** So you will remember and do all my commands, and you will be holy to your God.

**41** I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt to be your God. I am the LORD your God."

### Chapter 16

**1** Korah son of Izhar, son of Kohath, son of Levi, took his stand along with Dathan and Abiram, sons of Eliab, and On son of Peleth, descendants of Reuben.

**2** They rose up against Moses together with 250 Israelites, community leaders, men called to the assembly, men of renown.

**3** They gathered against Moses and Aaron and told them, "You have taken too much on yourselves! The entire community, every one of them, is holy \[H-QADOSH\], and the LORD is in their midst. Why do you set yourselves above the LORD's assembly?"

**4** When Moses heard this, he threw himself on his face.

**5** He said to Korah and his whole company, "In the morning the LORD will make known who is his, and who is the holy one he will bring near to himself. Whomever he chooses, he will bring near.

**6** Do this: Korah, and all your company, take censers,

**7** put fire in them tomorrow and lay incense on the fire before the LORD. The man the LORD chooses is the one who is holy. It is you who have taken too much, sons of Levi!"

**8** Moses said to Korah, "Listen now, sons of Levi.

**9** Is it too small a thing for you that the God of Israel has set you apart from the rest of the community of Israel, to bring you close to himself, to serve at the LORD's tabernacle and stand before the community to minister to them?

**10** He has brought you close, you and all your fellow Levites with you, and now you are reaching for the priesthood as well!

**11** It is against the LORD that you and all your company have gathered. As for Aaron, who is he that you should murmur against him?"

**12** Moses sent for Dathan and Abiram, the sons of Eliab. They answered, "We will not come up.

**13** Is it not enough that you have brought us up from a land flowing with milk and honey to kill us in the wilderness, and now you want to make yourself ruler over us as well?

**14** You certainly have not brought us to a land flowing with milk and honey, nor given us fields and vineyards to inherit. Do you think you can put out the eyes of these men? We will not come up."

**15** Moses was furious. He said to the LORD, "Do not look favourably on their offering. I have not taken so much as one donkey from them, nor have I harmed any of them."

**16** Moses said to Korah, "You and your whole company are to present yourselves before the LORD tomorrow, you, they and Aaron.

**17** Each man is to take his censer and put incense on it, and bring his censer before the LORD, 250 censers. You and Aaron are to bring your censers as well."

**18** Each man took his censer, put fire in it, laid incense on it and stood at the entrance of the tent of meeting, with Moses and Aaron.

**19** Korah had gathered the whole community against them at the entrance of the tent of meeting. Then the glory of the LORD appeared to the whole community.

**20** The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron:

**21** "Stand apart from this community, and I will finish them in an instant."

**22** They fell on their faces and said, "God, you who give breath to every living creature, will you be furious with the whole community when only one man has sinned?"

**23** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**24** "Tell the community: Move away from the tents of Korah, Dathan and Abiram."

**25** Moses got up and went to Dathan and Abiram, with the elders of Israel following him.

**26** He told the community, "Move away from the tents of these wicked men and touch nothing that belongs to them, or you will be swept away in all their sins."

**27** So they moved away from the tents of Korah, Dathan and Abiram. Dathan and Abiram came out and stood at the entrance of their tents, with their wives, their sons and their small children.

**28** Moses said, "This is how you will know that the LORD sent me to do all these things, and that I did not act on my own:

**29** If these men die as all people die, and share the fate of all humankind, then the LORD has not sent me.

**30** But if the LORD does something never done before, and the ground opens its mouth and swallows them and everything they have, and they go down alive into Sheol \[H-SHEOL\], then you will know that these men have scorned the LORD."

**31** As soon as he had finished saying all this, the ground beneath them split open.

**32** The earth opened its mouth and swallowed them, with their households, all Korah's people and all their property.

**33** They went down alive into Sheol with everything they had. The earth closed over them, and they vanished from the assembly.

**34** All the Israelites around them fled at their screams, crying, "The earth will swallow us too!"

**35** And fire came out from the LORD and consumed the 250 men who were offering the incense.

**36** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**37** "Tell Eleazar son of Aaron, the priest, to lift the censers out of the blaze and scatter the coals far off, for the censers have become holy,

**38** the censers of these men who sinned at the cost of their own lives. Let them be beaten into thin plates to cover the altar, since they were brought before the LORD and so are holy. They will be a sign to the Israelites."

**39** So Eleazar the priest took the bronze censers that the men who were burned had brought, and they were beaten into plates to cover the altar,

**40** as a reminder to the Israelites that no outsider, no one who is not a descendant of Aaron, may come near to burn incense before the LORD, so that he will not end up like Korah and his company, as the LORD had told him through Moses.

**41** The next day the whole community of the Israelites murmured against Moses and Aaron: "You have killed the LORD's people!"

**42** As the community gathered against Moses and Aaron and turned towards the tent of meeting, the cloud suddenly covered it, and the glory of the LORD appeared.

**43** Moses and Aaron came to the front of the tent of meeting,

**44** and the LORD spoke to Moses:

**45** "Stand apart from this community, and I will finish them in an instant." They fell on their faces.

**46** Moses said to Aaron, "Take your censer, put fire from the altar on it, lay incense on it and take it quickly to the community to make atonement for them. Wrath has gone out from the LORD. The plague has begun."

**47** Aaron took the censer as Moses said and ran into the middle of the assembly. The plague had already begun among the people. He laid on the incense and made atonement for them.

**48** He stood between the dead and the living, and the plague stopped.

**49** The dead from the plague numbered 14,700, besides those who died in the affair of Korah.

**50** Aaron went back to Moses at the entrance of the tent of meeting, for the plague had stopped.

### Chapter 17

**2** "Speak to the Israelites and collect a staff from each of them, one for each ancestral house, twelve staffs from all their leaders. Write each man's name on his staff.

### Chapter 18

**1** The LORD said to Aaron, "You and your sons and your ancestral house with you are to carry the guilt connected with the sanctuary, and you and your sons alone are to carry the guilt connected with your priesthood.

**2** Bring with you also your relatives from the tribe of Levi, your ancestral tribe, so that they may join you and serve you, while you and your sons are before the tent of the Testimony.

**3** They are to carry out your duties and the duties of the whole tent, but they must not come near the furnishings of the sanctuary or the altar, or they and you will both die.

**4** They are to join you and keep charge of the tent of meeting, for all the work of the tent, and no outsider may come near you.

**5** You are to keep charge of the sanctuary and of the altar, so that wrath will not fall on the Israelites again.

**6** I myself have taken your fellow Levites from among the Israelites as a gift to you, given to the LORD to do the work of the tent of meeting.

**7** But you and your sons are to attend to your priesthood in everything that concerns the altar and what is behind the curtain. You are to do the work. I give you your priesthood as a gift for service, and any outsider who comes near is to be put to death."

**8** The LORD said to Aaron, "I myself have given you charge of the contributions made to me. All the holy gifts of the Israelites I give to you and to your sons as your portion, a permanent share.

**9** This is to be yours from the most holy gifts, kept back from the fire: every offering of theirs that they bring to me as most holy, whether grain offering, sin offering or guilt offering, will be yours and your sons'.

**10** You are to eat it as most holy. Every male may eat it. It is to be holy to you.

**11** This also is yours: the contribution of their gift, all the elevation offerings of the Israelites. I give them to you, and to your sons and daughters with you, as a permanent share. Everyone in your household who is clean may eat it.

**12** All the best of the oil, and all the best of the new wine and grain, the first produce that they give to the LORD, I give to you.

**13** The first ripe fruits of all that is in their land, which they bring to the LORD, will be yours. Everyone in your household who is clean may eat it.

**14** Everything devoted \[H-CHEREM\] in Israel will be yours.

**15** Everything that opens the womb, of all flesh, that they present to the LORD, whether human or animal, will be yours. But you are to redeem the firstborn of humans, and the firstborn of unclean animals you are to redeem.

**16** Their redemption price, from a month old, you are to set at five shekels of silver, by the shekel of the sanctuary, which is twenty gerahs.

**17** But the firstborn of an ox, a sheep or a goat you are not to redeem. They are holy. You are to splash their blood on the altar and burn their fat as a food offering, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**18** Their flesh will be yours, just as the breast of the elevation offering and the right thigh are yours.

**19** All the contributions from the holy gifts that the Israelites set aside for the LORD I give to you, and to your sons and daughters with you, as a permanent share. It is a covenant \[H-BERIT\] of salt for ever before the LORD, for you and for your descendants with you."

**20** The LORD said to Aaron, "You are to have no inheritance in their land, and no portion among them. I am your portion and your inheritance among the Israelites.

**21** To the Levites I have given every tithe in Israel as an inheritance, in return for the work that they do, the work of the tent of meeting.

**22** From now on the Israelites must not come near the tent of meeting, or they will bear the consequences of their sin and die.

**23** It is the Levites who are to do the work of the tent of meeting, and they will carry the consequences of their guilt. This is a permanent rule through your generations. Among the Israelites they will have no inheritance.

**24** For the tithe of the Israelites, which they present as a contribution to the LORD, I have given to the Levites as an inheritance. That is why I have said of them that they are to have no inheritance among the Israelites."

**25** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**26** "Speak to the Levites and tell them: When you receive from the Israelites the tithe that I have given you from them as your inheritance, you are to present a contribution from it to the LORD, a tithe of the tithe.

**27** Your contribution will be counted for you as though it were grain from the threshing floor or the full yield of the winepress.

**28** In this way you too are to present a contribution to the LORD from all your tithes that you receive from the Israelites. From it you are to give the LORD's contribution to Aaron the priest.

**29** From all that is given to you, you are to present every contribution due to the LORD, from all the best of it, the part that is holy.

**30** Tell them: When you have presented the best of it, it will be counted for the Levites as the yield of the threshing floor and the yield of the winepress.

**31** You may eat it anywhere, you and your households, for it is your wages for your work in the tent of meeting.

**32** You will bring no guilt on yourselves by this, when you have presented the best of it. You will not profane the holy gifts of the Israelites, and you will not die."

### Chapter 19

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron:

**2** "This is the statute of the teaching that the LORD has commanded: Tell the Israelites to bring you a red heifer, one without defect, with no blemish, which has never carried a yoke.

**3** You are to give it to Eleazar the priest. It is to be taken outside the camp and slaughtered in front of him.

**4** Eleazar the priest is to take some of its blood on his finger and sprinkle it seven times towards the front of the tent of meeting.

**5** The heifer is to be burned in his sight: its hide, its flesh and its blood, with its dung, are to be burned.

**6** The priest is to take cedar wood, hyssop and crimson yarn and throw them into the fire where the heifer is burning.

**7** Then the priest is to wash his clothes and bathe his body in water, and afterwards he may come into the camp. The priest will be unclean \[H-TAMEH\] until evening.

**8** The one who burns it is also to wash his clothes in water and bathe his body in water. He will be unclean until evening.

**9** A man who is clean is to gather up the ashes of the heifer and place them outside the camp in a clean place. They are to be kept for the community of the Israelites for use in the water for impurity. It is a sin offering \[H-CHATTAT\].

**10** The one who gathers up the ashes of the heifer is to wash his clothes, and he will be unclean until evening. This is to be a permanent rule for the Israelites and for the foreigner who lives among them.

**11** Whoever touches the dead body of any person will be unclean for seven days.

**12** He is to cleanse himself with the water on the third day and on the seventh day, and then he will be clean. But if he does not cleanse himself on the third day and on the seventh day, he will not be clean.

**13** Whoever touches a dead body, the body of anyone who has died, and does not cleanse himself, defiles the tabernacle of the LORD, and that person will be cut off from Israel. Because the water for impurity was not thrown over him, he is unclean. His uncleanness is still on him.

**14** This is the teaching when someone dies in a tent: everyone who comes into the tent and everyone who is in the tent will be unclean for seven days.

**15** Every open vessel that has no cover tied on it will be unclean.

**16** Anyone in the open field who touches someone killed by the sword, or a body that has died naturally, or a human bone, or a grave, will be unclean for seven days.

**17** For the unclean person they are to take some of the ashes of the burnt sin offering and put fresh water over them in a vessel.

**18** A clean person is to take hyssop, dip it in the water and sprinkle it on the tent, on all the furnishings, on the persons who were there, and on the one who touched the bone, or the person killed, or the one who died naturally, or the grave.

**19** The clean person is to sprinkle it on the unclean on the third day and on the seventh day. On the seventh day he is to cleanse him. The person is to wash his clothes and bathe in water, and in the evening he will be clean.

**20** But the man who is unclean and does not cleanse himself, that person will be cut off from the midst of the assembly, because he has defiled the sanctuary of the LORD. The water for impurity has not been thrown over him. He is unclean.

**21** It is to be a permanent rule for them. The one who sprinkles the water for impurity is to wash his clothes, and whoever touches the water for impurity will be unclean until evening.

**22** Whatever the unclean person touches will be unclean, and anyone who touches it will be unclean until evening."

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **1:1** The English name "Numbers" comes from the two censuses (chapters 1 and 26). The Hebrew name is *Bemidbar*, "in the wilderness", the fifth word of the book.
- **1:2** The census counts the men able to fight and also sets the order of the camp. "Counting by name" means each man is listed individually.
- **1:3** "Company by company" is Hebrew *tsiva'ot*, military divisions.
- **1:5** The twelve leaders are named in 1:5-15 and again in 2:3-29, in 7:12-83 and in 10:14-27.
- **1:16** "Called from the community" means chosen from the assembly.
- **1:20** Reuben is called Israel's firstborn, and is listed first, though Judah leads the march (2:9).
- **1:21** The Hebrew word often rendered "thousand" (*elef*) can also mean a clan or military unit. The numbers are reproduced as in the Hebrew text. Whether they are meant literally is debated. The total of 603,550 matches Exodus 38:26.
- **1:46** The total counts only men over twenty who could go to war. It does not include women, children or Levites.
- **1:47** The Levites are not counted for war. They are counted separately in chapters 3 and 4.
- **1:50** "The tabernacle of the Testimony" is Hebrew *mishkan ha-edut*, the dwelling place of the tablets.
- **1:51** "Outsider" is Hebrew *zar*, someone who is not a Levite or priest.
- **1:53** "Wrath" shows that the Levites' camp round the tabernacle protects the people from the danger of the holy.
- **2:2** "Standard" is Hebrew *degel* and "banner" is *ot*, both signs for tribal groups. The camp is laid out round the tent of meeting, with three tribes on each side.
- **2:9** Judah leads the march, as the tribe of the east side.
- **2:17** The tent of meeting and the Levites set out in the middle, so that the holy stays at the centre of the people.
- **2:31** Dan, on the north side, marches last.
- **2:32** The total is the same as in 1:46.
- **3:1** "Generations" is Hebrew *toledot*, the word used for the family accounts in Genesis. It introduces the family of Aaron and Moses.
- **3:4** The deaths of Nadab and Abihu are told in Leviticus 10:1-2. They died without sons, so only Eleazar and Ithamar continue the priestly line.
- **3:6** The Levites serve the priests and the community, but they are not priests themselves.
- **3:10** "Outsider" is Hebrew *zar*, someone who is not a priest, and the same word is used in 1:51.
- **3:12** The Levites are taken in place of the firstborn of Israel. See Exodus 13:1-2, where every firstborn belongs to the LORD.
- **3:13** The firstborn belong to the LORD from the day of the tenth plague, when the firstborn of Egypt died and Israel's were spared.
- **3:15** The Levites are counted from one month old and upwards, to match the firstborn. For service, the age is thirty to fifty (4:3).
- **3:17** The three sons of Levi give the three Levite clans, Gershonites, Kohathites and Merarites.
- **3:22** The numbers of the clans (7,500; 8,600; 6,200) add up to 22,300, but verse 39 gives a total of 22,000. This is a known difficulty in the Hebrew text. Some have suggested that the Kohathite number should be 8,300, but no solution is certain.
- **3:38** Moses, Aaron and Aaron's sons camp on the east, in front of the entrance. They guard the tabernacle for the Israelites.
- **3:43** The firstborn total of 22,273 is 273 more than the Levite total of 22,000.
- **3:46** "Redemption" is Hebrew *padah*, to ransom or set free by payment. See entry H-PADAH.
- **3:47** Five shekels is the standard price for redeeming a firstborn son (see 18:16).
- **3:50** The figure of 1,365 shekels is 273 times five shekels.
- **4:3** The service age is thirty to fifty. In 8:24 the age of starting is given as twenty-five, which may mean a period of training.
- **4:4** "The most holy things" are the ark, the table, the lampstand, the altars and their utensils.
- **4:6** "Fine leather" renders the uncertain Hebrew word *tachash*, as in Exodus 25:5.
- **4:7** "The table of the Presence" is the table of the bread of the Presence. "The regular bread" means the bread always kept on it.
- **4:15** The Kohathites must not touch the holy things, or they will die. Aaron's sons must first cover them.
- **4:16** Eleazar has oversight of the oil, the incense, the regular grain offering and the anointing oil.
- **4:18** The command not to let the Kohathites be "cut off" means the priests must protect them from the danger of the holy.
- **4:20** "Even for a moment" means that even a brief look at the uncovered holy things is dangerous.
- **4:28** Ithamar, Aaron's younger son, directs the Gershonites and Merarites.
- **4:48** The total of 8,580 is correct: 2,750 plus 2,630 plus 3,200.
- **5:2** The people sent outside the camp are those with a skin disease, a discharge or uncleanness from a dead body. See Leviticus 13 to 15.
- **5:3** "Where I live in their midst" gives the reason: the camp must be kept free of uncleanness because God lives there.
- **5:6** "Acting unfaithfully" is Hebrew *ma'al*, a breach of trust. Wronging another person is treated as unfaithfulness towards the LORD.
- **5:7** Adding a fifth repeats the rule in Leviticus 6:5.
- **5:8** "A close relative" is the *go'el*, the person with the duty to act for the wronged one (see Leviticus 25:25).
- **5:11** The ritual in 5:11-31 is an ordeal for a wife whose husband suspects unfaithfulness without proof. It is traditionally called the *sotah* ritual, from the Hebrew word for "goes astray". Its language is difficult and its meaning is debated.
- **5:14** "A spirit of jealousy" is Hebrew *ruach qin'ah*, a sudden feeling that comes over the husband.
- **5:15** The offering is barley flour, the cheapest grain, with no oil or frankincense, because it brings guilt to remembrance and is not a joyful gift.
- **5:17** "Holy water" is water taken from the sanctuary. The dust from the tabernacle floor is added to make it bitter.
- **5:18** "Uncover her head" may mean loosening her hair. It marks her as one under examination.
- **5:21** "Thigh" and "belly" are probably euphemisms for the reproductive organs. The exact effect is uncertain. The wife is to be a curse and an oath, that is, an example used in curses.
- **5:22** "Amen" is the Hebrew word for "truly", showing that she accepts the oath.
- **5:23** Writing the curses on a scroll and washing the ink into the water makes the words part of the drink.
- **5:28** If she is innocent, she will be cleared and able to have children. The outcome is left entirely to God.
- **5:31** The ritual puts the test on the wife alone. No parallel ritual is given for a wife who suspects her husband, and the passage is unique in the Torah.
- **6:2** "Nazirite" is Hebrew *nazir*, "one set apart" or "consecrated". Both men and women could take the vow. See entry H-NAZIR.
- **6:3** The ban on grape products goes beyond wine, to everything that comes from the vine.
- **6:5** The uncut hair is the visible sign of the vow. It is called "his consecrated head" in verses 9, 18 and 19.
- **6:7** The Nazirite avoids contact with the dead, even for close relatives. This is a rule like that for the high priest (Leviticus 21:11).
- **6:9** If someone dies suddenly beside him, the Nazirite is made unclean and must shave his head and start again.
- **6:12** "The earlier days are lost" means the time already served does not count.
- **6:18** The hair is burned on the fire under the peace offering, giving it back to God.
- **6:20** After the vow is completed, the Nazirite may drink wine again.
- **6:24-26** The priestly blessing has three lines, which grow in length (three, five and seven words in Hebrew). "Bless" is Hebrew *barak* (see entry H-BARAK). "Peace" is Hebrew *shalom*, which means wholeness and well-being (see entry H-SHALOM). A version of the blessing is written on two small silver scrolls found at Ketef Hinnom in Jerusalem, dated to the seventh or sixth century BC.
- **6:27** "Put my name on the Israelites" means the blessing marks the people as belonging to the LORD.
- **7:1** The events of chapter 7 happened on the day the tabernacle was set up (Exodus 40:17), about a month before the census in 1:1. The book groups them by subject here, not by date.
- **7:3** "Covered carts" is Hebrew *eglot tsav*, a term for wagons with covers. They were used to carry the tabernacle's heavier parts.
- **7:9** The Kohathites receive no carts because they carry the holy things on their shoulders (4:15).
- **7:12** The leaders present their offerings on twelve days, one tribe a day, in the order of the camp's march (see 2:3-29).
- **7:13** A shekel is about 11 grams, so the silver plate weighs about 1.4 kilograms and the bowl about 0.8 kilograms.
- **7:84** The totals check: twelve plates at 130 shekels and twelve bowls at 70 shekels make 2,400 shekels. Twelve gold dishes at ten shekels make 120 shekels.
- **7:89** "Spoke to him" uses a Hebrew form that can also be read as "speaking with himself". The voice comes from above the cover of atonement between the cherubim, as in Exodus 25:22.
- **8:2** The lamps stand in front of the lampstand so that they light the table and the space before it.
- **8:7** "The water of purification" is literally "water of sin", water used to cleanse from impurity. Shaving the whole body marks a new start.
- **8:11** The Levites are presented as an "elevation offering", that is, offered as a gift from Israel to the LORD.
- **8:16** "Given, wholly given" is Hebrew *netunim netunim*, a doubled form that stresses that the Levites are the LORD's own gift.
- **8:19** "Plague" refers to the danger of approaching the sanctuary. The Levites stand between the people and the holy.
- **8:24** The service age is twenty-five here, but thirty in 4:3. One explanation is that the Levites began at twenty-five as trainees and served fully from thirty.
- **8:26** The retired Levites may still help, but they do not carry or do the heavy work.
- **9:1** The Passover of chapter 9 was in the first month of the second year, before the census of 1:1. Again, the book orders the events by subject.
- **9:6** The men are unclean because of a dead body. Chapter 9 does not say who they were. See Leviticus 10:4-5 for the possible link.
- **9:11** The second Passover in the second month is an allowance for those who are unclean or travelling. It is also known by its Hebrew name *Pesach Sheni*.
- **9:12** The rule not to break any bone is also in Exodus 12:46. See John 19:36.
- **9:14** The foreigner may keep the Passover under the same rule as the native-born.
- **9:15** The cloud and fire guide and show God's presence. See Exodus 13:21-22 and 40:34-38.
- **9:19** "Kept the charge of the LORD" means they obeyed his instruction to stay, even when waiting was long.
- **10:2** The silver trumpets (Hebrew *chatsotserot*) are straight metal trumpets, different from the ram's horn.
- **10:5** "Alarm" is Hebrew *teru'ah*, a series of short, broken blasts. It differs from the plain, long blast used to gather the assembly.
- **10:10** "The beginnings of your months" means the new moons.
- **10:11** The twentieth day of the second month is nineteen days after the census began in 1:1. Israel has now been at Sinai for about a year.
- **10:12** The wilderness of Paran lies north of Sinai, on the way to Canaan.
- **10:14** The order of march follows the camp order in chapter 2, but the Gershonites and Merarites go after Judah, and the Kohathites after Reuben, so the tabernacle is set up before the holy things arrive.
- **10:29** Hobab is Moses' brother-in-law or father-in-law. The Hebrew word *chotan* can mean either, and Reuel is another name for Jethro (Exodus 2:18). Judges 4:11 calls Hobab Moses' father-in-law.
- **10:31** "You will be our eyes" means Hobab will act as a guide, and not an observer.
- **10:33** The ark goes ahead of the people to find a resting place. In 2:17 it travels in the middle with the Levites.
- **10:35** The two short poems in verses 35 and 36 are very old. In some medieval Hebrew manuscripts they are marked off by a backwards letter *nun*. A related line appears in Psalm 68:1.
- **10:36** "Ten thousand thousands" is Hebrew *revavot alfe*, a figure for a countless host.
- **11:1** "Taberah" in verse 3 means "burning". The fire consumes the edge of the camp, the part farthest from the tabernacle.
- **11:4** "The mixed crowd" is Hebrew *asafsuf*, a word for hangers-on. It probably refers to the non-Israelites who left Egypt with Israel (Exodus 12:38).
- **11:5** "For nothing" means without cost. The people remember the food of Egypt and forget the slavery.
- **11:7** "Resin" renders the Hebrew *bedolach*, which may be a gum resin, or a pale pearl-like substance. See also Genesis 2:12.
- **11:8** "Cakes baked with oil" translates a difficult phrase. In Exodus 16:31 the manna tastes like wafers with honey.
- **11:12** Moses uses the language of pregnancy and nursing to protest that the people are not his own children.
- **11:17** "Spirit" is Hebrew *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH. The same spirit that is on Moses is shared with the seventy.
- **11:25** "They prophesied, but they did not do it again" can also be read "they did not cease". The Hebrew form is debated.
- **11:26** Eldad and Medad receive the spirit in the camp, outside the formal gathering.
- **11:29** Moses' wish that all the LORD's people were prophets looks forward to Joel 2:28-29.
- **11:31** The quail are carried from the sea by a wind. "Two cubits" is about 90 centimetres. A "day's journey" is the distance a person could walk in a day.
- **11:32** A homer is about 220 litres, so ten homers is about 2,200 litres.
- **11:34** "Kibroth-hattaavah" means "graves of craving".
- **12:1** "Cushite" means from Cush, a region south of Egypt (Nubia or Ethiopia). The woman is not named. Some think she is Zipporah, who is a Midianite, since Cush and Midian may have been linked. Others think she is a second wife.
- **12:3** "Humble" is Hebrew *anav*. It can also mean "poor, afflicted" or "patient under suffering". The verse is probably a later note.
- **12:6** The LORD's reply is in poetry. It sets Moses apart from other prophets, who receive visions and dreams.
- **12:8** "Mouth to mouth" means direct speech, without a go-between. "The form of the LORD" is Hebrew *temunah*, a likeness or appearance.
- **12:10** "Skin disease" renders *tsara'at*. See Leviticus 13 and entry H-TSARAAT. Only Miriam is struck, though Aaron joined in the complaint.
- **12:12** The picture of a stillborn child with decaying flesh shows Aaron's horror.
- **12:14** Spitting in the face is a sign of deep shame. The seven days match the time of isolation in Leviticus 13.
- **12:15** The whole camp waits for Miriam. The people's delay shows her standing.
- **13:2** "Canaan" is the land west of the Jordan promised to Israel.
- **13:6** Caleb is of Judah, and Joshua of Ephraim, the two leading tribes of the south and of the north.
- **13:8** "Hoshea" means "salvation". Moses renames him Joshua (*Yehoshua*, "the LORD saves"), as in verse 16.
- **13:21** Rehob and Lebo-hamath mark the far north of the land. Zin lies in the south.
- **13:22** Hebron is one of the oldest towns in the land. Zoan is the Egyptian city Tanis in the Nile delta. The sons of Anak are a people known for their size.
- **13:24** "Eshcol" means "cluster".
- **13:25** Forty days is a round period of testing or preparation.
- **13:30** Caleb quietens the people so that he can speak.
- **13:32** "A bad report" is Hebrew *dibbah*, a slander or discouraging account. "Eats up its own people" is a figure for a land that is harsh or hostile.
- **13:33** "The Nephilim" is the name used in Genesis 6:4 for giant figures. The report says they saw the sons of Anak, who were said to come from them.
- **14:2** "Murmured" renders the Hebrew word used throughout the wilderness stories for grumbling against the LORD or his servants.
- **14:9** "They are no more than food for us" is literally "they are our bread". It means they can be consumed or overcome. "Their shelter" is literally "their shade", their protection.
- **14:14** "Face to face" repeats the language of Exodus 33:11.
- **14:18** Moses quotes the LORD's self-description from Exodus 34:6-7. "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED.
- **14:21** "The whole earth is filled with the glory of the LORD" anticipates Isaiah 6:3.
- **14:22** "Ten times" is probably a round number for repeated testing.
- **14:24** "A different spirit" means Caleb has a different attitude, one of wholehearted trust.
- **14:29** "Corpses" is literally "carcasses" or "bodies", a blunt word for the judgement.
- **14:33** "Shepherds" is a figure for people who wander with their flocks, and shows the generation living as nomads.
- **14:34** "A year for each day" is the pattern of the forty years. "My opposition" is a rare Hebrew word, *tenu'ah*, whose sense is uncertain. It may mean "alienation".
- **14:44** "Arrogance" is Hebrew *'apal*, a rare word that means presumption or recklessness.
- **14:45** "Hormah" is a place in the south. Its name means "devotion to destruction".
- **15:2** After the sentence on the old generation, chapter 15 looks forward to life in the land: "when you enter". It gives rules for offerings, with a promise that the people will enter.
- **15:4** A tenth of an ephah is about 2.2 litres. A hin is about 3.7 litres, so a quarter of a hin is under one litre.
- **15:14** "Foreigner" is Hebrew *ger*, a resident alien. The same rule applies to the foreigner who offers, as in verses 15 and 16.
- **15:15** "You and the foreigner are to be alike before the LORD" makes the foreigner equal in worship.
- **15:20** "Ground meal" is Hebrew *arisah*. Its exact meaning is uncertain. It may be coarse meal, or dough.
- **15:22** "Without meaning to" is the Hebrew *shegagah*. See the note on Leviticus 4:2.
- **15:30** "Defiantly" is literally "with a high hand" (*beyad ramah*), a phrase for deliberate, open defiance. For this kind of offence no offering is provided.
- **15:32** The Sabbath-breaker is held in custody because the penalty for breaking the Sabbath by gathering wood had not yet been declared.
- **15:38** "Tassels" is Hebrew *tsitsit*. The blue cord is a thread of blue, a colour linked with heaven and with the covenant.
- **15:39** "Roaming" translates the verb used elsewhere for chasing after other gods or for unfaithfulness. See the note on Exodus 34:15.
- **16:1** The rebellion has two parts: Korah and the Levites who want the priesthood, and Dathan and Abiram of Reuben, who object to Moses' leadership. On son of Peleth is not mentioned again.
- **16:3** "Holy" is Hebrew *qadosh*. See entry H-QADOSH. The rebels draw on the phrase "a holy nation" in Exodus 19:6, but use it to oppose Moses and Aaron.
- **16:6** "Censers" are *machtot*, fire pans for burning incense.
- **16:13** Dathan and Abiram call Egypt "a land flowing with milk and honey", using the phrase that belongs to Canaan.
- **16:14** "Put out the eyes" means deceive or blind. It is a phrase for misleading someone.
- **16:22** "God of the spirits of all flesh" means the God who gives life to all living things.
- **16:30** "Creates something new" is Hebrew *beri'ah*, a word that elsewhere is used of creation. "Sheol" is the realm of the dead. See entry H-SHEOL.
- **16:32** Numbers 26:11 says that the sons of Korah did not die. They continued as a Levite family and are named in the titles of several psalms.
- **16:38** The censers become holy because they were presented to the LORD, even though the men were judged.
- **16:41** The people murmur again the next day. The incense of Aaron in verse 46 stops the plague.
- **16:48** Aaron stands "between the dead and the living", as a mediator. The word "atonement" in verse 46 here means rescue from judgement.
- **16:49** The number 14,700 is in addition to those who died with Korah.
- **17:1** In the Hebrew Bible this chapter begins at 17:16, and chapter 17 has 28 verses, so the English chapter 18 begins at Hebrew 17:28.
- **17:2** "Staff" is Hebrew *matteh*, which also means "tribe". The twelve staffs stand for the twelve tribes.
- **17:8** The almond (Hebrew *shaqed*) is the first tree to blossom in spring. Its name is connected with the verb "to watch".
- **17:10** The staff is kept before the Testimony as a sign. See Hebrews 9:4.
- **17:12** The people's cry shows their fear that anyone who comes near the holy will die.
- **18:1** "Carry the guilt" means the priests accept the responsibility for offences connected with the sanctuary.
- **18:3** The Levites must not come near the altar or the furnishings of the sanctuary, so that no one dies.
- **18:7** "A gift for service" means the priesthood is given as a task, not as a privilege.
- **18:9** The most holy gifts are those "kept back from the fire", the part not burned on the altar.
- **18:14** "Devoted" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. Here things that are devoted to the LORD pass to the priests.
- **18:16** The five-shekel redemption price repeats 3:47.
- **18:19** "A covenant of salt" means a permanent agreement. See Leviticus 2:13. See entry H-BERIT.
- **18:20** "I am your portion and your inheritance" is the phrase that sums up the position of the priests and Levites, who receive no land.
- **18:21** The tithe goes to the Levites as payment for their work.
- **18:26** "A tithe of the tithe" means the Levites give a tenth of what they receive to the priests.
- **18:31** The Levites may eat their share anywhere, because it is wages, not a holy gift.
- **19:2** "The statute of the teaching" is Hebrew *chuqqat ha-torah*, a rule whose reason is not given. The "red heifer" is *parah adumah*.
- **19:6** Cedar, hyssop and crimson yarn are also used in the cleansing from skin disease (Leviticus 14:4).
- **19:7** The priest and the others who prepare the ashes become unclean until evening, though the ashes cleanse others.
- **19:9** "The water for impurity" is Hebrew *mei niddah*. "Sin offering" is *chatta't*, here meaning an offering for cleansing. See entry H-CHATTAT.
- **19:11** Contact with a corpse is the strongest form of uncleanness. It lasts seven days.
- **19:13** Uncleanness of this kind defiles the tabernacle, if it is not dealt with.
- **19:15** An open vessel with no cover tied on is unclean because it is open to what is in the tent.
- **19:18** Hyssop is a small plant with bunched stems, used for sprinkling.
- **19:22** The uncleanness passes on by touch, one step at a time.
- **20:1** The first month probably refers to the fortieth year (see 33:38). The long years of wandering are passed over in silence. Miriam's death and burial at Kadesh are told in one line.
- **20:3** "When our kinsmen died" refers to those who died in the rebellion of Korah or in the plague.
- **20:8** "The staff" is the staff that was kept before the LORD (17:10), or Moses' own staff. The text does not say which.
- **20:10** "You rebels" is Hebrew *morim*, "those who are stubborn". Moses strikes the rock twice, when he had been told to speak to it. The exact nature of his failure is debated.
- **20:12** "Treat me as holy" is Hebrew *qadash*, to treat as holy by trusting and honouring. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **20:13** "Meribah" means "quarrel". See also Exodus 17:7.
- **20:14** Edom is called "your brother" because Edom descends from Esau, the brother of Jacob (Genesis 36).
- **20:17** "The King's Road" is Hebrew *derekh ha-melekh*, a main road through Transjordan.
- **20:22** The location of Mount Hor is uncertain. The mountain is on the border of Edom.
- **20:24** "Gathered to his people" is a Hebrew way of speaking of death.
- **20:26** Passing on the garments to Eleazar marks the transfer of the high priesthood.
- **20:29** Thirty days of mourning is the same period as for Moses (Deuteronomy 34:8).
- **21:1** "The road of Atharim" is uncertain. Some read it as "the road of the spies".
- **21:2** "Devote to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. The name Hormah is linked with *cherem*.
- **21:6** "Poisonous snakes" is literally "burning snakes" (*seraphim*), probably named for the burning effect of the venom.
- **21:9** The bronze snake is later called Nehushtan and was destroyed by Hezekiah (2 Kings 18:4). See John 3:14.
- **21:14** "The Book of the Wars of the LORD" is a lost collection of songs. The Hebrew of the quotation is badly preserved. "Waheb in Suphah" is uncertain, and may name a place.
- **21:17** The well song is a short work song. It is one of the oldest pieces of Hebrew poetry in the Bible.
- **21:18** "Sceptre" is Hebrew *mechoqeq*, a staff of office or a ruler's staff.
- **21:27** "Ballad-singers" is Hebrew *moshelim*, those who speak in *mashal*, a poem or proverb.
- **21:29** Chemosh is the god of Moab.
- **21:30** The Hebrew of this verse is very uncertain, and many scholars emend it. The meaning "We have shot them down" is tentative.
- **21:33** Og is the king of Bashan, remembered for his great size (Deuteronomy 3:11).
- **22:1** "The plains of Moab" is the area east of the Jordan, opposite Jericho. Israel camps here until the end of the book.
- **22:5** Balaam son of Beor is also known from an inscription found at Deir Alla in Jordan, dated about 800 BC. "The River" is the Euphrates.
- **22:7** "The fee for divination" is Hebrew *qesamim*, the payment for his work as a diviner.
- **22:20** God allows Balaam to go, but only if he says what God tells him. Verse 22 says that God's anger blazed "because he was going". The relation between the two verses is much discussed.
- **22:22** "Adversary" is Hebrew *satan*, a word that here means someone who opposes. See also verse 32.
- **22:28** The donkey's speech is told without comment. See 2 Peter 2:16.
- **22:32** "Reckless" translates a rare Hebrew word, *yarat*, whose meaning is uncertain. It may mean "steep" or "perverse".
- **22:41** "Bamoth-baal" means "the high places of Baal".
- **23:3** "A bare height" is Hebrew *shefi*, a bare hilltop.
- **23:7** "Poem" is Hebrew *mashal*, a poetic saying or oracle.
- **23:9** "A people that dwells alone" means a people set apart, not counted with the nations.
- **23:10** The Hebrew of "a quarter of Israel" is uncertain. It may mean a quarter, or the dust-cloud of Israel.
- **23:19** "God is not a man" says God is not changeable or deceitful. Other texts, such as Exodus 32:14, say that the LORD "changed his mind" in response to prayer. The two are held together in Scripture.
- **23:22** "The horns of a wild ox" is Hebrew *to'afot re'em*. The *re'em* is the aurochs, a large wild ox, and the phrase may mean "the strength of".
- **23:23** "Spell" is Hebrew *nachash*, a word for an omen or enchantment.
- **24:1** Balaam had gone to look for omens in the earlier attempts. He stops that here.
- **24:2** "The spirit of God came on him" shows that the oracle comes from God, not from Balaam's own art.
- **24:3** "Oracle" is Hebrew *ne'um*, an utterance. "Whose eye is opened" can also be read "whose eye is closed" or "is clear". The Hebrew word is rare and uncertain.
- **24:7** Agag is the name of an Amalekite king. Some ancient manuscripts have "Gog".
- **24:17** "A star" and "a sceptre" are images of a ruler. The verse looks to the king who will rise in Israel. "The sons of Sheth" is uncertain. It may mean "sons of Seth", or "sons of tumult".
- **24:18** Edom and Seir are two names for the same region and people.
- **24:20** Amalek is called "first of the nations". The oracle recalls the war in Exodus 17.
- **24:21** The play on words between Kenites (*qeni*) and "nest" (*qen*) and Kain is lost in English.
- **24:22** "Asshur" is Assyria.
- **24:24** "Kittim" refers to the coastlands of the west, probably Cyprus. "Eber" is uncertain.
- **25:1** "Shittim" is the last camp before the Jordan. The name means "acacia trees".
- **25:2** The Moabite women invite Israel to the feasts of their gods. Sexual union and worship go together.
- **25:3** "Joined" is Hebrew *tsamad*, "yoked". The Baal of Peor is the local form of Baal worshipped at Mount Peor.
- **25:4** "Exposed" is Hebrew *yaqa'*, a rare word. It may mean impaled, hanged or dismembered. The exact form of the punishment is uncertain.
- **25:8** "The inner room" translates the rare Hebrew word *qubbah*. It may be a tent or a shrine.
- **25:11** "Zealous" is Hebrew *qinne*. See entry H-QINAH. The word means jealous for God's honour.
- **25:12** "Covenant of peace" is Hebrew *berit shalom*. See entries H-BERIT and H-SHALOM.
- **25:13** Phinehas and his line receive a permanent priesthood, as in 1 Chronicles 6:4-15.
- **25:14** Zimri and Cozbi are named so that they are not forgotten as unknown people.
- **25:17** The command to fight the Midianites is carried out in chapter 31.
- **26:1** The second census follows the plague at Peor (25:9). It is taken about thirty-eight years after the first, on the plains of Moab.
- **26:5** The clan names correspond to the lists in Genesis 46 and Exodus 6, with some differences in spelling.
- **26:9** Dathan and Abiram are recalled as warnings. The sons of Korah did not die (verse 11), and a branch of the Korahites survives among the Levites (verse 58).
- **26:19** Er and Onan died in Canaan (see Genesis 38).
- **26:33** The daughters of Zelophehad are named here ahead of their case in 27:1-11.
- **26:46** Serah is one of the few women named in such lists. She is also named in Genesis 46:17.
- **26:51** The total of 601,730 is a little lower than the 603,550 of 1:46. The tribe of Simeon fell from 59,300 to 22,200, and Manasseh rose from 32,200 to 52,700.
- **26:55** The land is divided by lot, so that God, not human choice, assigns the shares. Size follows the numbers.
- **26:59** Jochebed is said to be born to Levi "in Egypt". Exodus 6:20 calls her Amram's father's sister. The dates make her unusually old.
- **26:62** The Levites number 23,000 here, and 22,000 in 3:39.
- **26:64** Of the first generation counted at Sinai, only Caleb and Joshua remain (see 14:28-35).
- **27:1** The daughters of Zelophehad ask for the right to inherit land. Their case becomes a rule for the Israelites and is taken up again in chapter 36.
- **27:3** They state that their father's death had nothing to do with Korah's rebellion. He "died for his own sin", that is, like everyone in that generation.
- **27:12** "The Abarim range" is the mountain range east of the Dead Sea. Moses will see the land from Mount Nebo (Deuteronomy 34:1).
- **27:14** The waters of Meribah are those of 20:2-13.
- **27:16** "The God of the spirits of all flesh" is the same title Moses used in 16:22.
- **27:17** "Go out and come in" is a Hebrew phrase for leadership, especially in war. "Sheep without a shepherd" is a figure for a people without a leader.
- **27:18** "Spirit" is Hebrew *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH. Laying a hand on Joshua passes on authority.
- **27:21** "The decision of the Urim" refers to the Urim and Thummim of Exodus 28:30.
- **28:2** "My food for my food offerings" is a Hebrew way of speaking of the offerings as God's food. They are presented to God, not eaten by him.
- **28:3** "Regular" is Hebrew *tamid*, a continual offering made every day.
- **28:7** "Strong drink" is Hebrew *shekhar*. Here it is poured out as an offering, not drunk.
- **28:9** This is the only Sabbath offering in the calendar of Numbers 28 and 29. It doubles the daily offering.
- **28:11** "The beginnings of your months" are the new moons, with a larger offering than a normal day.
- **28:16** Passover and Unleavened Bread follow the account in Leviticus 23:5-8.
- **28:26** "The day of the first produce" is the Festival of Weeks, fifty days after the first sheaf.
- **29:1** The first day of the seventh month is later known as the festival of the new year.
- **29:7** "Humble yourselves" is the fast of the Day of Atonement. See Leviticus 16:29.
- **29:12** The Festival of Booths has a daily offering of bulls that decreases from thirteen to seven. The total over the seven days is seventy bulls.
- **29:35** "A closing assembly" is Hebrew *atseret*, the closing day after Booths.
- **29:39** The offerings of chapters 28 and 29 are public offerings made by the community, in addition to private vows and freewill gifts.
- **30:2** The chapter sets rules for vows. A man's vow always binds him.
- **30:3** A woman's vow can be annulled by her father (if she is unmarried) or her husband (if she is married), but only on the day he hears of it. The rules reflect the system of authority in the household.
- **30:5** "Release" is Hebrew *salach*, a word elsewhere used for forgiving. Here it means the LORD will not hold her to the vow.
- **30:9** A widow's or divorced woman's vow stands. She is not under the authority of a father or husband.
- **30:15** If a husband delays and then annuls the vow, he bears the guilt of her breaking it.
- **31:2** The war on Midian is described as "vengeance", an answer to the events at Peor (25:16-18). Moses is told that his death will follow.
- **31:6** Phinehas goes with the army, carrying the holy vessels and the trumpets.
- **31:8** Balaam son of Beor is killed here. This is the last time he appears in the book.
- **31:14** Moses is angry because the army has spared the women who were involved in the Peor affair.
- **31:16** Verse 16 gives Balaam a role in the sin at Peor, which is not stated in chapters 22 to 25. See Revelation 2:14.
- **31:17** The command to kill the boys and the women who have had sexual relations is a hard text. It is rendered as written, with no softening. The children are "little ones", and the girls who are spared are kept alive for the Israelites (verse 18).
- **31:19** Contact with the dead makes the soldiers unclean, and they must be purified by the water of chapter 19.
- **31:23** Metal that can stand fire is purified by fire, and then by the water for impurity.
- **31:28** The tribute is one in five hundred from the soldiers' half, and one in fifty from the community's half.
- **31:35** The 32,000 persons are young women. The totals match the halves given in verses 36 to 46.
- **31:50** The officers offer gold to "make atonement" because no soldier was lost, as a thanksgiving.
- **31:52** 16,750 shekels is about 190 kilograms.
- **32:1** The tribes of Reuben and Gad ask to settle east of the Jordan because the land suits their large herds. Their request tests whether they will help the other tribes.
- **32:5** "Do not make us cross the Jordan" is the heart of the request. Moses fears it is a repeat of the refusal at Kadesh.
- **32:8** "Kadesh-barnea" is the place where the twelve scouts were sent (13:26).
- **32:12** "Caleb the Kenizzite" shows that Caleb's family was of Kenizzite origin, joined to Judah.
- **32:14** "A brood of sinful men" is a harsh phrase for a new generation that is following the pattern of the old.
- **32:23** "Your sin will find you out" means the consequences of broken promises cannot be avoided.
- **32:33** The half-tribe of Manasseh is included here, though it is not named in the request. The text does not explain how it joined the others.
- **32:38** "Their names were changed" refers to names containing the name of Baal, such as Nebo and Baal-meon.
- **32:41** "Havvoth-jair" means "villages of Jair".
- **33:1** The chapter lists forty-two stages from Egypt to the plains of Moab. Many of the places cannot now be identified, and some are not mentioned in the earlier narrative.
- **33:2** "Moses wrote down their starting places" says the list was recorded at the LORD's command, as a record.
- **33:3** "With a high hand" means boldly and openly. The date is the day after the Passover.
- **33:4** The LORD's judgements on the gods of Egypt recalls Exodus 12:12.
- **33:8** "Passed through the sea" refers to the crossing of the Red Sea.
- **33:9** Elim had twelve springs and seventy palm trees. The numbers may echo the twelve tribes and the seventy elders.
- **33:38** Aaron's death is dated to the first day of the fifth month in the fortieth year. He was 123 years old, which is a precise note not found elsewhere.
- **33:40** This verse about the king of Arad comes from 21:1, and seems out of place in the list.
- **33:45** "Iyim" is a short form of Iye-abarim.
- **33:52** "Carved figures" is Hebrew *maskit*, a term of uncertain meaning (see 26:1 in Leviticus).
- **33:55** "Barbs in your eyes and thorns in your sides" means that the people left in the land will become a constant trouble.
- **34:2** The boundaries describe the ideal land of Canaan. The same boundaries appear in a different form in Ezekiel 47:13-20.
- **34:3** The southern boundary begins at the south end of the Dead Sea, called the Salt Sea in Hebrew.
- **34:4** "The ascent of Akrabbim" means "Scorpion Pass".
- **34:5** "The Wadi of Egypt" is probably the Wadi el-Arish in the northern Sinai.
- **34:6** "The Great Sea" is the Mediterranean.
- **34:8** "Lebo-hamath" means the entrance to Hamath, on the northern edge of the land. The northern places are uncertain.
- **34:11** "The Sea of Chinnereth" is the Sea of Galilee.
- **34:13** "The nine and a half tribes" are those who receive land west of the Jordan.
- **34:17** Eleazar and Joshua divide the land, with one leader from each tribe. The list in 34:19-28 matches the tribes in the order of the land.
- **35:2** The forty-eight towns for the Levites make up for the fact that they receive no tribal land.
- **35:4** The text gives the pasture land as a thousand cubits from the wall in verse 4, and as two thousand cubits on each side in verse 5. The two measurements are not easy to reconcile. Later Jewish tradition explains them as two zones.
- **35:6** The six cities of refuge are among the forty-eight Levite towns. They are named in Joshua 20.
- **35:11** "By mistake" is Hebrew *bishgagah*, as in Leviticus 4:2.
- **35:12** "The avenger" is the *go'el ha-dam*, the "redeemer of blood", a relative who acts for the dead person. See entry H-GAAL.
- **35:16** The three cases of an iron tool, a stone and a wooden tool show that the weapon could cause death.
- **35:25** The release "at the death of the high priest" has been explained in different ways. His death may stand for a kind of atonement that ends the exile.
- **35:30** Two witnesses are needed, as in Deuteronomy 17:6.
- **35:31** The life of a murderer cannot be redeemed with money. Human life is too valuable.
- **35:33** "Blood pollutes the land" means that bloodshed defiles the place where God lives. Only the blood of the killer can cleanse it.
- **36:1** The family heads of Gilead return to the case in chapter 27 because of its effect on the tribe's land.
- **36:4** "The Jubilee" would make the transfer of land from one tribe to another permanent. See Leviticus 25.
- **36:6** The daughters may marry anyone they choose, but only within their father's tribal clan.
- **36:7** "No inheritance ... is to be passed from one tribe to another" is the principle that keeps the tribal land intact.
- **36:11** The daughters marry their cousins and so keep the inheritance in the clan.
- **36:13** The closing verse sums up the commands of the book, given on the plains of Moab. The setting is the same as in 22:1, and the book ends facing the Jordan.
