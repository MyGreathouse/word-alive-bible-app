## Judges 1:1-3:6: The Unfinished Conquest and the Cycle

### Chapter 1

**1** After the death of Joshua, the Israelites asked the LORD, "Who is to go up first for us against the Canaanites, to fight against them?"

**2** The LORD said, "Judah is to go up. See, I have given the land into his hand."

**3** Judah said to his brother Simeon, "Come up with me into the territory allotted to me, so that we may fight against the Canaanites. Then I will go with you into the territory allotted to you." So Simeon went with him.

**4** Judah went up, and the LORD gave the Canaanites and the Perizzites into their hands, and they struck down ten thousand of them at Bezek.

**5** They found Adoni-bezek at Bezek and fought against him, and they struck down the Canaanites and the Perizzites.

**6** Adoni-bezek ran away, but they chased him and caught him and cut off his thumbs and his big toes.

**7** Adoni-bezek said, "Seventy kings with their thumbs and big toes cut off used to pick up scraps under my table. As I have done, so God has repaid me." They brought him to Jerusalem, and he died there.

**8** The people of Judah fought against Jerusalem and captured it. They struck it down with the edge of the sword and set the city on fire.

**9** Afterwards the people of Judah went down to fight against the Canaanites who lived in the hill country, in the Negev and in the lowland.

**10** Judah marched against the Canaanites who lived in Hebron (formerly called Kiriath-arba) and struck down Sheshai, Ahiman and Talmai.

**11** From there they marched against the people of Debir (formerly called Kiriath-sepher).

**12** Caleb said, "I will give my daughter Achsah as a wife to the man who attacks and captures Kiriath-sepher."

**13** Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother, captured it, and Caleb gave him his daughter Achsah as a wife.

**14** When she came to him, she urged him to ask her father for a field. She got down from her donkey, and Caleb asked her, "What do you want?"

**15** She said to him, "Give me a blessing. Since you have given me land in the Negev, give me also springs of water." So Caleb gave her the upper springs and the lower springs.

**16** The descendants of the Kenite, Moses' father-in-law, went up with the people of Judah from the City of Palms into the wilderness of Judah, in the Negev near Arad, and went and lived among the people.

**17** Judah went with his brother Simeon, and they struck down the Canaanites who lived in Zephath and devoted it to destruction \[H-CHEREM\]. So the town was called Hormah.

**18** Judah also took Gaza with its territory, Ashkelon with its territory and Ekron with its territory.

**19** The LORD was with Judah. They took possession of the hill country, but they could not drive out the people of the plain, because they had iron chariots.

**20** As Moses had promised, Hebron was given to Caleb, who drove out from it the three sons of Anak.

**21** The Benjaminites did not drive out the Jebusites who lived in Jerusalem. To this day the Jebusites live with the Benjaminites in Jerusalem.

**22** The house of Joseph also went up against Bethel, and the LORD was with them.

**23** The house of Joseph sent men to spy out Bethel (the town was formerly called Luz).

**24** The spies saw a man coming out of the town and said to him, "Show us the way into the town, and we will deal kindly with you."

**25** He showed them the way into the town, and they struck the town down with the edge of the sword, but they let the man and all his family go.

**26** The man went to the land of the Hittites and built a town and named it Luz, which is its name to this day.

**27** Manasseh did not drive out the people of Beth-shean and its villages, or Taanach and its villages, or the people of Dor and its villages, or the people of Ibleam and its villages, or the people of Megiddo and its villages, for the Canaanites were determined to stay in that land.

**28** When Israel grew strong, they put the Canaanites to forced labour, but did not drive them out completely.

**29** Ephraim did not drive out the Canaanites who lived in Gezer, so the Canaanites lived among them in Gezer.

**30** Zebulun did not drive out the people of Kitron or the people of Nahalol, so the Canaanites lived among them, but they became forced labourers.

**31** Asher did not drive out the people of Acco, or the people of Sidon, Ahlab, Achzib, Helbah, Aphek or Rehob.

**32** So the Asherites lived among the Canaanites who lived in the land, for they did not drive them out.

**33** Naphtali did not drive out the people of Beth-shemesh or the people of Beth-anath, but lived among the Canaanites who lived in the land. The people of Beth-shemesh and Beth-anath became forced labourers for them.

**34** The Amorites pressed the Danites back into the hill country, for they did not let them come down into the plain.

**35** The Amorites were determined to stay in Mount Heres, in Aijalon and in Shaalbim, but when the house of Joseph grew strong, they too were put to forced labour.

**36** The border of the Amorites ran from the ascent of Akrabbim, from Sela and upwards.

### Chapter 2

**1** The angel of the LORD went up from Gilgal to Bochim and said, "I brought you up out of Egypt and led you into the land that I swore to give to your ancestors. I said, 'I will never break my covenant \[H-BERIT\] with you.

**2** You are not to make a covenant with the people of this land. You are to tear down their altars.' But you have not listened to my voice. What is this that you have done?

**3** So now I say: I will not drive them out before you. They will be thorns in your sides, and their gods will be a snare to you."

**4** When the angel of the LORD spoke these words to all the Israelites, the people raised their voices and wept.

**5** So they called that place Bochim, and there they sacrificed to the LORD.

**6** When Joshua had sent the people away, the Israelites went each to his own inheritance to take possession of the land.

**7** The people served the LORD all the days of Joshua and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua, who had seen all the great work that the LORD had done for Israel.

**8** Joshua son of Nun, the servant of the LORD, died at the age of a hundred and ten.

**9** They buried him in the land of his own inheritance, at Timnath-heres in the hill country of Ephraim, north of Mount Gaash.

**10** That whole generation was also gathered to their ancestors. Another generation grew up after them who did not know the LORD or the work that he had done for Israel.

**11** The Israelites did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD and served the Baals.

**12** They abandoned the LORD, the God of their ancestors, who had brought them out of the land of Egypt. They followed other gods, from among the gods of the peoples around them, and bowed down to them. They made the LORD angry.

**13** They abandoned the LORD and served Baal and the Ashtoreths.

**14** The anger of the LORD blazed against Israel, and he handed them over to raiders who plundered them. He sold them into the hands of their enemies all round, whom they could no longer stand against.

**15** Whenever they went out, the hand of the LORD was against them for harm, as the LORD had warned and had sworn to them. They were in great distress.

**16** Then the LORD raised up judges, who saved them from the hands of those who plundered them.

**17** Yet they did not listen to their judges, for they prostituted themselves to other gods and bowed down to them. They quickly turned from the way that their ancestors had walked, in obedience to the commands of the LORD. They did not do as their ancestors had done.

**18** Whenever the LORD raised up judges for them, the LORD was with the judge and saved them from the hand of their enemies all the days of the judge, for the LORD was moved to pity by their groaning because of those who oppressed and crushed them.

**19** But when the judge died, they would turn back and act even more corruptly than their ancestors, following other gods, serving them and bowing down to them. They would not give up their evil practices or their stubborn ways.

**20** So the anger of the LORD blazed against Israel, and he said, "Because this people has broken my covenant that I commanded their ancestors, and has not listened to my voice,

**21** I too will no longer drive out before them any of the nations that Joshua left when he died,

**22** so that by them I may test Israel, to see whether they will keep the way of the LORD and walk in it, as their ancestors did."

**23** So the LORD left those nations. He did not drive them out at once, and he did not hand them over to Joshua.

### Chapter 3 (verses 1-6)

**1** These are the nations that the LORD left, to test all those in Israel who had not experienced any of the wars of Canaan.

**2** (This was only so that the generations of the Israelites might know war, at least those who had not known it before.)

**3** These were the five rulers of the Philistines, all the Canaanites, the Sidonians, and the Hivites living in the hill country of Lebanon, from Mount Baal-hermon to Lebo-hamath.

**4** They were left to test Israel, to find out whether they would listen to the commands of the LORD that he gave to their ancestors through Moses.

**5** So the Israelites lived among the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites.

**6** They took their daughters as wives for themselves, and gave their own daughters to their sons, and served their gods.

*Review note: Judges 1:1 to 3:6 was drafted in WABV wording. The opening of Judges repeats and in places conflicts with Joshua. For example, Judges 1:8 has Judah burning Jerusalem, while 1:21 and Joshua 15:63 say the Jebusites stayed, and Judges 1:10-15 repeats the story of Caleb and Othniel from Joshua 15:13-19. The footnotes record these points without harmonising them. The wording follows Joshua for the shared passages. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Judges 3:7-5:31: Othniel, Ehud, Deborah and Jael

### Chapter 3 (verses 7-31)

**7** The Israelites did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD. They forgot the LORD their God and served the Baals and the Asherahs.

**8** The anger of the LORD blazed against Israel, and he sold them into the hand of Cushan-rishathaim king of Aram-naharaim, and the Israelites served Cushan-rishathaim for eight years.

**9** When the Israelites cried out to the LORD, the LORD raised up a deliverer for the Israelites, who saved them: Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother.

**10** The spirit \[H-RUACH\] of the LORD came on him, and he judged Israel. He went out to war, and the LORD gave Cushan-rishathaim king of Aram into his hand, and he overpowered Cushan-rishathaim.

**11** The land had rest for forty years. Then Othniel son of Kenaz died.

**12** The Israelites again did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, and the LORD made Eglon king of Moab strong against Israel, because they had done what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.

**13** Eglon gathered the Ammonites and the Amalekites to himself, and went and struck Israel down, and they took possession of the City of Palms.

**14** The Israelites served Eglon king of Moab for eighteen years.

**15** When the Israelites cried out to the LORD, the LORD raised up a deliverer for them, Ehud son of Gera, the Benjaminite, a left-handed man. The Israelites sent tribute by him to Eglon king of Moab.

**16** Ehud made himself a double-edged sword, a cubit long, and strapped it on his right thigh under his clothes.

**17** He presented the tribute to Eglon king of Moab. Now Eglon was a very fat man.

**18** When Ehud had finished presenting the tribute, he sent away the people who had carried it.

**19** But he himself turned back at the carved stones near Gilgal and said, "I have a secret word for you, O king." The king said, "Silence." All those standing by him left him.

**20** Ehud came to him as he was sitting alone in his cool upper room. Ehud said, "I have a word from God for you." And the king rose from his seat.

**21** Ehud reached out with his left hand, took the sword from his right thigh and thrust it into his belly.

**22** Even the hilt went in after the blade, and the fat closed over the blade, for he did not pull the sword out of his belly. And the filth came out.

**23** Then Ehud went out through the porch and shut the doors of the upper room on him and locked them.

**24** When he had gone, the servants came and looked, and the doors of the upper room were locked. They said, "He must be relieving himself in the cool chamber."

**25** They waited until they were embarrassed. But when he still did not open the doors of the upper room, they took the key and opened them, and there was their lord lying dead on the floor.

**26** Ehud got away while they delayed. He passed the carved stones and escaped to Seirah.

**27** When he arrived, he blew the trumpet in the hill country of Ephraim, and the Israelites went down with him from the hill country, with him at their head.

**28** He said to them, "Follow me, for the LORD has given your enemies the Moabites into your hands." They went down after him and seized the fords of the Jordan against Moab, and let no one cross over.

**29** At that time they struck down about ten thousand Moabites, all strong and able-bodied men. Not one escaped.

**30** So Moab was brought under the hand of Israel that day. And the land had rest for eighty years.

**31** After him came Shamgar son of Anath, who struck down six hundred Philistines with an ox-goad. He too saved Israel.

### Chapter 4

**1** The Israelites again did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, after Ehud died.

**2** So the LORD sold them into the hand of Jabin king of Canaan, who ruled in Hazor. The commander of his army was Sisera, who lived in Harosheth-haggoyim.

**3** The Israelites cried out to the LORD, for Jabin had nine hundred iron chariots and had oppressed the Israelites harshly for twenty years.

**4** Now Deborah, a prophet \[H-NAVI\], the wife of Lappidoth, was judging Israel at that time.

**5** She used to sit under the palm of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim, and the Israelites came up to her for judgement.

**6** She sent and called Barak son of Abinoam from Kedesh in Naphtali and said to him, "The LORD, the God of Israel, commands you: 'Go, take up your position on Mount Tabor, with ten thousand men from Naphtali and Zebulun.

**7** I will draw out Sisera, the commander of Jabin's army, to you at the Wadi Kishon, with his chariots and his troops, and I will give him into your hand.'"

**8** Barak said to her, "If you will go with me, I will go. But if you will not go with me, I will not go."

**9** She said, "I will certainly go with you. But the road you are taking will not bring you glory, for the LORD will sell Sisera into the hand of a woman." So Deborah got up and went with Barak to Kedesh.

**10** Barak called Zebulun and Naphtali to Kedesh, and ten thousand men went up behind him, and Deborah went up with him.

**11** Now Heber the Kenite had separated from the other Kenites, the descendants of Hobab, Moses' father-in-law, and had pitched his tent as far away as the oak of Zaanannim, which is near Kedesh.

**12** When Sisera was told that Barak son of Abinoam had gone up to Mount Tabor,

**13** Sisera called out all his chariots, nine hundred iron chariots, and all the men with him, from Harosheth-haggoyim to the Wadi Kishon.

**14** Deborah said to Barak, "Up. This is the day on which the LORD has given Sisera into your hand. Has the LORD not gone out ahead of you?" So Barak went down from Mount Tabor with ten thousand men behind him.

**15** The LORD threw Sisera and all his chariots and all his army into confusion before Barak, at the edge of the sword. Sisera got down from his chariot and ran away on foot.

**16** Barak chased the chariots and the army as far as Harosheth-haggoyim, and all the army of Sisera fell by the edge of the sword. Not one was left.

**17** But Sisera ran away on foot to the tent of Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, for there was peace between Jabin king of Hazor and the household of Heber the Kenite.

**18** Jael went out to meet Sisera and said to him, "Turn aside, my lord, turn aside to me. Do not be afraid." So he turned aside to her into the tent, and she covered him with a rug.

**19** He said to her, "Please give me a little water to drink, for I am thirsty." She opened a skin of milk and gave him a drink, and covered him.

**20** He said to her, "Stand at the entrance of the tent, and if anyone comes and asks you, 'Is there a man here?' say, 'No.'"

**21** But Jael wife of Heber took a tent peg and picked up a hammer. She went quietly to him and drove the peg through his temple and into the ground, as he lay fast asleep, exhausted. So he died.

**22** Just then Barak came, chasing Sisera, and Jael went out to meet him and said to him, "Come, and I will show you the man you are looking for." He went in to her, and there lay Sisera dead, with the tent peg through his temple.

**23** So on that day God brought Jabin king of Canaan down before the Israelites.

**24** The hand of the Israelites pressed harder and harder against Jabin king of Canaan, until they had destroyed Jabin king of Canaan.

*Review note: Judges 3:7 to 5:31 was drafted in WABV wording. The Song of Deborah (chapter 5) is one of the oldest and most difficult poems in the Hebrew Bible, and many lines are uncertain, notably 5:2, 5:8, 5:11, 5:13-15, 5:21 and 5:30. It is set as verse lines. The prose account of the same battle in chapter 4 and the poem differ in some details, for example in the way that Sisera dies and the part played by the weather, and the footnotes record these. The killing of Eglon (3:21-22) and of Sisera (4:21; 5:26-27) are rendered as written. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Judges 6-8: Gideon

### Chapter 6

**1** The Israelites did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, and the LORD handed them over to Midian for seven years.

**2** The hand of Midian was strong against Israel. Because of Midian the Israelites made for themselves the hideouts in the mountains, the caves and the strongholds.

**3** Whenever the Israelites sowed seed, the Midianites, the Amalekites and the people of the east would come up against them.

**4** They camped against them and destroyed the produce of the land as far as Gaza, and left nothing for Israel to live on, no sheep, ox or donkey.

**5** They came up with their livestock and their tents, as thick as locusts. They and their camels could not be counted, and they came into the land to destroy it.

**6** Israel was brought very low because of Midian, and the Israelites cried out to the LORD.

**7** When the Israelites cried out to the LORD because of Midian,

**8** the LORD sent a prophet to the Israelites. He said to them, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: I brought you up out of Egypt, and I brought you out of the house of slavery.

**9** I rescued you from the hand of the Egyptians and from the hand of all who oppressed you. I drove them out before you and gave you their land.

**10** I said to you, 'I am the LORD your God. You are not to fear the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you live.' But you have not listened to my voice."

**11** The angel of the LORD came and sat under the oak at Ophrah that belonged to Joash the Abiezrite, while his son Gideon was beating out wheat in the winepress, to hide it from the Midianites.

**12** The angel of the LORD appeared to him and said, "The LORD is with you, mighty warrior."

**13** Gideon said to him, "Please, my lord, if the LORD is with us, why has all this happened to us? Where are all his wonders that our ancestors told us about, saying, 'Did the LORD not bring us up out of Egypt?' But now the LORD has abandoned us and handed us over to Midian."

**14** The LORD turned to him and said, "Go in this strength of yours and save Israel from the hand of Midian. Am I not sending you?"

**15** He said to him, "Please, Lord, how can I save Israel? My clan is the weakest in Manasseh, and I am the youngest in my father's house."

**16** The LORD said to him, "But I will be with you, and you will strike down Midian as if it were one man."

**17** He said to him, "If I have found favour in your eyes, give me a sign that it is you who are speaking with me.

**18** Please do not leave here until I come back to you and bring out my gift and set it before you." And he said, "I will stay until you return."

**19** Gideon went in and prepared a young goat and unleavened bread from an ephah of flour. He put the meat in a basket and the broth in a pot, and brought them out to him under the oak and presented them.

**20** The angel of God said to him, "Take the meat and the unleavened bread and put them on this rock, and pour the broth over them." And he did so.

**21** The angel of the LORD put out the tip of the staff that was in his hand and touched the meat and the unleavened bread. Fire came up from the rock and consumed the meat and the unleavened bread, and the angel of the LORD vanished from his sight.

**22** Then Gideon saw that he was the angel of the LORD. Gideon said, "Alas, Lord GOD, for I have seen the angel of the LORD face to face."

**23** But the LORD said to him, "Peace \[H-SHALOM\] to you. Do not be afraid. You will not die."

**24** Then Gideon built an altar there to the LORD and called it The LORD Is Peace. It is still at Ophrah of the Abiezrites to this day.

**25** That night the LORD said to him, "Take your father's bull, the second bull, seven years old, and pull down the altar of Baal that belongs to your father, and cut down the Asherah beside it.

**26** Then build an altar to the LORD your God on top of this stronghold, in the proper way. Take the second bull and offer it as a burnt offering with the wood of the Asherah that you cut down."

**27** So Gideon took ten of his servants and did as the LORD had told him. But because he was too afraid of his household and the men of the town to do it by day, he did it by night.

**28** When the men of the town got up early in the morning, there was the altar of Baal torn down, and the Asherah beside it cut down, and the second bull offered on the altar that had been built.

**29** They said to one another, "Who did this?" After they had searched and asked, they were told, "Gideon son of Joash did it."

**30** Then the men of the town said to Joash, "Bring out your son, so that he may die, for he has pulled down the altar of Baal and cut down the Asherah beside it."

**31** But Joash said to all who stood against him, "Will you defend Baal? Will you save him? Whoever defends him will be put to death by morning. If he is a god, let him defend himself, because his altar has been torn down."

**32** So on that day Gideon was called Jerubbaal, meaning "Let Baal defend himself against him," because he had torn down his altar.

**33** All the Midianites, the Amalekites and the people of the east gathered together, crossed the Jordan and camped in the Valley of Jezreel.

**34** But the spirit \[H-RUACH\] of the LORD clothed Gideon, and he blew the trumpet, and the Abiezrites were called to follow him.

**35** He sent messengers throughout Manasseh, and they too were called to follow him. He also sent messengers to Asher, Zebulun and Naphtali, and they came up to meet them.

**36** Then Gideon said to God, "If you will save Israel by my hand, as you have said,

**37** look, I am putting a fleece of wool on the threshing floor. If there is dew on the fleece alone, and it is dry on all the ground, then I will know that you will save Israel by my hand, as you have said."

**38** And it was so. When he got up early the next morning and squeezed the fleece, he wrung out the dew from the fleece, a bowlful of water.

**39** Then Gideon said to God, "Do not let your anger blaze against me. Let me speak just once more. Let me test just once more with the fleece. Let it be dry on the fleece alone, and let there be dew on all the ground."

**40** And God did so that night. It was dry on the fleece alone, and there was dew on all the ground.

### Chapter 7

**1** Jerubbaal, that is Gideon, and all the people with him got up early and camped beside the spring of Harod. The camp of Midian was north of them, by the hill of Moreh, in the valley.

**2** The LORD said to Gideon, "There are too many people with you for me to hand the Midianites over to them, or Israel will boast against me and say, 'My own hand has saved me.'

**3** So now announce to the people, 'Whoever is afraid and trembling may turn back and leave Mount Gilead.'" Twenty-two thousand of the people turned back, and ten thousand were left.

**4** The LORD said to Gideon, "There are still too many people. Take them down to the water, and I will sift them for you there. Whoever I say to you, 'This one will go with you,' he is to go with you. And whoever I say to you, 'This one will not go with you,' he is not to go."

**5** So he took the people down to the water. The LORD said to Gideon, "Everyone who laps the water with his tongue, as a dog laps, you are to set apart by himself. Do the same with everyone who kneels down to drink."

**6** The number of those who lapped, bringing their hands to their mouths, was three hundred men. All the rest of the people knelt down to drink water.

**15** On that day the Benjaminites mustered from their towns twenty-six thousand men who drew the sword, besides the people of Gibeah, who mustered seven hundred chosen men.

**16** Among all these troops there were seven hundred chosen men who were left-handed. Each one could sling a stone at a hair and not miss.

**17** The men of Israel, apart from Benjamin, mustered four hundred thousand men who drew the sword. All these were men of war.

**18** The Israelites got up and went up to Bethel and asked God, "Which of us is to go up first to fight against the Benjaminites?" The LORD said, "Judah is to go first."

**19** In the morning the Israelites got up and camped against Gibeah.

**20** The men of Israel went out to fight against Benjamin, and drew up their battle line against them at Gibeah.

**21** The Benjaminites came out of Gibeah and struck down twenty-two thousand of the Israelites on that day.

**22** But the Israelites took courage, and again drew up their battle line in the same place where they had drawn it up on the first day.

**23** The Israelites went up and wept before the LORD until evening. They asked the LORD, "Shall we again go into battle against our kinsmen the Benjaminites?" The LORD said, "Go up against them."

**24** So the Israelites advanced against the Benjaminites on the second day.

**25** Benjamin came out against them from Gibeah on the second day and struck down eighteen thousand more of the Israelites. All of these drew the sword.

**26** Then all the Israelites, the whole army, went up and came to Bethel and wept. They sat there before the LORD and fasted that day until evening, and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings before the LORD.

**27** The Israelites asked the LORD (the ark of the covenant of God was there in those days,

**28** and Phinehas son of Eleazar, son of Aaron, was serving before it in those days), "Shall we go out again to fight against our kinsmen the Benjaminites, or shall we stop?" The LORD said, "Go up, for tomorrow I will hand them over to you."

**29** So Israel set an ambush all round Gibeah.

**30** The Israelites went up against the Benjaminites on the third day and took their positions against Gibeah, as before.

**31** The Benjaminites came out against the army and were drawn away from the town. As before, they began to strike down some of the people on the roads, one of which goes up to Bethel and the other to Gibeah, and in the open country, about thirty men of Israel.

**32** The Benjaminites said, "They are being beaten before us, as at the first." But the Israelites said, "Let us run away and draw them away from the town to the roads."

**33** All the men of Israel got up from their places and took their positions at Baal-tamar, and the men of Israel who were in ambush rushed out from their place west of Geba.

**34** Ten thousand chosen men from all Israel came against Gibeah, and the battle was fierce. But the Benjaminites did not know that disaster was close on them.

**35** The LORD struck down Benjamin before Israel, and on that day the Israelites killed twenty-five thousand one hundred men of Benjamin, all of them men who drew the sword.

**36** Then the Benjaminites saw that they were beaten. The men of Israel had given ground to Benjamin, because they trusted the ambush that they had set against Gibeah.

**37** The men in ambush rushed quickly against Gibeah, spread out and struck down the whole town with the edge of the sword.

**38** Now the agreed sign between the men of Israel and the men in ambush was that they would send up a great cloud of smoke from the town,

**39** and then the men of Israel would turn in the battle. Benjamin had begun to strike down about thirty of the Israelites. They said, "Surely they are beaten before us, as in the first battle."

**40** But when the cloud began to rise from the town in a column of smoke, the Benjaminites looked behind them, and the whole town was going up in smoke to the sky.

**41** Then the men of Israel turned, and the men of Benjamin were terrified, for they saw that disaster was close on them.

**42** So they turned their backs before the men of Israel and ran towards the wilderness, but the battle overtook them. And those who came out of the towns struck them down in the middle.

**43** They surrounded the Benjaminites, chased them and trampled them down from Nohah as far as opposite Gibeah on the east.

**44** Eighteen thousand men of Benjamin fell, all of them mighty warriors.

**45** The rest turned and fled towards the wilderness to the rock of Rimmon. Five thousand of them were cut down on the roads. They were chased as far as Gidom, and two thousand of them were struck down.

**46** So all the Benjaminites who fell that day were twenty-five thousand men who drew the sword, all of them mighty warriors.

**47** But six hundred men turned and fled towards the wilderness to the rock of Rimmon, and stayed at the rock of Rimmon for four months.

**48** The men of Israel turned back against the Benjaminites and struck them down with the edge of the sword, the whole town, people and animals and everything they found. All the towns that they came to they set on fire.

### Chapter 21

**1** The men of Israel had sworn an oath at Mizpah: "None of us will give his daughter in marriage to a Benjaminite."

**2** The people came to Bethel and sat there before God until evening, and they raised their voices and wept bitterly.

**3** They said, "LORD, God of Israel, why has this happened in Israel, that today one tribe should be missing from Israel?"

**4** The next day the people got up early and built an altar there and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings.

**5** The Israelites said, "Who among all the tribes of Israel did not come up with the assembly to the LORD?" For they had taken a solemn oath about anyone who did not come up to the LORD at Mizpah: "He will surely be put to death."

**6** The Israelites felt sorry for their kinsman Benjamin and said, "Today one tribe has been cut off from Israel.

**7** What can we do to provide wives for those who are left, since we have sworn by the LORD not to give them any of our daughters as wives?"

**8** They said, "Which one of the tribes of Israel did not come up to the LORD at Mizpah?" And it turned out that no one from Jabesh-gilead had come to the camp, to the assembly.

**9** For when the people were counted, there was not one of the people of Jabesh-gilead there.

**10** So the community sent twelve thousand of their bravest men there and commanded them: "Go and strike down the people of Jabesh-gilead with the edge of the sword, with the women and the little ones.

**11** This is what you are to do: devote to destruction \[H-CHEREM\] every male, and every woman who has lain with a male."

**12** They found among the people of Jabesh-gilead four hundred young women who were virgins, who had not lain with a man, and they brought them to the camp at Shiloh, which is in the land of Canaan.

**13** Then the whole community sent word to the Benjaminites who were at the rock of Rimmon and offered them peace.

**14** The Benjaminites came back at that time, and they gave them the women of Jabesh-gilead whom they had kept alive. But there were not enough for them.

**15** The people felt sorry for Benjamin, because the LORD had made a gap in the tribes of Israel.

**16** The elders of the community said, "What can we do to provide wives for those who are left, since the women of Benjamin have been wiped out?"

**17** They said, "There must be an inheritance for the survivors of Benjamin, so that a tribe is not wiped out from Israel.

**18** But we cannot give them wives from our daughters." For the Israelites had sworn, "Cursed is anyone who gives a wife to Benjamin."

**19** So they said, "Look, there is a yearly festival of the LORD at Shiloh, which is north of Bethel, east of the road that goes up from Bethel to Shechem, and south of Lebonah."

**20** They commanded the Benjaminites: "Go and lie in wait in the vineyards,

**21** and watch. When the young women of Shiloh come out to dance in the dances, come out of the vineyards, and each of you seize a wife for himself from the young women of Shiloh, and go to the land of Benjamin.

**22** When their fathers or their brothers come to us to complain, we will say to them, 'Be kind and give them to us, because we did not take a wife for each man in the war, and you did not give them yourselves, or you would now be guilty.'"

**23** The Benjaminites did so. They took wives for themselves, according to their number, from the dancers whom they carried off. Then they went back to their inheritance and rebuilt the towns and lived in them.

**24** The Israelites left there at that time, each to his tribe and clan, each to his own inheritance.

**25** In those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did what was right in his own eyes.

*End of the book of Judges. Review note: Judges 17 to 21 was drafted in WABV wording. These chapters are the darkest in the book. Chapter 19 tells of the rape and death of the concubine at Gibeah and her husband's dismemberment of her body, rendered as written. Chapter 20 is a civil war that nearly destroys Benjamin, and chapter 21 describes the killing of the people of Jabesh-gilead and the seizure of the young women of Shiloh to provide wives. The refrain "in those days there was no king in Israel, everyone did what was right in his own eyes" (17:6; 18:1; 19:1; 21:25) is the book's comment on the whole. The footnotes explain the language and record the points where the text is uncertain, without comment on the ethics. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **1:1** The book is called "Judges" from the Hebrew *shofetim*. The word means leaders who deliver the people and govern them, not only those who settle legal cases. The people ask the LORD who is to go up first, as the book begins in the time after Joshua.
- **1:2** Judah is chosen first, as in Genesis 49:8-12 and Numbers 2:9.
- **1:3** Simeon's land lies inside the territory of Judah (Joshua 19:1, 9), so Simeon joins Judah.
- **1:6** "Cut off his thumbs and his big toes" is a mutilation that made a king unfit for war. Adoni-bezek ("lord of Bezek") had done the same (verse 7).
- **1:7** "Seventy kings" is a round number for many. "As I have done, so God has repaid me" states the justice of the punishment.
- **1:8** Judah is said to capture and burn Jerusalem. Verse 21 and Joshua 15:63 say the Jebusites remained. The text may mean that the lower city was taken for a time and then lost.
- **1:10** Judah takes Hebron here. In Joshua 15:14 Caleb drives out the three sons of Anak.
- **1:11** Verses 11 to 15 repeat the story of Caleb, Othniel and Achsah in Joshua 15:15-19.
- **1:13** "Caleb's younger brother" can also be read "the son of Caleb's younger brother", so that Othniel is Caleb's nephew. The Hebrew is ambiguous.
- **1:16** "The City of Palms" is Jericho, or another palm town. The Kenite is a descendant of Moses' father-in-law (see Exodus 18 and Numbers 10:29).
- **1:17** "Devoted to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. The name Hormah comes from the same root. Compare Numbers 21:1-3.
- **1:18** The Hebrew says Judah took Gaza, Ashkelon and Ekron. The Greek translation says he did not. Verses 19 and 3:3 suggest the Philistine cities were not held. The Hebrew is followed here.
- **1:19** "Iron chariots" are a military advantage of the people of the plain, as in Joshua 17:16.
- **1:21** The Benjaminites did not drive out the Jebusites. Joshua 15:63 says the same of Judah, and David takes the city in 2 Samuel 5:6-9.
- **1:23** "Luz" was the old name of Bethel (Genesis 28:19).
- **1:25** The man who shows the way into the town is spared. This is like the sparing of Rahab (Joshua 6:25).
- **1:27** "Determined to stay" is Hebrew *ho'il*, to resolve to do something. It repeats Joshua 17:12.
- **1:28** The failures listed in verses 27 to 36 repeat the notes of land not taken in Joshua 13 to 19. They are given by tribe, from south to north.
- **1:34** The Danites are pushed into the hill country. This prepares for their move north in Judges 18.
- **2:1** "The angel of the LORD" is Hebrew *mal'ak YHWH*. He speaks in the first person as the LORD. "Bochim" means "weepers". "I will never break my covenant" is the LORD's promise in Genesis 17 and Exodus 6:4. "Covenant" is *berit* (see entry H-BERIT).
- **2:3** "Thorns in your sides" and "a snare" repeat Numbers 33:55 and Joshua 23:13.
- **2:5** The people weep and sacrifice at Bochim.
- **2:6** Verses 6 to 9 repeat Joshua 24:28-31 and look back to the death of Joshua. They begin the second introduction to the book, which sets out the cycle of the judges.
- **2:9** "Timnath-heres" is the same place as Timnath-serah in Joshua 19:50 and 24:30.
- **2:10** "Did not know the LORD" means they had no experience of him and what he had done, and did not keep the covenant.
- **2:11** "The Baals" are the local forms of the god Baal. "The Ashtoreths" (verse 13) are the goddesses of the same type.
- **2:14** "He sold them" is a figure of handing over, as a slave is sold. See Deuteronomy 32:30.
- **2:16** The judges are raised up by the LORD to save the people, not by their own choice.
- **2:17** "Prostituted themselves" is the standard figure for unfaithful worship.
- **2:18** "Moved to pity" is Hebrew *nacham*, which can also mean "changed his mind" or "was sorry".
- **2:19** The cycle is set out: the people sin, are oppressed, cry out, are saved by a judge, and then sin again, often worse.
- **2:22** The nations that remain are a test (see also 3:1, 4).
- **3:1** The reason for the remaining nations is given in two ways, a test of Israel's obedience (verses 1 and 4), and a way for later generations to learn war (verse 2).
- **3:3** "The five rulers of the Philistines" is Hebrew *seranim*, as in Joshua 13:3.
- **3:6** The intermarriage forbidden in Deuteronomy 7:3-4 now takes place.
- **3:7** "The Asherahs" are the poles or images of the goddess Asherah, as in Exodus 34:13.
- **3:8** "He sold them" means he handed them over to an enemy. "Cushan-rishathaim" means "Cushan of double wickedness", a name that may be a deliberate mocking form. "Aram-naharaim" is northern Mesopotamia. In 3:10 some scholars read "Edom" for "Aram", since the two are written almost alike.
- **3:9** "A deliverer" is Hebrew *moshia'*, one who saves. Othniel is the first judge, and the story echoes Joshua 15:17 and Judges 1:13.
- **3:10** "The spirit of the LORD" is Hebrew *ruach YHWH*. See entry H-RUACH. The same phrase marks Gideon (6:34), Jephthah (11:29) and Samson (13:25; 14:6). "Judged" means he led the people and governed them.
- **3:11** "Forty years" is a round number for a generation, as are the other periods of rest in the book.
- **3:13** "The City of Palms" is Jericho, or another town with palms.
- **3:15** "Left-handed" is Hebrew *itter yad yemino*, "restricted in his right hand". Ehud is a Benjaminite (*ben-yemini*, "son of the right hand"), so there is a play on words.
- **3:19** "The carved stones" is Hebrew *pesilim*, which can mean idols or stone markers at Gilgal.
- **3:20** "His cool upper room" is a roof chamber for the heat of the day. The Hebrew term is uncertain.
- **3:22** "The filth" is Hebrew *parshdonah*, a rare word of uncertain meaning. It may refer to the contents of the bowels. The word "porch" in verse 23 is also uncertain.
- **3:24** "Relieving himself" is literally "covering his feet", a polite expression.
- **3:26** Seirah is a place in the hill country of Ephraim. Its exact site is unknown.
- **3:30** "Eighty years" is a round number, twice the forty years.
- **3:31** Shamgar is named briefly. He is called "son of Anath", a name that matches the Canaanite goddess Anat. He fights the Philistines with an ox-goad.
- **4:2** "Jabin king of Canaan" who ruled in Hazor recalls Joshua 11:1-11. Sisera is the army commander, not the king.
- **4:3** "Nine hundred iron chariots" show the military power of Sisera.
- **4:4** "Wife of Lappidoth" can also be read "woman of torches" (*eshet lappidot*), since *lappidot* means torches. Deborah is a prophet \[H-NAVI\] and a judge, the only woman judge in the book.
- **4:6** "Barak" means "lightning".
- **4:9** "The LORD will sell Sisera into the hand of a woman" may refer to Deborah or to Jael. The story later shows it is Jael.
- **4:11** Heber the Kenite has separated from the other Kenites (see 1:16). He is at peace with Jabin (verse 17).
- **4:15** "Threw ... into confusion" is Hebrew *hamam*, the same word as in Joshua 10:10 and Exodus 14:24. The prose says only that the LORD did it. The poem names the stars and the Kishon (5:20-21).
- **4:18** Jael offers Sisera hospitality, a rug and a drink, and then kills him. Hospitality to a guest was a binding duty in the ancient world. The narrative celebrates her act and does not comment on this.
- **4:21** In the prose Sisera is lying asleep and she drives the peg into his temple. In the poem (5:26-27) the account differs in detail. The WABV text keeps both as written.
- **5:1** "Deborah and Barak sang" introduces a poem about the same battle. The Hebrew uses forms of the verb that are older than the rest of the book, and the poem is one of the earliest texts in the Bible.
- **5:2** The first line is uncertain. The Hebrew *pera'ot pera' be-Yisra'el* can mean "when hair was let loose in Israel" (as a sign of a vow to fight), or "when leaders led". The WABV text follows the second.
- **5:4** The LORD's coming from Seir and Edom recalls Deuteronomy 33:2. The storm is a sign of his presence.
- **5:5** "Flowed" is Hebrew *nazlu*, which can also mean "shook". "The One of Sinai" is *zeh Sinai*, a short phrase that may also be "this Sinai".
- **5:6** "In the days of Jael" is puzzling, since Jael is a Kenite woman in the later story. The text may refer to an earlier Jael, or the phrase may be an error.
- **5:8** "New gods" is the cause of the war. "Forty thousand" is a round number for the whole army, without a shield or spear.
- **5:10** "Tawny donkeys" is Hebrew *tsechorot*, perhaps reddish-white. They were ridden by the well off.
- **5:11** "Far from the sound of the archers" is uncertain. "Righteous acts" is Hebrew *tsidqot*, the saving deeds of the LORD.
- **5:13** The Hebrew of this verse is very hard. It may mean that a remnant of the nobles came down, or that the people came down to the nobles.
- **5:14** The tribes that came are Ephraim, Benjamin, Machir (the name for Manasseh) and Zebulun, and in verse 15 Issachar. Judah and Simeon are not named in the poem.
- **5:15** "Searching of heart" is Hebrew *chiqqeqei lev*. Some manuscripts read *chiqqeqei lev* as "resolves" in verse 15 and "searchings" in verse 16. The tribe of Reuben talks but does not come.
- **5:17** Gilead (Gad), Dan and Asher stayed away. Naphtali (verse 18) came, with Zebulun.
- **5:19** "They took no plunder of silver" means the kings of Canaan were defeated and gained nothing. Taanach and Megiddo are on the edge of the plain of Jezreel.
- **5:20** "From heaven the stars fought" says that heavenly forces joined the battle. A storm may be meant. See Joshua 10:11.
- **5:21** The Kishon floods and sweeps away the chariots. The prose of chapter 4 does not mention rain.
- **5:23** "Meroz" is a place that is otherwise unknown. The "angel of the LORD" pronounces a curse on it for not helping.
- **5:26** The death of Sisera is told in the style of a victory song. "Crushed his head" and "pierced his temple" are the poem's words.
- **5:27** "Between her feet" is repeated three times. The word *kara'*, "sank" or "knelt", is also used. Some readers see sexual overtones in the verse. The text does not say.
- **5:28** The scene turns to the mother of Sisera, waiting at the window. It is one of the most vivid parts of the poem.
- **5:30** "A girl or two for every man" is the Hebrew idiom *rechem rachamatayim*, literally "a womb, two wombs". It describes captive women taken as plunder. The words show the expectation of the mother and her ladies.
- **5:31** "Like the sun going out in its strength" is a figure for the rising sun. The poem closes with the contrast between the enemies and those who love the LORD.
- **6:3** "The people of the east" is Hebrew *bene qedem*, the desert tribes who raid the land with the Midianites and Amalekites.
- **6:5** The camels are the first large use of camels in raids in the Bible. They let the raiders move quickly across the land.
- **6:8** The prophet is not named. His message recalls the exodus and the command not to fear the gods of the Amorites.
- **6:11** "The angel of the LORD" speaks and acts as the LORD himself, and the text moves between "the angel" and "the LORD" in verses 11 to 24. Gideon beats wheat in a winepress, a small place chosen to hide the grain.
- **6:12** "Mighty warrior" is Hebrew *gibbor chayil*. The title is ironic, since Gideon is hiding.
- **6:15** "My clan" is literally "my thousand". "The youngest" is Hebrew *tse'ir*, the smallest or youngest. Gideon stresses his weakness.
- **6:19** The meal of a young goat and unleavened bread is the gift Gideon offers to the visitor.
- **6:21** Fire from the rock consumes the offering and shows that the visitor is divine.
- **6:22** "Face to face" and the fear of death follow the belief in Exodus 33:20 that no one can see God and live.
- **6:23** "Peace" is Hebrew *shalom*. See entry H-SHALOM. The altar is named *YHWH-shalom* (verse 24).
- **6:25** "Take your father's bull, the second bull, seven years old" is unclear. It can mean two bulls (the father's bull and a second one) or one bull described by two phrases. The WABV text follows one reading and records the other.
- **6:32** "Jerubbaal" means "let Baal defend himself". In 2 Samuel 11:21 the name is given as Jerubbesheth, where *bosheth* ("shame") replaces Baal.
- **6:34** "The spirit of the LORD clothed Gideon" is literally "clothed itself with Gideon". The spirit \[H-RUACH\] puts him on like a garment.
- **6:37** The fleece is a test of the LORD's promise. Gideon has already been given a sign (verse 21), and he asks for further signs. The story does not set the fleece as a model for seeking guidance.
- **7:3** "Mount Gilead" is uncertain. The place is near the spring of Harod, so it may be an error for another name.
- **7:5** The test of those who "lap" is hard to follow. Verse 6 says the three hundred put their hands to their mouths, which is different from lapping like a dog. The Hebrew text is kept as it is.
- **7:8** The trumpets are the ram's horns of the troops.
- **7:13** A barley loaf was a poor man's food. The dream uses it as a figure for Gideon and Israel.
- **7:19** "The middle watch" is the middle of the three night watches.
- **7:20** The shout "A sword for the LORD and for Gideon" has the word "sword" in the Hebrew here, and not in the earlier command of verse 18. Some manuscripts read the two verses in the same way.
- **7:22** The enemy turns on itself in panic. The places Beth-shittah, Zererah, Abel-meholah and Tabbath are uncertain.
- **7:25** "Oreb" means "raven" and "Zeeb" means "wolf". The princes are killed at places named for them.
- **8:2** "The gleaning of Ephraim" and "the grape harvest of Abiezer" is a saying that Ephraim's smaller share is better than Gideon's whole.
- **8:7** "Thresh your bodies with desert thorns" is a threat of a harsh punishment.
- **8:10** "A hundred and twenty thousand" is a large round number for the dead of the eastern army.
- **8:14** The seventy-seven officials and elders of Succoth are written down by a young man.
- **8:16** "Taught a lesson" is literally "made them know". The punishment is told in terms that the Hebrew leaves unclear.
- **8:17** The killing of the men of Penuel is told in one line, with no comment.
- **8:18** The killing of Gideon's brothers at Tabor is not told elsewhere in the book. Gideon acts as the avenger of blood (see entry H-GAAL).
- **8:20** Jether, Gideon's firstborn, cannot kill the kings because he is still a boy.
- **8:21** "Crescent ornaments" were ornaments for camels.
- **8:23** Gideon refuses to rule, saying that the LORD will rule. Yet he names his son by the concubine Abimelech ("my father is king", verse 31), and the story that follows is of Abimelech's attempt to be king.
- **8:24** "Ishmaelites" is another name for the Midianites here (see Genesis 37:25-28).
- **8:26** Seventeen hundred shekels is about 19 kilograms of gold.
- **8:27** "Ephod" is a priestly garment (Exodus 28), but here it is an object of worship, perhaps an image or a covered frame. "Prostituted themselves" says the people made it an idol. It is a "snare" to Gideon and his house.
- **8:28** "Forty years" is the period of rest.
- **8:30** "Seventy sons" prepares for Abimelech's killing of them in chapter 9.
- **8:33** "Baal-berith" means "lord of the covenant". It was a god at Shechem (see 9:4).
- **8:35** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED.
- **9:1** "Abimelech" means "my father is king" (see 8:31). His mother was from Shechem, and he turns to her family.
- **9:2** "Your own flesh and blood" is literally "your bone and your flesh".
- **9:4** The silver from "the house of Baal-berith" shows the Shechemites paying for the plot from the temple treasury. "Worthless and reckless men" are hired thugs.
- **9:5** "Seventy sons ... on one stone" says the killing was an act of public slaughter. Seventy is a round number for a large family (8:30). Jotham survives by hiding.
- **9:6** "Beth-millo" is a part of Shechem, "the house of the fill", probably a fortified area. "The oak of the pillar" is a sacred tree at Shechem (see Joshua 24:26).
- **9:7** Jotham calls from Mount Gerizim, the mountain of blessing (Deuteronomy 11:29). The fable in verses 8 to 15 is one of the longest in the Bible.
- **9:9** The olive, fig and vine are the three best trees. They refuse to give up what they produce to rule. The oil and the wine "honour" or "cheer" gods and people, because they are used in offerings.
- **9:14** "The thornbush" is Hebrew *atad*, a thorny shrub that gives no real shade. The fable criticises the choice of a king who is worthless.
- **9:15** The thornbush offers false shelter and threatens fire. Fire does come out later (verse 49).
- **9:22** "Ruled" is Hebrew *sarar*, not the usual word for "reigned" (*malak*). The book avoids calling Abimelech a king of Israel in the full sense, even though he is made king at Shechem (verse 6).
- **9:23** "An evil spirit" is Hebrew *ruach ra'ah*. It is sent by God, as on Saul in 1 Samuel 16:14. It brings about the breakdown of trust between Abimelech and Shechem, as the means of punishment.
- **9:26** Gaal son of Ebed is a leader who sets himself against Abimelech.
- **9:37** "The centre of the land" is literally "the navel of the land". "The Diviners' Oak" is a sacred tree.
- **9:45** "Sowed it with salt" is a sign of curse, to make a place barren for ever.
- **9:46** "El-berith" ("God of the covenant") is the same god as Baal-berith in 8:33 and 9:4.
- **9:49** Setting fire to the stronghold fulfils the fire of Jotham's curse (verse 20) and of the thornbush.
- **9:53** A woman's millstone kills Abimelech. In 2 Samuel 11:21 this is recalled as a warning about getting close to a wall.
- **9:54** Abimelech asks his armour-bearer to kill him to avoid the shame of death at a woman's hand.
- **9:56** The chapter ends by stating that God repaid the crimes. Jotham's curse (verse 57) is fulfilled.
- **10:1** The brief notices of Tola, Jair, Ibzan, Elon and Abdon (10:1-5; 12:8-15) are sometimes called the "lesser judges". They give short lists, with no stories.
- **10:4** "Thirty sons who rode on thirty donkeys" is a sign of status. "Havvoth-jair" means "villages of Jair" (see Numbers 32:41).
- **10:6** The seven groups of gods are a list of the neighbours of Israel and mark the full extent of the unfaithfulness.
- **10:8** "That year" begins a period of eighteen years of oppression. Verse 8 describes the oppression on the east side of the Jordan.
- **10:11** The LORD's reply recalls his earlier rescues. "I will not save you again" is stark, and it is followed by the people's repentance and the LORD's change of heart (verse 16).
- **10:16** "He could bear Israel's misery no longer" is literally "his soul grew short over the misery of Israel", a figure for pity and impatience.
- **11:1** "The son of a prostitute" uses the word *zonah*. Jephthah is treated as an outsider because of his mother.
- **11:3** "The land of Tob" is in the north of Gilead. His followers are raiders.
- **11:9** Jephthah asks the elders to promise that he will be head if he wins. Their agreement is made before the LORD (verse 11).
- **11:12** Jephthah's message to the Ammonite king is a long diplomatic argument from history in verses 15 to 27. It follows the account in Numbers 20 and 21.
- **11:24** "Chemosh your god" is the god of Moab (Numbers 21:29), while the god of the Ammonites is Milcom. The text either mixes the two or the land in dispute belonged to Moab.
- **11:26** "Three hundred years" is a long period. It is hard to fit with other dates in the book, and it may be a round figure.
- **11:27** "The LORD, the Judge" is a rare title. Jephthah appeals to him to decide.
- **11:29** "The spirit of the LORD" is Hebrew *ruach YHWH*. See entry H-RUACH. The vow in verse 30 comes after it, and the text does not say the vow was commanded.
- **11:31** "Will belong to the LORD, and I will offer it up as a burnt offering" has a Hebrew conjunction (*vav*) that can mean "and" or "or". The verse has been read as a vow to sacrifice whatever came out (and so his daughter), or as a vow to dedicate the person to the LORD and, if it was an animal, to offer it. Human sacrifice is forbidden in Leviticus 18:21 and Deuteronomy 12:31 and 18:10. The WABV text follows the plain Hebrew, and the footnote gives the alternatives.
- **11:35** "You have brought me very low" is Jephthah's anguish. He blames his daughter for it, but she speaks for obedience to the vow.
- **11:37** "Weep for my youth" is literally "weep for my virginity" (*betulai*). The text stresses that she had never lain with a man (verse 39).
- **11:39** "He did to her what he had vowed" is rendered as written. The Hebrew does not say plainly what was done. Many readers have taken it to mean he sacrificed her, and others that she was kept in lifelong virginity as a dedicated woman. Both readings have a long history in Jewish and Christian interpretation.
- **11:40** "To mourn" is Hebrew *letannot*, a rare verb that can also mean "to tell the story" or "to commemorate". The custom of four days each year is noted.
- **12:1** The Ephraimites quarrel with Jephthah as they did with Gideon (8:1), because they were not called.
- **12:4** The Hebrew of the Ephraimite taunt, "fugitives of Ephraim", is obscure and may be a corruption.
- **12:6** "Shibboleth" means a stream or ear of grain. The Gileadites tested a dialect difference in the pronunciation. "Forty-two thousand" is a large round number.
- **12:7** Jephthah's six years of judging is the shortest of the major judges.
- **12:9** The marriages of Ibzan's thirty sons and daughters show wide family alliances. Bethlehem here may be the Bethlehem in Judah, or one in Zebulun (see Joshua 19:15).
- **12:15** "The hill country of the Amalekites" is a place in Ephraim named for a branch of the Amalekites.
- **13:1** "Forty years" is the length of the Philistine oppression, the longest in the book.
- **13:2** Manoah's wife is not named. A barren woman who is promised a son is a pattern in Genesis and 1 Samuel.
- **13:3** The angel appears first to the woman, not to her husband.
- **13:5** "Nazirite" is Hebrew *nazir*. See entry H-NAZIR. The rules are in Numbers 6, with three requirements: no wine or grape products, no cutting of the hair, and no contact with a dead body. Here the vow is for life, from the womb, as with Samuel in 1 Samuel 1:11. "He will begin to save Israel" says Samson will not finish the work.
- **13:6** "A man of God" is a title for a prophet or messenger. The woman's description shows awe.
- **13:16** "Manoah did not know that he was the angel of the LORD" shows he is slower than his wife to see who the visitor is.
- **13:18** "Wonderful" is Hebrew *pil'i*, a word that also means "beyond understanding". The angel does not give his name.
- **13:20** The angel goes up in the flame, as the angel did for Gideon in 6:21.
- **13:22** Manoah's fear that they will die repeats the belief of Exodus 33:20. His wife's reply shows more understanding.
- **13:24** "Samson" is Hebrew *Shimshon*, probably from *shemesh*, "sun". Beth-shemesh ("house of the sun") is nearby.
- **13:25** "Stir" is Hebrew *pa'am*, to push or move. The spirit begins to move him.
- **14:1** Timnah is a town on the border between Dan and the Philistines.
- **14:3** "She is right in my eyes" is the phrase that recurs in the book (17:6; 21:25) for doing what one pleases. His parents object that she is an "uncircumcised" Philistine.
- **14:4** "From the LORD" says the LORD was using Samson's desire as a way to strike the Philistines. His parents did not know this.
- **14:6** "Rushed on him" is Hebrew *tsalach*, a word of sudden power. It is used again in verse 19 and in 15:14.
- **14:8** Taking honey from the lion's body brings Samson into contact with a dead animal. This breaks the Nazirite rule of Numbers 6:6-7. The story does not say so.
- **14:10** "A feast" is Hebrew *mishteh*, a drinking feast. This is in tension with the Nazirite ban on wine (13:4).
- **14:14** The riddle is a short poem. Its point is that the answer, honey from the lion, was seen only by Samson.
- **14:15** The threat to burn the bride and her father's house is later carried out (15:6).
- **14:18** "If you had not ploughed with my heifer" is a saying that insults his wife.
- **14:19** Samson kills thirty men at Ashkelon to pay the debt, in a burst of anger.
- **14:20** His wife is given to his best man. This starts the new round of revenge in chapter 15.
- **15:4** "Foxes" is Hebrew *shu'alim*, which can also mean jackals.
- **15:6** The Philistines burn his wife and her father, which is what she feared (14:15). Samson's revenge follows.
- **15:8** "Leg and thigh" is an idiom of uncertain meaning. It may mean a complete slaughter.
- **15:11** Three thousand men of Judah hand Samson over to the Philistines. They fear the Philistines more than they trust their own judge.
- **15:15** "A fresh jawbone" is one that is not dry, and so strong.
- **15:16** The verse is a short poem with a play on the Hebrew *chamor*, "donkey", and *chamor*, "heap". This play on words is lost in English.
- **15:17** "Ramath-lehi" means "hill of the jawbone".
- **15:19** "En-hakkore" means "spring of the caller". The "hollow place" may be a hollow in the ground or in the jawbone. Samson prays only here for himself.
- **15:20** "Twenty years" is repeated in 16:31.
- **16:1** Gaza is a Philistine city. Samson visits a prostitute and then escapes by taking the gates.
- **16:3** The gate of Gaza is carried to a hill facing Hebron, many miles away.
- **16:4** Delilah is not called a Philistine, and her origin is not given. She lives in the Valley of Sorek.
- **16:5** "Eleven hundred pieces of silver" from each of the five rulers is a very large sum, 5,500 in all.
- **16:7** "Seven fresh bowstrings" are strings not yet dried, which would be strong.
- **16:13** The Hebrew of verses 13 and 14 is short. The Greek has a longer text that describes the weaving more fully. The Hebrew is followed here.
- **16:17** The secret is the Nazirite vow, with the hair as the sign. The strength comes from God, not from the hair.
- **16:19** "Bring him low" is Hebrew *'innot*, to humble or to afflict.
- **16:20** "He did not know that the LORD had left him" is the central statement of the chapter.
- **16:21** "Ground grain in the prison" was work for slaves and often for women.
- **16:23** Dagon was the god of the Philistines, associated with grain. See also 1 Samuel 5:1-5.
- **16:25** "Entertain" is Hebrew *tsachaq*, to laugh or make sport. The Philistines make fun of him.
- **16:27** "Three thousand on the roof" shows that the house was a very large building.
- **16:28** Samson prays for strength "just this once" and for revenge "for my two eyes". The Hebrew can also be read "one act of revenge for my two eyes".
- **16:30** "Let me die with the Philistines" is rendered as written. His death kills many more than his life had done.
- **16:31** Samson is buried in his father's tomb. The notice of twenty years' judging ends the story.
- **17:1** "Micah" is a short form of *Mikayehu*, "who is like the LORD?". The name is used with irony in a story of false worship.
- **17:2** The silver is eleven hundred pieces, the same amount each of the Philistine rulers offers Delilah in 16:5. The mother's curse on the thief, and then her blessing on her son, is a way of cancelling the curse.
- **17:3** The mother dedicates the silver "to the LORD" and uses it to have an image made. This is against the second of the Ten Words (Exodus 20:4).
- **17:5** "A shrine" is Hebrew *bet elohim*, "a house of God". "Household gods" is *teraphim*, which were small images used in the household. Micah "filled the hand" of one of his sons, that is, installed him as a priest, though he was not a Levite.
- **17:6** "In those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did what was right in his own eyes" is the refrain of the last chapters (also 18:1; 19:1; 21:25).
- **17:7** The young man is a Levite from Bethlehem, who is living as a resident. A Levite without a town of his own had no secure place.
- **17:10** "A father and a priest" shows that the priest was an honoured household figure.
- **17:13** Micah thinks that having a Levite as priest will guarantee the LORD's favour.
- **18:1** The Danites are looking for land because they have been pressed back by the Amorites (1:34).
- **18:7** "Laish" is a distant town in the north. "In the way of the Sidonians" says it lived securely, with no close allies.
- **18:12** "Mahaneh-dan" means "the camp of Dan" (see 13:25).
- **18:19** "Put your hand over your mouth" is an order to keep quiet. The Danites offer the Levite a more important post, and he accepts (verse 20).
- **18:27** The attack on Laish, a peaceful town, is told with no comment. It is carried out with the sword and fire.
- **18:30** "Jonathan son of Gershom, son of Moses" has a raised letter *nun* in the Hebrew text, which turns "Moses" (*Mosheh*) into "Manasseh" (*Menasheh*). The scribes who made the change did not want Moses' grandson to be a priest of an unlawful shrine. The WABV text follows the reading "Moses" and records the scribal change. "The day the land went into exile" refers to the exile of the northern tribes.
- **18:31** The Danite shrine lasts as long as the house of God is at Shiloh. A shrine with a calf was later at Dan (1 Kings 12:29-30).
- **19:1** "A concubine" is Hebrew *pilegesh*, a woman with a secondary legal status in a household. The Levite lives in the far hill country of Ephraim.
- **19:2** "Was unfaithful" is Hebrew *tiznEh*, from the root for sexual unfaithfulness. The Greek and Syriac texts read "was angry with him". The WABV text follows the Hebrew and records the other reading.
- **19:3** "To speak kindly to her" is literally "to speak to her heart". The Levite seeks to bring her back.
- **19:4** The father-in-law's long hospitality (verses 4 to 9) delays the departure until late in the day, which has dangerous consequences.
- **19:12** "A foreign town" is Jebus, the Jebusite city that was to become Jerusalem. The Levite prefers an Israelite town and goes on to Gibeah.
- **19:15** No one offers them a house for the night. Hospitality to a stranger was a duty, and this failure is the first sign of how corrupt the town is.
- **19:16** The old man is from Ephraim and is a resident in Benjaminite territory. He is the only one to help them.
- **19:18** "The house of the LORD" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek has "my house". The Levite may be going to Shiloh.
- **19:22** "So that we can have sex with him" is literally "so that we may know him", the verb *yada*. The scene closely follows Genesis 19:4-8.
- **19:24** The old man offers his virgin daughter and the Levite's concubine to the men. The offer is made as in Genesis 19:8, and the text does not approve of it. Rendered as written.
- **19:25** "They raped her and abused her" translates a Hebrew phrase that says "they knew her and dealt wantonly with her all night". The Levite is the one who pushes her out.
- **19:28** "But there was no answer" does not say that she was dead. The Greek adds "for she was dead". In 20:5 the Levite says that she died.
- **19:29** "Twelve pieces" are sent to the twelve tribes, as a summons. A similar act is in 1 Samuel 11:7. The cutting of the body is rendered as written.
- **19:30** "Nothing like this has ever happened" is the response of all Israel. It recognises that this is a crime without precedent.
- **20:1** "From Dan to Beersheba" is the phrase for the whole land from north to south. The assembly is at Mizpah, in Benjamin's territory.
- **20:2** "Four hundred thousand" is a large round number. The numbers in this chapter are very large and may be symbolic.
- **20:4** The Levite's account to the assembly differs from the story in chapter 19. He says the men meant to kill him, and does not mention that he pushed his concubine out to them.
- **20:6** "A lewd and disgraceful act" is Hebrew *zimmah u-nevalah*. The two words are used in the law for the worst kinds of sexual wrong.
- **20:13** "Clear the evil out of Israel" repeats the formula of Deuteronomy 13:5 and 17:7.
- **20:16** The seven hundred left-handed slingers recall Ehud (3:15). The Benjaminites are also described as "son of the right hand".
- **20:18** Judah goes first, as in 1:1-2. The LORD tells them to go up, and yet they lose twice (verses 21 and 25). The text does not explain this.
- **20:27** The ark is at Bethel in these years, and Phinehas son of Eleazar is serving before it. This places the events early in the period of the judges, soon after Joshua.
- **20:31** The ambush and the feigned retreat resemble the capture of Ai in Joshua 8.
- **20:35** The total of the Benjaminite dead is given as 25,100 here and 25,000 in verse 46. The subtotals in verses 44 and 45 (18,000, 5,000 and 2,000) add up to 25,000.
- **20:47** Six hundred men survive and hide at the rock of Rimmon for four months.
- **20:48** The destruction of the towns, people and animals of Benjamin is rendered as written.
- **21:1** The oath not to give a daughter to Benjamin was sworn at Mizpah. It was not mentioned in chapter 20.
- **21:5** "He will surely be put to death" is the oath against any tribe that did not come to Mizpah.
- **21:11** "Devote to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. The ban is used here against a town in Israel, as in Deuteronomy 13:12-18. The order to kill males and women who have had sex, and to keep the virgins, is rendered as written.
- **21:12** Four hundred virgins are brought to Shiloh. They are not enough for the six hundred survivors (verse 14).
- **21:15** "The LORD had made a gap in the tribes" says the people see the near loss of a tribe as the LORD's act.
- **21:19** "The yearly festival of the LORD at Shiloh" is probably the autumn festival (see 1 Samuel 1:3).
- **21:21** "Seize" is Hebrew *chatav*, to snatch or catch. The Benjaminites carry off the dancers by force. This is rendered as written.
- **21:22** The argument that the fathers will not be guilty of breaking the oath, because they did not give the women, is a legal excuse.
- **21:25** The final refrain is the book's judgement on the period. It does not say whether the time would be better with a king.
