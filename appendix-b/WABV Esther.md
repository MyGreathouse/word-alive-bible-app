## Esther 1-2: Vashti and the Making of a Queen

### Chapter 1

**1** In the days of Ahasuerus (the Ahasuerus who ruled from India to Cush, over a hundred and twenty-seven provinces),

**2** in those days, when King Ahasuerus sat on his royal throne in Susa the citadel,

**3** in the third year of his reign he gave a banquet for all his officials and servants. The army of Persia and Media, the nobles and the governors of the provinces were before him,

**4** while he displayed the wealth of his glorious kingdom and the splendour and pride of his greatness for many days, a hundred and eighty days.

**5** When these days were over, the king gave a banquet lasting seven days for all the people, great and small, who were in Susa the citadel, in the court of the garden of the king's palace.

**6** There were white cotton curtains and blue hangings, fastened with cords of fine linen and purple to silver rings on marble pillars, and couches of gold and silver on a floor of porphyry, marble, mother-of-pearl and other precious stones.

**7** Drinks were served in golden cups, each cup different from the others, and the royal wine was plentiful, as the king could afford.

**8** The drinking followed this order: "No one is to be forced." For the king had ordered all the officials of his house to do as each man wished.

**9** Queen Vashti also gave a banquet for the women in the royal palace of King Ahasuerus.

**10** On the seventh day, when the king was merry with wine, he ordered Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha, Abagtha, Zethar and Carkas, the seven eunuchs who served King Ahasuerus,

**11** to bring Queen Vashti before the king, wearing her royal crown, to show her beauty to the peoples and the officials, for she was beautiful to look at.

**12** But Queen Vashti refused to come at the king's command delivered by the eunuchs. The king was very angry, and his anger burned in him.

**13** Then the king spoke to the wise men who understood the times (for this was how the king handled matters with all who knew law and legal judgement,

**14** and those closest to him were Carshena, Shethar, Admatha, Tarshish, Meres, Marsena and Memucan, the seven officials of Persia and Media who had access to the king and held the first places in the kingdom):

**15** "By law, what is to be done with Queen Vashti, because she did not obey the command of King Ahasuerus delivered by the eunuchs?"

**16** Memucan said in front of the king and the officials, "Queen Vashti has wronged not only the king, but also all the officials and all the peoples in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus.

**17** For the queen's conduct will become known to all women, and they will look down on their husbands, saying, 'King Ahasuerus ordered Queen Vashti to be brought before him, and she did not come.'

**18** This very day the noble women of Persia and Media who have heard of the queen's conduct will say the same to all the king's officials, and there will be plenty of contempt and anger.

**19** If it pleases the king, let a royal order go out from him, and let it be written in the laws of Persia and Media so that it cannot be changed, that Vashti is never again to come before King Ahasuerus. And let the king give her royal position to another who is better than she.

**20** When the king's decree is announced throughout his kingdom, large as it is, all women will give honour to their husbands, from the greatest to the least."

**21** The advice pleased the king and the officials, and the king did as Memucan said.

**22** He sent letters to all the royal provinces, to each province in its own script and to each people in its own language, that every man should be master in his own house and speak in the language of his own people.

### Chapter 2

**1** After these things, when the king's anger had cooled, he remembered Vashti and what she had done and what had been decreed against her.

**2** Then the king's attendants said, "Let beautiful young virgins be sought for the king.

**3** Let the king appoint officers in all the provinces of his kingdom to gather every beautiful young virgin to the women's quarters in Susa the citadel, under the care of Hegai the king's eunuch, who is in charge of the women. Let their beauty treatments be given them.

**4** Then let the young woman who pleases the king be queen in place of Vashti." The advice pleased the king, and he did so.

**5** In Susa the citadel there was a Jew whose name was Mordecai son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, a Benjaminite,

**6** who had been taken from Jerusalem among the captives who were taken with Jeconiah king of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had taken captive.

**7** He was bringing up Hadassah, that is Esther, his uncle's daughter, for she had neither father nor mother. The young woman had a beautiful figure and was beautiful to look at, and when her father and mother died, Mordecai adopted her as his own daughter.

**8** When the king's order and decree were announced and many young women were gathered in Susa the citadel under the care of Hegai, Esther was also taken to the king's palace and put under the care of Hegai, who was in charge of the women.

**9** The young woman pleased him and won his favour. He quickly provided her with her beauty treatments and her food, and seven chosen young women from the king's palace, and moved her and her young women to the best place in the women's quarters.

**10** Esther had not told anyone about her people or her family, for Mordecai had told her not to.

**11** Every day Mordecai walked back and forth in front of the court of the women's quarters to find out how Esther was and what was happening to her.

**12** When the turn came for each young woman to go in to King Ahasuerus, after twelve months of the treatment for women, since that was the length of their time of beautifying, six months with oil of myrrh and six months with perfumes and women's treatments,

**13** when the young woman went in to the king, she was given whatever she asked for to take with her from the women's quarters to the king's palace.

**14** In the evening she went in, and in the morning she came back to a second women's quarters, under the care of Shaashgaz the king's eunuch, who was in charge of the concubines. She did not go in to the king again unless he delighted in her and she was called by name.

**15** When the turn came for Esther daughter of Abihail, the uncle of Mordecai, who had adopted her as his own daughter, to go in to the king, she asked for nothing except what Hegai the king's eunuch, in charge of the women, advised. And Esther won the favour of everyone who saw her.

**16** Esther was taken to King Ahasuerus in his royal palace in the tenth month, the month of Tebeth, in the seventh year of his reign.

**17** The king loved Esther more than all the other women, and she won more favour and kindness from him than all the other virgins, so that he put the royal crown on her head and made her queen in place of Vashti.

**18** Then the king gave a great banquet for all his officials and servants, Esther's banquet. He also announced a holiday for the provinces and gave gifts with royal generosity.

**19** When virgins were gathered a second time, Mordecai was sitting at the king's gate.

**20** Esther had not told anyone about her family or her people, as Mordecai had told her, for Esther still did what Mordecai said, as she had when he brought her up.

**21** In those days, while Mordecai was sitting at the king's gate, Bigthan and Teresh, two of the king's eunuchs who guarded the doorway, became angry and plotted to lay hands on King Ahasuerus.

**22** The matter became known to Mordecai, and he told Queen Esther, and Esther told the king in Mordecai's name.

**23** When the matter was looked into and found to be true, both men were hanged on a gallows. It was written in the book of the records in the king's presence.

## Esther 3-4: Haman's Decree and Esther's Decision

### Chapter 3

**1** After these things King Ahasuerus promoted Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite and raised him up and set his seat above all the officials who were with him.

**2** All the king's servants who were at the king's gate knelt and bowed down to Haman, for the king had commanded this for him. But Mordecai did not kneel or bow down.

**3** Then the king's servants at the king's gate said to Mordecai, "Why do you disobey the king's command?"

**4** When they spoke to him day after day and he would not listen to them, they told Haman, to see whether Mordecai's words would hold, for he had told them that he was a Jew.

**5** When Haman saw that Mordecai did not kneel or bow down to him, Haman was filled with rage.

**6** But it seemed a small thing to him to lay hands on Mordecai alone, for they had told him who Mordecai's people were. So Haman sought to destroy all the Jews, Mordecai's people, throughout the whole kingdom of Ahasuerus.

**7** In the first month, the month of Nisan, in the twelfth year of King Ahasuerus, Pur, that is the lot, was cast in front of Haman, day by day and month by month, until the twelfth month, the month of Adar.

**8** Then Haman said to King Ahasuerus, "There is a certain people, scattered and separate among the peoples in all the provinces of your kingdom. Their laws are different from those of every other people, and they do not keep the king's laws, so it is not in the king's interest to leave them alone.

**9** If it pleases the king, let it be written that they are to be destroyed, and I will pay ten thousand talents of silver into the hands of those who do the king's business, to put into the king's treasuries."

**10** So the king took his signet ring from his hand and gave it to Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of the Jews.

**11** The king said to Haman, "The silver is given to you, and the people too, to do with them as you see fit."

**12** Then the king's scribes were called on the thirteenth day of the first month, and an order was written, exactly as Haman commanded, to the king's satraps and to the governors of each province and to the officials of each people, to each province in its own script and each people in its own language. It was written in the name of King Ahasuerus and sealed with the king's ring.

**13** Letters were sent by couriers to all the king's provinces, to destroy, kill and wipe out all the Jews, young and old, women and children, in one day, the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, the month of Adar, and to plunder their possessions.

**14** A copy of the document was to be issued as a law in every province and made known to all the peoples, so that they would be ready for that day.

**15** The couriers went out in haste at the king's command, and the law was issued in Susa the citadel. The king and Haman sat down to drink, but the city of Susa was thrown into confusion.

### Chapter 4

**1** When Mordecai learned all that had been done, Mordecai tore his clothes and put on sackcloth and ashes, and went out into the middle of the city, crying with a loud and bitter cry.

**2** He came as far as the front of the king's gate, for no one could enter the king's gate wearing sackcloth.

**3** In every province, wherever the king's command and law reached, there was great mourning among the Jews, with fasting, weeping and wailing, and many lay in sackcloth and ashes.

**4** When Esther's young women and her eunuchs came and told her, the queen was in great distress. She sent clothes for Mordecai to wear, to take off his sackcloth, but he would not accept them.

**5** Then Esther called for Hathach, one of the king's eunuchs whom he had appointed to attend her, and ordered him to go to Mordecai to find out what this was and why.

**6** Hathach went out to Mordecai in the town square in front of the king's gate,

**7** and Mordecai told him all that had happened to him, and the exact sum of silver that Haman had promised to pay into the king's treasuries for the destruction of the Jews.

**8** He also gave him a copy of the written law that had been issued in Susa for their destruction, to show Esther and tell her, and to order her to go to the king to ask for his favour and plead with him for her people.

**9** Hathach went and told Esther what Mordecai had said.

**10** Then Esther spoke to Hathach and told him to say to Mordecai:

**11** "All the king's servants and the people of the king's provinces know that any man or woman who goes in to the king in the inner court without being called has one law: to be put to death, unless the king holds out the golden sceptre to him so that he may live. And I have not been called to come in to the king for these thirty days."

**12** They told Mordecai what Esther had said.

**13** Then Mordecai told them to reply to Esther: "Do not think that you will escape in the king's house any more than all the other Jews.

**14** For if you keep silent at this time, relief and deliverance will come to the Jews from another place, but you and your father's house will perish. And who knows whether you have come to the kingdom for a time like this?"

**15** Then Esther told them to reply to Mordecai:

**16** "Go, gather all the Jews who are in Susa, and fast for me. Do not eat or drink for three days, night or day. I and my young women will fast in the same way. Then I will go in to the king, though it is against the law, and if I perish, I perish."

**17** So Mordecai went away and did all that Esther had told him.

## Esther 5-7: The Banquets and the Fall of Haman

### Chapter 5

**1** On the third day Esther put on her royal robes and stood in the inner court of the king's palace, opposite the king's quarters. The king was sitting on his royal throne in the throne room, opposite the entrance of the palace.

**2** When the king saw Queen Esther standing in the court, she won his favour, and the king held out to Esther the golden sceptre in his hand. Esther came near and touched the tip of the sceptre.

**3** The king said to her, "What is it, Queen Esther? What is your request? It will be given to you, even up to half the kingdom."

**4** Esther said, "If it pleases the king, let the king and Haman come today to the banquet that I have prepared for him."

**5** The king said, "Bring Haman at once, so that we may do as Esther asks." So the king and Haman came to the banquet that Esther had prepared.

**6** While they were drinking wine, the king said to Esther, "What is your wish? It will be granted to you. What is your request? Even up to half the kingdom, it will be done."

**7** Esther answered, "My wish and my request is this:

**8** if I have found favour with the king, and if it pleases the king to grant my wish and do what I ask, let the king and Haman come to the banquet that I will prepare for them, and tomorrow I will do as the king says."

**9** Haman went out that day glad and happy. But when Haman saw Mordecai at the king's gate, who did not stand up or tremble before him, Haman was filled with rage against Mordecai.

**10** But Haman held himself back and went home, and he sent for his friends and his wife Zeresh.

**11** Haman told them about the splendour of his riches, and the number of his sons, and all the ways the king had honoured him, and how he had raised him above the officials and the king's servants.

**12** Haman said, "Even Queen Esther let no one but me come with the king to the banquet she had prepared. And tomorrow too I am invited by her, with the king.

**13** But all this is worth nothing to me as long as I see Mordecai the Jew sitting at the king's gate."

**14** His wife Zeresh and all his friends said to him, "Have a gallows made, fifty cubits high, and in the morning ask the king to have Mordecai hanged on it. Then go with the king to the banquet and enjoy yourself." The advice pleased Haman, and he had the gallows made.

### Chapter 6

**1** That night the king could not sleep. He ordered the book of records, the chronicles, to be brought, and they were read to the king.

**2** It was found written there that Mordecai had told about Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king's eunuchs who guarded the doorway, who had plotted to lay hands on King Ahasuerus.

**3** The king said, "What honour or reward has been given to Mordecai for this?" The king's attendants said, "Nothing has been done for him."

**4** The king said, "Who is in the court?" Now Haman had just come into the outer court of the king's palace to speak to the king about hanging Mordecai on the gallows that he had prepared for him.

**5** The king's attendants said to him, "Haman is standing in the court." The king said, "Let him come in."

**6** So Haman came in, and the king said to him, "What should be done for the man whom the king wants to honour?" Haman said to himself, "Whom would the king want to honour more than me?"

**7** So Haman said to the king, "For the man whom the king wants to honour,

**8** let royal robes be brought, which the king has worn, and a horse that the king has ridden, with a royal crown on its head.

**9** Let the robes and the horse be handed over to one of the king's most noble officials, and let them dress the man whom the king wants to honour, and lead him on the horse through the town square, calling out in front of him, 'This is what is done for the man whom the king wants to honour.'"

**10** Then the king said to Haman, "Quickly, take the robes and the horse as you have said, and do this for Mordecai the Jew, who sits at the king's gate. Do not leave out anything of all that you have said."

**11** So Haman took the robes and the horse, and dressed Mordecai and led him on horseback through the town square, calling out in front of him, "This is what is done for the man whom the king wants to honour."

**12** Then Mordecai went back to the king's gate. But Haman hurried home, mourning, with his head covered.

**13** Haman told his wife Zeresh and all his friends everything that had happened to him. His advisers and his wife Zeresh said to him, "If Mordecai, before whom you have begun to fall, is of the Jewish people, you will not win against him, but will surely fall before him."

**14** While they were still talking with him, the king's eunuchs arrived and hurried to bring Haman to the banquet that Esther had prepared.

### Chapter 7

**1** So the king and Haman went to the banquet with Queen Esther.

**2** On the second day, while they were drinking wine, the king said again to Esther, "What is your wish, Queen Esther? It will be granted to you. What is your request? Even up to half the kingdom, it will be done."

**3** Queen Esther answered, "If I have found favour with you, king, and if it pleases the king, let my life be given to me as my wish, and my people as my request.

**4** For we have been sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, to be killed and to be wiped out. If we had only been sold as male and female slaves, I would have kept silent, for the trouble would not be worth the king's loss."

**5** King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther, "Who is he, and where is he, who has dared to do this?"

**6** Esther said, "An enemy and adversary, this wicked Haman." Then Haman was terrified before the king and queen.

**7** The king got up in anger from the wine and went out into the palace garden, but Haman stayed to beg Queen Esther for his life, for he saw that the king had decided on harm for him.

**8** When the king came back from the palace garden to the place where they were drinking wine, Haman had thrown himself on the couch where Esther was. The king said, "Will he even assault the queen in front of me, in my own house?" As the word left the king's mouth, they covered Haman's face.

**9** Then Harbona, one of the eunuchs who served the king, said, "There is also the gallows that Haman made for Mordecai, who spoke up for the king. It is standing at Haman's house, fifty cubits high." The king said, "Hang him on it."

**10** So they hanged Haman on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai. Then the king's anger cooled.

## Esther 8-10: The Second Decree and Purim

### Chapter 8

**1** On that day King Ahasuerus gave Queen Esther the house of Haman, the enemy of the Jews. Mordecai came before the king, for Esther had told what he was to her.

**2** The king took off his signet ring, which he had taken back from Haman, and gave it to Mordecai. And Esther put Mordecai in charge of Haman's house.

**3** Then Esther spoke to the king again and fell at his feet, weeping, and begged him to put an end to the evil of Haman the Agagite and his plan that he had made against the Jews.

**4** The king held out the golden sceptre to Esther, and Esther got up and stood in front of the king.

**5** She said, "If it pleases the king, and if I have found favour with him, and if the matter seems right to the king, and I am pleasing to him, let an order be written to cancel the letters that Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite wrote to destroy the Jews in all the king's provinces.

**6** For how can I bear to see the disaster that will come on my people? How can I bear to see the destruction of my family?"

**7** King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther and Mordecai the Jew, "Look, I have given Esther the house of Haman, and they have hanged him on the gallows because he lifted his hand against the Jews.

**8** Now you write about the Jews as you see fit, in the king's name, and seal it with the king's ring, for what is written in the king's name and sealed with the king's ring cannot be reversed."

**9** The king's scribes were called at that time, in the third month, the month of Sivan, on the twenty-third day. An order was written, exactly as Mordecai commanded, to the Jews and to the satraps, governors and officials of the provinces from India to Cush, a hundred and twenty-seven provinces, to each province in its own script and each people in its own language, and to the Jews in their own script and language.

**10** He wrote in the name of King Ahasuerus and sealed it with the king's ring, and sent the letters by mounted couriers riding fast horses from the royal stables.

**11** The letters said that the king allowed the Jews in every town to gather and defend their lives, to destroy, kill and wipe out any armed force of any people or province that would attack them, with their women and children, and to plunder their possessions,

**12** on one day in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, the month of Adar.

**13** A copy of the document was to be issued as a law in every province and made known to all the peoples, so that the Jews would be ready on that day to take revenge on their enemies.

**14** The couriers, riding their fast horses from the royal stables, went out in haste at the king's command. The law was issued in Susa the citadel.

**15** Mordecai went out from the king's presence in royal robes of blue and white, with a large gold crown and a robe of fine linen and purple. The city of Susa shouted and rejoiced.

**16** For the Jews there was light and gladness and joy and honour.

**17** In every province and in every town, wherever the king's command and law reached, there was gladness and joy among the Jews, a banquet and a holiday. Many from the peoples of the land declared themselves Jews, for fear of the Jews had fallen on them.

### Chapter 9

**1** In the twelfth month, the month of Adar, on the thirteenth day, when the king's command and law were due to be carried out, on the very day when the enemies of the Jews had hoped to overpower them, it was turned round, so that the Jews overpowered those who hated them.

**2** The Jews gathered in their towns in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus to lay hands on those who sought their harm. No one could stand against them, for fear of them had fallen on all the peoples.

**3** All the officials of the provinces, the satraps, the governors and the king's agents helped the Jews, because fear of Mordecai had fallen on them.

**4** For Mordecai was great in the king's house, and his fame spread through all the provinces, for the man Mordecai became greater and greater.

**5** The Jews struck all their enemies with the sword, killing and destroying, and did as they pleased to those who hated them.

**6** In Susa the citadel the Jews killed and destroyed five hundred men,

**7** and they killed Parshandatha, Dalphon, Aspatha,

**8** Poratha, Adalia, Aridatha,

**9** Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai and Vaizatha,

**10** the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews. But they did not lay a hand on the plunder.

**11** That day the number of those killed in Susa the citadel was reported to the king.

**12** The king said to Queen Esther, "In Susa the citadel the Jews have killed and destroyed five hundred men and the ten sons of Haman. What have they done in the rest of the king's provinces? Now what is your wish? It will be granted to you. What more do you ask? It will be done."

**13** Esther said, "If it pleases the king, let the Jews in Susa be allowed to do tomorrow also as they did today, and let the ten sons of Haman be hanged on the gallows."

**14** So the king ordered that it be done. A law was issued in Susa, and the ten sons of Haman were hanged.

**15** The Jews in Susa gathered also on the fourteenth day of the month of Adar and killed three hundred men in Susa, but they did not lay a hand on the plunder.

**16** The rest of the Jews in the king's provinces gathered and defended their lives and had rest from their enemies. They killed seventy-five thousand of those who hated them, but they did not lay a hand on the plunder.

**17** This was on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar, and on the fourteenth day they rested and made it a day of banqueting and gladness.

**18** But the Jews in Susa gathered on the thirteenth and on the fourteenth, and on the fifteenth they rested and made it a day of banqueting and gladness.

**19** For this reason the Jews of the villages, who live in unwalled towns, keep the fourteenth day of the month of Adar as a day of gladness and banqueting, a holiday, a day for sending portions of food to one another.

**20** Mordecai wrote down these things and sent letters to all the Jews in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, near and far,

**21** requiring them to keep the fourteenth day of the month of Adar and the fifteenth day of the same month, every year,

**22** as the days on which the Jews had rest from their enemies, and as the month that had been turned for them from sorrow to gladness and from mourning to a holiday. They were to make them days of banqueting and gladness, days for sending portions of food to one another and gifts to the poor.

**23** So the Jews took on what they had begun to do, and what Mordecai had written to them.

**24** For Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews, had plotted against the Jews to destroy them, and had cast Pur, that is the lot, to crush and destroy them.

**25** But when it came before the king, he ordered by letter that Haman's evil plan that he had made against the Jews should come back on his own head, and that he and his sons should be hanged on the gallows.

**26** So they called these days Purim, after the word Pur. Because of all that was written in this letter, and what they had seen in this matter, and what had happened to them,

**27** the Jews took on themselves and their descendants and all who joined them the duty, never to be dropped, of keeping these two days as written and at the time set, every year,

**28** so that these days would be remembered and kept in every generation, in every family, province and town, and that these days of Purim would never cease among the Jews, and their memory would never fade among their descendants.

**29** Queen Esther daughter of Abihail and Mordecai the Jew wrote with full authority to confirm this second letter about Purim.

**30** Letters were sent to all the Jews in the hundred and twenty-seven provinces of the kingdom of Ahasuerus, in words of peace and truth,

**31** to confirm these days of Purim at their set times, as Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had laid down for them, and as they had taken on themselves and their descendants, with the matters of the fasts and their crying out.

**32** Esther's command confirmed these rules of Purim, and it was written in a book.

### Chapter 10

**1** King Ahasuerus imposed forced labour on the land and on the coasts of the sea.

**2** All his acts of power and strength, and the full account of the greatness of Mordecai, to which the king raised him, are they not written in the Book of the Records of the Kings of Media and Persia?

**3** For Mordecai the Jew was second to King Ahasuerus, and great among the Jews and loved by the multitude of his relatives, for he sought the good of his people and spoke peace for all his descendants.

*End of the book of Esther. Review note: Esther 8 to 10 was drafted in WABV wording from the Hebrew text. The second decree allows the Jews to destroy, kill and wipe out any armed force that attacks them, "with their women and children" (8:11), and chapter 9 tells of the killing of five hundred men in Susa, the ten sons of Haman, three hundred more the next day, and seventy-five thousand in the provinces. These are rendered as written, with no softening and no comment on the ethics, and the footnotes explain the language and the questions of the text (for example whether 8:11 allows the killing of the attackers' women and children, or describes what was threatened in the first decree). The Hebrew book does not name God. The Greek additions are not part of this text. The wording of 9:31 is hard, and the footnotes record the matter. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **Title** Esther is a story set at the Persian court in the reign of Ahasuerus (Xerxes I, 486 to 465 BCE). It tells how a Jewish woman became queen and saved her people from a plan to destroy them, and it gives the origin of the festival of Purim. The Hebrew text never names God. The Greek translation adds six passages, including prayers and the divine name, and they are not part of this translation. The book is a tale of reversals. Its date and its historical details (for example the name of the queen) are discussed by scholars.
- **1:1** "Ahasuerus" is the Hebrew form (*Achashverosh*) of the Persian name Xerxes. "From India to Cush" is the full extent of the Persian empire, from the Indus to Ethiopia. "A hundred and twenty-seven provinces" is the number of administrative districts (Hebrew *medinot*). They are smaller than the twenty satrapies named by Greek writers.
- **1:2** "Susa the citadel" is the Persian winter capital, Hebrew *Shushan ha-birah*. See Nehemiah 1:1.
- **1:3** "The third year of his reign" is 483 BCE. The gathering of "the army of Persia and Media" may be connected with the plans for the war against Greece.
- **1:4** "A hundred and eighty days" is a long display of royal wealth.
- **1:6** The description of the court uses rare Persian and Hebrew words (*chur*, *karpas*, *tekhelet*, *bahat*, *shesh*, *dar*, *sokheret*). "White cotton curtains and blue hangings", "porphyry, marble, mother-of-pearl" and "other precious stones" are the best guesses. Some of the names of the stones are uncertain.
- **1:8** "No one is to be forced" translates a hard Hebrew phrase (*ein ones*). The drinking was by the king's "order" (*dat*), a Persian loan-word for law. It says that each guest might drink as he wished.
- **1:10** "Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha, Abagtha, Zethar and Carkas" are seven eunuchs (Hebrew *sarisim*), whose names are Persian. They are the king's messengers.
- **1:11** "Wearing her royal crown" is all the text says about how she was to appear. Later Jewish tradition says that she was to come only wearing the crown, but the Hebrew does not say this.
- **1:12** "Queen Vashti refused to come" gives no reason. The text does not say why she refused or whether the refusal was right. "His anger burned in him" is the Hebrew idiom for great anger.
- **1:13** "The wise men who understood the times" are advisers who knew law and custom. "Law and legal judgement" is Hebrew *dat va-din*.
- **1:14** "The seven officials of Persia and Media" are the king's seven closest advisers (see Ezra 7:14).
- **1:16** "Memucan" gives the advice. His claim is that the queen's conduct would harm the rule of husbands in every house.
- **1:19** "Let it be written in the laws of Persia and Media so that it cannot be changed" refers to the Persian idea that a royal order could not be cancelled (see Daniel 6:8, 12, 15 and Esther 8:8).
- **1:22** "Each province in its own script and each people in its own language" is the practice of the Persian chancery. "That every man should be master in his own house and speak in the language of his own people" is obscure in the Hebrew, especially the last clause. It may refer to marriages between peoples, where the husband's language is to be used.
- **2:1** "He remembered Vashti and what she had done and what had been decreed against her" says that the king regretted the decree. The text does not say whether he changed his mind.
- **2:2** "Let beautiful young virgins be sought for the king" is the plan of the attendants. The women are "gathered" by officers. The text does not say whether they came willingly. It is rendered as written.
- **2:3** "Hegai the king's eunuch" is the keeper of the women. "Beauty treatments" translates Hebrew *tamruqim*, the oils and spices of verse 12.
- **2:5** "Mordecai son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, a Benjaminite" gives a line that goes back to Kish, the father of Saul (1 Samuel 9:1). The Persian form of his name may come from the god Marduk.
- **2:6** "Who had been taken from Jerusalem among the captives who were taken with Jeconiah" may refer to Mordecai or to Kish. If it refers to Mordecai, he would be over a hundred years old in this time (597 BCE to 479 BCE). The Hebrew allows both.
- **2:7** "Hadassah, that is Esther" gives her two names. *Hadassah* is Hebrew for "myrtle". *Esther* may be a Persian word for "star" or may be linked to the goddess Ishtar. "His uncle's daughter" means his cousin. "Adopted her as his own daughter" says that Mordecai took her as a daughter after her parents died.
- **2:8** "Esther was also taken to the king's palace" is told with the same passive verb as for all the women. The text does not say whether she went willingly.
- **2:9** "Won his favour" is Hebrew *nasa chesed*, "gained kindness". Hegai gives her seven attendants and the best place.
- **2:10** "Esther had not told anyone about her people or her family, for Mordecai had told her not to" is repeated in verse 20. It is the hidden identity that the story turns on.
- **2:12** "Twelve months" of beauty treatment before her turn is the Persian custom. "Six months with oil of myrrh and six months with perfumes" is the order of the treatment.
- **2:14** "In the evening she went in, and in the morning she came back to a second women's quarters" says that each woman went to the king for one night and then was sent to the quarters of the concubines. The text does not describe the night further. It is rendered as written.
- **2:16** "The tenth month, the month of Tebeth" is December and January. "The seventh year of his reign" is 479 to 478 BCE, four years after Vashti's refusal.
- **2:17** "The king loved Esther more than all the other women" is the reason for the choice. "The royal crown" is the same as in 1:11.
- **2:18** "A holiday for the provinces" translates Hebrew *hanachah*, which can mean rest, release from taxes or from service. "Esther's banquet" is the feast of her crowning.
- **2:19** "When virgins were gathered a second time" is obscure. It may mean a second gathering of women, or it may be a later addition. "Mordecai was sitting at the king's gate" says that he held an office at the palace.
- **2:21** "Bigthan and Teresh" are two of the king's eunuchs. The plot is told here to prepare for chapter 6.
- **2:22** "Told the king in Mordecai's name" says that Esther gave Mordecai the credit.
- **2:23** "Hanged on a gallows" is Hebrew *al etz*, "on a tree" or "on wood". It may mean hanging or impaling. "The book of the records" is Hebrew *sefer divre ha-yamim*, the royal chronicle of Persia. It is the name that the books of Chronicles carry in Hebrew.
- **3:1** "Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite" is named as an Agagite. Agag was the king of the Amalekites whom Saul spared and Samuel killed (1 Samuel 15:8, 32-33). The name may point to Haman's descent, and it sets him against Mordecai, a descendant of Saul's father Kish (2:5). "Set his seat above all the officials" makes him the king's chief minister.
- **3:2** "Knelt and bowed down" is Hebrew *kor'im u-mishtachavim*, the homage given to a ruler. "For the king had commanded this for him" says that the king ordered it. "Mordecai did not kneel or bow down" is told without a reason. The text does not say why he refused. Later tradition suggests idolatry or hatred of Amalek, but the Hebrew gives no reason beyond 3:4.
- **3:4** "To see whether Mordecai's words would hold" means to see whether his reason would be accepted. "He had told them that he was a Jew" is the only reason that the text gives.
- **3:6** "It seemed a small thing to him to lay hands on Mordecai alone" gives the move from a personal quarrel to a plan to destroy a whole people. It is rendered as written.
- **3:7** "The twelfth year" is 474 BCE. "Pur" is a word for "lot" that is also found in an Akkadian form. The text explains it for the reader, and it gives the name of the festival of Purim (9:24-26). "Day by day and month by month" says that the lot was cast to choose a day and a month. The Hebrew text names the month but gives the result only in verse 13.
- **3:8** "Scattered and separate among the peoples" is Hebrew *mefuzzar u-meforad*. "Their laws are different" says that the Jews' laws (*dat*) are different from those of other peoples. "It is not in the king's interest" is Haman's argument.
- **3:9** "Ten thousand talents of silver" is about three hundred and forty tonnes. It is a very large sum. Greek writers give the yearly income of the Persian empire as about fifteen thousand talents.
- **3:10** "Took his signet ring from his hand and gave it to Haman" gives him the king's authority. "The enemy of the Jews" is Hebrew *tsorer ha-yehudim*.
- **3:11** "The silver is given to you, and the people too, to do with them as you see fit" can be read as a refusal of the money and a giving of the people, or as giving both. The Hebrew is ambiguous. It is rendered as written.
- **3:12** "The thirteenth day of the first month" is Nisan, in the spring of 474 BCE. "Satraps" is Hebrew *achashdarpenim*, a Persian title. "Sealed with the king's ring" makes the order binding (see 8:8).
- **3:13** "To destroy, kill and wipe out all the Jews, young and old, women and children, in one day" uses three verbs (*lehashmid, laharog u-le'abbed*). It is rendered as written. In the Greek, the text of the letter (Addition B) follows this verse. It is not part of this translation. "The thirteenth day of the twelfth month" is the thirteenth of Adar.
- **3:14** "A copy of the document was to be issued as a law in every province" translates *dat*. The order was to be made known to every people.
- **3:15** "The king and Haman sat down to drink, but the city of Susa was thrown into confusion" ends the chapter with a contrast. The Hebrew word for "confusion" is *navokhah*.
- **4:1** "Tore his clothes and put on sackcloth and ashes" are the signs of mourning (Genesis 37:34; 2 Samuel 13:19; Jonah 3:6). "A loud and bitter cry" echoes Genesis 27:34.
- **4:2** "No one could enter the king's gate wearing sackcloth" is a Persian court rule.
- **4:3** "Fasting, weeping and wailing" is the response in every province. The text does not mention prayer.
- **4:4** "She sent clothes for Mordecai to wear" shows that Esther did not yet know the cause. Mordecai refused them.
- **4:5** "Hathach" is one of the king's eunuchs who attended Esther.
- **4:7** "The exact sum of silver" is the amount in 3:9.
- **4:8** "A copy of the written law" is shown to Esther so that she can explain it to the king. Mordecai tells her to "go to the king to ask for his favour and plead with him for her people".
- **4:11** "Anyone who goes in to the king in the inner court without being called has one law: to be put to death, unless the king holds out the golden sceptre" is a Persian court rule. "I have not been called ... these thirty days" says that her place with the king is not secure.
- **4:14** "Relief and deliverance will come to the Jews from another place" is Hebrew *revach ve-hatsalah ya'amod la-yehudim mi-makom acher*. "Another place" may mean another source, or it may point to God without naming him. The Hebrew does not say. "Who knows whether you have come to the kingdom for a time like this?" leaves open whether it is chance or purpose.
- **4:16** "Fast for me. Do not eat or drink for three days, night or day" is an unusual fast, with no food or drink. "If I perish, I perish" is Esther's decision. The text does not say whether the fast included prayer.
- **4:17** "Went away" translates the Hebrew verb *va-ya'avor*, "passed over" or "went on". It says that Mordecai did what she asked.
- **5:1** "On the third day" is the third day of the fast (4:16). "Her royal robes" are the dress of the queen. She stands in the inner court, in sight of the king, and does not enter unsummoned.
- **5:2** "She won his favour" is Hebrew *nasa chen*. "He held out to Esther the golden sceptre" is the sign that she may live (4:11). "Touched the tip of the sceptre" is a sign of respect.
- **5:3** "Even up to half the kingdom" is a formula of generous promise. It is not meant to be taken as a literal offer (compare Mark 6:23).
- **5:4** "Let the king and Haman come today to the banquet" is Esther's request. She asks for a banquet, not for the people's lives.
- **5:6** "While they were drinking wine" is the second part of the banquet, the wine feast (Hebrew *mishteh ha-yayin*). The king asks a second time.
- **5:8** "Tomorrow I will do as the king says" puts off her request to a second banquet. The text does not give the reason for the delay.
- **5:9** "Who did not stand up or tremble before him" gives Mordecai's behaviour as the cause of Haman's rage.
- **5:10** "His wife Zeresh" has a Persian name. His friends and wife appear again in 6:13.
- **5:11** "The number of his sons" is ten (9:10).
- **5:14** "A gallows ... fifty cubits high" is about twenty-two metres. The Hebrew word is *etz*, "tree" or "wood", which may mean a gallows or a stake for impaling. The advice of his wife and friends "pleased Haman".
- **6:1** "That night the king could not sleep" is literally "the king's sleep fled". The text does not say why. It is one of the places where the book is silent about God. "The book of records, the chronicles" is Hebrew *sefer ha-zikhronot divre ha-yamim*, the royal chronicle (2:23).
- **6:2** "Bigthana and Teresh" are the two eunuchs of 2:21. "Bigthana" is another form of Bigthan.
- **6:3** "What honour or reward has been given to Mordecai for this?" shows that the king had not rewarded him. "Nothing has been done for him."
- **6:4** "Who is in the court?" brings Haman, who had come to ask for the hanging of Mordecai, before the king at the right moment.
- **6:6** "Haman said to himself, 'Whom would the king want to honour more than me?'" gives his thought. The king does not name the man.
- **6:8** "A horse that the king has ridden, with a royal crown on its head" follows the Hebrew, in which the crown may be on the horse or on the man. Persian kings had royal horses that wore a crown or head-dress.
- **6:9** "One of the king's most noble officials" is to lead the man. "This is what is done for the man whom the king wants to honour" is the call.
- **6:10** "Do this for Mordecai the Jew" names Mordecai and his people. The king does not yet know that Haman has planned the destruction of the Jews with the king's own order.
- **6:12** "Mourning, with his head covered" is a sign of grief and shame.
- **6:13** "If Mordecai, before whom you have begun to fall, is of the Jewish people, you will not win against him, but will surely fall before him" is the opinion of Haman's advisers.
- **7:1** "The banquet with Queen Esther" is the second banquet.
- **7:3** "Let my life be given to me as my wish, and my people as my request" is Esther's first mention of her people to the king.
- **7:4** "We have been sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, to be killed and to be wiped out" repeats the words of the decree (3:13). "I would have kept silent, for the trouble would not be worth the king's loss" is hard in the Hebrew (*ki ein ha-tsar shoveh be-nezeq ha-melekh*). It may mean that slavery would not be worth the king's loss, or that the enemy cannot make up for the king's loss.
- **7:6** "An enemy and adversary, this wicked Haman" is Esther's answer. She names Haman and not the king.
- **7:8** "Haman had thrown himself on the couch where Esther was" is what the king sees as he comes in. "Will he even assault the queen in front of me?" is the king's reading of it. "They covered Haman's face" is a sign that he was condemned.
- **7:9** "Harbona" is one of the seven eunuchs of 1:10. "Who spoke up for the king" is literally "who spoke good for the king", and refers to 2:22.
- **7:10** "They hanged Haman on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai" is rendered as written. "The king's anger cooled" uses the same Hebrew verb (*shakhekhah*) as in 2:1.
- **8:1** "The house of Haman" passes to the queen because the property of a condemned man went to the crown. "Esther had told what he was to her" says that Esther had told the king that Mordecai was her cousin and guardian.
- **8:2** "The king took off his signet ring, which he had taken back from Haman, and gave it to Mordecai" makes Mordecai the chief minister. Esther puts him over Haman's house.
- **8:3** "Put an end to the evil of Haman" is a request to avert the decree. Esther falls at his feet and weeps.
- **8:4** "The king held out the golden sceptre" is the same sign as in 5:2.
- **8:5** "If it pleases the king, and if I have found favour ... and if the matter seems right ... and I am pleasing to him" has four conditions, which show how careful her request is. "Cancel the letters that Haman ... wrote" asks for the first decree to be undone.
- **8:8** "What is written in the king's name and sealed with the king's ring cannot be reversed" is the reason why a new decree is made and the first is not cancelled (see 1:19).
- **8:9** "The month of Sivan, on the twenty-third day" is in June, more than two months after the first decree (3:12). "To the Jews in their own script and language" says that the letter was also sent in Hebrew or the Aramaic script of the Jews.
- **8:10** "Fast horses from the royal stables" translates a rare word, *ha-ramakhim*, which may mean "mares" or "royal stud". The meaning is uncertain.
- **8:11** "To gather and defend their lives, to destroy, kill and wipe out any armed force of any people or province that would attack them, with their women and children, and to plunder their possessions" is hard to read. The Hebrew phrase "women and children" (*taf ve-nashim*) can refer to the women and children of the attackers, as in 3:13, or to the Jews' own women and children who might be attacked. The WABV text keeps the order of the Hebrew and the footnote gives both readings. It is rendered as written.
- **8:13** "To take revenge on their enemies" says that the day is for settling accounts.
- **8:15** "Royal robes of blue and white" are the colours of the Persian court. "A large gold crown" and "a robe of fine linen and purple" are signs of high office.
- **8:16** "Light and gladness and joy and honour" is a list of four words. The word "light" echoes Psalm 97:11.
- **8:17** "Many from the peoples of the land declared themselves Jews" uses the verb *mityahadim*, which is found only here in the Hebrew Bible. It may mean that they became Jews or that they pretended to be Jews. "For fear of the Jews had fallen on them" gives the reason.
- **9:1** "It was turned round" is Hebrew *nahafokh hu*, a verb for reversal, which is the book's main theme. "The enemies of the Jews had hoped to overpower them" refers to the day set by the lot (3:7).
- **9:2** "To lay hands on those who sought their harm" says that the Jews were not only defending themselves. "No one could stand against them" is a figure of the fear that had spread.
- **9:3** "All the officials ... helped the Jews, because fear of Mordecai had fallen on them" says that the provincial leaders took the Jews' side.
- **9:5** "Did as they pleased to those who hated them" is rendered as written. The killing is told without any comment.
- **9:6** "Five hundred men" were killed in Susa on the first day. The killing of the ten sons of Haman is told in verses 7 to 10.
- **9:7** "Parshandatha, Dalphon, Aspatha, Poratha, Adalia, Aridatha, Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai and Vaizatha" are the ten sons, with Persian names. They are listed in a column in the Hebrew.
- **9:10** "They did not lay a hand on the plunder" is repeated in verses 15 and 16. The first decree and the second both allowed plunder (3:13; 8:11), so this is a note that the Jews did not take it, in contrast to Saul (1 Samuel 15:19).
- **9:13** "Let the ten sons of Haman be hanged on the gallows" asks for the display of the bodies, since they were already killed (verse 10). It is rendered as written. See Deuteronomy 21:22-23.
- **9:15** "Three hundred men" were killed in Susa on the second day.
- **9:16** "Seventy-five thousand" is the number killed in the provinces. The Greek text has a different number (fifteen thousand in some manuscripts). It is rendered as written.
- **9:17** "The thirteenth day ... the fourteenth day they rested" explains why the festival is kept on the fourteenth in the provinces. The Jews in Susa kept it on the fifteenth (verse 18).
- **9:19** "The Jews of the villages, who live in unwalled towns" keep the fourteenth. Those in walled towns, like Susa, keep the fifteenth (see verse 18). "Sending portions of food to one another" is Hebrew *mishlo'ach manot*.
- **9:22** "Gifts to the poor" is a duty of the festival. "The month that had been turned for them from sorrow to gladness" repeats the theme of reversal.
- **9:24** "Cast Pur, that is the lot" recalls 3:7.
- **9:25** "When it came before the king" can also be read "when she came before the king", since the Hebrew verb is ambiguous (*u-vevo'ah*). "He and his sons should be hanged" refers to chapter 7 and verse 14.
- **9:26** "Purim, after the word Pur" is the name of the festival, in the plural. "This letter" is Mordecai's letter of verse 20.
- **9:27** "All who joined them" is Hebrew *kol ha-nilvim alehem*, and may refer to those who became Jews (see 8:17).
- **9:29** "Full written authority" is Hebrew *tokef*. "This second letter about Purim" is the second of two letters (verses 20 to 22 and verses 29 to 32).
- **9:30** "In words of peace and truth" is Hebrew *divre shalom ve-emet*.
- **9:31** "With the matters of the fasts and their crying out" is hard in the Hebrew (*divre ha-tsomot ve-za'aqatam*). It may refer to the fast before Purim, kept in memory of the fast of Esther (4:16), or to the mourning in the first decree. The sense is uncertain.
- **9:32** "It was written in a book" refers to a record of the rules of Purim.
- **10:1** "Forced labour on the land and on the coasts of the sea" is a tax or a levy imposed by the king. It is a short note on his rule.
- **10:2** "The Book of the Records of the Kings of Media and Persia" is the royal chronicle (2:23; 6:1), the same word as in the name of the books of Chronicles.
- **10:3** "Second to King Ahasuerus" is Mordecai's place as chief minister (see 1 Chronicles 18:17 and Genesis 41:40). "Spoke peace for all his descendants" is literally "spoke peace to all his seed". "Peace" is Hebrew *shalom*. See entry H-SHALOM. The book ends with the good of the people and not with God, and with no word of God anywhere in the Hebrew text.
