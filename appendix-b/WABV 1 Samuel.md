# WABV: 1 Samuel

## 1 Samuel 1-3: Hannah, Samuel and the House of Eli

### Chapter 1

**1** There was a certain man from Ramathaim, a Zuphite from the hill country of Ephraim, whose name was Elkanah son of Jeroham, son of Elihu, son of Tohu, son of Zuph, an Ephraimite.

**2** He had two wives. The name of one was Hannah, and the name of the other was Peninnah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah had none.

**3** Every year this man went up from his town to worship and to sacrifice to the LORD of hosts at Shiloh, where the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were priests of the LORD.

**4** On the day that Elkanah sacrificed, he would give portions to his wife Peninnah and to all her sons and daughters.

**5** But to Hannah he would give a double portion, because he loved her, though the LORD had closed her womb.

**6** Her rival would provoke her bitterly to make her miserable, because the LORD had closed her womb.

**7** This went on year after year. Whenever she went up to the house of the LORD, her rival would provoke her, and she would weep and not eat.

**8** Her husband Elkanah said to her, "Hannah, why are you weeping? Why do you not eat? Why is your heart sad? Am I not better to you than ten sons?"

**9** After they had eaten and drunk at Shiloh, Hannah got up. Now Eli the priest was sitting on his seat by the doorpost of the temple of the LORD.

**10** She was deeply bitter, and she prayed to the LORD and wept without ceasing.

**11** She made a vow and said, "LORD of hosts, if you will look on the misery of your servant and remember me, and not forget your servant, but give your servant a son, then I will give him to the LORD all the days of his life, and no razor will touch his head."

**12** As she kept on praying before the LORD, Eli watched her mouth.

**13** Hannah was speaking in her heart. Only her lips moved, and her voice could not be heard. So Eli thought she was drunk.

**14** Eli said to her, "How long will you go on being drunk? Put away your wine."

**15** But Hannah answered, "No, my lord, I am a woman with a heavy spirit. I have drunk no wine or strong drink, but I have been pouring out my soul before the LORD.

**16** Do not take your servant for a worthless woman, for I have been speaking out of my great anxiety and grief until now."

**17** Then Eli answered, "Go in peace, and may the God of Israel grant what you have asked of him."

**18** She said, "May your servant find favour in your eyes." Then the woman went on her way and ate, and her face was no longer sad.

**19** They got up early in the morning and bowed down before the LORD, and then went back to their house at Ramah. Elkanah knew his wife Hannah, and the LORD remembered her.

**20** In due time Hannah conceived and bore a son. She named him Samuel, "for," she said, "I asked the LORD for him."

**21** The man Elkanah went up with all his household to offer the yearly sacrifice to the LORD and to keep his vow.

**22** But Hannah did not go up, for she said to her husband, "After the child is weaned, I will bring him, so that he may appear before the LORD and stay there for ever."

**23** Her husband Elkanah said to her, "Do what seems best to you. Stay until you have weaned him. Only may the LORD make his word come true." So the woman stayed and nursed her son until she weaned him.

**24** When she had weaned him, she took him up with her, along with a three-year-old bull, an ephah of flour and a skin of wine, and brought him to the house of the LORD at Shiloh. The boy was young.

**25** Then they slaughtered the bull and brought the boy to Eli.

**26** She said, "Please, my lord, as you live, my lord, I am the woman who stood here beside you, praying to the LORD.

**27** I prayed for this boy, and the LORD has granted me what I asked of him.

**28** So I have lent him to the LORD. As long as he lives, he is lent to the LORD." And he worshipped the LORD there.

### Chapter 2

**1** Hannah prayed and said:

"My heart exults in the LORD. My horn is lifted high in the LORD. My mouth opens wide against my enemies, because I rejoice in your saving help.

**2** There is no one holy \[H-QADOSH\] like the LORD, for there is no one but you. There is no rock like our God.

**3** Do not go on talking so proudly. Do not let arrogance come out of your mouth, for the LORD is a God of knowledge, and by him deeds are weighed.

**4** The bows of the mighty are broken, but the stumbling gird on strength.

**5** Those who were full hire themselves out for bread, but those who were hungry are hungry no more. The barren woman has borne seven, but she who has many sons languishes.

**6** The LORD kills and brings to life. He brings down to Sheol \[H-SHEOL\] and raises up.

**7** The LORD makes poor and makes rich. He brings low, he also lifts up.

**8** He raises the poor from the dust. He lifts the needy from the ash heap, to make them sit with princes and to give them a seat of honour. For the pillars of the earth are the LORD's, and on them he has set the world.

**9** He guards the feet of his faithful ones, but the wicked are silenced in darkness, for not by strength does a person prevail.

**10** Those who oppose the LORD will be shattered. The Most High will thunder against them from heaven. The LORD will judge the ends of the earth. He will give strength to his king and raise the horn of his anointed."

**11** Then Elkanah went home to Ramah. But the boy served the LORD in the presence of Eli the priest.

**12** Now the sons of Eli were worthless men. They did not know the LORD.

**13** This was the custom of the priests with the people: when anyone offered a sacrifice, the priest's servant would come while the meat was boiling, with a three-pronged fork in his hand,

**14** and would thrust it into the pan, or kettle, or cauldron, or pot. Whatever the fork brought up, the priest would take for himself. This is what they did at Shiloh to all the Israelites who came there.

**15** Even before the fat was burned, the priest's servant would come and say to the man who was sacrificing, "Give the priest some meat to roast, for he will not accept boiled meat from you, only raw."

**16** If the man said to him, "Let them burn the fat first, and then take as much as you want," he would say, "No, give it now, or I will take it by force."

**17** So the sin of the young men was very great in the sight of the LORD, for they were treating the offering of the LORD with contempt.

**18** Samuel was serving in the presence of the LORD, a boy wearing a linen ephod.

**19** His mother used to make him a little robe and take it to him each year when she went up with her husband to offer the yearly sacrifice.

**20** Then Eli would bless Elkanah and his wife, saying, "May the LORD give you children by this woman, in place of the one she lent to the LORD." Then they would go to their home.

**21** The LORD visited Hannah, and she conceived and bore three sons and two daughters. And the boy Samuel grew up in the presence of the LORD.

**22** Now Eli was very old. He heard all that his sons were doing to all Israel, and how they lay with the women who served at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**23** He said to them, "Why do you do such things? I hear of your evil dealings from all the people.

**24** No, my sons. The report I hear the LORD's people spreading is not good.

**25** If one person sins against another, God will mediate for him. But if someone sins against the LORD, who will intercede for him?" But they would not listen to the voice of their father, for it was the LORD's will to put them to death.

**26** The boy Samuel continued to grow in stature and in favour, both with the LORD and with people.

**27** A man of God came to Eli and said to him, "This is what the LORD says: Did I not plainly reveal myself to your father's house when they were in Egypt, in slavery to Pharaoh's house?

**28** Did I not choose him out of all the tribes of Israel to be my priest, to go up to my altar, to burn incense, to wear an ephod before me? Did I not give to your father's house all the offerings by fire of the Israelites?

**29** Why do you look with greed at my sacrifice and my offering that I commanded for my dwelling, and honour your sons above me by fattening yourselves on the best of every offering of my people Israel?

**30** Therefore the LORD, the God of Israel, declares: I did say that your house and your father's house would walk before me for ever. But now the LORD declares: Far be it from me. For those who honour me I will honour, and those who despise me will be treated as nothing.

**31** The days are coming when I will cut off your strength and the strength of your father's house, so that there will not be an old man in your house.

**32** You will see distress in my dwelling, in all the good that I do for Israel, and there will never again be an old man in your house.

**33** The man of yours whom I do not cut off from my altar will be left to weep out his eyes and grieve his heart. All the descendants of your house will die by the sword.

**34** This will be the sign for you: what happens to your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, will happen to both of them on the same day.

**35** And I will raise up for myself a faithful priest, who will do what is in my heart and in my mind. I will build him a lasting house, and he will walk before my anointed for ever.

**36** Everyone who is left in your house will come and bow down to him for a piece of silver and a loaf of bread, and say, 'Please put me in one of the priestly offices, so that I may eat a morsel of bread.'"

### Chapter 3

**1** The boy Samuel was serving the LORD in the presence of Eli. In those days the word of the LORD was rare. There was no widespread vision.

**2** At that time Eli, whose eyesight had begun to grow dim so that he could not see, was lying down in his room.

**3** The lamp of God had not yet gone out, and Samuel was lying down in the temple of the LORD, where the ark of God was.

**4** Then the LORD called, "Samuel." He answered, "Here I am."

**5** He ran to Eli and said, "Here I am, for you called me." But he said, "I did not call. Go back and lie down." So he went and lay down.

**6** The LORD called again, "Samuel." Samuel got up and went to Eli and said, "Here I am, for you called me." But he said, "I did not call, my son. Go back and lie down."

**7** Now Samuel did not yet know the LORD, and the word of the LORD had not yet been revealed to him.

**8** The LORD called Samuel again, a third time. He got up and went to Eli and said, "Here I am, for you called me." Then Eli understood that the LORD was calling the boy.

**9** So Eli said to Samuel, "Go and lie down. If he calls you, say, 'Speak, LORD, for your servant is listening.'" So Samuel went and lay down in his place.

**10** The LORD came and stood and called as before, "Samuel, Samuel." Samuel said, "Speak, for your servant is listening."

**11** Then the LORD said to Samuel, "See, I am about to do something in Israel that will make both ears of everyone who hears of it tingle.

**12** On that day I will carry out against Eli everything I have spoken about his house, from beginning to end.

**13** For I told him that I am going to judge his house for ever, for the guilt that he knew, because his sons were bringing a curse on themselves, and he did not restrain them.

**14** So I have sworn to the house of Eli that the guilt of Eli's house will never be atoned for by sacrifice or offering."

**15** Samuel lay down until morning. Then he opened the doors of the house of the LORD. But Samuel was afraid to tell the vision to Eli.

**16** But Eli called Samuel and said, "Samuel, my son." He said, "Here I am."

**17** Eli said, "What was it that he told you? Do not hide it from me. May God do this to you, and more also, if you hide anything from me of all that he told you."

**18** So Samuel told him everything and hid nothing from him. Eli said, "It is the LORD. Let him do what seems good to him."

**19** Samuel grew up, and the LORD was with him and let none of his words fall to the ground.

**20** All Israel from Dan to Beersheba knew that Samuel was established as a prophet of the LORD.

**21** The LORD continued to appear at Shiloh, for the LORD revealed himself to Samuel at Shiloh by the word of the LORD.

## 1 Samuel 4-7: The Ark Taken and Returned

### Chapter 4

**1** And Samuel's word came to all Israel. Israel went out to meet the Philistines in battle and camped at Ebenezer, while the Philistines camped at Aphek.

**2** The Philistines drew up their lines against Israel, and when the battle spread, Israel was beaten by the Philistines, who struck down about four thousand men on the battlefield.

**3** When the people came back to the camp, the elders of Israel said, "Why has the LORD beaten us today before the Philistines? Let us bring the ark of the covenant of the LORD here from Shiloh, so that it may come among us and save us from the hand of our enemies."

**4** So the people sent to Shiloh and brought from there the ark of the covenant of the LORD of hosts, who sits enthroned on the cherubim. The two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were there with the ark of the covenant of God.

**5** When the ark of the covenant of the LORD came into the camp, all Israel gave a great shout, so that the ground shook.

**6** When the Philistines heard the sound of the shouting, they said, "What is this great shouting in the camp of the Hebrews?" Then they learned that the ark of the LORD had come into the camp.

**7** The Philistines were afraid, for they said, "A god has come into the camp." They said, "Woe to us. Nothing like this has happened before.

**8** Woe to us. Who will save us from the hand of these mighty gods? These are the gods who struck the Egyptians with every kind of plague in the wilderness.

**9** Be strong and be men, Philistines, or you will be slaves to the Hebrews, as they have been to you. Be men and fight."

**10** So the Philistines fought, and Israel was beaten, and each man fled to his tent. The slaughter was very great, for thirty thousand foot soldiers of Israel fell.

**11** The ark of God was captured, and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, died.

**12** A man of Benjamin ran from the battle line and came to Shiloh that same day, with his clothes torn and dirt on his head.

**13** When he arrived, Eli was sitting on his seat by the road, watching, because his heart trembled for the ark of God. When the man came into the town and told the news, the whole town cried out.

**14** When Eli heard the sound of the outcry, he said, "What is this noise of uproar?" The man hurried and came and told Eli.

**15** Eli was ninety-eight years old, and his eyes were fixed so that he could not see.

**16** The man said to Eli, "I am the one who has come from the battle line. I ran away from the battle line today." He said, "How did it go, my son?"

**17** The messenger answered, "Israel has fled before the Philistines, and there has also been a great slaughter among the people. Your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, are dead, and the ark of God has been captured."

**18** When he mentioned the ark of God, Eli fell backwards from his seat by the side of the gate. His neck was broken and he died, for he was old and heavy. He had judged Israel for forty years.

**19** His daughter-in-law, the wife of Phinehas, was pregnant and near to giving birth. When she heard the news that the ark of God had been captured, and that her father-in-law and her husband were dead, she knelt and gave birth, for her pains came on her.

**20** As she was dying, the women standing by her said, "Do not be afraid, for you have given birth to a son." But she did not answer or take notice.

**21** She named the boy Ichabod, saying, "The glory has gone from Israel," because the ark of God had been captured and because of her father-in-law and her husband.

**22** She said, "The glory has gone from Israel, for the ark of God has been captured."

### Chapter 5

**1** When the Philistines captured the ark of God, they brought it from Ebenezer to Ashdod.

**2** The Philistines took the ark of God and brought it into the temple of Dagon and placed it beside Dagon.

**3** When the people of Ashdod got up early the next day, there was Dagon, fallen face down on the ground before the ark of the LORD. So they took Dagon and set him back in his place.

**4** But when they got up early the next morning, there was Dagon, fallen face down on the ground before the ark of the LORD, and the head of Dagon and both his hands were lying cut off on the threshold. Only the trunk of Dagon was left.

**5** This is why the priests of Dagon and all who enter the temple of Dagon in Ashdod do not step on the threshold to this day.

**6** The hand of the LORD was heavy on the people of Ashdod, and he terrified them and struck them with tumours, Ashdod and its territory.

**7** When the people of Ashdod saw how it was, they said, "The ark of the god of Israel must not stay with us, for his hand is hard on us and on our god Dagon."

**8** So they sent and gathered all the rulers of the Philistines to them and said, "What should we do with the ark of the god of Israel?" They answered, "Let the ark of the god of Israel be moved to Gath." So they moved the ark of the god of Israel there.

**9** But after they had moved it, the hand of the LORD was against the town, causing a very great panic. He struck the people of the town, small and great, so that tumours broke out on them.

**10** So they sent the ark of God to Ekron. But as the ark of God was coming into Ekron, the people of Ekron cried out, "They have moved the ark of the god of Israel to us, to kill us and our people."

**11** They sent and gathered all the rulers of the Philistines and said, "Send away the ark of the god of Israel, and let it go back to its own place, so that it does not kill us and our people." For there was a deadly panic throughout the whole town. The hand of God was very heavy there.

**12** The people who did not die were struck with tumours, and the cry of the town went up to heaven.

### Chapter 6

**1** The ark of the LORD was in the land of the Philistines seven months.

**2** The Philistines called for the priests and the diviners and said, "What should we do with the ark of the LORD? Tell us how we should send it back to its place."

**3** They said, "If you send away the ark of the god of Israel, do not send it empty. You must send a guilt offering \[H-ASHAM\] with it. Then you will be healed, and it will be known to you why his hand has not turned away from you."

**4** They said, "What is the guilt offering that we should send back to him?" They answered, "Five gold tumours and five gold mice, according to the number of the rulers of the Philistines, for the same plague was on all of you and on your rulers.

**5** Make images of your tumours and images of your mice that are ravaging the land, and give glory to the god of Israel. Perhaps he will lighten his hand on you and on your gods and on your land.

**6** Why should you harden your hearts as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts? When he had dealt harshly with them, did they not let the people go, so that they left?

**7** Now then, get a new cart ready, and two milking cows that have never been under a yoke. Yoke the cows to the cart, but take their calves home, away from them.

**8** Take the ark of the LORD and put it on the cart, and put the gold objects that you are sending to him as a guilt offering in a box at its side. Then send it off, and let it go.

**9** And watch. If it goes up on the road to its own land, to Beth-shemesh, then it is he who has done us this great harm. But if not, then we will know that it was not his hand that struck us. It happened to us by chance."

**10** The men did so. They took two milking cows and yoked them to the cart and shut their calves up at home.

**11** They put the ark of the LORD on the cart, and the box with the gold mice and the images of their tumours.

**12** The cows went straight ahead on the road towards Beth-shemesh, keeping to one road, lowing as they went. They did not turn to the right or to the left, and the rulers of the Philistines followed them as far as the border of Beth-shemesh.

**13** Now the people of Beth-shemesh were reaping their wheat harvest in the valley. When they looked up and saw the ark, they were glad to see it.

**14** The cart came to the field of Joshua of Beth-shemesh and stopped there, where there was a large stone. They split up the wood of the cart and offered the cows as a burnt offering to the LORD.

**15** The Levites took down the ark of the LORD and the box beside it that held the gold objects, and put them on the large stone. The people of Beth-shemesh offered burnt offerings and made sacrifices that day to the LORD.

**16** When the five rulers of the Philistines saw it, they went back to Ekron that day.

**17** These are the gold tumours that the Philistines sent back as a guilt offering to the LORD: one for Ashdod, one for Gaza, one for Ashkelon, one for Gath and one for Ekron,

**18** and the gold mice, according to the number of all the towns of the Philistines belonging to the five rulers, both fortified towns and unwalled villages. The large stone on which they set down the ark of the LORD is a witness to this day in the field of Joshua of Beth-shemesh.

**19** He struck down some of the people of Beth-shemesh, because they looked into the ark of the LORD. He struck down seventy of them, and the people mourned because the LORD had struck the people with a great blow.

**20** The people of Beth-shemesh said, "Who can stand before the LORD, this holy \[H-QADOSH\] God? And to whom will he go up from us?"

**21** So they sent messengers to the people of Kiriath-jearim, saying, "The Philistines have brought back the ark of the LORD. Come down and take it up to you."

### Chapter 7

**1** The people of Kiriath-jearim came and took up the ark of the LORD and brought it to the house of Abinadab on the hill. They set apart his son Eleazar to guard the ark of the LORD.

**2** From the day that the ark stayed at Kiriath-jearim a long time passed, some twenty years, and the whole house of Israel longed for the LORD.

**3** Samuel said to the whole house of Israel, "If you are returning \[H-SHUV\] to the LORD with all your heart, then put away the foreign gods and the Ashtoreths from among you, and set your hearts on the LORD and serve him alone, and he will rescue you from the hand of the Philistines."

**4** So the Israelites put away the Baals and the Ashtoreths, and served the LORD alone.

**5** Then Samuel said, "Gather all Israel at Mizpah, and I will pray to the LORD for you."

**6** So they gathered at Mizpah and drew water and poured it out before the LORD. They fasted that day and said there, "We have sinned against the LORD." And Samuel judged the Israelites at Mizpah.

**7** When the Philistines heard that the Israelites had gathered at Mizpah, the rulers of the Philistines went up against Israel. When the Israelites heard of it, they were afraid of the Philistines.

**8** The Israelites said to Samuel, "Do not stop crying out to the LORD our God for us, so that he may save us from the hand of the Philistines."

**9** So Samuel took a suckling lamb and offered it whole as a burnt offering to the LORD. Samuel cried out to the LORD for Israel, and the LORD answered him.

**10** While Samuel was offering the burnt offering, the Philistines came near to attack Israel. But the LORD thundered with a loud voice that day against the Philistines and threw them into confusion, and they were beaten before Israel.

**11** The men of Israel went out from Mizpah and chased the Philistines and struck them down, as far as below Beth-car.

**12** Then Samuel took a stone and set it up between Mizpah and Shen. He called it Ebenezer, saying, "The LORD has helped us up to here."

**13** So the Philistines were subdued and did not come again into the territory of Israel. The hand of the LORD was against the Philistines all the days of Samuel.

**14** The towns that the Philistines had taken from Israel were returned to Israel, from Ekron to Gath, and Israel took their territory back from the hand of the Philistines. There was peace also between Israel and the Amorites.

**15** Samuel judged Israel all the days of his life.

**16** Each year he went on a circuit to Bethel, Gilgal and Mizpah, and judged Israel in all those places.

**17** Then he would come back to Ramah, for his home was there, and there too he judged Israel. And he built an altar there to the LORD.

## 1 Samuel 8-12: The Request for a King

### Chapter 8

**1** When Samuel grew old, he appointed his sons as judges over Israel.

**2** The name of his firstborn son was Joel, and the name of his second son was Abijah. They were judges at Beersheba.

**3** But his sons did not follow his ways. They turned aside after dishonest gain, took bribes and twisted justice.

**4** So all the elders of Israel gathered and came to Samuel at Ramah

**5** and said to him, "Look, you are old, and your sons do not follow your ways. Now appoint a king for us to judge us, like all the nations."

**6** But what they said, "Give us a king to judge us," was evil in Samuel's eyes. So Samuel prayed to the LORD.

**7** The LORD said to Samuel, "Listen to the voice of the people in everything they say to you, for it is not you they have rejected, but me, from being king over them.

**8** As they have done from the day I brought them up out of Egypt to this day, abandoning me and serving other gods, so they are doing to you also.

**9** Now then, listen to their voice. But you must solemnly warn them and tell them how the king who will rule over them will behave."

**10** So Samuel told all the words of the LORD to the people who were asking him for a king.

**11** He said, "This is how the king who will rule over you will behave: he will take your sons and put them to work on his chariots and with his horsemen, and some will run in front of his chariots.

**12** He will appoint for himself commanders of thousands and commanders of fifties, and some to plough his ground and reap his harvest, and to make his weapons of war and the equipment for his chariots.

**13** He will take your daughters as perfumers, cooks and bakers.

**14** He will take the best of your fields, vineyards and olive groves and give them to his servants.

**15** He will take a tenth of your grain and of your vineyards and give it to his officials and his servants.

**16** He will take your male and female servants and your best young men and your donkeys, and put them to work for him.

**17** He will take a tenth of your flocks, and you yourselves will become his slaves.

**18** On that day you will cry out because of your king, whom you have chosen for yourselves, but the LORD will not answer you on that day."

**19** But the people refused to listen to the voice of Samuel. They said, "No. There will be a king over us,

**20** so that we too may be like all the nations, and our king may judge us and go out before us and fight our battles."

**21** When Samuel had heard all the words of the people, he repeated them in the hearing of the LORD.

**22** The LORD said to Samuel, "Listen to their voice and make a king for them." Samuel said to the men of Israel, "Each of you, go back to your town."

### Chapter 9

**1** There was a man of Benjamin whose name was Kish son of Abiel, son of Zeror, son of Becorath, son of Aphiah, a Benjaminite, a man of standing.

**2** He had a son whose name was Saul, a fine young man. There was no one among the Israelites finer than he. He stood a head taller than anyone else.

**3** The donkeys of Saul's father Kish had strayed. So Kish said to his son Saul, "Take one of the servants with you and go and look for the donkeys."

**4** They passed through the hill country of Ephraim and through the land of Shalisha, but they did not find them. They passed through the land of Shaalim, but they were not there. They passed through the land of Benjamin, but they did not find them.

**5** When they came to the land of Zuph, Saul said to his servant who was with him, "Come, let us go back, or my father will stop worrying about the donkeys and start worrying about us."

**6** But he said to him, "Look, there is a man of God in this town, and he is a man held in honour. Everything he says comes true. Let us go there now. Perhaps he can tell us which way we should go."

**7** Saul said to his servant, "But if we go, what can we take the man? The bread in our sacks is gone, and we have no gift to take to the man of God. What do we have?"

**8** The servant answered Saul again, "Look, I have a quarter of a shekel of silver in my hand. I will give it to the man of God, and he will tell us our way."

**9** (In former times in Israel, when someone went to ask God, he would say, "Come, let us go to the seer," for the prophet \[H-NAVI\] of today used to be called a seer.)

**10** Saul said to his servant, "Well said. Come, let us go." So they went to the town where the man of God was.

**11** As they were going up the slope to the town, they met some young women coming out to draw water and said to them, "Is the seer here?"

**12** They answered, "He is. He is just ahead of you. Hurry, for he has come to the town today, because the people have a sacrifice at the high place today.

## 1 Samuel 16-19: David Anointed, Goliath and Saul's Jealousy

### Chapter 16

**1** The LORD said to Samuel, "How long will you grieve over Saul, when I have rejected him as king over Israel? Fill your horn with oil and go. I am sending you to Jesse of Bethlehem, for I have chosen a king for myself from among his sons."

**2** Samuel said, "How can I go? If Saul hears of it, he will kill me." The LORD said, "Take a heifer with you and say, 'I have come to sacrifice to the LORD.'

**3** Invite Jesse to the sacrifice, and I will show you what you are to do. You are to anoint for me the one I name to you."

**4** Samuel did what the LORD said and came to Bethlehem. The elders of the town came trembling to meet him and said, "Do you come in peace?"

**5** He said, "In peace. I have come to sacrifice to the LORD. Make yourselves holy and come with me to the sacrifice." And he made Jesse and his sons holy and invited them to the sacrifice.

**6** When they came, he looked at Eliab and thought, "Surely the LORD's anointed stands here before him."

**7** But the LORD said to Samuel, "Do not look at his appearance or at his height, for I have rejected him. The LORD does not see as people see. People look at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart."

**8** Then Jesse called Abinadab and had him pass before Samuel. He said, "The LORD has not chosen this one either."

**9** Then Jesse had Shammah pass by. He said, "The LORD has not chosen this one either."

**10** Jesse had seven of his sons pass before Samuel, but Samuel said to Jesse, "The LORD has not chosen any of these."

**11** Samuel said to Jesse, "Are these all the young men?" He said, "There is still the youngest, but he is out keeping the sheep." Samuel said to Jesse, "Send for him, for we will not sit down until he comes here."

**12** So he sent and brought him in. He was ruddy, with fine eyes and a handsome appearance. The LORD said, "Get up and anoint him, for this is the one."

**13** Then Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the middle of his brothers. And the spirit \[H-RUACH\] of the LORD rushed on David from that day on. Samuel got up and went to Ramah.

**14** Now the spirit of the LORD left Saul, and a harmful spirit from the LORD tormented him.

**15** Saul's servants said to him, "Look, a harmful spirit from God is tormenting you.

**16** Let our lord command your servants here in front of you to look for someone who can play the lyre. When the harmful spirit from God comes on you, he will play, and you will feel better."

**17** So Saul said to his servants, "Find me someone who plays well and bring him to me."

**18** One of the young men answered, "I have seen a son of Jesse of Bethlehem who knows how to play. He is a brave man and a fighter, wise in speech and handsome, and the LORD is with him."

**19** So Saul sent messengers to Jesse and said, "Send me your son David, who is with the sheep."

**20** Jesse took a donkey loaded with bread, a skin of wine and a young goat, and sent them with his son David to Saul.

**21** David came to Saul and entered his service, and Saul loved him greatly, and he became his armour-bearer.

**22** Saul sent word to Jesse: "Let David stay in my service, for he has found favour in my eyes."

**23** Whenever the harmful spirit from God came on Saul, David would take the lyre and play it. Saul would be relieved and feel better, and the harmful spirit would leave him.

### Chapter 17

**1** The Philistines gathered their armies for war. They gathered at Socoh in Judah and camped between Socoh and Azekah, at Ephes-dammim.

**2** Saul and the men of Israel gathered and camped in the Valley of Elah and drew up their battle line to meet the Philistines.

**3** The Philistines stood on one hill, and the Israelites stood on the other hill, with the valley between them.

**4** A champion came out from the Philistine camp, named Goliath, from Gath. His height was six cubits and a span.

**5** He had a bronze helmet on his head, and he wore a coat of scale armour, and the weight of the coat was five thousand shekels of bronze.

**6** He had bronze armour on his legs, and a bronze javelin slung between his shoulders.

**7** The shaft of his spear was like a weaver's beam, and the iron head of his spear weighed six hundred shekels. His shield-bearer walked in front of him.

**8** He stood and shouted to the ranks of Israel, "Why do you come out and line up for battle? Am I not a Philistine, and are you not servants of Saul? Choose a man for yourselves, and let him come down to me.

**9** If he is able to fight me and kill me, we will be your servants. But if I defeat him and kill him, you will be our servants and serve us."

**10** The Philistine said, "I defy the ranks of Israel today. Give me a man, so that we can fight each other."

**11** When Saul and all Israel heard these words of the Philistine, they were shaken and very afraid.

**12** Now David was the son of an Ephrathite from Bethlehem in Judah, whose name was Jesse. He had eight sons, and in the days of Saul he was already old, well on in years.

**13** The three oldest sons of Jesse had followed Saul to the war. The names of the three sons who went to the war were Eliab the firstborn, then Abinadab, and the third, Shammah.

**14** David was the youngest. The three oldest followed Saul,

**15** but David went back and forth from Saul to tend his father's sheep at Bethlehem.

**16** For forty days the Philistine came forward morning and evening and took his stand.

**17** Jesse said to his son David, "Take for your brothers an ephah of this roasted grain and these ten loaves, and hurry to the camp to your brothers.

**18** Also take these ten cuts of cheese to the commander of their unit. See how your brothers are, and bring back some token from them."

**19** Saul and the brothers and all the men of Israel were in the Valley of Elah, fighting the Philistines.

**20** David got up early in the morning and left the sheep with a keeper, and took the food and went, as Jesse had commanded him. He came to the camp as the army was going out to the battle line, shouting the war cry.

**21** Israel and the Philistines drew up their lines, army against army.

**22** David left his things with the keeper of the baggage and ran to the battle line and went and greeted his brothers.

**23** While he was talking with them, the champion, the Philistine from Gath named Goliath, came up out of the Philistine ranks and spoke the same words as before. And David heard him.

**24** All the men of Israel, when they saw the man, ran away from him and were very afraid.

**25** The men of Israel said, "Have you seen this man who keeps coming up? He comes up to defy Israel. The king will make the man who kills him very rich, and will give him his daughter and make his father's house free in Israel."

**26** David said to the men standing near him, "What will be done for the man who kills this Philistine and removes the disgrace from Israel? Who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?"

**27** The people told him the same thing: "This is what will be done for the man who kills him."

**28** His eldest brother Eliab heard him speaking to the men, and Eliab's anger blazed against David. He said, "Why have you come down here? And who have you left those few sheep with in the wilderness? I know your arrogance and the evil of your heart. You have come down to watch the battle."

**29** David said, "What have I done now? It was only a question."

**30** He turned away from him to someone else and said the same thing, and the people answered him as before.

**31** When the words that David spoke were heard, they told them to Saul, and he sent for him.

**32** David said to Saul, "Let no one lose heart because of him. Your servant will go and fight this Philistine."

**33** Saul said to David, "You cannot go against this Philistine and fight him, for you are only a boy, and he has been a fighter from his youth."

**34** But David said to Saul, "Your servant has been keeping his father's sheep. When a lion or a bear came and took a lamb from the flock,

**35** I went after it and struck it and rescued the lamb from its mouth. If it turned on me, I seized it by its beard and struck it and killed it.

**36** Your servant has killed both lions and bears, and this uncircumcised Philistine will be like one of them, for he has defied the armies of the living God."

**37** David said, "The LORD who rescued me from the paw of the lion and the paw of the bear will rescue me from the hand of this Philistine." Saul said to David, "Go, and the LORD be with you."

**38** Saul dressed David in his own armour. He put a bronze helmet on his head and dressed him in a coat of armour.

**39** David strapped his sword over the armour and tried to walk, but he was not used to them. David said to Saul, "I cannot go with these, for I am not used to them." So David took them off.

**40** He took his staff in his hand and chose five smooth stones from the stream and put them in his shepherd's bag, his pouch. With his sling in his hand he went towards the Philistine.

**41** The Philistine came closer and closer to David, with his shield-bearer in front of him.

**42** When the Philistine looked and saw David, he looked down on him, for he was only a boy, ruddy and handsome.

**43** The Philistine said to David, "Am I a dog, that you come at me with sticks?" And the Philistine cursed David by his gods.

**44** The Philistine said to David, "Come to me, and I will give your flesh to the birds of the sky and the wild animals of the field."

**45** David said to the Philistine, "You come against me with sword and spear and javelin. But I come against you in the name of the LORD of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied.

**46** Today the LORD will hand you over to me, and I will strike you down and cut off your head. Today I will give the corpses of the Philistine army to the birds of the sky and the wild animals of the earth, so that all the earth may know that there is a God in Israel,

**47** and that all this assembly may know that the LORD does not save by sword or spear. For the battle is the LORD's, and he will hand you over to us."

**48** When the Philistine started forward to meet David, David ran quickly towards the battle line to meet the Philistine.

**49** David put his hand in his bag and took out a stone and slung it, and struck the Philistine on the forehead. The stone sank into his forehead, and he fell on his face to the ground.

**50** So David defeated the Philistine with a sling and a stone. He struck the Philistine and killed him. There was no sword in David's hand.

**51** David ran and stood over the Philistine and took his sword and drew it from its sheath and killed him, and cut off his head with it. When the Philistines saw that their champion was dead, they ran away.

**52** The men of Israel and Judah got up with a shout and chased the Philistines as far as Gath and the gates of Ekron, and the wounded Philistines fell along the road from Shaaraim as far as Gath and Ekron.

**53** The Israelites came back from chasing the Philistines, and plundered their camp.

**54** David took the Philistine's head and brought it to Jerusalem, but he put the Philistine's weapons in his own tent.

**55** When Saul saw David going out to meet the Philistine, he said to Abner, the commander of the army, "Abner, whose son is this boy?" Abner said, "As you live, O king, I do not know."

**56** The king said, "Find out whose son the young man is."

**57** When David came back from killing the Philistine, Abner took him and brought him to Saul, with the Philistine's head in his hand.

**58** Saul said to him, "Whose son are you, young man?" David said, "I am the son of your servant Jesse of Bethlehem."

## 1 Samuel 20-24: David the Fugitive

### Chapter 20

**1** David ran away from Naioth in Ramah. He came to Jonathan and said, "What have I done? What is my guilt? What is my sin against your father, that he is trying to take my life?"

**2** Jonathan said to him, "Far from it. You will not die. Look, my father does nothing, great or small, without telling me. Why would my father hide this from me? It is not so."

**3** But David swore again and said, "Your father knows well that I have found favour in your eyes, and he thinks, 'Jonathan must not know this, or he will be grieved.' But as the LORD lives and as you live, there is only a step between me and death."

**4** Jonathan said to David, "Whatever you say, I will do for you."

**5** David said to Jonathan, "Look, tomorrow is the new moon, and I am supposed to sit with the king at the meal. Let me go, and I will hide in the field until the evening of the third day.

**6** If your father misses me at all, say, 'David asked me earnestly for leave to run to Bethlehem, his town, for there is a yearly sacrifice there for the whole clan.'

**7** If he says, 'Good,' then your servant is safe. But if he is angry, you will know that he has decided on harm.

**8** So show faithful love \[H-HESED\] to your servant, for you have brought your servant into a covenant of the LORD with you. But if there is guilt in me, kill me yourself. Why bring me to your father?"

**9** Jonathan said, "Far be it from you. If I knew for certain that my father had decided to bring harm on you, would I not tell you?"

**10** David said to Jonathan, "Who will tell me if your father answers you harshly?"

**11** Jonathan said to David, "Come, let us go out into the field." So the two of them went out into the field.

**12** Jonathan said to David, "The LORD, the God of Israel, is witness. When I have sounded out my father about this time tomorrow, or the third day, if there is good for David, will I not send and tell you?

**13** But if my father means to do you harm, may the LORD do this to Jonathan, and more also, if I do not tell you and send you away, so that you can go in safety. May the LORD be with you, as he has been with my father.

**14** If I am still alive, show me the faithful love of the LORD, so that I do not die.

**15** And do not ever cut off your faithful love from my household, not even when the LORD has cut off every one of David's enemies from the face of the earth."

**16** So Jonathan made a covenant with the house of David: "May the LORD call David's enemies to account."

**17** Jonathan made David swear again by his love for him, because he loved him as he loved his own life.

**18** Jonathan said to him, "Tomorrow is the new moon, and you will be missed, because your seat will be empty.

**19** On the third day you will be missed even more. Go to the place where you hid on the day of the trouble, and stay beside the stone heap.

**20** I will shoot three arrows to the side of it, as if I were aiming at a target.

**21** Then I will send the boy, saying, 'Go and find the arrows.' If I say to the boy, 'Look, the arrows are on this side of you, get them,' then come, for as the LORD lives, you are safe and there is no danger.

**22** But if I say to the young man, 'Look, the arrows are beyond you,' then go, for the LORD has sent you away.

**23** As for the matter that you and I have spoken about, the LORD is between you and me for ever."

**24** So David hid in the field. When the new moon came, the king sat down to eat the meal.

**25** The king sat on his seat, as usual, the seat by the wall. Jonathan sat opposite, and Abner sat at Saul's side, but David's place was empty.

**26** Saul said nothing that day, for he thought, "Something has happened to him. He is unclean. Surely he is unclean."

**27** But on the second day of the month, the day after the new moon, David's place was empty again. Saul said to his son Jonathan, "Why has the son of Jesse not come to the meal, either yesterday or today?"

**28** Jonathan answered Saul, "David earnestly asked me for leave to go to Bethlehem.

**29** He said, 'Please let me go, for our clan has a sacrifice in the town, and my brother has commanded me to be there. Now, if I have found favour in your eyes, let me get away and see my brothers.' That is why he has not come to the king's table."

**30** Then Saul's anger blazed against Jonathan, and he said to him, "You son of a twisted and rebellious woman. Do I not know that you have sided with the son of Jesse, to your own shame and to the shame of your mother's nakedness?

**31** For as long as the son of Jesse lives on the earth, neither you nor your kingdom will be established. Now send and bring him to me, for he must die."

**32** Jonathan answered his father Saul, "Why should he be put to death? What has he done?"

**33** But Saul hurled his spear at him to strike him down. So Jonathan knew that his father had decided to put David to death.

**34** Jonathan got up from the table in fierce anger and ate no food on the second day of the month, for he was grieved for David, because his father had humiliated him.

**35** In the morning Jonathan went out into the field for the meeting with David, with a small boy.

**36** He said to his boy, "Run and find the arrows I shoot." As the boy ran, he shot an arrow beyond him.

**37** When the boy came to the place of the arrow that Jonathan had shot, Jonathan called after the boy, "Is the arrow not beyond you?"

**38** Jonathan called after the boy, "Hurry. Be quick. Do not stop." So Jonathan's boy gathered up the arrow and came to his master.

**39** But the boy knew nothing. Only Jonathan and David knew the matter.

**40** Jonathan gave his weapons to his boy and said to him, "Go and take them to the town."

**41** As soon as the boy had gone, David got up from beside the stone heap and fell on his face to the ground and bowed down three times. They kissed each other and wept together, David most of all.

**42** Then Jonathan said to David, "Go in peace, since we have sworn to each other in the name of the LORD, saying, 'The LORD will be between you and me, and between my descendants and your descendants, for ever.'" Then David got up and left, and Jonathan went back into the town.

### Chapter 21

### Chapter 22

**7** Saul said to his servants who stood round him, "Listen, you Benjaminites. Will the son of Jesse give every one of you fields and vineyards? Will he make you all commanders of thousands and commanders of hundreds,

## 1 Samuel 25-27: Nabal and Abigail, a Second Sparing and Ziklag

**6** David was in great distress, for the people spoke of stoning him, because all of them were bitter in spirit, each for his sons and daughters. But David found strength in the LORD his God.

**7** David said to the priest Abiathar son of Ahimelech, "Bring me the ephod." Abiathar brought the ephod to David.

**8** David asked the LORD, "Shall I chase this raiding band? Will I overtake them?" He said to him, "Chase them, for you will certainly overtake them, and you will certainly rescue the captives."

**9** So David set out, he and the six hundred men with him, and they came to the Wadi Besor, where some stayed behind.

**10** David went on with four hundred men, for two hundred stayed behind, too exhausted to cross the Wadi Besor.

**11** They found an Egyptian in the open country and brought him to David. They gave him bread and he ate, and they gave him water to drink,

**12** and gave him a piece of a fig cake and two bunches of raisins. When he had eaten, his strength returned, for he had not eaten bread or drunk water for three days and three nights.

**13** David said to him, "Whose man are you? Where are you from?" He said, "I am a young Egyptian, the servant of an Amalekite. My master left me behind because I fell ill three days ago.

**14** We raided the Negev of the Cherethites and the territory of Judah and the Negev of Caleb, and we burned Ziklag down."

**15** David said to him, "Will you take me down to this raiding band?" He said, "Swear to me by God that you will not kill me or hand me over to my master, and I will take you down to this band."

**16** He took him down, and there they were, spread out over the whole ground, eating and drinking and dancing because of all the great plunder they had taken from the land of the Philistines and from the land of Judah.

**17** David struck them down from twilight until the evening of the next day. Not one of them escaped, except four hundred young men who rode off on camels.

**18** David recovered everything the Amalekites had taken, and David rescued his two wives.

**19** Nothing was missing, small or great, sons or daughters, plunder or anything they had taken. David brought everything back.

**20** David also took all the flocks and herds, and the people drove them ahead of the other livestock and said, "This is David's plunder."

**21** David came to the two hundred men who had been too exhausted to follow him, who had been left at the Wadi Besor. They came out to meet David and the people with him. When David came near the people, he greeted them.

**22** Then all the wicked and worthless men among those who had gone with David said, "Because they did not go with us, we will not give them any of the plunder we recovered, except that each man may take his wife and children and go."

**23** But David said, "You must not do that, my brothers, with what the LORD has given us. He has kept us safe and handed over to us the raiding band that came against us.

**24** Who will listen to you in this matter? The share of the one who goes down into battle and the share of the one who stays by the baggage will be the same. They will share alike."

**25** From that day on it has been a statute and a rule for Israel, to this day.

**26** When David came to Ziklag, he sent some of the plunder to the elders of Judah, his friends, saying, "Here is a gift for you from the plunder of the LORD's enemies."

**27** It was for those in Bethel, in Ramoth of the Negev, in Jattir,

**28** in Aroer, in Siphmoth, in Eshtemoa,

**29** in Racal, in the towns of the Jerahmeelites, in the towns of the Kenites,

**30** in Hormah, in Bor-ashan, in Athach,

**31** in Hebron, and in all the places where David and his men had roamed.

### Chapter 31

**1** The Philistines fought against Israel, and the men of Israel fled before the Philistines and fell dead on Mount Gilboa.

**2** The Philistines caught up with Saul and his sons, and the Philistines killed Jonathan, Abinadab and Malchi-shua, the sons of Saul.

**3** The battle pressed hard on Saul, and the archers found him, and he was badly wounded by the archers.

**4** Saul said to his armour-bearer, "Draw your sword and run me through, so that these uncircumcised men do not come and run me through and make sport of me." But his armour-bearer would not, for he was terrified. So Saul took his own sword and fell on it.

**5** When his armour-bearer saw that Saul was dead, he too fell on his sword and died with him.

**6** So Saul died, and his three sons, and his armour-bearer, and all his men, on the same day together.

**7** When the men of Israel on the other side of the valley and those beyond the Jordan saw that the army of Israel had fled and that Saul and his sons were dead, they abandoned their towns and fled, and the Philistines came and lived in them.

**8** The next day, when the Philistines came to strip the dead, they found Saul and his three sons lying on Mount Gilboa.

**9** They cut off his head and stripped off his armour, and sent messengers throughout the land of the Philistines to carry the news to their idols and to the people.

**10** They put his armour in the temple of the Ashtoreths, and they fastened his body to the wall of Beth-shan.

**11** When the people of Jabesh-gilead heard what the Philistines had done to Saul,

**12** all the brave men set out and travelled through the night, and took the body of Saul and the bodies of his sons from the wall of Beth-shan, and they came to Jabesh and burned them there.

**13** Then they took their bones and buried them under the tamarisk tree at Jabesh, and fasted for seven days.

*End of the book of 1 Samuel. Review note: 1 Samuel 28 to 31 was drafted in WABV wording. The Hebrew text differs from the Greek translation and from the Dead Sea Scrolls in several places, and the footnotes record the main differences. The story of Saul and the medium at En-dor (28:3-25) contains a scene of calling up the dead that the Hebrew presents without comment on what exactly takes place. The text is followed as written, and the footnotes note the questions. The death of Saul is told here and again, differently, in 2 Samuel 1. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **Title** 1 Samuel and 2 Samuel were one book in the Hebrew Bible, divided later. The book follows Samuel, Saul and David. The Hebrew text of Samuel is in many places different from the Greek translation and from the Dead Sea Scrolls (4QSam), and the footnotes record the main differences.
- **1:1** "A Zuphite from the hill country of Ephraim" places Elkanah's family in Ephraim. In 1 Chronicles 6:22-28 Samuel is listed among the Levites. "Ramathaim" is probably the same place as Ramah (verse 19).
- **1:2** "Hannah" means "grace". Having two wives, with one without children, is a pattern in Genesis (Sarah, Rachel).
- **1:3** "The LORD of hosts" is Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*. It is a title that first appears in the book here. "Hosts" are armies, either of Israel or of heaven. Shiloh is the place of the tent of meeting (Joshua 18:1).
- **1:5** "A double portion" is Hebrew *manah achat appayim*, which is obscure. It may mean a portion for two, or a portion of equal rank, or a single large portion. The WABV text follows "double" and records the other readings.
- **1:9** "The temple of the LORD" is Hebrew *heikhal*, a word for a palace or sanctuary, here used of the tent shrine at Shiloh. It had doorposts.
- **1:11** "No razor will touch his head" is a vow like the Nazirite vow (see Numbers 6 and entry H-NAZIR). The Dead Sea Scrolls and the Greek add "wine and strong drink" here.
- **1:13** Hannah prays silently, so that only her lips move. Eli, who knew only loud prayer, thinks she is drunk.
- **1:15** "A heavy spirit" is Hebrew *qeshat ruach*, a heart that is hard-pressed.
- **1:20** "Samuel" (*Shemu'el*) is explained as "I asked the LORD for him" (*sha'al*). The name may also be heard as *shem El*, "the name of God".
- **1:23** "May the LORD make his word come true" is the Hebrew reading. The Dead Sea Scrolls and the Greek read "your word", meaning Hannah's vow.
- **1:24** "A three-year-old bull" follows the Dead Sea Scrolls, the Greek and the Syriac. The Hebrew text reads "three bulls", though verse 25 then speaks of a single bull. Here the WABV text departs from the Hebrew, and the choice is flagged for the scholar.
- **1:28** "Lent" is Hebrew *sha'ul*, the same word as "Saul". The play on words is between *sha'al* (asked) and *sha'ul* (lent, asked for). "He worshipped" is the Hebrew reading, and some manuscripts read "they worshipped".
- **2:1** Hannah's prayer is a poem. "My horn is lifted high" is a figure for strength and honour, drawn from the horns of an animal.
- **2:2** "No one holy" is Hebrew *qadosh*. See entry H-QADOSH. "Rock" (*tsur*) is a title of God (see Deuteronomy 32:4 and entry H-TSUR).
- **2:3** "By him deeds are weighed" is the Hebrew reading. Some ancient texts read "he does not weigh deeds" and have a different sense.
- **2:5** "Seven" is a number of fullness. The line states a reversal for the barren woman.
- **2:6** "Sheol" is the place of the dead. See entry H-SHEOL.
- **2:8** The raising of the poor and the "pillars of the earth" resemble Psalm 113:7-9.
- **2:9** "His faithful ones" is Hebrew *chasidav*. See entry H-HESED. The line "not by strength does a person prevail" is a theme of the book.
- **2:10** "His king ... his anointed" is the first use in the Bible of the word for "anointed" (*mashiach*) in the sense of a king. The song speaks of a king before Israel had one.
- **2:12** "Did not know the LORD" says they were not loyal to him. The phrase is repeated in 3:7 of Samuel, who had not yet had a revelation.
- **2:13** The priests' custom is of taking meat from the pot with a fork. The law (Leviticus 7:31-34) fixed the priests' share. Taking it by force, before the fat was burned, treats the offering "with contempt".
- **2:18** "A linen ephod" is a simple garment worn by a priest's servant. It is not the high priest's ephod.
- **2:22** The verse about Eli's sons lying with the women who serve at the tent entrance is missing in some Greek and Hebrew manuscripts. It is included here. "The women who served" refers to Exodus 38:8.
- **2:25** "Mediate" is Hebrew *pillel*, to judge or intercede. "It was the LORD's will to put them to death" says the sons had gone past the point of repentance.
- **2:27** "A man of God" is an unnamed prophet. His message is a judgement on Eli's house.
- **2:30** The promise that Eli's house would "walk before me for ever" refers to the priesthood of Aaron's line (Exodus 29:9). The promise is conditional on honouring the LORD.
- **2:33** The man left at the altar will weep. This refers to Abiathar, the survivor of the massacre at Nob (22:20-23).
- **2:34** The sign of the death of Hophni and Phinehas on the same day is fulfilled in 4:11.
- **2:35** "A faithful priest" is probably Zadok (1 Kings 2:35). "My anointed" refers to the king.
- **3:1** "The word of the LORD was rare" and "there was no widespread vision" describes the time before Samuel.
- **3:3** "The lamp of God" is the lamp that burned through the night in the sanctuary (Exodus 27:20-21). "The temple of the LORD, where the ark of God was" is the shrine at Shiloh.
- **3:7** "Did not yet know the LORD" means he had not yet received a revelation of him.
- **3:11** "Make both ears tingle" is a figure for shock at news. It is used in 2 Kings 21:12 and Jeremiah 19:3.
- **3:13** "His sons were bringing a curse on themselves" is the Hebrew reading as the scribes handed it down. An ancient tradition says the original text read "were cursing God", and the scribes changed it out of respect. The Greek reads "cursing God".
- **3:14** "Will never be atoned for by sacrifice or offering" says the sin of Eli's house is beyond the reach of the offering system.
- **3:17** "May God do this to you, and more also" is an oath formula, as in Ruth 1:17.
- **3:19** "Let none of his words fall to the ground" says that all that Samuel said came true.
- **3:20** "A prophet" is Hebrew *navi*. See entry H-NAVI. "From Dan to Beersheba" is the whole land.
- **4:1** "Samuel's word came to all Israel" is the Hebrew text. The Greek has a longer opening that includes the Philistine attack. Ebenezer and Aphek are the sites of the battle. Ebenezer means "stone of help" (see 7:12).
- **4:3** The elders ask why the LORD has beaten them, but then they bring the ark as if it would work by itself. The ark is treated as a means of victory, with no turning to the LORD.
- **4:4** "Who sits enthroned on the cherubim" is the title of the LORD in connection with the ark (see Exodus 25:18-22 and 2 Samuel 6:2).
- **4:7** The Philistines say "a god has come" and speak of "mighty gods" in the plural. They think in terms of many gods.
- **4:8** The Philistines mix up the exodus. The plagues were in Egypt, not in the wilderness.
- **4:9** "Be men" is a call to courage.
- **4:11** The capture of the ark and the death of Hophni and Phinehas fulfil the sign of 2:34.
- **4:13** Eli sits by the road because his heart trembles for the ark, not for his sons.
- **4:15** "Ninety-eight years old" and "his eyes were fixed" show that Eli's sight had gone (see 3:2).
- **4:18** Eli's death at the news of the ark ends his forty years of judging. The Greek text has twenty years, and the two numbers may be a difference of tradition.
- **4:19** The wife of Phinehas names her son in her last hour.
- **4:21** "Ichabod" can be read "no glory" or "where is the glory?". The "glory" is the presence of the LORD with the ark.
- **5:2** "Dagon" is the god of the Philistines. The temple at Ashdod holds the captured ark as a trophy.
- **5:4** The head and hands of Dagon cut off recall the way a victor displays a defeated enemy. The threshold custom is also known from Zephaniah 1:9.
- **5:6** "Tumours" is Hebrew *ophalim* (the reading tradition has *techorim*), swellings or sores. The nature of the disease is uncertain. Some have thought of a plague carried by rats. The Hebrew does not mention mice until 6:4. Some Greek manuscripts add mice in 5:6.
- **5:8** "The rulers of the Philistines" is Hebrew *seranim*, as in Joshua 13:3. They decide to move the ark from city to city.
- **5:11** "A deadly panic" shows the fear that the ark's presence brings.
- **6:3** "A guilt offering" is Hebrew *asham*. See entry H-ASHAM. In Israel it is a payment for a wrong. The priests of the Philistines understand the plague as needing such a payment.
- **6:4** "Five gold tumours and five gold mice" are images made to represent the disease and its cause, and given to the god as a payment for healing. This was a custom of the ancient world.
- **6:6** The warning not to harden their hearts like Pharaoh shows that the Philistine priests know the story of the exodus (see also 4:8).
- **6:7** "Two milking cows that have never been under a yoke" is a test. Cows with calves would not leave them, so the road they take shows a higher power.
- **6:9** "By chance" is Hebrew *miqreh*, the same word as in Ruth 2:3.
- **6:15** The Levites' part in taking the ark down is the Hebrew text. Some think the verse is an addition, since the people of Beth-shemesh act in verse 14.
- **6:18** "The large stone" is the Hebrew reading in many manuscripts. Some read "the large meadow" (*abel*) instead of "stone" (*even*). The WABV text follows "stone".
- **6:19** "Because they looked into the ark" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek text differs. The Hebrew number of the dead is "seventy men, fifty thousand men". The very large figure is probably a scribal error. The WABV text follows the shorter "seventy", which is also in the Jewish historian Josephus.
- **6:20** "This holy God" is Hebrew *qadosh*. See entry H-QADOSH. The people are afraid of the LORD's holiness.
- **7:1** Kiriath-jearim is a town near Beth-shemesh. The ark stays there until David moves it (2 Samuel 6).
- **7:2** "Some twenty years" is the time the ark stays at Kiriath-jearim before David brings it up.
- **7:3** "Returning" is Hebrew *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV. "The Ashtoreths" are the goddesses of the nations (see Judges 2:13).
- **7:6** "Poured it out before the LORD" is an act of repentance. The meaning is not fully clear, and it may be a sign of pouring out one's heart (see Lamentations 2:19).
- **7:10** "The LORD thundered" is a storm that defeats the Philistines, as in Judges 5:20 and Joshua 10:11.
- **7:12** "Shen" is uncertain. The Greek reads "Jeshanah". "Ebenezer" means "stone of help".
- **7:13** "The hand of the LORD was against the Philistines all the days of Samuel" is in tension with the Philistine control in chapters 9 to 13.
- **7:14** The peace with the Amorites is a note that the Canaanite peoples are quiet at this time.
- **7:16** Samuel's circuit to Bethel, Gilgal and Mizpah was to judge, that is, to govern and decide cases.
- **7:17** Ramah is Samuel's home. His altar there is a sign of his own priestly role.
- **8:1** Samuel's sons are made judges, as Eli's sons had been priests. Both pairs of sons do not follow their fathers.
- **8:3** "Took bribes and twisted justice" breaks the command in Exodus 23:8 and Deuteronomy 16:19.
- **8:5** "A king ... like all the nations" repeats the words of Deuteronomy 17:14. In the law a king was allowed, but in a different pattern. The elders' request is for a king "like all the nations".
- **8:7** "They have rejected me from being king over them" says the request is a rejection of the LORD's rule, even though the LORD tells Samuel to give them what they ask.
- **8:11** "The way of the king" is Hebrew *mishpat ha-melekh*, the custom or rights of a king. The list (verses 11 to 17) describes military service, forced labour and taxes. Later kings did these things, especially Solomon (1 Kings 4 to 5; 12:4).
- **8:15** "A tenth" is Hebrew *ma'aser*, the same word as the tithe. The king takes what God's law gave to the sanctuary.
- **8:17** "You yourselves will become his slaves" reverses the exodus.
- **8:18** "The LORD will not answer you" is a stark warning. The people have chosen the king, so the LORD will not rescue them from his demands.
- **8:22** "Make a king for them" is the LORD's command. The text holds together the warning against the king and the permission to have one.
- **9:1** "A man of standing" is Hebrew *gibbor chayil*, as in Ruth 2:1. Kish is of Benjamin, the smallest tribe (verse 21).
- **9:2** "Saul" (*Sha'ul*) means "asked for", from the same root as in 1:20 and 1:28. "A head taller" is a mark of the strong figure the people want.
- **9:4** The places named on Saul's search are not now known.
- **9:6** "A man of God" is Samuel, whom the servant knows by reputation.
- **9:9** The note that the prophet was formerly called a "seer" (*ro'eh*) is a comment added later. "Prophet" is *navi*. See entry H-NAVI.
- **9:12** "The high place" is Hebrew *bamah*, a hilltop shrine. Sacrifice at such places was common before the temple.
- **9:16** "Ruler" is Hebrew *nagid*, "leader" or "prince", not *melek*, "king". The LORD uses this word for Saul. "Their cry has come to me" repeats Exodus 3:7, and shows the LORD's care for the people.
- **9:17** "Govern" is Hebrew *atsar*, which means "restrain" or "hold in check".
- **9:21** Benjamin is "the smallest of the tribes" after the near destruction in Judges 20 and 21. Saul's reply shows modesty, as with Gideon in Judges 6:15.
- **9:24** "The leg and what was on it" is a difficult phrase. The Hebrew may mean the leg and the fat tail. It was a prime portion, kept for the honoured guest.
- **10:1** The Hebrew text is short here. The Greek has a longer text that adds that Saul will reign over the LORD's people and save them from their enemies, as a sign. The Hebrew is followed.
- **10:2** "Rachel's tomb" is in Genesis 35:19 near Bethlehem. This verse places it at Zelzah on the border of Benjamin, which is another place. The location is debated.
- **10:5** "Gibeath-elohim" means "hill of God". A group of prophets with music is a company who prophesy in a state of ecstasy.
- **10:6** "The spirit of the LORD" is Hebrew *ruach YHWH*. See entry H-RUACH. "Changed into a different man" says Saul is given a new heart (verse 9).
- **10:8** The command to wait seven days at Gilgal is the basis of the conflict in 13:8-14.
- **10:12** "Is Saul also among the prophets?" becomes a proverb. It is used again in 19:24, with a different sense.
- **10:17** Samuel summons the people to Mizpah for the choosing of the king, as in the time of the judges (Judges 20:1).
- **10:21** "The family of the Matrites" is the reading of the Hebrew. The Greek differs. The choosing is by lot, as in Joshua 7:14-18.
- **10:24** "Long live the king" is Hebrew *yechi ha-melekh*, a shout of acclamation.
- **10:25** "The rules of the kingship" is Hebrew *mishpat ha-melukhah*. The written rules are laid up before the LORD, probably the same as Deuteronomy 17:14-20. They are different from the "way of the king" in 8:11.
- **10:27** The Dead Sea Scrolls and the Jewish historian Josephus have an added passage before 11:1. It says that Nahash king of the Ammonites had gouged out the right eyes of the Gadites and Reubenites, and that seven thousand escaped to Jabesh-gilead. Some Greek manuscripts begin 11:1 "About a month later". The Hebrew is followed, and the passage is not in the text.
- **11:2** "Gouge out the right eye" is a mutilation to shame the people and make soldiers unfit.
- **11:3** The elders ask for seven days, which gives time to send for help.
- **11:7** Cutting the oxen in pieces and sending them round the tribes is like the summons in Judges 19:29.
- **11:8** "Three hundred thousand" and "thirty thousand" are the Hebrew numbers. The Greek has six hundred thousand and seventy thousand. The numbers are large and may be symbolic.
- **11:12** The people want to put to death those who had doubted Saul. His reply in verse 13 shows mercy.
- **11:14** "Renew the kingship" is the third stage of Saul's rise: the private anointing (9:1 to 10:16), the lot at Mizpah (10:17-27), and the acclamation after the victory at Gilgal.
- **12:1** Samuel's speech is a farewell address, like those of Moses (Deuteronomy 31 and 32) and Joshua (Joshua 23 and 24).
- **12:3** "His anointed" is Saul. "A bribe to blind my eyes" repeats Exodus 23:8.
- **12:9** "He sold them" is a figure of handing over to an enemy, as in Judges 2:14.
- **12:11** "Bedan" is the Hebrew reading, a judge who is not named in the book of Judges. The Greek and Syriac read "Barak". Some Syriac manuscripts read "Samson" in place of "Samuel". The Hebrew is followed, and the other readings are recorded.
- **12:12** Samuel says it was Nahash's threat that made the people ask for a king. In chapter 8 the request came before Nahash appears. The two accounts are kept.
- **12:17** "The wheat harvest" is in the dry season, when rain and thunder are rare, so the storm is a sign.
- **12:21** "Empty things" is Hebrew *tohu*, the word for formless emptiness in Genesis 1:2. It means idols that have no power.
- **12:22** "For his great name's sake" says the LORD keeps his people because of his own name, not because of their worth.
- **12:23** Samuel promises to keep praying for the people and teaching them.
- **13:1** The Hebrew text of this verse is damaged. The numbers of Saul's age and the years of his reign are missing, and the verse is left with gaps in the WABV text. Some later Hebrew manuscripts and the Greek add numbers, such as "thirty" and "forty-two", but these are not reliable. Acts 13:21 gives forty years for Saul's reign.
- **13:2** The Hebrew moves between "Gibeah" and "Geba" (see verses 3 and 16), two names for places in Benjamin that are very close and possibly the same.
- **13:3** Jonathan strikes the garrison, but Saul blows the trumpet and the victory is told as Saul's. "The Hebrews" is the name the Philistines use for Israel.
- **13:4** "A stench" is a figure for being hated.
- **13:5** "Thirty thousand chariots" is the Hebrew text. The Greek has three thousand. The Hebrew figure is large and may be an error.
- **13:8** The seven days are the time Samuel set in 10:8. Saul waits the full seven days, but Samuel arrives at the end of it.
- **13:9** Saul offers the burnt offering himself. In the law only priests could offer sacrifice, but the story presents his offence as not waiting for Samuel and not keeping the LORD's command (verse 13). The text does not give the reason in more detail.
- **13:12** "I forced myself" is Saul's excuse for doing what he knew to be wrong.
- **13:14** "A man after his own heart" refers to David. "Ruler" is *nagid*, as in 9:16 and 10:1.
- **13:15** The Hebrew text is short and uncertain here. The Greek reads differently, and says Samuel went up from Gilgal and the rest of the people went after Saul.
- **13:19** The Philistine control of metalworking meant that Israel lacked iron weapons. The text shows how little Israel had.
- **13:21** "Two-thirds of a shekel" translates the Hebrew *pim*, a weight that is also known from weights found in excavations. The Hebrew of the verse is difficult.
- **14:2** "Under the pomegranate tree at Migron" is the Hebrew reading. Some take "pomegranate" as the place name Rimmon.
- **14:3** Ahijah is the priest. He is of the family of Eli. Some think he is the same as Ahimelech, who is killed in chapter 22.
- **14:6** "These uncircumcised men" is a taunt. "By many or by few" recalls Gideon's story (Judges 7:2-7).
- **14:14** "Half a furrow's length" is a difficult Hebrew phrase. It means a small area of ground.
- **14:15** "A panic from God" is Hebrew *charedat elohim*. The earth shook, and the Philistines panicked.
- **14:18** The Hebrew text has "the ark of God". The Greek has "the ephod". Since the ark was not with the army at this time, "ephod" is likely to be the original reading, but the Hebrew is followed here.
- **14:19** "Withdraw your hand" means stop the consultation of the priest.
- **14:24** Saul's oath is a rash oath. It is meant to keep the army fighting, but it leaves the army weak and leads to the sin of eating with the blood (verse 32).
- **14:27** Jonathan has not heard the oath and tastes the honey. His "eyes brightened" shows the energy it gave him.
- **14:32** Eating meat with the blood is forbidden in Leviticus 17:10-14. Saul's order to slaughter at a stone and altar (verses 33 to 35) deals with it.
- **14:35** "The first altar he built" marks a step in Saul's reign.
- **14:41** The Hebrew text reads only "Give a true lot" (*tamim*). The Greek has a fuller text: "If the guilt is in me or in Jonathan, give Urim. But if it is in your people, give Thummim." The Hebrew is followed. The lot works by Urim and Thummim (see Exodus 28:30).
- **14:45** "Rescued" is Hebrew *padah*. See entry H-PADAH. The people redeem Jonathan, and the text does not say how.
- **14:47** The list of Saul's wars is a summary of his reign. It shows him as an active military leader.
- **14:49** "Ishvi" may be another name for Ish-bosheth, Saul's son who later reigns (2 Samuel 2:8). The daughters are Merab and Michal.
- **14:50** Abner is called Saul's uncle. It may also mean a more distant relative.
- **15:2** "What he did to Israel" refers to Amalek's attack in Exodus 17:8-16, and the command in Deuteronomy 25:17-19.
- **15:3** "Devote to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. The command includes "man and woman, child and infant", as well as the animals. It is rendered as written, and is among the ban passages for editorial decision.
- **15:6** The Kenites are spared because of their "faithful love" (*hesed*, see entry H-HESED) to Israel at the time of the exodus (see Exodus 18).
- **15:8** "Agag" was the king of the Amalekites. Agag is also the name of an Amalekite king in Numbers 24:7.
- **15:9** The people spared Agag and the best of the animals. The command was to devote everything, so Saul's act is disobedience. The text then speaks of everything "worthless and weak" being destroyed.
- **15:11** "I regret" is Hebrew *nacham*, to change one's mind or feel sorry. It is also used in verse 35. Verse 29 says that the LORD does not "change his mind", using the same verb. The two statements are kept as written.
- **15:12** "A monument for himself" is Hebrew *yad*, a memorial or a hand. Saul sets up a monument to his own victory at Carmel in Judah.
- **15:22** Samuel's saying is poetry. "To obey is better than sacrifice" is quoted in the prophets (see Hosea 6:6 and Micah 6:6-8).
- **15:23** "Rebellion is the sin of divination" compares disobedience to the sin of seeking other powers.
- **15:24** "I was afraid of the people" is Saul's confession. He blames the people.
- **15:28** "A neighbour of yours who is better than you" is David, though he is not named.
- **15:29** "The Glory of Israel" is Hebrew *netsach yisra'el*, which can also be read "the Eternal One of Israel" or "the Victor of Israel". The verse says the LORD does not change his mind, in tension with verses 11 and 35.
- **15:32** "In chains" is Hebrew *ma'adannot*, a word of uncertain meaning. It can mean "in chains", "cheerfully" or "trembling". Agag's words about the bitterness of death follow.
- **15:33** Samuel cutting Agag to pieces is rendered as written. Samuel acts in a priestly role at the sanctuary at Gilgal, "before the LORD".
- **15:35** "Samuel grieved over Saul" and "the LORD regretted" close the chapter.
- **16:1** "A king for myself" is literally "I have seen among his sons a king for me". "Your horn" is a horn used as a flask for oil.
- **16:2** The LORD tells Samuel to say he has come to sacrifice. He does come to sacrifice (verse 5), so the cover story is true, though it leaves out his main purpose.
- **16:5** "Make yourselves holy" is Hebrew *qadash*. See entry H-QADOSH. It means to prepare by washing and avoiding impurity.
- **16:7** "The LORD looks at the heart" is the key verse of the story. It sets David against the tall, handsome Saul (9:2).
- **16:10** Seven sons pass before Samuel and David is the eighth (see 17:12). The list in 1 Chronicles 2:13-15 names only seven sons of Jesse including David.
- **16:12** "Ruddy" is Hebrew *admoni*, reddish in complexion or hair.
- **16:13** "The spirit of the LORD" is Hebrew *ruach YHWH*. See entry H-RUACH. "Rushed on David" is the same verb used of Samson (Judges 14:6). The anointing is done privately, "in the middle of his brothers".
- **16:14** "A harmful spirit from the LORD" is Hebrew *ruach ra'ah*. It is also in verses 15 and 16 and in 18:10 and 19:9. It is sent by God, as in Judges 9:23.
- **16:18** "The LORD is with him" is a phrase that recurs about David (18:12, 14, 28).
- **16:21** David becomes Saul's armour-bearer and musician. In 17:55 to 58 Saul asks whose son David is, as though he did not know him. The two accounts are kept as they stand.
- **17:1** Socoh, Azekah and Ephes-dammim are in the Valley of Elah, on the border between Judah and the Philistines.
- **17:4** "Six cubits and a span" is the Hebrew text (about 2.9 metres). The Dead Sea Scrolls, some Greek manuscripts and Josephus read "four cubits and a span" (about 2 metres). The Hebrew is followed here.
- **17:5** "Five thousand shekels" is about 57 kilograms. The spear head of 600 shekels (verse 7) is about 6.8 kilograms.
- **17:12** Verses 12 to 31, and also 17:41, 17:50, 17:55 to 18:6, 18:9 to 11, 18:17 to 19, and 18:29 to 30, are missing from the main Greek manuscripts. The Hebrew text is much longer. The Hebrew is followed here, and the Greek omissions are recorded.
- **17:16** "Forty days" is a round number.
- **17:26** "Uncircumcised" is a taunt. "The living God" is a title that sets the LORD against the dead gods of the nations.
- **17:28** Eliab's anger at David may reflect his being passed over in 16:6-7.
- **17:40** "Five smooth stones" were enough to try several shots. The sling was a weapon of skilled shepherds and soldiers (see Judges 20:16).
- **17:43** "Am I a dog" is the Philistine's scorn at the staff. The Philistine "cursed David by his gods".
- **17:45** "In the name of the LORD of hosts" is David's answer to the spear and sword.
- **17:47** "The battle is the LORD's" is a central statement of the story. It echoes 14:6.
- **17:49** The stone strikes the forehead, the one place not covered by the helmet in the account.
- **17:54** "Jerusalem" is mentioned here, though the city was not yet in Israelite hands (see 2 Samuel 5:6-9). The verse may look ahead.
- **17:55** Saul's question, "Whose son is this boy?" is hard to fit with 16:21, where David already serves Saul. Some see two traditions. The Greek omits the passage.
- **18:1** "The soul of Jonathan was bound to the soul of David" uses *nefesh* (see entry H-NEPHESH). The text speaks of Jonathan's love for David as his own self, and of a covenant (verse 3, see entry H-BERIT). The text does not say more about the nature of this love.
- **18:4** Giving David his robe and armour is a gift of his own place as heir. Jonathan acknowledges David's place.
- **18:6** "Three-stringed instruments" is Hebrew *shalishim*, a word of uncertain meaning. It may be a triangle or a three-stringed instrument.
- **18:7** The song "Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his tens of thousands" is repeated in 21:11 and 29:5. It is a poetic parallelism, but Saul hears it as a comparison.
- **18:10** "Raved" is Hebrew *hitnabbe*, the same verb as "prophesied". It describes Saul in a frenzy.
- **18:13** Saul gives David a command of a thousand in order to send him away, but it increases David's fame.
- **18:17** The offer of Merab is a trap. Saul hopes the Philistines will kill David.
- **18:19** Merab is given to Adriel. Her five sons by Adriel are later handed over to the Gibeonites (2 Samuel 21:8-9), though the Hebrew there names Michal.
- **18:20** "Michal loved David" is one of the very few places in the narrative books where a woman is said to love a man, and the text gives her feeling as the reason for the match.
- **18:25** A hundred foreskins were a bride-price chosen to put David in danger.
- **18:27** "Two hundred" is the Hebrew number. The Greek has a hundred.
- **19:1** Jonathan warns David, and the story shows his loyalty to David against his father.
- **19:5** "Innocent blood" is blood shed without cause.
- **19:13** "The household god" is Hebrew *teraphim* (see Judges 17:5). It was large enough to look like a man in a bed. Michal had an image of this kind in her house. "A net of goats' hair" is uncertain. It may be a pillow or a mesh cloth.
- **19:17** Michal tells Saul that David threatened her. This is a lie, told to protect herself.
- **19:18** "Naioth" is a place within Ramah where the prophets lived. It may mean "dwellings".
- **19:20** The spirit of God overpowers Saul's messengers, and then Saul himself, with prophesying. It protects David.
- **19:24** "He lay naked" is the Hebrew. The saying "Is Saul also among the prophets?" is explained here in a way different from 10:11-12.
- **20:1** David's questions to Jonathan, "What have I done?", repeat his plea in 17:29 and show his innocence.
- **20:3** "As the LORD lives and as you live" is an oath formula. "A step between me and death" is a figure for great danger.
- **20:5** "The new moon" was a monthly festival with a sacrificial meal. David is expected at the king's table.
- **20:8** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. "A covenant of the LORD" is the covenant made in 18:3.
- **20:14** Jonathan asks David to show him faithful love, and not to cut off his household. This is carried out in 2 Samuel 9.
- **20:19** "The stone heap" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek has a different name for the place. The exact site is not known.
- **20:26** "He is unclean" refers to ritual impurity, which would keep a man from a sacrificial meal (see Leviticus 7:20-21).
- **20:30** Saul's insult, "son of a twisted and rebellious woman", is a crude one and is aimed at Jonathan's mother. "The shame of your mother's nakedness" means he claims Jonathan's choice shames his birth.
- **20:31** Saul sees that David is a rival for Jonathan's own claim to the throne.
- **20:33** Saul throws his spear at his own son, as he had done at David (18:11).
- **20:41** David bows three times, as to a superior, and then the two friends weep.
- **20:42** In the Hebrew Bible, the last clause of this verse ("and Jonathan went back into the town") begins chapter 21, so the numbering of chapter 21 is one higher than here.
- **21:1** Nob is a priestly town, near Jerusalem. The English chapter 21 has fifteen verses, and the Hebrew has sixteen, since Hebrew 21:2 is English 21:1. Ahimelech is the priest. He may be the same man as Ahijah in 14:3.
- **21:2** David's claim that the king sent him on a secret mission is not true. David later admits that this lie led to the death of the priests (22:22).
- **21:4** "Holy bread" is the bread of the Presence, which only the priests could eat (Leviticus 24:5-9). Ahimelech allows it, if the young men have kept from women, which is a rule of purity (see Exodus 19:15). Jesus refers to this passage in Matthew 12:3-4 and Mark 2:25-26.
- **21:5** "The young men's bodies are holy" translates Hebrew *keli*, "vessels". The word can mean "bodies" or "gear". The WABV text takes it as "bodies".
- **21:7** "Detained before the LORD" may mean he was held for a vow or a purification. The Edomite Doeg is present and later informs on David.
- **21:9** The sword of Goliath is kept at the sanctuary, "behind the ephod". David takes it (see 17:54).
- **21:10** Achish is the king of Gath, the Philistine city of Goliath.
- **21:11** "The king of the land" shows the Philistines recognise David's standing. The song is the one in 18:7.
- **21:13** David's pretending to be mad is said in the titles of Psalms 34 and 56.
- **22:1** "The cave of Adullam" is in the lowland of Judah. David's family joins him because they are no longer safe.
- **22:2** The men who join David are those who are in trouble, in debt or bitter. They are the same kind of people who joined Jephthah (Judges 11:3).
- **22:3** David leaves his parents with the king of Moab. David's great-grandmother Ruth was a Moabite.
- **22:5** "The prophet Gad" is the first mention of Gad. He later serves David (2 Samuel 24:11).
- **22:7** "Fields and vineyards" were given by kings to their men. Saul accuses his Benjaminite officers of plotting.
- **22:9** Doeg tells Saul about what he saw at Nob. He says that Ahimelech asked the LORD for David, which is not in chapter 21.
- **22:17** The guards will not strike "the priests of the LORD", which shows their respect for the priests.
- **22:18** "Eighty-five" is the Hebrew number. The Greek has three hundred and five. "The linen ephod" is the priest's garment.
- **22:19** The destruction of Nob, "men and women, children and infants, oxen, donkeys and sheep", uses the language of the ban of 15:3. Saul does at Nob what he failed to do at Amalek.
- **22:20** Abiathar's escape fulfils the word about Eli's house (2:33). He later becomes David's priest.
- **22:22** "I am responsible" is David's admission that his lie caused the deaths.
- **23:2** David "asked the LORD" through the ephod that Abiathar brings (verse 6).
- **23:7** Saul thinks David has trapped himself in a walled town.
- **23:11** David asks the LORD whether Saul will come and whether the people of Keilah will hand him over. The answer is that they will, though David has just saved them.
- **23:13** "About six hundred" is the size of David's force, up from the four hundred in 22:2.
- **23:16** "Strengthened his hand in God" means Jonathan gave David courage.
- **23:17** Jonathan says David will be king and that Saul knows it. Jonathan accepts second place.
- **23:19** The Ziphites betray David to Saul. See also Psalm 54, whose title refers to this event.
- **23:28** "The Rock of Parting" is Hebrew *sela hammachlekot*. The meaning of the second word is uncertain. It may mean "division" or "smooth places".
- **23:29** En-gedi is an oasis by the Dead Sea, with caves in the cliffs.
- **24:1** In the Hebrew Bible this verse is 24:2, so the English chapter 24 has twenty-two verses and the Hebrew has twenty-three.
- **24:3** "To relieve himself" is literally "to cover his feet".
- **24:4** "The day of which the LORD said to you" is the men's claim. No such word of the LORD is recorded.
- **24:5** "David's conscience troubled him" is literally "his heart struck him".
- **24:6** "The LORD's anointed" is Hebrew *mashiach YHWH*. See entry H-MASHIACH. David will not harm the king whom the LORD has anointed.
- **24:11** "My father" is David's address to Saul, who is his father-in-law.
- **24:13** "Wickedness comes from the wicked" is an old saying that David uses to say he is not wicked.
- **24:14** "A dead dog ... a single flea" is a figure for David's own insignificance.
- **24:17** "More righteous" is Hebrew *tsaddiq*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. Saul admits that David has been the better man.
- **24:20** "You will surely be king" is Saul's statement that he knows the LORD's choice (see 23:17).
- **24:21** Saul asks David to spare his descendants, which David promises. See 2 Samuel 9 and 21:1-9.
- **25:1** The notice of Samuel's death is repeated in 28:3, where it sets the scene for Saul and the medium. The Hebrew reads "Paran" for the wilderness where David went. Some Greek manuscripts read "Maon", which fits verse 2 better. The Hebrew is followed.
- **25:2** Maon and Carmel are towns in the hill country of Judah, south of Hebron, not the mountain in the north. Sheep-shearing was a time of feasting and of money coming in.
- **25:3** "Nabal" means "fool" or "senseless", as Abigail says in verse 25. "Abigail" means "my father is joy". The Hebrew word for "Calebite" (*kalibbi*) can also be read "doglike", and some ancient readers took it so. The WABV text keeps "Calebite".
- **25:6** The Hebrew of David's greeting, "to my brother" (literally "to the living"), is hard to read. The text may mean "to my brother" or "to one who is well". The translation follows the first.
- **25:8** "A feast day" refers to the shearing feast, when generosity was expected.
- **25:10** "Servants who break away from their masters" is Nabal's taunt that David is a runaway. He is partly right.
- **25:13** David takes four hundred men. The two hundred who stay are the baggage guard, as in 30:9-10.
- **25:17** "Worthless" is Hebrew *beliyya'al*, as in 2:12 and 10:27.
- **25:18** Abigail's gift is large: two hundred loaves, two skins of wine, five dressed sheep, grain, raisins and figs.
- **25:22** The Hebrew reads "May God do this to David's enemies". Scribes seem to have changed the original "to David" to avoid a curse on David's own name. The WABV text has "to David", as the oath formula requires, and the Hebrew reading is recorded.
- **25:26** Abigail's words say that the LORD has kept David from bloodguilt. She speaks as a prophet and a peacemaker.
- **25:28** "A lasting house" is a dynasty. She says David "fights the battles of the LORD", the title of 18:17.
- **25:29** "Bound up in the bundle of the living" is a figure for being kept safe, as a valuable thing is tied in a bundle. "The cup of a sling" is the pocket that holds the stone. The enemies' lives are flung away like a stone.
- **25:31** "A weight on the conscience" means that David will not later regret shedding blood for nothing.
- **25:37** "His heart died within him, and he became like a stone" may describe a stroke or a collapse from shock.
- **25:38** "The LORD struck Nabal" is the narrator's comment on his death ten days later.
- **25:39** David's marriage to Abigail brings him a rich Calebite family in the area of Hebron. His thanks to the LORD shows he accepts that Nabal's death spared him from bloodguilt.
- **25:44** Michal is given to Palti (called Paltiel in 2 Samuel 3:15). David is to demand her back in 2 Samuel 3:13-16.
- **26:1** Chapter 26 is a close parallel to chapter 24. Both have the Ziphites, a sparing of Saul, the words "the LORD's anointed", and Saul's confession. In chapter 24 David is in a cave at En-gedi and cuts the robe. Here he takes the spear and jug from a camp. Many scholars see two accounts of one event. The text keeps both.
- **26:6** "Ahimelech the Hittite" is not mentioned elsewhere. "Abishai son of Zeruiah" is David's nephew, and Joab's brother.
- **26:8** Abishai offers to kill Saul with one thrust of the spear, which was the king's own spear.
- **26:9** "The LORD's anointed" is Hebrew *mashiach YHWH*. See entry H-MASHIACH.
- **26:10** David says Saul will die by the LORD's act, in his own time, or in battle. This is fulfilled in chapter 31.
- **26:12** "A deep sleep from the LORD" is Hebrew *tardemah*, the same word as for Adam (Genesis 2:21) and Abram (Genesis 15:12).
- **26:19** "If the LORD has stirred you up against me, may he accept an offering" says that if the cause is the LORD's anger, an offering may turn it away. David says that those who drive him from the land are telling him to serve other gods, since a person outside the land was thought to be outside the LORD's care.
- **26:20** "A partridge" hunted in the mountains is a figure for a hunted fugitive, as in 24:14 ("a flea").
- **26:23** "The LORD repays each person for his righteousness and faithfulness" is David's principle.
- **27:1** David's decision to go to Gath shows how far his position has fallen. He is a fugitive in the land of Goliath.
- **27:2** "Achish son of Maoch" may be the same Achish as in 21:10-15.
- **27:6** Ziklag is a town on the southern edge of Philistine territory. Giving it to David makes him a Philistine vassal. "Belonged to the kings of Judah to this day" is a later comment.
- **27:7** "A year and four months" is literally "days, four months". The Hebrew may mean a year and four months, or four months. The WABV text follows the first.
- **27:8** The Geshurites, Girzites and Amalekites are peoples of the southern desert.
- **27:9** "Left neither man nor woman alive" describes the raids of David's men. This is rendered as written. The text does not use the word *cherem* here, but it records David killing everyone so that no one could report him.
- **27:10** David deceives Achish about where he is raiding, and Achish believes him.
- **27:12** "A stench" is the Hebrew figure for being loathed, as in 13:4.
- **28:1** Achish takes it for granted that David will march with the Philistine army. David's answer, "you will see what your servant can do", does not say whose side he will take.
- **28:3** The notice of Samuel's death repeats 25:1. Saul's expelling of mediums followed the law (Leviticus 19:31; 20:27; Deuteronomy 18:10-11).
- **28:4** Shunem is in the valley of Jezreel. The Philistines have moved far north, past the hill country to the Jezreel valley. Mount Gilboa is on its southern side.
- **28:6** "Dreams, the Urim and prophets" are the three ways of asking the LORD. The LORD does not answer by any of them. The silence is the reason Saul turns to a medium.
- **28:7** "A medium" is Hebrew *ba'alat 'ov*, "mistress of a spirit" or "owner of a pit". The *'ov* is a spirit of the dead or the pit it was called from.
- **28:8** Saul disguises himself and goes by night, which shows he knows it is forbidden.
- **28:10** Saul swears "by the LORD" to protect a medium, which sits oddly beside his own ban on mediums.
- **28:12** The woman cries out when she sees Samuel, and she knows who the visitor is only then. The text does not say what she did to bring Samuel up.
- **28:13** "A divine being" is Hebrew *elohim*, a word that can mean "God", "gods" or "a spirit". It is used here of a figure coming "up out of the earth", the place of the dead (see entry H-SHEOL).
- **28:14** "A robe" is Hebrew *me'il*, the garment Samuel wore all his life (2:19; 15:27).
- **28:15** "Disturbed me" means to trouble his rest. The text speaks plainly: "Samuel said". Readers have long disagreed about what happened. Some have held that Samuel really appeared. Others have held that the woman deceived Saul. The text itself says only that Samuel spoke. Ecclesiasticus 46:20 says that Samuel prophesied after his death.
- **28:16** "Become your enemy" is the Hebrew reading (*'arekha*). The Greek reads "is with your neighbour". The Hebrew is followed.
- **28:17** The judgement repeats what Samuel said in 15:28.
- **28:18** "His burning anger against Amalek" refers to chapter 15.
- **28:19** "You and your sons will be with me" means that they will die and join the dead. It is fulfilled the next day (31:2).
- **28:24** The woman kills her fattened calf for Saul. She shows kindness and hospitality to a man who has just heard his own end.
- **29:1** Aphek is the Philistine camp. The Israelite camp is at the spring at Jezreel.
- **29:3** "Hebrews" is the Philistine name for the Israelites. Achish says David "came over" (Hebrew *naphal*, to fall to the other side).
- **29:5** The song is the one in 18:7 and 21:11.
- **29:9** Achish speaks of David as "an angel of God", which is an Israelite way of speaking, and which shows he has picked up David's own way of speaking.
- **30:1** Ziklag was given to David (27:6). The Amalekite raid is a result of David's own raids on them (27:8).
- **30:2** The Amalekites take captives and kill no one. This differs from David's own raids (27:9).
- **30:6** "Found strength in the LORD his God" is the turning point of the chapter. The men talk of stoning him.
- **30:7** David inquires by the ephod, as in 23:6-12.
- **30:9** "The Wadi Besor" is a stream south of Gaza.
- **30:14** "The Cherethites" are a people of the coast, linked with the Philistines and later with David's bodyguard (2 Samuel 8:18).
- **30:17** "From twilight until the evening of the next day" is the Hebrew reading. The word for "twilight" (*neshef*) can mean dawn or dusk. Four hundred young men escape on camels.
- **30:20** The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. It may mean that the flocks and herds taken from the Amalekites were driven in front of David's own livestock.
- **30:24** "They will share alike" is the rule of equal shares for those who fight and those who guard the baggage. It becomes "a statute and a rule" (verse 25), as also in Numbers 31:27.
- **30:26** David sends gifts to the elders of Judah. This builds his support in Judah before he is king.
- **30:27** The list of towns in verses 27 to 31 is in the south of Judah. "Racal" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek has "Carmel".
- **31:1** Chapter 31 is repeated, with differences, in 1 Chronicles 10. Saul's death is told again in a different way in 2 Samuel 1:1-16.
- **31:2** Jonathan, Abinadab and Malchi-shua are killed. Ishvi (Ish-bosheth) is not named among them (see 14:49 and 2 Samuel 2:8).
- **31:4** Saul asks his armour-bearer to kill him, to avoid being "made sport of" by the uncircumcised. The armour-bearer will not, and Saul takes his own sword. The text records this without comment. In 2 Samuel 1:6-10 a man claims to have killed Saul at his request.
- **31:6** "All his men" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek has "his men". Chronicles says "all his house".
- **31:9** "To carry the news to their idols" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek has "to their temples". In 1 Chronicles 10:9-10 the head is fastened in the temple of Dagon.
- **31:10** "The temple of the Ashtoreths" is the shrine of the goddess in Beth-shan. The body is fastened to the wall, as a trophy.
- **31:12** The men of Jabesh-gilead remember how Saul rescued them (chapter 11). Burning the bodies is not the usual way of burying in Israel and may have been to hide the mutilation. David later has the bones moved (2 Samuel 21:12-14).
- **31:13** "The tamarisk tree at Jabesh" is the place of burial. They fast for seven days, which is the usual period of mourning (Genesis 50:10).
