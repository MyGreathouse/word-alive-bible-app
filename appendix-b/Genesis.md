## Genesis

*Draft 1 of the WABV text. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-BARA\], point to BCLS entries.*

### Chapter 1

**1** In the beginning, God created \[H-BARA\] the heavens and the earth.

**2** The earth was shapeless and empty, with darkness over the face of the deep, and the Spirit \[H-RUACH\] of God moving over the surface of the waters.

**3** God said, "Light, be!" And light came to be.

**4** God saw that the light was good \[H-TOV\], and he set the light apart from the darkness.

**5** God named the light Day and the darkness Night. Evening came, and morning came: one day.

**6** God said, "Let there be a dome in the midst of the waters, dividing water from water."

**7** God made the dome and separated the waters beneath it from the waters above it. So it was.

**8** God named the dome Sky. Evening came, and morning came: a second day.

**9** God said, "Let the waters beneath the sky be gathered into a single place, and let dry ground appear." So it was.

**10** God named the dry ground Earth, and the gathered waters he named Seas. God saw that it was good.

**11** God said, "Let the earth sprout vegetation: seed-bearing plants, and fruit trees that bear fruit with their seed in it, each after its own kind." So it was.

**12** The earth brought forth vegetation: plants bearing seed after their kinds, and trees bearing fruit with seed in it, after their kinds. God saw that it was good.

**13** Evening came, and morning came: a third day.

**14** God said, "Let there be lights in the dome of the sky to separate day from night, and let them mark the festivals, the days and the years.

**15** Let them be lights in the dome of the sky, giving light to the earth." So it was.

**16** God made the two great lights, the greater to rule the day and the lesser to rule the night, and he made the stars as well.

**17** God set them in the dome of the sky to give light to the earth,

**18** to rule over day and night, and to divide light from darkness. God saw that it was good.

**19** Evening came, and morning came: a fourth day.

**20** God said, "Let the waters teem with swarms of living creatures, and let birds fly over the earth, across the dome of the sky."

**21** God created the great sea monsters and every living creature that glides and swarms in the waters, after their kinds, and every winged bird after its kind. God saw that it was good.

**22** God blessed them: "Bear young and multiply. Fill the waters of the seas, and let the birds multiply on the earth."

**23** Evening came, and morning came: a fifth day.

**24** God said, "Let the earth bring forth living creatures after their kinds: cattle, crawling things and wild animals of the earth, each after its kind." So it was.

**25** God made the wild animals of the earth after their kinds, the cattle after their kinds, and all the crawling things of the ground after their kinds. God saw that it was good.

**26** God said, "Let us make humankind \[H-ADAM\] in our image, as our likeness, and let them have charge of the fish of the sea, the birds of the sky, the cattle, the whole earth, and every crawling thing that crawls on the earth."

**27** God created humankind in his own image; in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them.

**28** God blessed them and said to them, "Bear children and multiply. Fill the earth and subdue it. Have charge of the fish of the sea, the birds of the sky, and every living thing that moves on the earth."

**29** God said, "Look, I have given you every seed-bearing plant on the face of all the earth, and every tree whose fruit bears seed. These shall be your food.

**30** To every animal of the earth, every bird of the sky, and every creature that crawls on the earth, everything that has the breath of life in it, I give every green plant as food." So it was.

**31** God looked at everything he had made, and it was very good. Evening came, and morning came: the sixth day.

### Chapter 2, verses 1-3

**1** So the heavens and the earth were completed, with everything that fills them.

**2** On the seventh day God finished the work he had been doing, and on the seventh day he rested from all the work he had done.

**3** God blessed the seventh day and made it holy \[H-QADOSH\], because on that day he rested from all the work that God had created and made.

### Chapter 2, verses 4-25

**4** This is the story of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD \[H-YHWH\] God made earth and heaven.

**5** No wild shrub had yet appeared on the earth, and no field plant had yet sprung up, for the LORD God had not sent rain on the earth, and there was no human being to farm the ground.

**6** Instead, a stream would rise from the earth and water the whole face of the ground.

**7** Then the LORD God shaped the man out of the dust of the ground and breathed the breath of life into his nostrils, and the man became a living being \[H-NEPHESH\].

**8** The LORD God planted a garden in Eden, to the east, and set there the man he had shaped.

**9** Out of the ground the LORD God caused to grow every tree that is lovely to look at and good for food, with the tree of life in the middle of the garden, and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

**10** A river flowed out of Eden to water the garden, and beyond the garden it divided into four headwaters.

**11** The name of the first is the Pishon; it flows around the whole land of Havilah, where there is gold.

**12** The gold of that land is excellent; there is also bdellium and carnelian.

**13** The name of the second river is the Gihon; it flows around the whole land of Cush.

**14** The name of the third river is the Tigris; it runs east of Asshur. The fourth river is the Euphrates.

**15** The LORD God took the man and placed him in the garden of Eden to farm it and take care of it.

**16** The LORD God gave the man this command: "From every tree in the garden you may eat as much as you like,

**17** but from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat; for on the day you eat from it, you will surely die."

**18** The LORD God said, "It is not good for the man to be alone. I will make a companion \[H-EZER\] to stand beside him."

**19** Out of the ground the LORD God had shaped every wild animal and every bird of the sky, and he brought them to the man to see what he would name them. Whatever the man called each living creature, that was its name.

**20** The man gave names to all the cattle, to the birds of the sky and to every wild animal; but for the man himself no companion was found to stand beside him.

**21** So the LORD God cast a deep sleep over the man, and while he slept, God took one of his ribs and closed up the flesh in its place.

**22** From the rib that the LORD God had taken from the man he fashioned a woman, and he brought her to the man.

**23** The man said, "This one, at last, is bone from my bones and flesh from my flesh! She shall be called Woman, because from Man she was taken."

**24** For this reason a man leaves his father and mother and clings to his wife, and the two become one flesh.

**25** The man and his wife were both naked, and they felt no shame before each other.

### Chapter 3

**1** The serpent was the most cunning of all the wild animals the LORD God had made. It said to the woman, "Is it true that God said, 'You must not eat from any tree in the garden'?"

**2** The woman said to the serpent, "We may eat the fruit of the garden's trees.

**3** It is only the fruit of the tree in the middle of the garden about which God said, 'Do not eat from it, and do not touch it, or you will die.'"

**4** The serpent said to the woman, "You will certainly not die!

**5** God knows that on the day you eat from it your eyes will be opened, and you will be like God, knowing good and evil."

**6** The woman saw that the tree was good to eat, delightful to look at, and desirable for making one wise. She took some of its fruit and ate; she also gave some to her husband, who was beside her, and he ate.

**7** Then the eyes of both were opened, and they knew that they were naked. They stitched fig leaves together and made themselves loincloths.

**8** They heard the sound of the LORD God walking in the garden at the breezy time of day, and the man and his wife hid from the LORD God among the trees of the garden.

**9** The LORD God called to the man: "Where are you?"

**10** He said, "I heard the sound of you in the garden, and I was afraid because I was naked, so I hid."

**11** He said, "Who told you that you were naked? Have you eaten from the tree that I told you not to eat from?"

**12** The man said, "The woman you placed beside me, she gave me fruit from the tree, and I ate."

**13** The LORD God said to the woman, "What have you done?" The woman said, "The serpent deceived me, and I ate."

**14** The LORD God said to the serpent: "Because you have done this, you are cursed beyond all cattle and beyond every wild animal. You shall crawl on your belly, and eat dust all the days of your life.

**15** I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will strike at your head, and you will strike at his heel."

**16** To the woman he said: "I will make your labour in childbearing very hard; in pain you will give birth to children. Your desire will be for your husband, yet he will rule over you."

**17** To the man he said: "Because you listened to the voice of your wife and ate from the tree of which I commanded you, 'You shall not eat from it,' the ground is cursed because of you; by hard labour you will eat from it all the days of your life.

**18** It will grow thorns and thistles for you, and you will eat the plants of the field.

**19** By the sweat of your face you will eat bread, until you return to the ground, for from it you were taken. You are dust, and to dust you will return."

**20** The man named his wife Eve, because she was the mother of all who live.

**21** The LORD God made clothing of skins for the man and his wife, and dressed them.

**22** The LORD God said, "Now the man has become like one of us in knowing good and evil. He must not be allowed to reach out his hand and take fruit from the tree of life also, and eat, and live for ever."

**23** So the LORD God sent him out of the garden of Eden to farm the ground from which he was taken.

**24** He drove the man out, and east of the garden of Eden he stationed the cherubim and the whirling flame of a sword, to guard the way to the tree of life.

### Chapter 4

**1** The man lay with his wife Eve; she conceived and gave birth to Cain, and said, "I have gained a man with the help of the LORD."

**2** She went on to bear his brother Abel. Abel became a shepherd, and Cain became a tiller of the soil.

**3** After a time, Cain brought some of the fruit of the soil as an offering to the LORD,

**4** and Abel also brought an offering, some of the firstborn of his flock, the fat portions. The LORD regarded Abel and his offering with favour,

**5** but he did not regard Cain and his offering with favour. Cain was furious, and his face fell.

**6** The LORD said to Cain, "Why are you furious? Why has your face fallen?

**7** If you do well, will you not hold your head high? But if you do not do well, sin \[H-CHATTAT\] lies in wait at the door. Its desire is for you, but you can master it."

**8** Cain said to his brother Abel, "Let us go out into the field." And when they were in the field, Cain turned on his brother Abel and killed him.

**9** The LORD said to Cain, "Where is your brother Abel?" He said, "I don't know. Am I my brother's guardian?"

**10** He said, "What have you done? Listen! Your brother's blood is crying out to me from the ground.

**11** Now you are under a curse, banished from the ground that opened its mouth to receive your brother's blood from your hand.

**12** When you farm the ground, it will no longer yield its strength for you. You will be a fugitive and a wanderer on the earth."

**13** Cain said to the LORD, "My guilt is too great to bear.

**14** You have driven me today from the face of the ground, and I shall be hidden from your face. I shall be a fugitive and a wanderer on the earth, and anyone who meets me will kill me."

**15** The LORD said to him, "Not so! Anyone who kills Cain will suffer vengeance seven times over." And the LORD set a sign on Cain, so that no one who met him would strike him down.

**16** Cain left the LORD's presence and settled in the land of Nod, east of Eden.

**17** Cain lay with his wife; she conceived and gave birth to Enoch. He was building a city, and he named the city after his son Enoch.

**18** To Enoch was born Irad; Irad fathered Mehujael; Mehujael fathered Methushael; Methushael fathered Lamech.

**19** Lamech took two wives: one was named Adah, the other Zillah.

**20** Adah gave birth to Jabal; he was the ancestor of those who live in tents and raise livestock.

**21** His brother's name was Jubal; he was the ancestor of all who play the lyre and the pipe.

**22** Zillah, for her part, gave birth to Tubal-cain, who forged all kinds of tools of bronze and iron. Tubal-cain's sister was Naamah.

**23** Lamech said to his wives: "Adah and Zillah, hear my voice; wives of Lamech, listen to my words: I have killed a man for wounding me, a youth for striking me.

**24** If Cain is avenged seven times over, then Lamech seventy-seven times."

**25** Adam lay with his wife again, and she gave birth to a son and named him Seth, "for God has set another child in place of Abel," she said, "since Cain killed him."

**26** To Seth also a son was born, and he named him Enosh. At that time people began to call on the name of the LORD.

### Chapter 5

**1** This is the written record of the descendants of Adam. When God created humankind, he made them in the likeness of God;

**2** male and female he created them. He blessed them and gave them the name Humankind on the day they were created.

**3** When Adam had lived 130 years, he became the father of a son in his own likeness, after his own image, and he named him Seth.

**4** After Seth's birth Adam lived another 800 years, and had other sons and daughters.

**5** All the days of Adam's life came to 930 years, and then he died.

**6** When Seth had lived 105 years, he became the father of Enosh.

**7** After Enosh's birth Seth lived another 807 years, and had other sons and daughters.

**8** All the days of Seth came to 912 years, and then he died.

**9** When Enosh had lived 90 years, he became the father of Kenan.

**10** After Kenan's birth Enosh lived another 815 years, and had other sons and daughters.

**11** All the days of Enosh came to 905 years, and then he died.

**12** When Kenan had lived 70 years, he became the father of Mahalalel.

**13** After Mahalalel's birth Kenan lived another 840 years, and had other sons and daughters.

**14** All the days of Kenan came to 910 years, and then he died.

**15** When Mahalalel had lived 65 years, he became the father of Jared.

**16** After Jared's birth Mahalalel lived another 830 years, and had other sons and daughters.

**17** All the days of Mahalalel came to 895 years, and then he died.

**18** When Jared had lived 162 years, he became the father of Enoch.

**19** After Enoch's birth Jared lived another 800 years, and had other sons and daughters.

**20** All the days of Jared came to 962 years, and then he died.

**21** When Enoch had lived 65 years, he became the father of Methuselah.

**22** After Methuselah's birth Enoch walked with God for 300 years, and had other sons and daughters.

**23** All the days of Enoch came to 365 years.

**24** Enoch walked with God, and then he was gone, for God took him.

**25** When Methuselah had lived 187 years, he became the father of Lamech.

**26** After Lamech's birth Methuselah lived another 782 years, and had other sons and daughters.

**27** All the days of Methuselah came to 969 years, and then he died.

**28** When Lamech had lived 182 years, he became the father of a son.

**29** He named him Noah, saying, "This one will give us rest from our work, from the hard labour of our hands, on the ground that the LORD has cursed."

**30** After Noah's birth Lamech lived another 595 years, and had other sons and daughters.

**31** All the days of Lamech came to 777 years, and then he died.

**32** When Noah was 500 years old, he became the father of Shem, Ham and Japheth.

### Chapter 6

**1** When people began to grow in number on the face of the earth, and daughters were born to them,

**2** the sons of God saw that the daughters of humankind were beautiful, and they took wives for themselves from any they chose.

**3** The LORD said, "My spirit will not stay in humankind for ever, since they are flesh. Their days will be 120 years."

**4** The Nephilim were on the earth in those days, and afterwards too, when the sons of God went in to the daughters of humankind and they bore children to them. These were the warriors of old, the famous men.

**5** The LORD saw that human wickedness on the earth was great, and that every thought formed in the human heart was nothing but evil all the time.

**6** The LORD regretted \[H-NACHAM\] that he had made humankind on the earth, and his heart was deeply pained.

**7** The LORD said, "I will wipe humankind, whom I created, from the face of the ground, and with them the animals, the crawling things and the birds of the sky, for I regret that I made them."

**8** But Noah found favour with the LORD.

**9** This is the story of Noah. Noah was a righteous man \[H-TSADDIQ\], whole-hearted among his contemporaries. Noah walked with God.

**10** Noah became the father of three sons: Shem, Ham and Japheth.

**11** The earth was ruined in God's sight, and filled with violence \[H-CHAMAS\].

**12** God looked at the earth, and it was ruined, for all flesh on the earth had ruined its way.

**13** God said to Noah, "I have decided to put an end to all flesh, for the earth is full of violence because of them. I am about to destroy them along with the earth.

**14** Make yourself an ark of gopher wood. Make compartments in the ark, and seal it inside and out with pitch.

**15** This is how you are to build it: the length of the ark, 300 cubits; its width, 50 cubits; its height, 30 cubits.

**16** Make a roof for the ark, and finish it to within a cubit of the top. Set the door of the ark in its side. Build it with a lower, a second and a third deck.

**17** I am about to bring a flood of waters over the earth, to destroy every living thing under heaven that has the breath of life in it. Everything on the earth will perish.

**18** But with you I will establish my covenant \[H-BERIT\]. You will enter the ark, you and your sons, your wife and your sons' wives with you.

**19** From every living thing, from all flesh, you are to bring two of each into the ark to keep them alive with you; they are to be male and female.

**20** From the birds after their kinds, the animals after their kinds, and every crawling thing of the ground after its kind, two of each will come to you, to be kept alive.

**21** As for you, take every kind of food that is eaten and gather it, and it will serve as food for you and for them."

**22** Noah did this. He did all that God commanded him.

### Chapter 7

**1** The LORD said to Noah, "Go into the ark, you and all your household, for in this generation I have seen that you are righteous before me.

**2** Take with you seven pairs of every clean animal, male and female, and one pair of every animal that is not clean, male and female;

**3** also seven pairs of every kind of bird of the sky, male and female, to keep their offspring alive over all the earth.

**4** In seven days' time I will make it rain on the earth for forty days and forty nights, and I will wipe from the face of the ground every living thing I have made."

**5** Noah did all that the LORD had commanded him.

**6** Noah was 600 years old when the flood came, with its waters over the earth.

**7** Noah went into the ark, together with his sons, his wife and his sons' wives, to escape the waters of the flood.

**8** Clean animals and animals that are not clean, birds and everything that crawls on the ground,

**9** came in pairs to Noah into the ark, male and female, as God had commanded Noah.

**10** After the seven days, the waters of the flood came over the earth.

**11** In the six hundredth year of Noah's life, in the second month, on the seventeenth day of the month, on that very day all the fountains of the great deep burst open and the windows of the heavens were opened.

**12** The rain fell on the earth for forty days and forty nights.

**13** On that very day Noah went into the ark, with Noah's sons Shem, Ham and Japheth, and Noah's wife and his sons' three wives.

**14** They had with them every wild animal after its kind, all the cattle after their kinds, every crawling thing that crawls on the earth after its kind, and every bird after its kind, every winged creature.

**15** They came to Noah into the ark in pairs, of all flesh that has the breath of life in it.

**16** Those that came were male and female of all flesh, as God had commanded him. Then the LORD shut him in.

**17** The flood came upon the earth for forty days. The waters rose and lifted the ark, so that it rose high above the earth.

**18** The waters surged and grew greatly on the earth, and the ark floated on the face of the waters.

**19** The waters surged ever higher on the earth, and all the high mountains under all the heavens were covered.

**20** The waters rose fifteen cubits higher, and the mountains were covered.

**21** All flesh that moved on the earth perished: birds, cattle, wild animals, every swarming creature that swarms on the earth, and all humankind.

**22** Everything on dry land that had the breath of the spirit of life in its nostrils died.

**23** He wiped out every living thing on the face of the ground: human beings and animals, crawling things and birds of the sky. They were wiped from the earth, and only Noah was left, and those who were with him in the ark.

**24** The waters surged over the earth for 150 days.

### Chapter 8

**1** God remembered Noah, and all the wild animals and all the cattle that were with him in the ark. God sent a wind \[H-RUACH\] over the earth, and the waters began to go down.

**2** The fountains of the deep and the windows of the heavens were closed, and the rain from the sky was held back.

**3** The waters flowed back from the earth, receding steadily. At the end of 150 days the waters had decreased,

**4** and in the seventh month, on the seventeenth day of the month, the ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat.

**5** The waters kept on decreasing until the tenth month. In the tenth month, on the first day of the month, the tops of the mountains became visible.

**6** After forty days Noah opened the window of the ark that he had made,

**7** and sent out a raven; it went back and forth until the waters had dried up from the earth.

**8** Then he sent out a dove, to see whether the waters had gone down from the surface of the ground.

**9** But the dove found nowhere to set its foot, and it returned to him in the ark, for there was water over the whole face of the earth. He reached out his hand, took it and brought it in to him in the ark.

**10** He waited another seven days, and again he sent out the dove from the ark.

**11** The dove came back to him at evening, and there, in its beak, was a fresh olive leaf! So Noah knew that the waters had gone down from the earth.

**12** He waited another seven days and sent out the dove, but it did not return to him again.

**13** In the six hundred and first year, in the first month, on the first day of the month, the waters dried up from the earth. Noah removed the covering of the ark and looked, and the surface of the ground was dry.

**14** In the second month, on the twenty-seventh day of the month, the earth was dry.

**15** Then God spoke to Noah:

**16** "Go out of the ark, you and your wife, your sons and your sons' wives with you.

**17** Bring out with you every living thing that is with you, of all flesh, birds, animals and every crawling thing that crawls on the earth, so that they may swarm over the earth, and be fruitful and multiply on it."

**18** So Noah went out, with his sons, his wife and his sons' wives.

**19** Every animal, every crawling thing, every bird, everything that moves on the earth, went out of the ark by their families.

**20** Noah built an altar to the LORD, and took some of every clean animal and every clean bird and offered burnt offerings on the altar.

**21** The LORD smelled the soothing aroma, and the LORD said in his heart, "I will never again curse the ground because of humankind, for the plans of the human heart are evil from youth; and I will never again strike down every living creature as I have done.

**22** As long as the earth lasts, planting and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night will never cease."

### Chapter 9

**1** God blessed Noah and his sons, saying to them, "Bear children and multiply, and fill the earth.

**2** The fear and terror of you will rest on every animal of the earth, on every bird of the sky, on everything that crawls on the ground, and on all the fish of the sea. They are placed in your hands.

**3** Every creature that moves and lives will be food for you. As I gave you the green plants, I now give you all of them.

**4** Only, you must not eat flesh with its life in it, that is, its blood.

**5** For your own lifeblood, too, I will demand an account: from every animal I will demand it, and from every human being, from each one's fellow, I will demand an account for the life of a human being.

**6** Whoever sheds the blood of a human being, by a human being shall that person's blood be shed; for God made humankind in his own image.

**7** As for you, bear children and multiply; teem over the earth and multiply in it."

**8** God said to Noah and to his sons with him,

**9** "See, I am establishing my covenant \[H-BERIT\] with you and with your descendants after you,

**10** and with every living creature that is with you: the birds, the cattle and all the wild animals with you, all that came out of the ark, every animal of the earth.

**11** I establish my covenant with you: never again will all flesh be cut off by the waters of a flood, and never again will a flood destroy the earth."

**12** God said, "This is the sign \[H-OT\] of the covenant that I am making between me and you and every living creature that is with you, for all future generations:

**13** I have set my bow in the clouds, and it will be the sign of the covenant between me and the earth.

**14** When I bring clouds over the earth and the bow appears in the clouds,

**15** I will remember my covenant between me and you and every living creature of all flesh: the waters will never again become a flood to destroy all flesh.

**16** When the bow is in the clouds, I will see it and remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature of all flesh on the earth."

**17** God said to Noah, "This is the sign of the covenant that I have established between me and all flesh on the earth."

**18** The sons of Noah who went out of the ark were Shem, Ham and Japheth. (Ham was the father of Canaan.)

**19** These three were Noah's sons, and from them the whole earth was spread with people.

**20** Noah, a farmer, was the first to plant a vineyard.

**21** He drank some of the wine, became drunk, and lay uncovered inside his tent.

**22** Ham, the father of Canaan, saw his father's nakedness and told his two brothers outside.

**23** Shem and Japheth took a cloak, laid it across both their shoulders, and walked backwards to cover their father's nakedness. Their faces were turned away, so that they did not see their father naked.

**24** When Noah woke from his wine and learned what his youngest son had done to him,

**25** he said, "Cursed be Canaan! He will be the lowest slave to his brothers."

**26** He said also, "Blessed be the LORD, the God of Shem, and let Canaan be his slave.

**27** May God make room for Japheth, and may he live in the tents of Shem; and let Canaan be his slave."

**28** After the flood Noah lived 350 years.

**29** All the days of Noah came to 950 years, and then he died.

### Chapter 10

**1** This is the record of the descendants of Noah's sons, Shem, Ham and Japheth. Sons were born to them after the flood.

**2** The sons of Japheth were Gomer, Magog, Madai, Javan, Tubal, Meshech and Tiras.

**3** The sons of Gomer were Ashkenaz, Riphath and Togarmah.

**4** The sons of Javan were Elishah, Tarshish, Kittim and Dodanim.

**5** From these the peoples of the coasts spread out in their lands, each with its own language, by clan, within their nations.

**6** The sons of Ham were Cush, Egypt, Put and Canaan.

**7** The sons of Cush were Seba, Havilah, Sabtah, Raamah and Sabteca. The sons of Raamah were Sheba and Dedan.

**8** Cush was the father of Nimrod, who was the first to be a mighty man on the earth.

**9** He was a mighty hunter before the LORD; hence the saying, "Like Nimrod, a mighty hunter before the LORD."

**10** The first centres of his kingdom were Babylon, Uruk, Akkad and Kalneh, in the land of Shinar.

**29** Joseph had his chariot made ready and went up to meet his father Israel in Goshen. As soon as he appeared before him, he threw his arms around his neck and wept on his neck for a long time.

**30** Israel said to Joseph, "Now I am ready to die, since I have seen your face and know that you are still alive."

**31** Joseph said to his brothers and to his father's household, "I will go up and tell Pharaoh, 'My brothers and my father's household, who were in the land of Canaan, have come to me.

**32** The men are shepherds, for they have always kept livestock, and they have brought their flocks and herds and all that they own.'

**33** When Pharaoh summons you and asks, 'What is your occupation?'

**34** you are to say, 'Your servants have kept livestock from our youth until now, both we and our fathers.' Then you will be allowed to settle in the land of Goshen, because all shepherds are detestable to the Egyptians."

### Chapter 47

**1** So Joseph went in and told Pharaoh, "My father and my brothers, with their flocks and herds and all that they own, have come from the land of Canaan. They are now in the land of Goshen."

**21** and as for the people, he made slaves of them from one end of Egypt to the other.

**27** Israel settled in the land of Egypt, in the region of Goshen. They gained possessions in it, and were fruitful and multiplied greatly.

**28** Jacob lived in the land of Egypt seventeen years, so the length of Jacob's life was 147 years.

**29** When the time drew near for Israel to die, he called for his son Joseph and said to him, "If I have found favour with you, put your hand under my thigh and deal with me in faithful love \[H-HESED\] and truth. Do not bury me in Egypt,

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by book, chapter and verse.*

### Genesis

- **1:1** Or "When God began to create the heavens and the earth." The Hebrew opening can be read either way.
- **1:2** Hebrew *tohu wabohu*, a pair of words that sound alike and describe an unformed, unfilled state.
- **1:2** Or "wind of God" or "breath of God." The Hebrew word *ruach* covers all three.
- **1:26** The plural "us" is debated: it may reflect the divine council, a plural of majesty, or God addressing his heavenly court. WABV keeps the plural without choosing.
- **2:4** "LORD" in small capitals represents the divine name YHWH. "LORD God" joins that name to the Hebrew word *Elohim*.
- **2:7** Hebrew *nephesh*, a word covering "being", "life" and "person". It is often rendered "soul" or "living creature". The Hebrew also plays on *adam* (man) and *adamah* (ground).
- **2:18** Hebrew *ezer kenegdo*, literally "a help corresponding to him". The word *ezer* is also used of God as helper, so it does not imply inferiority.
- **2:21** Hebrew *tsela* can mean "rib" or "side".
- **3:15** "Offspring" translates Hebrew *zera*, literally "seed", a singular word that can mean one descendant or many. Christian tradition has often read this verse as pointing to the Messiah.
- **3:16** "Your desire will be for your husband" is debated. The Hebrew may mean longing, or a pull towards, or a struggle against.
- **3:20** The name Eve (Hebrew *Chavvah*) sounds like the Hebrew for "living".
- **4:1** The Hebrew of the second sentence is uncertain. It can be read "I have acquired a man with the LORD" or "I have brought forth a man, the LORD's gift". The name Cain sounds like the Hebrew *qanah*, "acquire".
- **4:7** The Hebrew is difficult. "Will you not be lifted up" can also be read "will you not be accepted" or "will your face not lift".
- **4:25** The name Seth sounds like the Hebrew for "granted" or "appointed".
- **4:26** Or "people began to call themselves by the name of the LORD".
- **5:24** Literally "he was not, for God took him". The Hebrew verb "took" is used elsewhere of being taken up by God.
- **5:29** The name Noah (Hebrew *Noach*) sounds like the Hebrew for "relief" or "comfort".
- **6:2** "Sons of God" is debated. It has been read as heavenly beings, as descendants of Seth, or as rulers and kings.
- **6:3** The meaning of "remain in" is uncertain; it can also be read "contend with" or "judge". The 120 years may be a limit on human life or a period of warning before the flood.
- **6:4** The meaning of *Nephilim* is uncertain. It is often taken as "fallen ones" or "giants".
- **6:6** Hebrew *nacham* means to feel sorrow, to change course, or to be moved to compassion. It does not imply that God made a mistake.
- **6:9** "Blameless" translates Hebrew *tamim*, "whole" or "complete". It means integrity, not sinlessness.
- **6:14** The kind of wood called "gopher" is unknown and the word occurs only here. "Pitch" translates a Hebrew word related to *kopher*, the word for atonement.
- **6:15** A cubit is about 45 centimetres (18 inches).
- **7:11** "The great deep" (Hebrew *tehom*) is the same word used in Genesis 1:2.
- **8:1** The Hebrew word is *ruach*, which can mean wind, breath or spirit.
- **8:4** Ararat refers to a mountain region, the ancient land of Urartu, not to one named peak.
- **8:21** "Inclination" translates Hebrew *yetser*, the shaping of the heart's thoughts and desires.
- **9:4** Blood stands for life in Hebrew thought, which is why the flesh is not to be eaten with its blood. See also Leviticus 17:11.
- **9:6** "By a human" can also be read "for a human" or "in exchange for a human". The Hebrew phrase is compact.
- **9:13** Hebrew *qeshet* is the ordinary word for a war bow. A bow hung in the clouds is often read as a weapon laid down.
- **9:22** "Saw his father's nakedness" may mean more than a glance. The Hebrew expression is debated, and some read it as a serious offence against Noah.
- **9:25** The curse falls on Canaan, Ham's son, not on Ham. Why it does is debated. "The lowest of slaves" is literally "a slave of slaves".
- **10:2** The chapter names seventy nations in all (fourteen from Japheth, thirty from Ham and twenty-six from Shem). Many names are uncertain and some are place names.
- **10:8** Hebrew *gibbor* means a mighty man, a warrior or a hero.
- **10:10** The Hebrew *Babel* is the name of the city of Babylon.
- **10:25** The name Peleg means "division". What was divided is not explained: it may refer to the dispersion of nations or to a division of land.
- **11:4** "Make a name" is a play on the Hebrew *shem*, "name", which is also the name of Noah's son Shem.
- **11:9** *Babel* sounds like the Hebrew *balal*, "to confuse". In the Akkadian language the city name meant "gate of God".
- **11:10-26** The ages in this list differ in the Septuagint and the Samaritan Pentateuch. WABV follows the Masoretic Text.
- **11:28** The location of Ur of the Chaldeans is debated. Many identify it with the southern Mesopotamian city of Ur.
- **11:31** Haran was a city in northern Mesopotamia, on a trade route.
- **12:1** The Hebrew command *lech lecha* can be read "go for yourself" or "go on your way".
- **12:3** "Will be blessed" can also be read "will bless themselves", meaning they will use Abram's name in their blessings. The New Testament quotes the passage in the first sense.
- **12:13** Sarai was Abram's half sister (Genesis 20:12), so the statement was partly true.
- **13:10** "The garden of the LORD" refers to Eden.
- **14:1** The kings and places in this chapter are not named in other surviving ancient records, and some names are uncertain.
- **14:13** "Hebrew" (Hebrew *ivri*) is used here of Abram. Its origin is debated.
- **14:18** Melchizedek means "king of righteousness". Salem is often identified with Jerusalem (Psalm 76:2).
- **14:19** "God Most High" is Hebrew *El Elyon*.
- **15:6** "Believed" translates Hebrew *he'emin*, from the root *aman*, "to be firm, to trust". "Counted" is a word from the language of accounting.
- **15:9-10** The cutting of animals in two was part of making a covenant. The phrase "cut a covenant" is the usual Hebrew idiom. See Jeremiah 34:18-19.
- **15:13** "Four hundred years" is a round number. Exodus 12:40 gives 430.
- **15:18** "The river of Egypt" is either the Wadi el-Arish or a branch of the Nile. This is debated.
- **16:2** Giving a servant to a husband to bear children for a childless wife was a recognised custom in the ancient Near East.
- **16:11** Ishmael means "God hears".
- **16:13** The Hebrew name is *El Roi*, "God of seeing" or "God who sees me". The second half of the verse is difficult.
- **17:1** "God Almighty" is Hebrew *El Shaddai*. The meaning of *Shaddai* is uncertain.
- **17:5** Abraham means "father of many". The name Abram means "exalted father".
- **17:17** Isaac (Hebrew *Yitschak*) means "he laughs".
- **17:20** The twelve princes of Ishmael are listed in Genesis 25:12-16.
- **18:1-2** The LORD appears as three men. The text moves between "the LORD", "the men" and "he", and readers have understood this in different ways.
- **18:10** "When the season comes round again" is literally "at the time of life", a phrase of uncertain meaning, usually taken as about a year later.
- **18:12** Sarah's laughter gives Isaac his name (see 21:6).
- **18:19** The words "righteousness and justice" (Hebrew *tsedaqah* and *mishpat*) often appear together in the Hebrew Bible for right and fair conduct.
- **18:25** "Act justly" is Hebrew *mishpat*, the same word used for justice.
- **19:5** The Hebrew verb "know" can mean to have sexual relations, and the sense here is demanding sexual violence against the visitors.
- **19:8** Lot's offer reflects a strong duty of hospitality to guests. The text does not approve it.
- **19:22** Zoar sounds like the Hebrew for "small".
- **19:26** "Pillar of salt" is Hebrew *netsiv melach*. The Dead Sea region has many salt formations.
- **19:37** Moab sounds like the Hebrew for "from father". Ben-ammi means "son of my people".
- **20:3** Abimelech is a title or name of Philistine or Canaanite kings of Gerar.
- **20:16** The Hebrew of this verse is difficult, and the "vindication" may be a payment or a covering of Sarah's honour.
- **21:6** Isaac means "he laughs". Sarah's laughter turns from doubt to joy.
- **21:9** The Hebrew word for "mocking" or "playing" is the same root as Isaac's name. It can mean play, laughter or mockery.
- **21:31** Beersheba can mean "well of the oath" or "well of seven".
- **21:33** "Everlasting God" is Hebrew *El Olam*.
- **22:1** "Tested" translates Hebrew *nasah*, to try or prove. It does not mean that God was unsure of the outcome.
- **22:2** "Moriah" is named again in 2 Chronicles 3:1 as the site of the temple. The location of the mountain here is uncertain.
- **22:12** "Fear" (Hebrew *yare*) means reverence and trust as well as dread.
- **22:14** "The LORD will provide" is Hebrew *YHWH yireh*, literally "the LORD will see".
- **23:2** Kiriath-arba means "city of Arba" and is the older name for Hebron.
- **23:4** "Stranger and settler" describes a resident foreigner with no land rights of his own, which is why Abraham must buy a burial place.
- **23:6** "Mighty prince" is literally "a prince of God", a way of saying "a great prince".
- **23:16** A shekel was a unit of weight, about 11 grams, used to weigh silver. Four hundred shekels was a large sum.
- **24:2** "Place your hand under my thigh" was a form of oath-taking that touched the place of descendants. The phrase is also used in Genesis 47:29.
- **24:12** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED.
- **24:22** The gold ring is described as being worn on the nose in verse 47; the Hebrew word can mean a nose-ring.
- **24:48** The Hebrew describes Rebekah as the daughter of Abraham's brother's family, meaning a close relative of his clan.
- **24:60** The blessing echoes the promise made to Abraham in Genesis 22:17.
- **24:63** The meaning of the Hebrew verb is uncertain. It has been read as "to meditate", "to walk" or "to relieve himself".
- **25:6** "Sons of his concubines" refers to the sons of Hagar and Keturah. Keturah is called a wife in verse 1 and a concubine in 1 Chronicles 1:32.
- **25:8** "Gathered to his people" is a phrase for death that speaks of joining the ancestors.
- **25:16** "Chieftains" is Hebrew *nasi*, a tribal leader or prince.
- **25:18** The Hebrew is difficult and could also mean they "fell" or "settled" against their kin.
- **25:23** The oracle is in poetry. "Two nations" refers to Edom (from Esau) and Israel (from Jacob).
- **25:25** Esau sounds like the Hebrew for "hairy". Edom (verse 30) means "red".
- **25:26** Jacob (Hebrew *Ya'aqov*) sounds like the Hebrew for "heel" and also carries the sense of "he supplants" or "he cheats" (see 27:36).
- **25:31** The birthright (Hebrew *bekhorah*) gave the firstborn a double share of the inheritance and a place of leadership in the family.
- **25:34** "Showed how little he valued" is Hebrew *bazah*, "to despise".
- **26:1** The earlier famine is the one in Genesis 12:10.
- **26:8** The verb "caressing" is *metsachek*, from the same root as Isaac's name, "laughing" or "playing".
- **26:20** Esek means "contention". Sitnah (verse 21) means "opposition" or "accusation". Rehoboth (verse 22) means "room".
- **26:33** Shibah sounds like both "seven" and "oath". See also Genesis 21:31.
- **26:34-35** Esau's marriages to Hittite women are given as the cause of the family trouble that leads to Jacob's journey.
- **27:19** Jacob's statement "I am Esau" is a direct lie. The text does not praise the deceit, and the consequences follow in later chapters.
- **27:27** Isaac's blessing starts with the smell of the field, a figure of fruitfulness.
- **27:36** The play on words connects Jacob (*Ya'aqov*) with the verb *'aqav*, "to supplant" or "to trick".
- **27:39-40** The Hebrew of "far from the richness of the earth" can also be read "of the richness of the earth will be your home". The blessing may be read either way.
- **28:3** "God Almighty" is Hebrew *El Shaddai*. See Genesis 17:1.
- **28:12** The Hebrew *sullam* is a rare word. "Stairway", "ramp" and "ladder" are all possible renderings.
- **28:19** Bethel means "house of God". Luz was the earlier name of the city.
- **28:22** The tenth, or tithe, is offered as a vow, before the Law of Moses.
- **29:17** The Hebrew word describing Leah's eyes means "soft", "tender" or "weak". It may be praise or a fault, and scholars are divided.
- **29:32-35** The names of Jacob's sons are wordplays. Reuben sounds like "see, a son", Simeon like "hear", Levi like "attached", and Judah like "praise".
- **30:3** "Give birth on my knees" means the child would be counted as Rachel's own.
- **30:6-24** The names Dan ("he judged"), Naphtali ("my struggle"), Gad ("fortune"), Asher ("happy"), Issachar ("wages"), Zebulun ("gift" or "honour") and Joseph ("may he add") are all explained by wordplay.
- **30:14** Mandrakes were believed to help conception.
- **30:27** "Divination" is a method of seeking omens. Laban's wording shows his own religious outlook.
- **30:37-39** The practice of peeling branches to mark the animals reflects an ancient belief. The text credits God, not the method, with the result (31:9-12).
- **31:19** Household gods (Hebrew *teraphim*) were small images. They may have been linked to family inheritance.
- **31:42** "The Fear of Isaac" is an unusual title for God, and may mean "the one whom Isaac feared" or "the Kinsman of Isaac".
- **31:47** Jegar-sahadutha is Aramaic and Galeed is Hebrew. Both mean "heap of witness".
- **31:49** Mizpah means "watchtower".
- **32:2** Mahanaim means "two camps".
- **32:10** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED.
- **32:22** The Jabbok is a river that flows into the Jordan from the east.
- **32:24** The "man" is later identified as God (verse 30). The text does not say more about who he is.
- **32:28** Israel (Hebrew *Yisra'el*) is explained as "he struggles with God". Its exact meaning is debated. It may also mean "God strives" or "God rules".
- **32:30** Peniel (verse 31 has Penuel) means "face of God".
- **32:32** The dietary custom is not commanded in the Law. It is recorded here as a practice.
- **33:10** "To see your face is like seeing the face of God" is a polite form of thanks, but it also recalls Jacob's experience at Peniel.
- **33:17** Succoth means "shelters".
- **33:20** El-elohe-israel means "God, the God of Israel" or "El is the God of Israel".
- **34:2** The Hebrew verbs describe violence against Dinah. The text records the facts without softening them.
- **34:7** "An outrage in Israel" is a phrase later used of grave sin within the community.
- **34:14-24** Circumcision is turned into a trap. The actions of Simeon and Levi are condemned in Genesis 49:5-7.
- **35:2** The foreign gods are given up before the journey to Bethel.
- **35:8** Allon-bacuth means "oak of weeping".
- **35:18** Ben-oni means "son of my sorrow". Benjamin means "son of the right hand" or "son of the south".
- **35:22** Reuben's act is an offence against his father's authority and costs him his place as firstborn (Genesis 49:3-4).
- **36:1** Edom means "red" and is another name for Esau (see 25:30).
- **36:24** The Hebrew word translated "hot springs" is uncertain. It has also been read "mules".
- **36:31** The reference to "any king reigned over the Israelites" may come from a later time than Moses.
- **37:3** The Hebrew *ketonet passim* is uncertain. It has been read as a "long robe with sleeves", a "coat of many colours" or an "ornamented tunic".
- **37:25** The Ishmaelites and Midianites (verse 28) may be names for the same group of traders.
- **37:28** Twenty pieces of silver was the usual price of a young slave. The text does not make clear who pulled Joseph from the pit.
- **38:1** The chapter interrupts the Joseph story to follow the line of Judah, from whom David and the kings of Judah descend.
- **38:8** The duty of a brother to raise up offspring for a dead brother is later set out in Deuteronomy 25:5-10.
- **38:14** Tamar uses a veil as a disguise. The text does not say she was a prostitute, only that Judah took her for one.
- **38:21** The Hebrew word for "shrine prostitute" (*qedeshah*) is a different word from the one in verse 15. It may describe a woman linked to a shrine.
- **38:26** Judah's words, "she is more in the right than I am", are an admission of fault.
- **38:29** Perez means "breaking out". Zerah (verse 30) means "dawn" or "scarlet".
- **39:9** Joseph's reason for refusing is loyalty to his master and to God.
- **39:14** "Hebrew" here is used by an Egyptian as a term of contempt for a foreigner.
- **39:21** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED.
- **40:8** Joseph's remark means that interpretation is God's gift and not a skill of his own.
- **40:19** "Lift up your head" is a pun. For the cupbearer it means restoration, and for the baker it means beheading, followed by hanging on a tree or pole.
- **41:8** "Magicians" is Hebrew *chartummim*, a word borrowed from Egyptian that points to priests or scribes skilled in sacred writing.
- **41:38** "Spirit of God" is Hebrew *ruach elohim*. The phrase could also be read "a divine spirit".
- **41:42** The signet ring, linen robes and gold chain are the marks of high Egyptian office.
- **41:45** Zaphenath-paneah is an Egyptian name of uncertain meaning, perhaps "God speaks and lives". On (Heliopolis) was the centre of the sun-god Re.
- **41:51-52** Manasseh sounds like "make forget". Ephraim sounds like "fruitful".
- **42:6** "The one in authority" is Hebrew *shallit*, a ruler or governor. It is a term for a person with real power.
- **42:9** "Where our land is weak" is literally "the nakedness of the land", meaning its unprotected places.
- **42:18** "Fear" (Hebrew *yare*) here means reverence for God and shows Joseph will act with justice.
- **42:23** The interpreter shows that Joseph was speaking Egyptian and that his brothers did not understand him.
- **42:38** Sheol is the Hebrew word for the place of the dead.
- **43:11** Balm, gum and resin were spices and aromatic gums. Pistachios and almonds were luxury foods.
- **43:14** "God Almighty" is Hebrew *El Shaddai*. See Genesis 17:1.
- **43:23** The steward's reply puts the matter in God's hands. "Treasure" suggests the silver was a gift.
- **43:32** Egyptians would not eat with foreigners such as Hebrews, probably for reasons of ritual purity.
- **43:34** The larger portion for Benjamin tests whether the brothers are jealous.
- **44:5** The Hebrew verb is *nachash*, to practise divination or read omens. Joseph's words fit his role as an Egyptian lord.
- **44:16** Judah's "God has found out the guilt" refers to an older wrong, probably the sale of Joseph.
- **44:18** "Like Pharaoh himself" is a way of showing that Joseph holds royal power.
- **44:32** "Surety" means one who takes on the responsibility for another. See 43:9.
- **45:8** "Father to Pharaoh" is a title of honour for a chief adviser.
- **45:10** Goshen was in the eastern part of the Nile delta, good land for grazing.
- **45:26** "His heart went numb" is literally "his heart grew cold".
- **46:2** God speaks to Jacob by both names, "Jacob, Jacob", as at the start of a call to a major task.
- **46:15** The totals of thirty-three, sixteen, fourteen and seven do not all add up to the numbers given in verses 26 and 27, because some people are counted differently. The Septuagint gives seventy-five.
- **46:27** The number seventy (see also Exodus 1:5) is a round total. It includes Jacob and Joseph's family.
- **46:34** Shepherds were looked down on by Egyptians, and this kept the Israelites apart in Goshen.
- **47:9** "Wandering" (Hebrew *megurim*) is the word for a stranger's sojourn, the same word used of his fathers.
- **47:11** Rameses is likely the region later called the land of Rameses, in the eastern Nile delta.
- **47:21** The Hebrew can also be read as Joseph moving the people into cities. The Septuagint reads this way.
- **47:29** "Put your hand under my thigh" is the same oath form as in Genesis 24:2.
- **47:31** "Head of the bed" can also be read "top of his staff", depending on how the Hebrew word is pointed. Hebrews 11:21 follows the second reading.
- **48:5** Adopting Ephraim and Manasseh gives Joseph a double share among the tribes.
- **48:16** "The angel who has redeemed me" is Hebrew *ha-mal'akh ha-go'el*. The word *go'el* means a redeemer or rescuer.
- **48:22** The Hebrew word *shekhem* means "shoulder" or "ridge", and may be a wordplay on the town of Shechem.
- **49:4** "Unstable" is a rare Hebrew word that can mean boiling, bubbling or reckless.
- **49:10** The Hebrew *shiloh* is debated. It has been read as "until he comes to Shiloh", "until tribute comes to him", or "until he comes to whom it belongs". The line has been read as pointing to a future king.
- **49:18** The cry to the LORD interrupts the blessings and is the only prayer in the poem.
- **49:24** "Mighty One of Jacob" (Hebrew *avir ya'aqov*) is an ancient title for God.
- **49:26** Hebrew *nazir* can mean "set apart", "consecrated" or "prince".
- **50:2** Embalming was an Egyptian practice and is not otherwise mentioned in Genesis.
- **50:11** Abel-mizraim means "mourning of Egypt" or "meadow of Egypt".
- **50:19** "Am I in the place of God?" is the same question Jacob asked Rachel in Genesis 30:2.
- **50:20** "God meant it for good" is the heart of the Joseph story.
- **50:26** The coffin in Egypt prepares for the Exodus, when Joseph's bones are carried out (Exodus 13:19).
