## Exodus 1-18: From Egypt to the Wilderness

### Chapter 1

**1** These are the names of the sons of Israel who came to Egypt with Jacob, each with his household:

**2** Reuben, Simeon, Levi and Judah,

**3** Issachar, Zebulun and Benjamin,

**4** Dan and Naphtali, Gad and Asher.

**5** All the descendants of Jacob numbered seventy persons. Joseph was already in Egypt.

**6** In time Joseph died, and all his brothers, and all that generation.

**7** But the Israelites were fruitful and teemed. They multiplied and grew exceedingly strong, until the land was filled with them.

**8** Then a new king arose over Egypt, one who did not know Joseph.

**9** He said to his people, "Look, the Israelite people are more numerous and stronger than we are.

**10** Come, let us deal shrewdly with them. Otherwise they will multiply further, and if war breaks out they may join our enemies and fight against us and leave the country."

**11** So they set slave-masters over them to oppress them with heavy labour, and they built supply cities for Pharaoh, Pithom and Rameses.

**12** But the more they were oppressed, the more they multiplied and spread, until the Egyptians came to dread the Israelites.

**13** The Egyptians made the Israelites work without mercy

**14** and made their lives bitter with hard labour at mortar and brick and with every kind of work in the fields. In all the work they forced on them, they were merciless.

**15** The king of Egypt spoke to the Hebrew midwives, one named Shiphrah and the other Puah:

**16** "When you help the Hebrew women give birth, look at the birthstool. If it is a boy, kill him. If it is a girl, let her live."

**17** But the midwives feared \[H-YARE\] God, and did not do what the king of Egypt had told them. They let the boys live.

**18** The king of Egypt summoned the midwives and said to them, "Why have you done this? Why have you let the boys live?"

**19** The midwives answered Pharaoh, "Hebrew women are not like Egyptian women. They are strong and give birth before the midwife arrives."

**20** God dealt well with the midwives, and the people multiplied and became very strong.

**21** And because the midwives feared God, he gave them families of their own.

**22** Then Pharaoh gave this order to all his people: "Every boy that is born to the Hebrews you are to throw into the Nile, but let every girl live."

### Chapter 2

**1** A man from the house of Levi went and married a Levite woman.

**2** The woman conceived and bore a son. When she saw that he was a fine child, she hid him for three months.

**3** When she could hide him no longer, she took a basket of papyrus, coated it with bitumen and pitch, put the child in it, and set it among the reeds at the edge of the Nile.

**4** His sister stood some distance away to see what would happen to him.

**5** Pharaoh's daughter came down to bathe at the Nile, while her maids walked along the bank. She saw the basket among the reeds and sent her slave woman to fetch it.

**6** She opened it and saw the child. He was crying, and she took pity on him. "This is one of the Hebrews' children," she said.

**7** Then his sister said to Pharaoh's daughter, "Shall I go and call a Hebrew woman to nurse the child for you?"

**8** "Yes, go," Pharaoh's daughter said. So the girl went and called the child's mother.

**9** Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Take this child and nurse him for me, and I will pay your wages." So the woman took the child and nursed him.

**10** When the child had grown, she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son. She named him Moses, saying, "Because I drew him out of the water."

**11** One day, when Moses had grown up, he went out to his own people and saw how hard they were made to work. He saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, one of his own people.

**12** He looked around, and seeing no one, he struck the Egyptian down and hid his body in the sand.

**13** The next day he went out and saw two Hebrews fighting. He said to the one in the wrong, "Why are you striking your own countryman?"

**14** The man answered, "Who made you a ruler and judge over us? Are you going to kill me as you killed the Egyptian?" Then Moses was afraid and thought, "The thing is known."

**15** When Pharaoh heard of it, he sought to kill Moses. But Moses fled from Pharaoh and settled in the land of Midian, and he sat down beside a well.

**16** The priest of Midian had seven daughters. They came to draw water and fill the troughs for their father's flock,

**17** but some shepherds came and drove them away. Moses got up and came to their defence and watered their flock.

**18** When they went back to their father Reuel, he asked, "Why have you come back so soon today?"

**19** "An Egyptian rescued us from the shepherds," they said. "He even drew water for us and watered the flock."

**20** "Where is he?" he asked his daughters. "Why did you leave the man? Invite him to eat with us."

**21** Moses agreed to stay with the man, and he gave his daughter Zipporah to Moses as his wife.

**22** She bore a son, and Moses named him Gershom, saying, "I have become a foreigner in a foreign land."

**23** During those many days, the king of Egypt died. The Israelites groaned under their slavery, and cried out, and their cry for help rose up to God.

**24** God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant \[H-BERIT\] with Abraham, with Isaac and with Jacob.

**25** God looked on the Israelites, and God took notice of them.

### Chapter 3

**1** Moses was shepherding the flock of Jethro his father-in-law, the priest of Midian. He led the flock to the far side of the wilderness and came to Horeb, the mountain of God.

**2** There the angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire from the heart of a bush. He looked, and saw that the bush was burning, yet it was not burned up.

**3** Moses said, "I will go over and look at this great sight, and see why the bush is not burning up."

**4** When the LORD saw that he had turned aside to look, God called to him from the middle of the bush: "Moses! Moses!" "Here I am," he said.

**5** God said, "Come no closer. Take your sandals off your feet, for the place where you are standing is holy \[H-QADOSH\] ground."

**6** He said, "I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob." Moses hid his face, for he was afraid to look at God.

**7** The LORD said, "I have seen the suffering of my people in Egypt, and I have heard their cry under their slave-drivers. I know their pain.

**8** I have come down to rescue them from the grip of the Egyptians, and to bring them up out of that land into a good and broad land, a land flowing with milk and honey, the country of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites.

**9** Now the cry of the Israelites has come to me, and I have seen how the Egyptians crush them.

**10** So go. I am sending you to Pharaoh, to bring my people, the Israelites, out of Egypt."

**11** But Moses said to God, "Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh, and bring the Israelites out of Egypt?"

**12** God said, "I will be with you. And this will be the sign to you that I have sent you: when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you will worship God on this mountain."

**13** Moses said to God, "If I go to the Israelites and tell them, 'The God of your fathers has sent me to you,' and they ask me, 'What is his name?' what am I to tell them?"

**14** God said to Moses, "I AM WHO I AM \[H-YHWH\]." And he said, "This is what you are to say to the Israelites: 'I AM has sent me to you.'"

**15** God also said to Moses, "Tell the Israelites this: 'The LORD, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you.' This is my name for ever, and this is how I am to be remembered from one generation to the next.

**16** Go and gather the elders of Israel, and say to them: 'The LORD, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, has appeared to me and said: I have taken note of you and of what has been done to you in Egypt.

**17** I have said that I will bring you up out of the misery of Egypt to the land of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites, a land flowing with milk and honey.'

**18** They will listen to you. Then you and the elders of Israel are to go to the king of Egypt and say to him: 'The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, has met with us. Now let us go a three days' journey into the wilderness, to offer sacrifice to the LORD our God.'

**19** I know that the king of Egypt will not let you go unless a mighty hand forces him.

**20** So I will stretch out my hand and strike Egypt with all the wonders I will do among them. After that he will let you go.

**21** I will make the Egyptians look kindly on this people, so that when you leave, you will not leave empty-handed.

**22** Every woman is to ask her neighbour, and any woman staying in her house, for articles of silver and gold and for clothing, and you will put them on your sons and daughters. In this way you will strip the Egyptians."

### Chapter 4

**1** Moses answered, "What if they will not believe me or listen to what I say? They may say, 'The LORD did not appear to you.'"

**2** The LORD said to him, "What is that in your hand?" "A staff," he said.

**3** The LORD said, "Throw it on the ground." He threw it on the ground and it became a snake, and Moses ran from it.

**4** The LORD said to Moses, "Reach out your hand and grab it by the tail." He reached out and took hold of it, and it became a staff in his hand.

**5** "This is so that they may believe that the LORD, the God of their fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, has appeared to you."

**6** Then the LORD said to him, "Put your hand inside your cloak." He put his hand inside his cloak, and when he took it out, his hand was diseased, white as snow.

**7** He said, "Put your hand back inside your cloak." He put his hand back inside his cloak, and when he took it out, it was healthy again, like the rest of his skin.

**8** "If they will not believe you or take notice of the first sign, they may believe the second.

**9** And if they will not believe even these two signs or listen to you, take some water from the Nile and pour it on the dry ground. The water you take from the Nile will turn to blood on the ground."

**10** Moses said to the LORD, "Please, Lord, I am not a man of words, neither in the past nor since you began speaking to your servant. I am slow of speech and slow of tongue."

**11** The LORD said to him, "Who gave human beings their mouths? Who makes them mute or deaf, sighted or blind? Is it not I, the LORD?

**12** Now go. I will be with your mouth and teach you what to say."

**13** But he said, "Please, Lord, send someone else."

**14** Then the LORD's anger burned against Moses, and he said, "Is there not your brother Aaron the Levite? I know that he can speak well. He is even now coming out to meet you, and when he sees you, he will be glad.

**15** You will speak to him and put the words in his mouth. I will be with your mouth and with his mouth, and I will teach you both what you are to do.

**16** He will speak for you to the people. He will be your mouth, and you will be as God to him.

**17** Take this staff in your hand, for with it you will perform the signs."

**18** Moses went back to Jethro his father-in-law and said to him, "Let me go back to my relatives in Egypt to see whether they are still alive." Jethro said to Moses, "Go in peace."

**19** The LORD had said to Moses in Midian, "Go back to Egypt, for all the men who wanted to kill you are dead."

**20** So Moses took his wife and his sons, set them on a donkey, and went back to the land of Egypt, with the staff of God in his hand.

**21** The LORD said to Moses, "When you go back to Egypt, make sure that you do before Pharaoh all the wonders I have put in your power. But I will harden his heart, and he will not let the people go.

**22** Then you will say to Pharaoh, 'This is what the LORD says: Israel is my firstborn son,

**23** and I told you, "Let my son go, so that he may serve me." You refused to let him go, so I will kill your firstborn son.'"

**24** On the way, at a lodging place, the LORD met him and tried to kill him.

**25** Then Zipporah took a flint, cut off her son's foreskin, and touched his feet with it. "You are a bridegroom of blood to me," she said.

**26** So he let him alone. (She said "bridegroom of blood" because of the circumcision.)

**27** The LORD said to Aaron, "Go to meet Moses in the wilderness." He went and met him at the mountain of God and kissed him.

**28** Moses told Aaron all the words of the LORD with which he had sent him, and all the signs he had commanded him to do.

**29** Moses and Aaron went and gathered all the elders of the Israelites.

**30** Aaron told them all the words that the LORD had spoken to Moses, and performed the signs in the sight of the people.

**31** The people believed, and when they heard that the LORD had visited the Israelites and had seen their misery, they bowed down and worshipped.

*Review note: Exodus 3 and 4 were drafted in WABV wording. The divine name in 3:14 and the account at the lodging place in 4:24-26 are difficult passages and need careful checking against the Hebrew. The section also needs an originality check before it is locked.*

### Chapter 5

**1** Afterwards Moses and Aaron went to Pharaoh and said, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: 'Let my people go, so that they may hold a festival to me in the wilderness.'"

**2** Pharaoh said, "Who is the LORD, that I should listen to his voice and let Israel go? I do not know the LORD, and I will not let Israel go."

**3** They said, "The God of the Hebrews has met with us. Please let us go a three days' journey into the wilderness to offer sacrifice to the LORD our God, or he may strike us with plague or with the sword."

**4** But the king of Egypt said to them, "Moses and Aaron, why are you pulling the people away from their work? Get back to your labour."

**5** Pharaoh said, "Look, the people of the land are many now, and you would have them stop their work."

**6** That same day Pharaoh gave orders to the slave-drivers and the foremen of the people:

**7** "You are no longer to give the people straw for making bricks, as you have done until now. Let them go and gather straw for themselves.

**8** But you are to require the same number of bricks as before. Do not reduce it. They are idle, and that is why they cry out, 'Let us go and sacrifice to our God.'

**9** Make the work heavier for the men, so that they are kept busy and pay no attention to lies."

**10** So the slave-drivers and the foremen of the people went out and said to the people, "This is what Pharaoh says: 'I will not give you straw.

**11** Go and get straw for yourselves wherever you can find it, but your work will not be reduced in the slightest.'"

**12** So the people scattered throughout the land of Egypt to gather stubble to use for straw.

**13** The slave-drivers kept pressing them: "Finish your work, your daily quota, just as when there was straw."

**14** And the Israelite foremen, whom Pharaoh's slave-drivers had set over them, were beaten and asked, "Why have you not completed your quota of bricks, yesterday and today, as you did before?"

**15** Then the Israelite foremen came and appealed to Pharaoh: "Why do you treat your servants like this?

**16** No straw is given to your servants, yet they say to us, 'Make bricks!' Your servants are beaten, but the fault is with your own people."

**17** He said, "You are idle, idle! That is why you say, 'Let us go and sacrifice to the LORD.'

**18** Now go and work. No straw will be given you, but you must deliver the same quota of bricks."

**19** The Israelite foremen saw that they were in trouble when they were told, "You must not reduce your daily quota of bricks."

**20** As they left Pharaoh, they met Moses and Aaron, who were standing there to meet them.

**21** They said to them, "May the LORD look on you and judge you, for you have made us a stench in the nostrils of Pharaoh and his servants, and have put a sword in their hand to kill us."

**22** Then Moses turned to the LORD and said, "Lord, why have you done evil to this people? Why did you ever send me?

**23** Since I came to Pharaoh to speak in your name, he has done evil to this people, and you have not rescued your people at all."

### Chapter 6

**1** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Now you will see what I will do to Pharaoh. Compelled by a mighty hand he will let them go; compelled by a mighty hand he will drive them out of his land."

**2** God spoke to Moses and said to him, "I am the LORD.

**3** I appeared to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob as God Almighty, but I did not make myself known to them by my name, the LORD \[H-YHWH\].

**4** I also established my covenant with them, to give them the land of Canaan, the land in which they lived as foreigners.

**5** I have also heard the groaning of the Israelites, whom the Egyptians hold as slaves, and I have remembered my covenant.

**6** Therefore say to the Israelites: 'I am the LORD. I will bring you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians, and I will deliver you from slavery to them. I will redeem \[H-GAAL\] you with an outstretched arm and with great acts of judgement.

**7** I will take you to be my people, and I will be your God. You will know that I am the LORD your God, who brought you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians.

**8** I will bring you into the land that I swore with raised hand to give to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob. I will give it to you as a possession. I am the LORD.'"

**9** Moses told this to the Israelites, but they would not listen to Moses, because of their broken spirit and their hard slavery.

**10** Then the LORD spoke to Moses:

**11** "Go in and tell Pharaoh king of Egypt to let the Israelites go out of his land."

**12** But Moses said to the LORD, "If the Israelites will not listen to me, how will Pharaoh listen to me, a man with uncircumcised lips?"

**13** But the LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron, and gave them a charge for the Israelites and for Pharaoh king of Egypt, to bring the Israelites out of the land of Egypt.

**14** These are the heads of their ancestral houses. The sons of Reuben, the firstborn of Israel: Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron and Carmi. These are the clans of Reuben.

**15** The sons of Simeon: Jemuel, Jamin, Ohad, Jachin, Zohar and Shaul, the son of a Canaanite woman. These are the clans of Simeon.

**16** These are the names of the sons of Levi, in the order of their records: Gershon, Kohath and Merari. Levi lived 137 years.

**17** The sons of Gershon, by their clans: Libni and Shimei.

**18** The sons of Kohath: Amram, Izhar, Hebron and Uzziel. Kohath lived 133 years.

**19** The sons of Merari: Mahli and Mushi. These are the clans of the Levites, in the order of their records.

**20** Amram took his father's sister Jochebed as his wife, and she bore him Aaron and Moses. Amram lived 137 years.

**21** The sons of Izhar: Korah, Nepheg and Zichri.

**22** The sons of Uzziel: Mishael, Elzaphan and Sithri.

**23** Aaron took as his wife Elisheba, daughter of Amminadab and sister of Nahshon, and she bore him Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar.

**24** The sons of Korah: Assir, Elkanah and Abiasaph. These are the clans of the Korahites.

**25** Aaron's son Eleazar took as his wife one of the daughters of Putiel, and she bore him Phinehas. These are the heads of the ancestral houses of the Levites by their clans.

**26** It was this Aaron and this Moses to whom the LORD said, "Bring the Israelites out of the land of Egypt, company by company."

**27** It was they who spoke to Pharaoh king of Egypt about bringing the Israelites out of Egypt, this same Moses and Aaron.

**28** On the day when the LORD spoke to Moses in the land of Egypt,

**29** the LORD said to Moses, "I am the LORD. Tell Pharaoh king of Egypt all that I say to you."

**30** But Moses said before the LORD, "Look, I am a man with uncircumcised lips. How will Pharaoh listen to me?"

### Chapter 7

**1** The LORD said to Moses, "Understand this: I have set you in God's place before Pharaoh, and your brother Aaron will be your spokesman.

**2** You will say everything I command you, and your brother Aaron will tell Pharaoh to release the Israelites from his land.

**3** But I will make Pharaoh's heart stubborn, and however many signs and wonders I perform in Egypt,

**4** Pharaoh will not listen to you. Then I will bring my hand down on Egypt, and I will lead out my companies, my people the Israelites, from Egypt with mighty acts of judgement.

**5** The Egyptians will know that I am the LORD, when I stretch out my hand over Egypt and lead the Israelites out from among them."

**6** Moses and Aaron did exactly what the LORD had commanded them.

**7** Moses was eighty years old, and Aaron eighty-three, when they spoke to Pharaoh.

**8** The LORD said to Moses and Aaron,

**9** "When Pharaoh says to you, 'Prove yourselves by a wonder,' tell Aaron, 'Take your staff and throw it down in front of Pharaoh.' It will turn into a great serpent."

**10** Moses and Aaron went to Pharaoh and did exactly as the LORD had commanded. Aaron threw his staff down in front of Pharaoh and his courtiers, and it turned into a serpent.

**11** Pharaoh called for his wise men and sorcerers, and the magicians of Egypt did the same with their secret arts.

**12** Each threw down his staff, and each became a serpent, but Aaron's staff swallowed theirs.

**13** Even so, Pharaoh's heart stayed hard, and he would not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.

**14** The LORD said to Moses, "Pharaoh's heart is stubborn. He refuses to release the people.

**15** Go to Pharaoh in the morning, when he goes out to the river. Wait for him on the bank of the Nile, and carry in your hand the staff that turned into a snake.

**16** Tell him, 'The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, has sent me to you with this message: Release my people, so that they may worship me in the wilderness. Until now you have not listened.

**17** This is what the LORD says: Here is how you will know that I am the LORD. I am about to strike the water of the Nile with the staff in my hand, and it will turn to blood.

**18** The fish in the river will die, the river will stink, and the Egyptians will find the Nile water undrinkable.'"

**19** The LORD said to Moses, "Tell Aaron: 'Take your staff and stretch out your hand over the waters of Egypt, over its rivers, canals, ponds and every reservoir, and they will turn to blood.' Throughout Egypt there will be blood, even in wooden buckets and stone jars."

**20** Moses and Aaron did exactly as the LORD commanded. Raising the staff in full view of Pharaoh and his courtiers, he struck the water of the Nile, and all the water in the river turned to blood.

**21** The fish in the Nile died, and the river stank so badly that the Egyptians could not drink from it. There was blood throughout Egypt.

**22** The Egyptian magicians did the same with their secret arts. So Pharaoh's heart stayed hard, and he would not listen to Moses and Aaron, just as the LORD had said.

**23** Pharaoh turned and went back to his palace, and he did not take even this to heart.

**24** All the Egyptians dug around the Nile for drinking water, because they could not drink the river water.

**25** Seven days went by after the LORD had struck the Nile.

### Chapter 8

**1** The LORD said to Moses, "Go to Pharaoh and tell him, 'This is what the LORD says: Release my people, so that they may worship me.

**2** If you refuse to release them, I will strike your whole country with frogs.

**3** The Nile will teem with frogs. They will come up and enter your palace, your bedroom and your bed, the houses of your courtiers and your people, your ovens and your kneading bowls.

**4** The frogs will climb over you, over your people and over all your courtiers.'"

**5** The LORD said to Moses, "Tell Aaron: 'Stretch out your hand with your staff over the rivers, canals and ponds, and bring frogs up over the land of Egypt.'"

**6** Aaron stretched out his hand over the waters of Egypt, and the frogs came up and covered the land.

**7** The magicians did the same with their secret arts, and they too brought frogs up over the land of Egypt.

**8** Pharaoh summoned Moses and Aaron and said, "Pray to the LORD to remove the frogs from me and from my people, and I will release the people to offer sacrifices to the LORD."

**9** Moses said to Pharaoh, "I give you the honour. Tell me when I am to pray for you, your courtiers and your people, so that the frogs will be removed from you and your houses and be left only in the Nile."

**10** "Tomorrow," he said. Moses said, "It will be as you say, so that you will know there is no one like the LORD our God.

**11** The frogs will leave you, your houses, your courtiers and your people. They will be left only in the Nile."

**12** Moses and Aaron left Pharaoh, and Moses cried out to the LORD concerning the frogs he had inflicted on Pharaoh.

**13** The LORD did as Moses asked. The frogs died in the houses, in the courtyards and in the fields.

**14** They were piled up in heaps, and the land stank.

**15** But when Pharaoh saw that relief had come, he made his heart heavy and would not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.

**16** The LORD said to Moses, "Tell Aaron: 'Stretch out your staff and strike the dust of the ground,' and it will turn into gnats throughout the land of Egypt."

**17** They did so. Aaron stretched out his hand with his staff and struck the dust of the ground, and gnats came on people and animals. All the dust of the ground turned into gnats throughout the land of Egypt.

**18** The magicians tried to produce gnats with their secret arts, but they could not. The gnats stayed on people and animals.

**19** The magicians said to Pharaoh, "This is the finger of God!" But Pharaoh's heart stayed hard, and he would not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.

**20** The LORD said to Moses, "Rise early in the morning and stand in Pharaoh's path as he goes out to the water. Tell him, 'This is what the LORD says: Release my people, so that they may worship me.

**21** If you do not release my people, I will send swarms of flies on you, your courtiers, your people and your houses. The houses of the Egyptians will be full of flies, and so will the very ground they stand on.

**22** But on that day I will treat the land of Goshen, where my people live, differently. No swarms of flies will be there, so that you will know that I, the LORD, am present in this land.

**23** I will set a separation between my people and yours. Tomorrow this sign will happen.'"

**24** The LORD did so. Thick swarms of flies poured into Pharaoh's palace and the houses of his courtiers, and throughout Egypt the land was ruined by the flies.

**25** Pharaoh summoned Moses and Aaron and said, "Go and sacrifice to your God here in this country."

**26** Moses said, "That would not be right. The sacrifices we offer to the LORD our God are detestable to the Egyptians. If we offer sacrifices that are detestable in their eyes, will they not stone us?

**27** We must go a three days' journey into the wilderness and sacrifice to the LORD our God, as he instructs us."

**28** Pharaoh said, "I will release you to sacrifice to the LORD your God in the wilderness, but you must not go very far. Pray for me."

**29** Moses said, "As soon as I leave you, I will pray to the LORD, and tomorrow the swarms of flies will leave Pharaoh, his courtiers and his people. Only let Pharaoh stop dealing deceitfully and refusing to release the people to sacrifice to the LORD."

**30** Moses left Pharaoh and prayed to the LORD.

**31** The LORD did as Moses asked, and removed the swarms of flies from Pharaoh, his courtiers and his people. Not one was left.

**32** But Pharaoh made his heart heavy this time too, and did not release the people.

### Chapter 9

**1** The LORD said to Moses, "Go to Pharaoh and tell him, 'This is what the LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says: Release my people, so that they may worship me.

**2** If you refuse to release them and keep holding on to them,

**3** the hand of the LORD will strike your livestock in the fields: your horses, donkeys and camels, your herds and flocks, with a severe plague.

**4** But the LORD will draw a line between the livestock of Israel and that of Egypt, so that nothing that belongs to the Israelites will die.'"

**5** The LORD set a time: "Tomorrow the LORD will do this in the land."

**6** The next day the LORD did it. All the Egyptians' livestock died, but not one animal of the Israelites died.

**7** Pharaoh sent men to check, and not one of the Israelites' animals had died. Yet Pharaoh's heart stayed stubborn, and he would not release the people.

**8** The LORD said to Moses and Aaron, "Take handfuls of soot from a brick kiln, and Moses is to scatter it towards the sky in Pharaoh's sight.

**9** It will become fine dust over the whole land of Egypt, and wherever it settles on people and animals it will cause festering sores."

**10** They took soot from a kiln and stood before Pharaoh, and Moses scattered it towards the sky, and it caused festering sores on people and animals.

**11** The magicians could not stand in front of Moses because of the sores, for the sores were on the magicians as on all the Egyptians.

**12** But the LORD strengthened Pharaoh's resolve, and he would not listen to them, just as the LORD had told Moses.

**23** He told them, "This is what the LORD said: Tomorrow is a day of rest, a holy Sabbath \[H-SHABBAT\] for the LORD. Bake what you will bake and boil what you will boil, and put aside all that is left over, to keep until morning."

**24** They put it aside until morning, as Moses had commanded, and it did not stink, nor did maggots appear in it.

**25** "Eat it today," Moses said, "for today is a Sabbath for the LORD. Today you will find none in the field.

**26** For six days you may gather it, but the seventh day is the Sabbath. On it there will be none."

**27** Even so, some of the people went out on the seventh day to gather, and found nothing.

**28** The LORD said to Moses, "How long will you people refuse to keep my commands and my teachings?

**29** Remember that the LORD has given you the Sabbath. That is why on the sixth day he gives you bread for two days. On the seventh day everyone is to stay in place. No one is to leave his spot."

**30** So the people rested on the seventh day.

**31** The house of Israel named it manna. It was white like coriander seed, and tasted like honey wafers.

**32** Moses said, "This is what the LORD has commanded: 'Fill an omer with it, to be kept for your descendants, so that they may see the bread I fed you in the wilderness when I brought you out of Egypt.'"

**33** Moses told Aaron, "Take a jar and put an omer of manna in it, and set it down before the LORD, to be kept for your descendants."

**34** As the LORD had commanded Moses, Aaron set it down before the Testimony for safekeeping.

**35** The Israelites ate manna for forty years, until they reached inhabited land. They ate manna until they came to the edge of Canaan.

**36** (An omer is a tenth of an ephah.)

### Chapter 17

**1** The whole assembly of Israel travelled on from the wilderness of Sin, stage by stage, at the LORD's command, and camped at Rephidim. But there was no water there for the people to drink.

**2** The people took Moses to task: "Give us water to drink!" Moses answered, "Why take me to task? Why put the LORD to the test?"

**3** But the people were parched with thirst there, and they murmured against Moses: "Why did you bring us up from Egypt? To kill us, our children and our livestock with thirst?"

**4** Moses cried out to the LORD, "What can I do with these people? Any moment now they will stone me!"

**5** The LORD said to Moses, "Pass on ahead of the people, and take some of the elders of Israel with you. Take along the staff you struck the Nile with, and go.

**6** I will stand before you there on the rock at Horeb. Strike the rock, and water will come out of it for the people to drink." Moses did so in front of the elders of Israel.

**8** Amalek came and attacked Israel at Rephidim.

**11** Whenever Moses raised his hand, Israel was winning. Whenever he lowered it, Amalek was winning.

**12** Moses' hands grew heavy, so they took a stone and set it beneath him, and he sat on it. Aaron and Hur supported his hands, one on each side, and so his hands stayed steady until sunset.

**13** Joshua cut Amalek and his people down with the sword.

**14** The LORD said to Moses, "Write this down in a scroll as a reminder, and recite it to Joshua: I will utterly wipe out the memory of Amalek from under the sky."

**15** Moses built an altar and named it "The LORD is my Banner."

**16** He said, "A hand is raised against the throne of the LORD! The LORD will be at war with Amalek from generation to generation."

**25** Moses chose capable men from all Israel and made them heads over the people: leaders of thousands, hundreds, fifties and tens.

**26** They judged the people at all times. The hard cases they brought to Moses, and the minor cases they decided themselves.

**27** Then Moses saw his father-in-law off, and he went back to his own country.

## Exodus 19-40: At Sinai

### Chapter 19

**1** In the third month after the Israelites had gone out from Egypt, on that very day, they came into the wilderness of Sinai.

**2** They set out from Rephidim and entered the wilderness of Sinai, and Israel camped there, facing the mountain.

**3** Moses went up to God, and the LORD called to him from the mountain: "This is what you are to say to the house of Jacob, and tell to the Israelites:

**4** 'You have seen for yourselves what I did to Egypt, and how I carried you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself.

**5** Now, if you will really listen to my voice and keep my covenant \[H-BERIT\], then out of all the peoples you will be my own treasured possession. All the earth is mine,

**6** but you will be for me a kingdom of priests and a holy \[H-QADOSH\] nation.' These are the words you are to speak to the Israelites."

**7** Moses came and called the elders of the people, and set before them all these words which the LORD had commanded him.

**8** All the people answered together, "Everything the LORD has said we will do." Moses took the people's answer back to the LORD.

**9** The LORD said to Moses, "I am coming to you in the thick of a cloud, so that the people will hear when I speak with you and will trust you for ever." Moses reported the people's words to the LORD.

**10** The LORD said to Moses, "Go to the people and consecrate them today and tomorrow. They are to wash their clothes

**11** and be ready for the third day, for on the third day the LORD will come down on Mount Sinai in the sight of all the people.

**12** Mark out a boundary for the people all round, and say: 'Take care not to go up the mountain or touch its edge. Anyone who touches the mountain will be put to death.

**13** No hand is to touch him. He is to be stoned or shot, whether animal or person. He must not live.' When the ram's horn sounds a long blast, they may go up the mountain."

**14** Moses went down from the mountain to the people. He consecrated the people, and they washed their clothes.

**15** He said to the people, "Be ready for the third day. Do not go near a woman."

**16** On the morning of the third day there was thunder and lightning, and a heavy cloud on the mountain, and a very loud blast of a ram's horn. All the people in the camp trembled.

**17** Moses brought the people out of the camp to meet God, and they took their stand at the foot of the mountain.

**18** Mount Sinai was wrapped in smoke, because the LORD had come down on it in fire. Its smoke went up like the smoke of a kiln, and the whole mountain shook violently.

**19** The sound of the horn grew louder and louder. Moses spoke, and God answered him with thunder.

**20** The LORD came down on Mount Sinai, to the top of the mountain, and the LORD called Moses to the top of the mountain, and Moses went up.

**21** The LORD said to Moses, "Go down and warn the people not to break through to the LORD to look, or many of them will perish.

**22** Even the priests who come near to the LORD must consecrate themselves, or the LORD will break out against them."

**23** Moses said to the LORD, "The people cannot come up Mount Sinai, because you warned us, saying, 'Set a boundary around the mountain and consecrate it.'"

**24** The LORD said to him, "Go down, and come up again with Aaron. But the priests and the people must not break through to come up to the LORD, or he will break out against them."

**25** So Moses went down to the people and told them.

### Chapter 20

**1** God spoke all these words:

**2** "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.

**3** You will have no other gods in my presence.

**4** You will not make for yourself a carved image, or any likeness of what is in the heavens above, or on the earth beneath, or in the waters under the earth.

**5** You will not bow down to them or serve them, for I, the LORD your God, am a jealous God, bringing the guilt of parents on their children, to the third and fourth generation of those who hate me,

**6** and showing faithful love \[H-HESED\] to the thousandth generation of those who love me and keep my commands.

**7** You will not use the name of the LORD your God for a worthless purpose, for the LORD will not leave unpunished anyone who uses his name in that way.

**8** Remember the Sabbath day \[H-SHABBAT\], to keep it holy.

**9** For six days you are to labour and do all your work,

**10** but the seventh day is a Sabbath for the LORD your God. On it you will do no work, neither you, nor your son or your daughter, nor your male or female servant, nor your livestock, nor the foreigner who lives within your gates.

**11** For in six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth and the sea and all that is in them, and on the seventh day he rested. That is why the LORD blessed the Sabbath day and made it holy.

**12** Honour your father and your mother, so that your days may be long in the land that the LORD your God is giving you.

**13** You will not murder.

**14** You will not commit adultery.

**15** You will not steal.

**16** You will not give false evidence against your neighbour.

**17** You will not covet your neighbour's house. You will not covet your neighbour's wife, or his male or female servant, or his ox or donkey, or anything that belongs to your neighbour."

**18** All the people saw the thunder and the lightning, and heard the horn, and saw the mountain smoking. When the people saw it, they trembled and stood at a distance.

**19** They said to Moses, "You speak to us, and we will listen. But do not let God speak to us, or we will die."

**20** Moses said to the people, "Do not be afraid. God has come to test you, so that the fear of him will be with you and you will not sin."

**21** The people stood at a distance, while Moses drew near to the thick darkness where God was.

**22** The LORD said to Moses, "This is what you are to say to the Israelites: 'You yourselves have seen that I have spoken with you from heaven.

**23** You will not make gods of silver or gods of gold alongside me. You are not to make them for yourselves.

**24** You are to make an altar of earth for me, and sacrifice on it your burnt offerings and your peace offerings, your sheep and your cattle. In every place where I cause my name to be remembered, I will come to you and bless you.

**25** If you make an altar of stone for me, you are not to build it of cut stones, for if you use a chisel on it, you profane it.

**26** You are not to go up by steps to my altar, so that your nakedness is not exposed on it.'

### Chapter 21

**1** These are the rulings you are to set before them:

**2** When you buy a Hebrew slave, he is to serve for six years, and in the seventh he is to go out free, owing nothing.

**3** If he came in single, he is to go out single. If he came in as the husband of a wife, his wife is to go out with him.

**4** If his master gave him a wife and she bore him sons or daughters, the wife and her children belong to her master, and he is to go out alone.

**5** But if the slave declares, "I love my master, my wife and my children. I do not want to go free,"

**6** then his master is to bring him before God. He is to bring him to the door or the doorpost, and his master is to pierce his ear with an awl, and he will be his slave for life.

**7** When a man sells his daughter as a slave, she is not to go out as the male slaves do.

**8** If she displeases the master who had designated her for himself, he is to let her be bought back. He has no right to sell her to a foreign people, since he has dealt unfairly with her.

**9** If he designates her for his son, he is to deal with her as with a daughter.

**10** If he takes another wife for himself, he is not to reduce the first one's food, her clothing or her marital rights.

**11** If he does not do these three things for her, she is to go out without payment, without any money.

**12** Whoever strikes a person and the person dies is to be put to death.

**13** But if he did not lie in wait for him, and God let it happen into his hand, I will appoint a place for you to which he may flee.

**14** But if someone acts with premeditation against his neighbour and kills him by cunning, you are to take him even from my altar, to be put to death.

**15** Whoever strikes his father or his mother is to be put to death.

**16** Whoever kidnaps a person and sells him, or is found with him in his possession, is to be put to death.

**17** Whoever curses his father or his mother is to be put to death.

**18** When people quarrel and one strikes the other with a stone or with a fist, and he does not die but takes to his bed,

**19** if he gets up and walks about outside on his staff, then the one who struck him is cleared. He is only to pay for his loss of time, and to have him thoroughly healed.

**20** When a man strikes his male or female slave with a rod and the slave dies under his hand, he is to be avenged.

**21** But if the slave survives a day or two, he is not to be avenged, for the slave is his money.

**22** When people fight and strike a pregnant woman so that her children come out, but there is no further harm, the one who struck her is to be fined as the woman's husband demands, and he is to pay as the judges decide.

**23** But if there is harm, then you are to give life for life,

**24** eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot,

**25** burn for burn, wound for wound, bruise for bruise.

**26** When a man strikes the eye of his male or female slave and destroys it, he is to let the slave go free in compensation for the eye.

**27** If he knocks out the tooth of his male or female slave, he is to let the slave go free in compensation for the tooth.

**28** When an ox gores a man or a woman to death, the ox is to be stoned and its flesh is not to be eaten, but the owner of the ox is not to be held responsible.

**29** But if the ox has been in the habit of goring, and its owner has been warned but has not kept it in, and it kills a man or a woman, the ox is to be stoned, and its owner too is to be put to death.

**30** If a ransom is imposed on him, he may pay the redemption price for his life, whatever is imposed on him.

**31** If the ox gores a son or a daughter, this same ruling applies to him.

**32** If the ox gores a male or female slave, the owner is to give thirty shekels of silver to the slave's master, and the ox is to be stoned.

**33** When a man opens a pit, or digs a pit and does not cover it, and an ox or a donkey falls into it,

**34** the owner of the pit is to make it good. He is to pay money to the animal's owner, and the dead animal will be his.

**35** When one man's ox hurts another's so that it dies, they are to sell the live ox and divide its price, and they are also to divide the dead animal.

**36** Or, if it was known that the ox was in the habit of goring, and its owner did not keep it in, he is to pay in full, ox for ox, and the dead animal will be his.

### Chapter 22

**1** When a man steals an ox or a sheep and slaughters it or sells it, he is to repay five oxen for the ox and four sheep for the sheep.

**2** If a thief is found breaking in and is struck so that he dies, there is no bloodguilt for the one who struck him,

**3** but if the sun has risen on him, there is bloodguilt for him. A thief must make full restitution. If he has nothing, he is to be sold for his theft.

**4** If the stolen animal is found alive in his possession, whether an ox or a donkey or a sheep, he is to repay double.

**5** When a man lets a field or a vineyard be grazed, and lets his livestock loose so that they graze in another man's field, he is to make restitution from the best of his own field and the best of his own vineyard.

**6** When a fire breaks out and catches in thorns, so that stacked grain or standing grain or the field is burned, the one who started the fire is to make full restitution.

**7** When a man gives his neighbour silver or goods to keep, and it is stolen from the man's house, if the thief is found, he is to repay double.

**8** If the thief is not found, the owner of the house is to come before God, to swear that he has not laid a hand on his neighbour's property.

**9** In every case of wrongdoing, whether it concerns an ox, a donkey, a sheep, a garment, or any lost thing about which someone says, "This is mine," the case of both parties is to come before God. The one whom God declares guilty is to repay double to his neighbour.

**10** When a man gives his neighbour a donkey or an ox or a sheep or any animal to keep, and it dies or is hurt or driven away, with no one seeing it,

**11** an oath by the LORD is to be between them both, that he has not laid a hand on his neighbour's property. The owner is to accept it, and the other is not to make restitution.

**12** But if it was stolen from him, he is to make restitution to its owner.

**13** If it was torn by a wild animal, he is to bring it as evidence. He is not to make restitution for what was torn.

**14** When a man borrows an animal from his neighbour and it is hurt or dies while its owner is not with it, he is to make full restitution.

**15** If its owner was with it, he is not to make restitution. If it was hired, the hire price covers the loss.

**16** When a man seduces a virgin who is not betrothed, and lies with her, he is to pay the bride-price for her and make her his wife.

**17** If her father refuses to give her to him, he is to pay silver equal to the bride-price for virgins.

**18** You are not to let a sorceress live.

**19** Whoever lies with an animal is to be put to death.

**20** Whoever sacrifices to any god other than the LORD alone is to be devoted to destruction.

**21** You are not to wrong a foreign resident or oppress him, for you were foreign residents in the land of Egypt.

**22** You are not to afflict any widow or orphan.

**23** If you do afflict them, and they cry out to me, I will surely hear their cry.

**24** My anger will burn, and I will kill you with the sword, and your wives will become widows and your children orphans.

**25** If you lend money to my people, to the poor among you, you are not to act as a creditor to him. You are not to charge him interest.

**26** If you take your neighbour's cloak in pledge, you are to return it to him before the sun goes down,

**27** for it is his only covering, the cloak for his body. What else will he sleep in? And when he cries out to me, I will hear, for I am compassionate.

**28** You are not to curse God or put a curse on a leader of your people.

**29** You are not to hold back the first of your harvest or of your vats. The firstborn of your sons you are to give to me.

**30** You are to do the same with your oxen and with your sheep. For seven days it is to be with its mother, and on the eighth day you are to give it to me.

**31** You are to be holy people to me. You are not to eat flesh torn by wild animals in the field. You are to throw it to the dogs.

### Chapter 23

**1** You are not to pass on a false report. Do not join hands with a wicked person to be a malicious witness.

**2** Do not follow the majority to do wrong, and do not give testimony in a dispute by siding with the majority to twist justice.

**3** Do not favour a poor person in his dispute.

**4** When you come across your enemy's ox or donkey straying, you must take it back to him.

**5** When you see the donkey of someone who hates you collapsed under its load, you must not leave it alone. You must help him free it.

**6** Do not twist the justice due to your needy people in their disputes.

**7** Stay far from a false charge. Do not put to death the innocent and the righteous, for I will not acquit the guilty.

**8** Do not take a bribe, for a bribe blinds those who see and distorts the words of the righteous.

**10** For six years you are to sow your land and gather its produce,

**11** but in the seventh year you are to let it rest and lie fallow, so that the poor of your people may eat, and what they leave the wild animals may eat. You are to do the same with your vineyard and your olive grove.

**12** For six days you are to do your work, but on the seventh day you are to cease, so that your ox and your donkey may rest, and the son of your female servant and the foreign resident may be refreshed.

**14** Three times in the year you are to hold a festival for me.

**18** You are not to offer the blood of my sacrifice with anything leavened, and the fat of my festival offering is not to remain until morning.

**19** The best of the first fruits of your ground you are to bring to the house of the LORD your God. You are not to boil a young goat in its mother's milk.

**20** I am sending an angel ahead of you to guard you on the way and to bring you to the place I have prepared.

**21** Pay attention to him and listen to his voice. Do not rebel against him, for he will not pardon your rebellion, since my name is in him.

**22** If you listen closely to his voice and do all that I say, I will be an enemy to your enemies and a foe to your foes.

**23** My angel will go ahead of you and bring you to the Amorites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Canaanites, the Hivites and the Jebusites, and I will wipe them out.

**24** You are not to bow down to their gods or serve them, and you are not to follow their practices. You are to demolish them completely and smash their pillars to pieces.

**25** You are to serve the LORD your God, and he will bless your bread and your water. I will take sickness away from among you.

**26** No woman will miscarry or be childless in your land. I will fill the number of your days.

**27** I will send my terror ahead of you and throw into confusion every people you come to. I will make all your enemies turn their backs to you.

**28** I will send hornets ahead of you, and they will drive out the Hivites, the Canaanites and the Hittites from before you.

**29** I will not drive them out from before you in a single year, or the land would become desolate and the wild animals would multiply against you.

**30** Little by little I will drive them out from before you, until you have increased and taken possession of the land.

**31** I will set your boundary from the Red Sea to the sea of the Philistines, and from the wilderness to the River. I will give the inhabitants of the land into your hand, and you will drive them out before you.

**32** You are not to make a covenant with them or with their gods.

**33** They must not live in your land, or they will make you sin against me. For if you serve their gods, it will surely be a snare to you.

### Chapter 25

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses:

*Review note: Exodus 25 to 31 was drafted in WABV wording, working from the Hebrew meaning. These chapters are technical, with many terms for building materials, furniture and priestly clothing that are uncertain, and the gem names in 28:17-20 in particular are debated. Much of the wording of the instructions is formulaic and may still sit close to existing translations. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

### Chapter 32

**2** Aaron said to them, "Take off the gold earrings from your wives, your sons and your daughters, and bring them to me."

**3** All the people took off the gold earrings from their ears and brought them to Aaron.

**4** He took what they handed him, worked it with an engraving tool and made it into a molten calf. They said, "Israel, here are your gods, who brought you up from the land of Egypt."

**5** When Aaron saw this, he built an altar in front of it and proclaimed, "Tomorrow is a festival for the LORD."

**6** They got up early the next morning, offered burnt offerings and brought peace offerings. The people sat down to eat and drink, and then got up to revel.

**7** The LORD said to Moses, "Go down at once, for your people, whom you brought up from Egypt, have ruined themselves.

**8** How quickly they have turned from the road I laid down for them! They have made themselves a molten calf, bowed down to it and sacrificed to it, and said, 'Israel, here are your gods, who brought you up from the land of Egypt.'"

**9** The LORD said to Moses, "I have looked at this people, and they are a stubborn, stiff-necked people.

**10** So now, let me be, so that my anger may blaze against them and consume them. Then I will make you into a great nation."

**11** But Moses tried to soften the LORD his God. "LORD," he said, "why should your anger blaze against your people, whom you brought out of Egypt with great power and a strong hand?

**12** Why should the Egyptians be able to say, 'He brought them out with evil intent, to kill them in the mountains and wipe them off the face of the earth'? Turn back from your blazing anger, and change your mind about harming your people.

**13** Remember your servants Abraham, Isaac and Israel, to whom you swore by your own self: 'I will make your descendants as many as the stars of the sky, and I will give your descendants all this land that I promised, to be theirs for ever.'"

**14** And the LORD changed his mind about the harm he had threatened to bring on his people.

**15** Moses turned and went down the mountain, the two tablets of the Testimony in his hands, tablets written on both sides, front and back.

**16** The tablets were God's work, and the writing was God's writing, engraved on the tablets.

**17** Joshua heard the people's noise as they shouted, and he said to Moses, "There is a sound of battle in the camp."

**18** Moses answered, "That is not the sound of a victory cry, nor the sound of a cry of defeat. The sound I hear is singing."

**19** As he came near the camp and saw the calf and the dancing, Moses' anger blazed. He hurled the tablets from his hands and shattered them at the foot of the mountain.

**20** He took the calf they had made, burned it in the fire, ground it to powder, scattered the powder on the water and made the Israelites drink it.

**21** Moses said to Aaron, "What did this people do to you, that you brought such great guilt on them?"

**22** Aaron said, "Do not let my lord's anger blaze. You know how bent on evil this people is.

**23** They said to me, 'Make us gods to lead the way for us. As for this Moses, the man who brought us up from Egypt, we do not know what has happened to him.'

**24** So I said to them, 'Whoever has gold, take it off.' They gave it to me, I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf."

**25** Moses saw that the people were out of control, because Aaron had let them run wild, a laughing-stock for their enemies.

**26** Moses stood at the gate of the camp and called, "Whoever is for the LORD, to me!" All the Levites gathered to him.

**27** He told them, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: 'Every man, strap on your sword. Go back and forth through the camp, from gate to gate, and kill your brother, your companion and your neighbour.'"

**28** The Levites did what Moses said, and about three thousand of the people fell that day.

**29** Moses said, "Today you have been set apart for the LORD, at the cost of your own sons and brothers, so that he may bless you today."

**30** The next day Moses said to the people, "You have committed a great sin. Now I will go up to the LORD. Perhaps I can atone for your sin."

**31** Moses went back to the LORD and said, "Oh, this people has committed a great sin: they have made themselves a god of gold.

**32** Now, if you will forgive their sin... but if not, wipe me out of the book you have written."

**33** The LORD said to Moses, "Whoever has sinned against me, I will wipe out of my book.

**34** Now go, lead the people to the place I told you of. My angel will go ahead of you. But on the day I call them to account, I will hold them to account for their sin."

**35** And the LORD struck the people with a plague because they had made the calf, the one Aaron made.

### Chapter 33

**1** The LORD said to Moses, "Set out from here, you and the people you brought up from the land of Egypt, to the land I promised on oath to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob when I said, 'I will give it to your descendants.'

**2** I will send an angel ahead of you, and I will drive out the Canaanites, Amorites, Hittites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites.

**3** Go to a land flowing with milk and honey. But I will not go up in your midst, because you are a stubborn people, and I might destroy you on the way."

**4** When the people heard this harsh word, they mourned, and none of them put on his ornaments.

**5** The LORD had said to Moses, "Tell the Israelites: 'You are a stubborn people. If I went up in your midst even for a moment, I would destroy you. Now take off your ornaments, and I will decide what to do with you.'"

**6** So the Israelites stripped off their ornaments from Mount Horeb onwards.

**7** Moses used to take the tent and pitch it outside the camp, a good distance away, and he called it the tent of meeting. Anyone who wished to consult the LORD would go out to the tent of meeting outside the camp.

**8** Whenever Moses went out to the tent, all the people would stand up, each at the entrance of his own tent, and watch Moses until he entered the tent.

**9** When Moses entered the tent, the pillar of cloud would come down and stand at its entrance, and the LORD would speak with Moses.

**10** When all the people saw the pillar of cloud standing at the entrance of the tent, they would all stand up and bow down, each at the entrance of his own tent.

**11** The LORD would speak to Moses face to face, as a man speaks to his friend. Then Moses would return to the camp, but his young assistant, Joshua son of Nun, never left the tent.

**12** Moses said to the LORD, "You tell me, 'Lead this people up,' but you have not told me whom you will send with me. And yet you said, 'I know you by name, and you have also found favour with me.'

**13** So now, if I have found favour with you, please make your ways known to me, so that I may know you and go on finding favour with you. And remember that this nation is your people."

**14** He said, "My presence will go with you, and I will give you rest."

**15** He said to him, "If your presence does not go with us, do not take us up from here.

**16** How else will it be known that I and your people have found favour with you? Will it not be by your going with us, so that we, I and your people, are set apart from every other people on the face of the earth?"

**17** The LORD said to Moses, "I will do this too, the thing you have asked, for you have found favour with me, and I know you by name."

**18** Moses said, "Show me your glory, I pray."

**19** He said, "I will make all my goodness pass before you, and I will call out the name 'the LORD' in front of you. I will be gracious to whom I choose to be gracious, and show compassion to whom I choose to show compassion."

**20** He added, "But you cannot see my face, for no human can see me and live."

**21** The LORD said, "Here is a place beside me. You are to stand on the rock,

**22** and while my glory passes by, I will set you in a cleft of the rock and cover you with my hand until I have passed by.

**23** Then I will take my hand away, and you will see my back, but my face will not be seen."

### Chapter 34

**1** The LORD said to Moses, "Carve out two stone tablets like the first ones, and I will write on them the words that were on the first tablets, which you smashed.

**2** Be ready by morning. In the morning you are to climb up Mount Sinai and present yourself to me there, on the top of the mountain.

**3** No one is to go up with you, and no one is to be seen anywhere on the mountain. Not even flocks and herds are to graze in front of that mountain."

**4** Moses carved out two stone tablets like the first ones, got up early in the morning and climbed Mount Sinai, as the LORD had commanded him, taking the two stone tablets in his hand.

**5** The LORD came down in the cloud and stood beside him there, and he called out the name of the LORD.

**6** The LORD passed in front of him and called out: "The LORD, the LORD, a God compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, rich in faithful love \[H-HESED\] and truth \[H-EMET\],

**7** keeping faithful love for thousands of generations, forgiving guilt, rebellion and sin, yet not leaving the guilty unpunished, holding the children and grandchildren to account for the guilt of their parents, to the third and fourth generation."

**8** Moses quickly bowed to the ground and worshipped.

**9** He said, "My Lord, if I have found favour with you, let my Lord go with us. Though this is a stubborn people, forgive our guilt and our sin, and take us as your own possession."

**10** He said, "See, I am making a covenant. In front of all your people I will do wonders such as have never been done anywhere on earth or among any nation. All the people around you will see the LORD's work, for what I am going to do with you is awe-inspiring.

**11** Keep what I command you today. I am going to drive out before you the Amorites, Canaanites, Hittites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites.

**12** Take care not to make a treaty with the people of the land you are entering, or it will become a trap in your midst.

**13** Instead, tear down their altars, smash their sacred pillars and cut down their sacred poles.

**14** You must not bow down to any other god, for the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God.

**15** Do not make a treaty with the people of the land. When they chase after their gods and sacrifice to them, they will invite you, and you will eat their sacrifices.

**16** You will take wives for your sons from among their daughters, and their daughters will chase after their gods and lead your sons to do the same.

**17** You are not to make yourselves any molten gods.

**18** Keep the Festival of Unleavened Bread. For seven days eat unleavened bread, as I commanded you, at the appointed time in the month of Abib, for in the month of Abib you came out of Egypt.

**19** Everything that first opens the womb is mine, every male of your livestock, the firstborn of cattle and sheep.

**20** A firstborn donkey you must redeem with a lamb, and if you do not redeem it, you must break its neck. Redeem every firstborn among your sons. No one is to appear before me empty-handed.

**21** You may work for six days, but on the seventh day you must rest. Even at ploughing time and harvest time you must rest.

**22** Keep the Festival of Weeks with the first of the wheat harvest, and the Festival of Ingathering at the turn of the year.

**23** Three times a year all your men are to appear before the Lord, the LORD, the God of Israel.

**24** For I will drive out nations before you and extend your borders, and no one will covet your land when you go up three times a year to appear before the LORD your God.

**25** You are not to offer the blood of my sacrifice with anything leavened, and the sacrifice of the Passover festival must not be left until morning.

**26** Bring the best of the first produce of your soil to the house of the LORD your God. You are not to boil a young goat in its mother's milk."

**27** The LORD said to Moses, "Write these words down, for on the basis of these words I have made a covenant with you and with Israel."

**28** Moses was there with the LORD for forty days and forty nights. He ate no bread and drank no water. And he wrote on the tablets the words of the covenant, the Ten Words.

**29** When Moses came down from Mount Sinai with the two tablets of the Testimony in his hand, he did not know that the skin of his face was radiant because he had spoken with the LORD.

**30** Aaron and all the Israelites saw Moses, and the skin of his face was radiant, and they were afraid to come near him.

**31** But Moses called to them, and Aaron and all the leaders of the community came back to him, and Moses spoke with them.

**32** After that all the Israelites came near, and he gave them all the commands that the LORD had spoken to him on Mount Sinai.

**33** When Moses had finished speaking to them, he put a veil over his face.

**34** But whenever Moses went in before the LORD to speak with him, he took the veil off until he came out. When he came out and told the Israelites what he had been commanded,

**35** the Israelites saw that the skin of Moses' face was radiant. Then Moses would put the veil back over his face until he went in to speak with the LORD.

**9** It was square. They made the breastpiece folded double, a span long and a span wide when folded.

**10** They mounted on it four rows of stones. A row of carnelian, chrysolite and emerald was the first row;

**11** the second row, a turquoise, a lapis lazuli and a moonstone;

**12** the third row, a jacinth, an agate and an amethyst;

**13** and the fourth row, a topaz, an onyx and a jasper. They were enclosed in settings of gold.

**18** The other two ends of the two cords they fastened to the two settings, and so attached them to the shoulder pieces of the ephod at the front.

**19** They made two gold rings and put them on the two lower corners of the breastpiece, on its inner edge next to the ephod.

**20** They made two more gold rings and attached them to the lower part of the two shoulder pieces of the ephod, at the front, near its seam, above the waistband of the ephod.

**21** They tied the breastpiece by its rings to the rings of the ephod with a blue cord, so that it rested above the waistband of the ephod and did not swing loose from the ephod, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**22** He made the robe of the ephod, woven, entirely of blue.

**23** The opening of the robe in the middle of it was like the opening of a coat of mail, with a binding round the opening so that it would not tear.

**24** On the hem of the robe they made pomegranates of blue, purple and crimson yarn and fine twisted linen.

**25** They also made bells of pure gold, and put the bells between the pomegranates all round the hem of the robe,

**26** a bell and a pomegranate, a bell and a pomegranate, all round the hem of the robe for serving, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**27** They made the tunics, woven of fine linen, for Aaron and his sons,

**28** and the turban of fine linen, the headbands of fine linen, and the linen undergarments of fine twisted linen,

**29** and the sash of fine twisted linen and blue, purple and crimson yarn, embroidered, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**30** They made the plate of the sacred diadem of pure gold, and wrote on it an inscription like the engraving of a seal: "Holy \[H-QADOSH\] to the LORD."

**31** They tied a blue cord to it, to fasten it on the turban above, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**32** So all the work of the tabernacle of the tent of meeting was completed. The Israelites did everything just as the LORD had commanded Moses. They did it so.

**33** Then they brought the tabernacle to Moses, the tent and all its furnishings, its clasps, its frames, its bars, its posts and its bases;

**34** the covering of rams' skins dyed red, the covering of fine leather, and the curtain for screening;

**35** the ark of the Testimony with its poles, and the cover of atonement;

**36** the table with all its utensils, and the bread of the Presence;

**38** the golden altar, the anointing oil and the fragrant incense, and the screen for the entrance of the tent;

**39** the bronze altar with its bronze grating, its poles and all its utensils, the basin with its stand;

**40** the hangings of the courtyard with its posts and its bases, and the screen for the gate of the courtyard, its ropes and its pegs, and all the utensils for the service of the tabernacle, for the tent of meeting;

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **1:5** The Hebrew Masoretic Text gives seventy persons. The Septuagint gives seventy-five, a figure also found in Acts 7:14.
- **1:11** Pithom and Rameses were store-cities in the eastern Nile delta.
- **1:15** "Hebrew midwives" can mean midwives who were Hebrew, or midwives who served the Hebrews. The text does not say which.
- **1:16** "Birthstool" is literally "the two stones", a seat or stones on which a woman gave birth.
- **1:17** "Feared God" means they held God in reverent awe and put his command above the king's. See entry H-YARE.
- **2:3** The Hebrew word for the basket, *tevah*, is the same word used for Noah's ark.
- **2:10** Moses is an Egyptian name meaning "son" or "born of". The text links it to the Hebrew *mashah*, "to draw out".
- **2:18** Reuel is also called Jethro (3:1) and elsewhere Hobab. These may be two names for one man, or names of two related people.
- **2:22** Gershom sounds like the Hebrew for "a foreigner there".
- **2:24** "Covenant" is Hebrew *berit*. See entry H-BERIT.
- **3:1** Horeb is another name for Sinai, the "mountain of God".
- **3:5** "Holy" is Hebrew *qodesh*, "set apart for God". See entry H-QADOSH.
- **3:8** "A land flowing with milk and honey" describes a land of abundance.
- **3:14** The Hebrew *ehyeh asher ehyeh* can be read "I AM WHO I AM", "I WILL BE WHAT I WILL BE" or "I AM THE ONE WHO IS". It is connected with the divine name YHWH, rendered LORD. See entry H-YHWH.
- **3:22** "Strip the Egyptians" means the Israelites would take the goods they had earned by their unpaid labour. The Hebrew verb is related to plunder.
- **4:10** "Not a man of words" is literally "not a man of words, neither yesterday nor the day before".
- **4:21** "I will harden his heart" is the first of many statements about Pharaoh's hardened heart. The Hebrew uses three different verbs for it, and the text also says Pharaoh hardened his own heart.
- **4:24-26** This is one of the most obscure passages in the Torah. "His feet" is a polite way of saying a more private part of the body. The "bridegroom of blood" is explained by the circumcision.
- **5:1** "Festival" is Hebrew *chag*, a pilgrim feast, which fits Moses' request for a journey to worship.
- **5:6** The slave-drivers were Egyptian overseers. The foremen (Hebrew *shoterim*) were Israelites responsible for their own people's work.
- **5:21** "A stench in the nostrils" means they had become hateful to Pharaoh.
- **6:3** The words "I did not make myself known to them by my name" are debated, since the name YHWH is used in Genesis. They may mean the patriarchs did not know the full meaning of the name.
- **6:6** "Redeem" is Hebrew *ga'al*, the verb for a family member who buys back a relative from slavery or debt. See entry H-GAAL.
- **6:9** "Broken spirit" is literally "shortness of spirit" or "shortness of breath", from despair and exhaustion.
- **6:12** "Uncircumcised lips" means lips that are not fit to speak, an unusual figure for slowness of speech.
- **6:14-25** This list gives the line of Levi, down to Aaron and Moses. Such lists are selective, and generations are often skipped.
- **7:1** "As God to Pharaoh" means Moses speaks with God's authority before Pharaoh.
- **7:3** Three Hebrew verbs are used for the hardening: *chazaq* (to make firm), *kaved* (to make heavy) and *qashah* (to make hard). They are used both of God's act and of Pharaoh's own.
- **7:11** "Magicians" is Hebrew *chartummim*, a word borrowed from Egyptian that points to priests or scribes skilled in sacred writing.
- **7:12** The Hebrew word for the creature is *tannin*, which can mean a snake, a crocodile or a sea serpent. Moses' staff turns into a *nachash* in 4:3.
- **8:1** The numbering of chapter 8 differs in the Hebrew Bible. English 7:26-29 is Hebrew 8:1-4, so the English verse numbers in this chapter run four lower than the Hebrew in the early part of the chapter.
- **8:19** "The finger of God" is a figure for God's direct power. See also Luke 11:20.
- **8:22** "Set apart" is the same Hebrew verb used for making something distinct or holy.
- **8:26** The Israelites would offer animals the Egyptians held to be sacred, which is why Moses says their sacrifices would be "detestable" to them.
- **9:3** "The hand of the LORD" is a common figure for his direct action.
- **9:8** A kiln is a furnace used for firing bricks, which links the plague to the work the Israelites did.
- **9:16** "Kept you standing" means he had been allowed to remain, or had been raised up for this purpose.
- **9:31-32** The details about the crops fix the plague in the season of late winter, when flax and barley are ripening.
- **10:2** "Made fools of" is Hebrew *hit'alalti*, a verb that can mean to deal harshly with or to mock.
- **10:19** "Red Sea" is Hebrew *yam suf*, which more literally means the "Sea of Reeds". Its location is debated.
- **10:21** "A darkness that can be felt" is a Hebrew phrase meaning a darkness so thick it is almost touchable.
- **11:2** The Hebrew verb *sha'al* means "to ask" and can also mean "to demand" or "to borrow". The text does not say the Egyptians were forced.
- **11:7** "Not a dog will growl" means complete quiet in Israelite homes on the night of the plague.
- **12:2** The month is Abib (later called Nisan), in spring. The year of the religious calendar begins here.
- **12:5** "A year-old male" is literally "a son of a year".
- **12:6** "At twilight" is literally "between the two evenings", which may mean the time from afternoon to sunset.
- **12:11** "Passover" is Hebrew *pesach*. It may mean "pass over" or "protect". See entry H-PESACH.
- **12:12** "The gods of Egypt" means the plagues are also a judgement on Egypt's idols and gods.
- **12:22** Hyssop is a bushy plant with leafy stems, used for sprinkling.
- **12:23** "The destroyer" is a figure for the agent of death. The text says elsewhere that the LORD himself strikes (verse 29).
- **12:37** "600,000 men" translates Hebrew *eleph*, which can mean "thousand", "clan" or "military unit". The size of the group is debated.
- **12:38** "A mixed crowd" is a group of non-Israelites who joined the exodus.
- **12:40** The Septuagint and Samaritan Pentateuch include the years in Canaan in the 430 years. The Masoretic Text refers to the time in Egypt.
- **12:46** The instruction not to break any bones is quoted in John 19:36.
- **13:2** "Set apart" is Hebrew *qadash*, "make holy". The firstborn belong to God because of the exodus.
- **13:9** "A sign on your hand and a reminder between your eyes" is a figure of speech. Later Jewish practice took it literally with the tefillin.
- **13:17** "The road through the land of the Philistines" is the coastal route to Canaan.
- **13:18** "Equipped for battle" translates a rare Hebrew word, *chamushim*, which may mean "armed", "in five groups" or "in orderly ranks".
- **14:2** Pi-hahiroth, Migdol and Baal-zephon were places in the eastern delta whose exact location is unknown.
- **14:21** The strong east wind is a natural means, but the text credits God with it.
- **14:24** The morning watch was the last part of the night, roughly from two to six in the morning.
- **15:1** "Triumphed gloriously" is Hebrew *ga'oh ga'ah*, a repeated verb that means "rose up high".
- **15:2** "Salvation" is Hebrew *yeshu'ah*, from the same root as the name Joshua and Jesus. See entry H-YESHUA.
- **15:8** "The blast of your nostrils" is a figure for the breath or wind of God.
- **15:13** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED.
- **15:20** Miriam is called a prophet, one of the few women with this title in the Old Testament.
- **15:25** "A rule and a ruling" translates Hebrew *choq u-mishpat*, a phrase meaning a regulation and a decision.
- **15:26** "Healer" is Hebrew *rofe*. It means one who restores health and wholeness.
- **16:1** The wilderness of Sin is a place name and is not related to the English word "sin".
- **16:3** The people remember the food of Egypt and forget the slavery. The complaint shows how quickly they lost trust.
- **16:4** "Bread from heaven" is a test of obedience in the daily gathering.
- **16:7** "Glory" is Hebrew *kavod*, the weight or splendour of God made visible.
- **16:13** Quails are small migrating birds that cross the Sinai in spring.
- **16:15** "What is it?" is Hebrew *man hu*. The word *man* gives the name manna. The Hebrew may also mean "it is a gift".
- **16:16** An omer is about 2.2 litres (roughly four pints).
- **16:23** "A day of complete rest" is Hebrew *shabbaton*, linked to the word *shabbat*. See entry H-SHABBAT.
- **16:31** Coriander seed is small, round and pale. "Manna" is described here by taste and appearance.
- **16:34** "The testimony" means the tablets of the covenant. The text is looking ahead to the ark, which has not yet been built.
- **16:35** Manna stopped when Israel ate the produce of the land (Joshua 5:12).
- **16:36** An ephah is about 22 litres, so an omer is a tenth of that.
- **17:1** Rephidim was somewhere in the southern Sinai. Its location is uncertain.
- **17:7** Massah means "testing" and Meribah means "quarrel". The pair of names recalls this event in later texts.
- **17:9** This is the first appearance of Joshua. He is introduced as a military leader.
- **17:14** "A scroll" is Hebrew *sefer*, meaning a book or written record.
- **17:15** The Hebrew name is *YHWH nissi*, "the LORD is my banner".
- **17:16** The Hebrew is difficult. Literally it reads "a hand on the throne of Yah". Some read "throne" (*kes*) as "banner" (*nes*), which would make the verse refer back to the altar's name.
- **18:1** Jethro is the priest of Midian. He is called Reuel in 2:18.
- **18:4** Eliezer means "my God is help".
- **18:12** "To eat bread before God" describes a meal that sealed a covenant or friendship.
- **18:19** "The people's representative" is literally "for the people towards God".
- **18:21** The divisions of thousands, hundreds, fifties and tens are military and civil units.
- **19:1** The third month is Sivan, about May or June. The arrival at Sinai was probably some six weeks after the exodus.
- **19:4** "On eagles' wings" is a figure for swift, strong and protective care.
- **19:5** "Treasured possession" is Hebrew *segullah*, a prized possession kept for one's own use, such as a king's own treasure.
- **19:6** "A kingdom of priests" means all Israel would serve God and represent him to the nations. "Holy" is Hebrew *qadosh*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **19:9** "In the thick of a cloud" speaks of God's presence being veiled.
- **19:10** "Consecrate" is Hebrew *qadash*, to set apart for God.
- **19:13** The "ram's horn" (Hebrew *yovel*) is the instrument whose name was later given to the Jubilee.
- **19:15** Abstaining from sexual relations was a form of ritual preparation, and is not a judgement against marriage.
- **19:22** "Break out against" is Hebrew *parats*, a word for a sudden and violent outbreak.
- **20:2** Jewish tradition counts this verse as the first commandment. Christian traditions generally count the commandments differently.
- **20:3** "In my presence" is literally "before my face". It can also be read "besides me" or "in preference to me".
- **20:4** "Carved image" is Hebrew *pesel*, an idol cut or shaped from wood or stone.
- **20:5** "Jealous" is Hebrew *qanna*, a word for passionate exclusive devotion, like the jealousy of a spouse.
- **20:6** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. "The thousandth generation" stands against the third and fourth generation, to show God's love outlasts his judgement.
- **20:7** "For a worthless purpose" is Hebrew *la-shav*, "to emptiness". It covers false oaths and any careless or deceitful use of the name.
- **20:8** Deuteronomy 5:12 has "Observe" in place of "Remember".
- **20:12** "Honour" is Hebrew *kabbed*, "to give weight" to someone.
- **20:13** "Murder" is Hebrew *ratsach*, the unlawful killing of a person. It is not the word used for killing in war or for lawful execution.
- **20:17** "Covet" is Hebrew *chamad*, which can mean to desire to the point of taking.
- **20:18** "Saw the thunder" is a Hebrew figure meaning they saw and heard the storm together.
- **20:24** An altar of earth is an altar of simple, uncut material.
- **20:26** The command about steps may have been to avoid exposure and to separate worship from practices at other shrines.
- **21:1** "Rulings" is Hebrew *mishpatim*, the name later given to this section, the "book of the covenant".
- **21:2** "Hebrew slave" may mean an Israelite who sold himself to pay a debt. This is not chattel slavery of the kind known later.
- **21:6** "Before God" is Hebrew *el ha-elohim*, which can mean to the judges, or to the place of worship.
- **21:8** "Dealt unfairly" means he has broken faith with her.
- **21:13** "God let it happen into his hand" describes an accident.
- **21:22** The Hebrew is difficult. "Her children come out" may describe a premature birth or a miscarriage, and "further harm" may refer to the woman or the child.
- **21:24** "Eye for eye" is known as the law of retaliation. It limits punishment to the harm done and is not a licence for revenge.
- **21:32** Thirty shekels was the price of a slave.
- **22:2** The law distinguishes a night break-in, when the owner cannot tell the thief's intent, from a break-in by day.
- **22:8** "Before God" means before the judges or at the sanctuary.
- **22:16** "Bride-price" is Hebrew *mohar*, a payment from the groom's family.
- **22:18** "Sorceress" is Hebrew *mekhashefah*, a woman who practised magic. Such practices were forbidden as a rejection of God.
- **22:25** "Interest" is Hebrew *neshekh*, "a bite".
- **22:28** "God" here is Hebrew *elohim*, which can also mean "judges". The two readings are both possible.
- **23:11** This is the sabbatical year, when the land lay fallow every seventh year.
- **23:15** Abib is the month of spring barley, later called Nisan.
- **23:16** The Festival of the Harvest is also called Weeks, and the Festival of the Ingathering is also called Booths.
- **23:19** The ban on boiling a young goat in its mother's milk may reject a Canaanite ritual practice. It is later used as the basis of Jewish dietary laws.
- **23:20** "My name is in him" means the angel carries God's own authority.
- **23:28** "Hornets" (Hebrew *tsir'ah*) may be actual insects, or a figure for panic or discouragement.
- **23:31** "The River" is the Euphrates.
- **24:1** Nadab and Abihu are Aaron's two eldest sons. Their death is told in Leviticus 10.
- **24:4** The twelve pillars stand for the twelve tribes.
- **24:7** "The book of the covenant" probably means the laws of chapters 20 to 23.
- **24:8** The sprinkling of blood seals the covenant, as in Genesis 15 and in later sacrifice.
- **24:10** "Sapphire" is Hebrew *sappir*, usually taken to be the blue stone lapis lazuli.
- **24:11** "Did not lay his hand" means they were not harmed by seeing God.
- **24:18** Forty days and forty nights is a round period, a time of testing or preparation.
- **25:2** "Contribution" is Hebrew *terumah*, something lifted up and set apart for God.
- **25:4** The blue, purple and crimson yarns were dyed wool or linen. Blue and purple dyes were costly and were associated with royalty.
- **25:5** "Acacia wood" is a hard, durable desert wood. The meaning of "fine leather" (Hebrew *tachash*) is uncertain. It has been read as dugong, porpoise or a kind of dyed leather.
- **25:7** The ephod is a priestly garment, described in 28:6-14. Its exact shape is debated.
- **25:8** "Sanctuary" is Hebrew *miqdash*, a holy place. "Live" is Hebrew *shakan*, related to the word *mishkan*, "tabernacle", and to the later word *shekinah*.
- **25:10** A cubit is about 45 centimetres (18 inches). The ark was about 110 centimetres long and 70 centimetres wide and high.
- **25:16** "The Testimony" (Hebrew *edut*) means the tablets of the covenant and by extension the ark that holds them.
- **25:17** "Cover of atonement" is Hebrew *kapporet*, from the verb *kipper*, "to atone, to cover". It is often rendered "mercy seat". See entry H-KAPPORET.
- **25:18** The cherubim are winged guardian figures. Their form is not described in detail.
- **25:30** "The bread of the Presence" is Hebrew *lechem panim*, literally "bread of the face", the bread set out before God.
- **25:31** The lampstand is known as the menorah. Its details are described again in Exodus 37.
- **25:39** A talent is about 34 kilograms.
- **26:1** "Tabernacle" is Hebrew *mishkan*, "dwelling place".
- **26:33** The curtain divides the holy place from the most holy place, where the ark stood.
- **27:2** The horns are projections at the corners of the altar. They were touched with blood in the rites of atonement.
- **27:21** "Tent of meeting" is Hebrew *ohel mo'ed*, the tent where God met his people.
- **28:3** "A spirit of wisdom" means skill and understanding as a gift from God.
- **28:15** "Breastpiece for decisions" is Hebrew *choshen mishpat*, a pouch worn on the chest.
- **28:17-20** The names of the twelve stones are uncertain, and ancient and modern identifications differ.
- **28:30** "Urim and Thummim" (see entry H-URIM) are objects used to receive God's answers. Their form and use are unknown. The names may mean "lights" and "perfections", or may play on the words for "cursed" and "perfect".
- **28:36** "Holy to the LORD" is engraved on the plate worn by the high priest.
- **28:38** "Carry the guilt" means the high priest took on the task of dealing with the people's offences in the holy gifts.
- **29:1** The Hebrew verb for installing a priest is *mille yad*, "fill the hand".
- **29:6** "Diadem" is Hebrew *nezer*, a sign of consecration.
- **29:14** "Sin offering" is Hebrew *chatta't*, an offering that cleanses a person or place from impurity or wrongdoing.
- **29:18** "Pleasing aroma" is Hebrew *reach nichoach*, the smell that is accepted by God. "Food offering" translates *ishsheh*, whose meaning is uncertain.
- **29:24** "Elevation offering" is Hebrew *tenufah*, often rendered "wave offering". The action may be raising or presenting the portion before God.
- **29:36** "Atonement" is Hebrew *kippur*, to cover or cleanse.
- **30:12** "Ransom" is Hebrew *kofer*, a payment to redeem a life.
- **30:13** A shekel is about 11 grams. A gerah is a twentieth of a shekel.
- **30:23** Myrrh, cinnamon, cane and cassia are fragrant plant products. Some identifications are uncertain.
- **30:34** The identification of onycha and galbanum is uncertain.
- **31:3** Bezalel is the first person in the Bible described as filled with the Spirit of God, here for craftsmanship. See entry H-RUACH.
- **31:13** The Sabbath is described as a sign of the covenant and a mark of the one who sanctifies Israel. See entry H-SHABBAT.
- **31:15** "Complete rest" is Hebrew *shabbat shabbaton*.
- **31:18** "The finger of God" means God's own act in writing the tablets.
- **32:1** "Gods" is Hebrew *elohim*, which can be plural ("gods") or singular ("God"). The verbs here are plural, though the calf may have been meant to represent the LORD himself (verse 5).
- **32:4** "Worked it with an engraving tool" is one reading of a difficult phrase. It can also be read "tied it up in a bag".
- **32:5** Aaron announces a festival "for the LORD", which suggests the calf was meant as a pedestal or image of the LORD, and not another god.
- **32:6** "Revel" (Hebrew *tsachaq*) means to play or laugh. In context it may include drunken or sexual revelry.
- **32:10** "Let me be" shows that Moses' intercession could change God's course.
- **32:12** "Change your mind" is Hebrew *nacham*, which can mean to relent or to have compassion. See also verse 14.
- **32:20** Making the people drink the powdered calf has been compared to the ordeal in Numbers 5:11-31.
- **32:26** The Levites' response sets them apart for service (see Deuteronomy 33:8-11).
- **32:29** "Set apart" is literally "fill your hands", the phrase used for installing a priest (see Exodus 29:1).
- **32:32** "The book you have written" is the register of the people of God. See also Psalm 69:28.
- **33:2** The angel is the same figure as in Exodus 23:20.
- **33:4** The ornaments were signs of festival and honour. Taking them off was a sign of mourning.
- **33:7** This tent of meeting stood outside the camp and was temporary. It is different from the tabernacle described in chapters 25 to 31.
- **33:11** "Face to face" describes the closeness of the LORD with Moses, not a physical sight (see verse 20).
- **33:14** "My presence" is Hebrew *panai*, "my face".
- **33:18** "Glory" is Hebrew *kavod*, the weight and splendour of God.
- **33:19** The LORD declares his name and his freedom to show grace and compassion as he chooses.
- **33:23** "My back" is a figure for the traces of God's presence after he has passed.
- **34:6-7** The LORD describes himself in a formula repeated in many later texts. "Rich in faithful love and truth" is Hebrew *rav hesed ve-emet*. See entries H-HESED and H-EMET.
- **34:7** The statement about children bearing the guilt of parents is balanced elsewhere by Deuteronomy 24:16 and Ezekiel 18.
- **34:13** "Sacred poles" is Hebrew *asherim*, wooden poles linked with the goddess Asherah.
- **34:14** "Jealous" is Hebrew *qanna*. See Exodus 20:5.
- **34:15** "Chase after" is Hebrew *zanah*, which literally means to prostitute oneself. It is a regular figure for unfaithful worship.
- **34:22** The Festival of Weeks is also called Pentecost, fifty days after Passover.
- **34:28** "The Ten Words" is Hebrew *aseret ha-devarim*, the origin of the name "Decalogue".
- **34:29** "Radiant" is Hebrew *qaran*, "to send out rays". A Latin translation took it as "horned", which is why Moses is sometimes shown with horns in art.
- **34:33-35** The veil was put on when Moses was not speaking God's word, so that the people were not afraid. See 2 Corinthians 3:7-18.
- **35:3** The ban on lighting a fire makes the rest of the Sabbath practical: no cooking and no work in the home.
- **35:5** "Contribution" is Hebrew *terumah*, as in 25:2.
- **35:22** "Offering" in this verse translates *terumah*. The gold included jewellery freely given.
- **35:25** Skilled women who spun the yarn contributed to the work as much as the men who built it.
- **35:30** "Called by name" repeats the wording of 31:2.
- **36:3** The freewill offerings were so generous that they had to be stopped (verses 5-7).
- **36:8** The making of the tabernacle in chapters 36 to 39 follows the instructions in chapters 25 to 31. Differences between the two accounts are small.
- **37:1** Bezalel personally makes the ark, the most sacred object.
- **37:25** The altar of incense is described here after the other furniture, in the order of the making, rather than in the order of the instructions.
- **38:8** The bronze mirrors were polished metal. The women "who served" gave them up for the basin. The Hebrew word for "served" is *tsava*, to serve in a group.
- **38:21** Ithamar, Aaron's younger son, oversaw the accounts. The Levites later carry the tabernacle (Numbers 4).
- **38:24** A talent is about 34 kilograms, so twenty-nine talents of gold is about a tonne.
- **38:26** A beka is a half shekel. The total of 603,550 men matches Numbers 1:46.
- **39:1** The sacred garments follow the description in chapter 28.
- **39:32** "Completed" echoes the language of creation in Genesis 2:1-3.
- **39:43** Moses inspects the work and blesses the people. The wording echoes God's view of creation in Genesis 1:31 and 2:3.
- **40:2** The first day of the first month is about a year after leaving Egypt (see verse 17).
- **40:34** "The glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle" marks the completion of the book and the beginning of God's dwelling among his people.
- **40:36-38** The cloud guides Israel's journeys. The wording points forward to the book of Numbers, which begins the travels from Sinai.
