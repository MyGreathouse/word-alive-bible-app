# WABV: Nehemiah

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-QADOSH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Nehemiah was one book with Ezra in the Hebrew Bible, and the wording of the shared lists and passages follows the WABV Ezra.*

## Nehemiah 1-3: The Call to Rebuild the Wall

### Chapter 1

**1** The words of Nehemiah son of Hacaliah. In the month of Chislev, in the twentieth year, when I was in Susa the citadel,

**2** Hanani, one of my brothers, came with some men from Judah. I asked them about the Jews who had escaped, who were left from the captivity, and about Jerusalem.

**3** They said to me, "Those who are left from the captivity there in the province are in great trouble and disgrace. The wall of Jerusalem is broken down, and its gates have been burned with fire."

**4** When I heard these words, I sat down and wept and mourned for days. I fasted and prayed before the God of heaven.

**5** I said: "LORD, God of heaven, the great and awesome God, who keeps covenant \[H-BERIT\] and faithful love \[H-HESED\] with those who love him and keep his commands,

**6** let your ear be attentive and your eyes open to hear the prayer of your servant that I am praying before you now, day and night, for your servants the people of Israel, confessing the sins of the people of Israel that we have committed against you. Both I and my father's house have sinned.

**7** We have acted very wickedly against you and have not kept the commands, the statutes and the rules that you gave to your servant Moses.

**8** Remember the word that you gave to your servant Moses: 'If you are unfaithful, I will scatter you among the peoples,

**9** but if you turn back \[H-SHUV\] to me and keep my commands and do them, even if your scattered people are at the farthest edge of the heavens, I will gather them from there and bring them to the place that I have chosen as a home for my name.'

**10** They are your servants and your people, whom you redeemed by your great power and your strong hand.

**11** Lord, let your ear be attentive to the prayer of your servant and to the prayer of your servants who delight to fear your name, and give success to your servant today, and grant him compassion before this man." I was cupbearer to the king.

### Chapter 2

**1** In the month of Nisan, in the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes, when wine was in front of him, I took the wine and gave it to the king. I had never been sad in his presence before.

**2** The king said to me, "Why is your face sad, when you are not ill? This can only be sadness of heart." Then I was very afraid.

**3** I said to the king, "May the king live for ever. Why should my face not be sad, when the city of my ancestors' graves lies in ruins and its gates have been burned with fire?"

**4** The king said to me, "What are you asking for?" So I prayed to the God of heaven.

**5** Then I said to the king, "If it pleases the king, and if your servant has found favour with you, send me to Judah, to the city of my ancestors' graves, so that I may rebuild it."

**6** The king, with the queen sitting beside him, said to me, "How long will your journey take, and when will you come back?" It pleased the king to send me, and I gave him a time.

**7** I also said to the king, "If it pleases the king, let letters be given to me for the governors of the province Beyond the River, so that they will let me pass until I reach Judah,

**8** and a letter to Asaph, keeper of the king's forest, so that he will give me timber to make beams for the gates of the fortress of the temple, and for the wall of the city, and for the house that I will live in." The king gave me what I asked, for the good hand of my God was on me.

**9** Then I came to the governors of the province Beyond the River and gave them the king's letters. The king had also sent army officers and horsemen with me.

**10** But when Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite official heard this, they were very displeased that someone had come to seek the good of the people of Israel.

**11** I came to Jerusalem and was there for three days.

**12** Then I got up in the night, I and a few men with me. I told no one what my God had put in my heart to do for Jerusalem. There was no animal with me except the one I was riding.

**13** I went out at night by the Valley Gate towards the Dragon Spring and the Dung Gate and inspected the walls of Jerusalem that were broken down and its gates that had been burned with fire.

**14** Then I went on to the Fountain Gate and the King's Pool, but there was no room for the animal under me to pass.

**15** So I went up in the night by the valley and inspected the wall, and I turned back and came in by the Valley Gate, and returned.

**16** The officials did not know where I had gone or what I was doing, and I had not yet told the Jews, the priests, the nobles, the officials or the rest who were to do the work.

**17** Then I said to them, "You see the trouble we are in, that Jerusalem lies in ruins and its gates are burned with fire. Come, let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem, so that we may no longer be a disgrace."

**18** I told them how the good hand of my God had been on me, and also what the king had said to me. They said, "Let us get up and build." So they strengthened their hands for the good work.

**19** But when Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite official and Geshem the Arab heard of it, they mocked us and despised us and said, "What is this that you are doing? Are you rebelling against the king?"

**20** I answered them, "The God of heaven will give us success. We, his servants, will get up and build, but you have no share or claim or memorial in Jerusalem."

### Chapter 3

**27** After them the Tekoites repaired another section opposite the great projecting tower as far as the wall of Ophel.

**28** Above the Horse Gate the priests repaired, each opposite his own house.

**29** After them Zadok son of Immer repaired opposite his own house. After him Shemaiah son of Shecaniah, keeper of the East Gate, repaired.

**30** After him Hananiah son of Shelemiah and Hanun the sixth son of Zalaph repaired another section. After him Meshullam son of Berechiah repaired opposite his room.

**31** After him Malchijah, one of the goldsmiths, repaired as far as the house of the temple servants and the merchants, opposite the Muster Gate, and as far as the upper room at the corner.

**32** Between the upper room at the corner and the Sheep Gate the goldsmiths and the merchants repaired.

## Nehemiah 4-6: Opposition, Debts and the Finished Wall

### Chapter 4

**1** When Sanballat heard that we were rebuilding the wall, he was angry and greatly enraged, and he mocked the Jews.

**2** In front of his associates and the army of Samaria he said, "What are these feeble Jews doing? Will they restore it for themselves? Will they offer sacrifices? Will they finish in a day? Will they bring the stones back to life from the heaps of rubble, burned as they are?"

**3** Tobiah the Ammonite was beside him and said, "Whatever they build, if a fox climbed on it, it would break down their stone wall."

**4** Hear, our God, for we are despised. Turn their taunts back on their own heads and hand them over to be plundered in a land of captivity.

**5** Do not cover their guilt, and do not let their sin be wiped out from before you, for they have insulted the builders.

**6** So we rebuilt the wall, and the whole wall was joined together to half its height, for the people had a mind to work.

**7** But when Sanballat and Tobiah and the Arabs and the Ammonites and the people of Ashdod heard that the repair of the walls of Jerusalem was moving ahead and that the gaps were beginning to be closed, they were very angry.

**8** They all plotted together to come and fight against Jerusalem and to cause confusion in it.

**9** But we prayed to our God and set a guard against them day and night because of them.

**10** In Judah it was said: "The strength of the carriers is failing, and there is so much rubble that we cannot rebuild the wall."

**11** Our enemies said, "They will not know or see anything until we are among them and kill them and stop the work."

**12** When the Jews who lived near them came, they told us ten times, "From every place you turn, they are against us."

**13** So I stationed people in the lowest places behind the wall, in the open places, by families, with their swords, spears and bows.

**14** I looked, and stood up and said to the nobles, the officials and the rest of the people, "Do not be afraid of them. Remember the Lord, who is great and awesome, and fight for your relatives, your sons and daughters, your wives and your homes."

**15** When our enemies heard that we knew about it and that God had frustrated their plan, we all went back to the wall, each to his work.

**16** From that day half of my young men did the work, while half held the spears, shields, bows and body armour. The officers stood behind the whole house of Judah,

**17** who were building the wall. The carriers took their loads in such a way that each worked with one hand and held a weapon with the other.

**18** Each of the builders wore his sword at his side as he built. The man who sounded the trumpet stayed beside me.

**19** I said to the nobles, the officials and the rest of the people, "The work is large and spread out, and we are separated on the wall, far from one another.

**20** Wherever you hear the sound of the trumpet, gather to us there. Our God will fight for us."

**21** So we carried on with the work, with half of them holding the spears from the first light of dawn until the stars came out.

**22** At that time I also said to the people, "Let every man and his servant spend the night inside Jerusalem, so that they may be a guard for us by night and workers by day."

**23** So neither I nor my relatives nor my servants nor the guards who followed me took off our clothes. Each kept his weapon in his right hand.

### Chapter 5

**1** There was a great outcry from the people and their wives against their fellow Jews.

**2** Some said, "We and our sons and daughters are many. We must get grain so that we can eat and stay alive."

**3** Others said, "We are mortgaging our fields, our vineyards and our houses to get grain in the famine."

**4** Still others said, "We have borrowed money against our fields and vineyards to pay the king's tax.

**5** Now our flesh is the same as the flesh of our relatives, and our children are the same as their children. Yet we are bringing our sons and daughters into slavery, and some of our daughters are already enslaved. We are powerless, for our fields and vineyards belong to others."

**6** I was very angry when I heard their outcry and these words.

**7** I thought it over, and I accused the nobles and the officials: "You are charging interest, each one to his own relative." I called a large assembly against them

**8** and said to them, "As far as we could, we bought back our fellow Jews who had been sold to the nations. Now you are selling your own relatives, so that they can be sold to us." They were silent and could not find a word to say.

**9** So I said, "What you are doing is not good. Should you not walk in the fear of our God, to avoid the taunts of the nations who are our enemies?

**10** I too, with my relatives and my servants, am lending them money and grain. Let us stop this charging of interest.

**11** Give back to them today their fields, their vineyards, their olive groves and their houses, and the percentage of the money, grain, new wine and oil that you have been charging them."

**12** They said, "We will give it back and ask nothing more from them. We will do as you say." Then I called the priests and made them swear to do what they had promised.

**13** I also shook out the fold of my robe and said, "In the same way may God shake out of his house and out of his property everyone who does not keep this promise. May he be shaken out and emptied." All the assembly said, "Amen," and praised the LORD. And the people did as they had promised.

**14** Also, from the time that I was appointed their governor in the land of Judah, from the twentieth year to the thirty-second year of King Artaxerxes, twelve years, neither I nor my relatives ate the governor's food allowance.

**15** The earlier governors before me had put heavy burdens on the people and had taken from them food and wine, besides forty shekels of silver. Their servants also lorded it over the people. But I did not do this, because of the fear of God.

**16** I also devoted myself to the work on this wall, and we did not buy any land, and all my servants were gathered there for the work.

**17** Also there were a hundred and fifty Jews and officials at my table, besides those who came to us from the nations round us.

**18** What was prepared each day at my expense was one ox and six choice sheep, and birds were prepared for me, and every ten days all kinds of wine in large amounts. Yet with all this I did not ask for the governor's food allowance, because the work was so heavy on this people.

**19** Remember me for good, my God, for all that I have done for this people.

### Chapter 6

**1** When Sanballat, Tobiah, Geshem the Arab and the rest of our enemies heard that I had rebuilt the wall and that no gap was left in it (though until then I had not set up doors in the gates),

**2** Sanballat and Geshem sent me this message: "Come, let us meet together in one of the villages in the plain of Ono." But they intended to harm me.

**3** So I sent messengers to them with this answer: "I am doing a great work and cannot come down. Why should the work stop while I leave it and come down to you?"

**4** They sent me the same message four times, and I answered them in the same way.

**5** The fifth time Sanballat sent his servant to me with the same message, and in his hand was an open letter.

**6** In it was written: "It is reported among the nations, and Geshem says it too, that you and the Jews are planning to rebel, and that is why you are rebuilding the wall. According to these reports you are to be their king.

**7** You have also appointed prophets \[H-NAVI\] to announce about you in Jerusalem, 'There is a king in Judah.' Now the king will hear of these reports. So come, let us take counsel together."

**8** Then I sent him this answer: "Nothing like what you say has happened. You are making it up out of your own heart."

**9** For they all wanted to frighten us, thinking, "Their hands will drop from the work, and it will not be done." But now, God, make my hands strong.

**10** I went to the house of Shemaiah son of Delaiah, son of Mehetabel, who was shut in at his home. He said, "Let us meet in the house of God, inside the temple, and let us shut the doors of the temple, for they are coming to kill you. They are coming to kill you at night."

**11** But I said, "Should a man like me run away? And would someone like me go into the temple to save his life? I will not go in."

**12** I saw that God had not sent him, but that he had spoken this prophecy against me because Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him.

**13** He had been hired so that I would be afraid and do this and sin, and so that they could give me a bad name to mock me with.

**14** Remember Tobiah and Sanballat, my God, for what they did, and also the prophet Noadiah and the other prophets who tried to make me afraid.

**15** The wall was finished on the twenty-fifth day of Elul, in fifty-two days.

**16** When all our enemies heard of it, all the nations round us were afraid and lost their confidence, for they saw that this work had been done with the help of our God.

**17** In those days the nobles of Judah also sent many letters to Tobiah, and Tobiah's letters came to them.

**18** For many in Judah were bound by oath to him, because he was the son-in-law of Shecaniah son of Arah, and his son Jehohanan had married the daughter of Meshullam son of Berechiah.

**19** They also spoke of his good deeds in my presence and reported my words to him. And Tobiah sent letters to make me afraid.

### Chapter 8

**1** All the people gathered as one in the square in front of the Water Gate. They told Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the law \[H-TORAH\] of Moses that the LORD had commanded Israel.

**2** So Ezra the priest brought the law before the assembly, of men and women and everyone who could understand what they heard, on the first day of the seventh month.

**3** He read from it in the square in front of the Water Gate from first light until midday, in the presence of the men and women and those who could understand. All the people were listening to the book of the law.

**4** Ezra the scribe stood on a wooden platform that had been made for the purpose. Beside him on his right stood Mattithiah, Shema, Anaiah, Uriah, Hilkiah and Maaseiah, and on his left Pedaiah, Mishael, Malchijah, Hashum, Hashbaddanah, Zechariah and Meshullam.

**5** Ezra opened the book in the sight of all the people, for he was above them all, and as he opened it all the people stood up.

**6** Ezra blessed the LORD, the great God, and all the people answered, "Amen, Amen," lifting up their hands. Then they bowed down and worshipped the LORD with their faces to the ground.

**7** The Levites Jeshua, Bani, Sherebiah, Jamin, Akkub, Shabbethai, Hodiah, Maaseiah, Kelita, Azariah, Jozabad, Hanan and Pelaiah helped the people to understand the law, while the people stood in their places.

**8** They read from the book, from the law of God, making it clear and giving the sense, so that the people understood what was read.

**9** Nehemiah the governor, and Ezra the priest and scribe, and the Levites who taught the people said to all the people, "This day is holy \[H-QADOSH\] to the LORD your God. Do not mourn or weep." For all the people were weeping as they heard the words of the law.

**10** He said to them, "Go and eat rich food and drink sweet drinks, and send portions to those who have nothing prepared, for this day is holy to our Lord. Do not be sad, for the joy of the LORD is your strength."

**11** So the Levites calmed all the people, saying, "Be quiet, for this day is holy. Do not be sad."

**12** All the people went to eat and drink and send portions and to celebrate with great joy, because they had understood the words that were made known to them.

**31** and provided for the wood offering at set times and for the first fruits. Remember me for good, my God.

The exclusion of the Ammonite and Moabite (13:1-3, from Deuteronomy 23:3-5), the beating of men who had married foreign women and the pulling out of their hair (13:25), the casting out of Tobiah's belongings (13:8) and the driving away of the priest's son (13:28) are rendered as written, with no softening and no comment on the ethics. The footnotes explain the language and link the passages to Ezra 9 and 10 and Nehemiah 10. Nehemiah's closing prayers (13:14, 22, 29, 31) are rendered as written. The text of 13:6 calls Artaxerxes "king of Babylon". The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **Title** Nehemiah was one book with Ezra in the Hebrew Bible. It is built on Nehemiah's own first-person memoir, with lists and documents. It tells of his coming to Jerusalem in 445 BCE (the twentieth year of Artaxerxes I), the rebuilding of the wall in fifty-two days, the reading of the law, the covenant, and his reforms. He was governor of Judah for two periods (5:14; 13:6).
- **1:1** "The words of Nehemiah son of Hacaliah" is the heading of the memoir. "Chislev" is the ninth month, in November and December. "The twentieth year" is of King Artaxerxes (2:1), 445 BCE. "Susa the citadel" is the Persian winter capital, Hebrew *ha-birah*.
- **1:2** "Hanani, one of my brothers" is probably his actual brother (see 7:2). "The Jews who had escaped, who were left from the captivity" means the survivors of the exile.
- **1:3** "The wall of Jerusalem is broken down, and its gates have been burned with fire" may refer to the destruction of 587 BCE or to a recent damage, perhaps the result of the stop in Ezra 4:23.
- **1:4** "I sat down and wept and mourned for days. I fasted and prayed" shows his grief. "The God of heaven" is the title of the LORD in the Persian period (see Ezra 1:2).
- **1:5** "Keeps covenant and faithful love" is Hebrew *shomer ha-berit ve-ha-hesed*. See entries H-BERIT and H-HESED. The words are from Deuteronomy 7:9 and Daniel 9:4.
- **1:6** "Both I and my father's house have sinned" is the confession of Nehemiah for himself and his family.
- **1:8** "If you are unfaithful, I will scatter you among the peoples" is a free quotation of Moses (Leviticus 26:33; Deuteronomy 4:27; 30:1-5). "Unfaithful" is Hebrew *ma'al*.
- **1:9** "If you turn back to me" is Hebrew *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV. "A home for my name" is the place the LORD chose (Deuteronomy 12:5).
- **1:11** "This man" is the king. "Cupbearer" is Hebrew *mashqeh*, a trusted official who served the king's wine.
- **2:1** "Nisan" is the first month, in April. The request is four months after Chislev. "I had never been sad in his presence before" says that it was unusual.
- **2:2** "Sadness of heart" is the king's reading of Nehemiah's face. Nehemiah was "very afraid", for sadness before the king could be dangerous.
- **2:4** "I prayed to the God of heaven" is a short prayer in the king's presence.
- **2:6** "The queen sitting beside him" uses the Hebrew word *shegal*, a rare word for queen or royal wife. "I gave him a time" is the length of leave. In 13:6 Nehemiah returns in the thirty-second year.
- **2:8** "Asaph, keeper of the king's forest" translates Hebrew *pardes*, a Persian word that means a royal park. "The fortress of the temple" is Hebrew *ha-birah*, a fortress by the temple. "The house that I will live in" is his house as governor.
- **2:9** "The king had also sent army officers and horsemen with me" is in contrast to Ezra's refusal of an escort (Ezra 8:22).
- **2:10** "Sanballat the Horonite" is the governor of Samaria. A letter from the Jews at Elephantine in 407 BCE names a Sanballat as governor of Samaria. "Tobiah the Ammonite official" is Hebrew "the servant, the Ammonite", and may be a title ("servant of the king"). He is an opponent throughout the book.
- **2:12** "What my God had put in my heart" is Nehemiah's sense of a call.
- **2:13** "The Valley Gate" and "the Dung Gate" are gates on the south and west of the city. "The Dragon Spring" is a rare name, perhaps "the spring of the jackal" or "of the serpent". It is not identified.
- **2:14** "The Fountain Gate and the King's Pool" are in the south-east. There was no room for the animal to pass through the rubble.
- **2:19** "Geshem the Arab" is called Gashmu in 6:6. He was a ruler of the Arab tribe of Qedar, known from inscriptions. "Are you rebelling against the king?" is the same charge as in Ezra 4:12-16.
- **2:20** "You have no share or claim or memorial in Jerusalem" is a refusal of any part in the city. The three Hebrew words are *helek*, *tsedaqah* and *zikkaron*. "Claim" translates *tsedaqah*, which here means a legal right.
- **3:1** "Eliashib the high priest" is the grandson of Jeshua (12:10). "They made it holy" is Hebrew *qiddeshuhu*. See entry H-QADOSH. The Sheep Gate is the first section of the wall, on the north-east, near the temple. "The Tower of the Hundred" and "the Tower of Hananel" are towers on the north wall.
- **3:2** The list of the builders (verses 2 to 32) goes round the wall to the left, from the Sheep Gate through the Fish Gate and the west and south to the Water Gate and the east, and back to the Sheep Gate. The numerous place names are given as in the Hebrew, and many of them cannot be located.
- **3:5** "Their nobles would not bend their necks to the work of their lords" is Hebrew *lo hevi'u tsavvaram be-avodat adonehem*. "Their lords" may be the foremen, or it may be the LORD (*adonai*). The Tekoites repaired a second section (verse 27).
- **3:6** "The Old Gate" is Hebrew *sha'ar ha-yeshanah*. It may also be read "the Gate of the Old City" or "the Mishneh Gate".
- **3:7** "Who were under the authority of the governor" translates a hard Hebrew phrase (*le-khisse pachat*, "for the seat of the governor"). It may mean that those men had a share of the governor's district.
- **3:8** "They restored Jerusalem as far as the Broad Wall" translates the Hebrew *va-ya'azvu*, which can mean "they left" or "they restored". The Broad Wall is a thick wall found by archaeologists in the western part of the city.
- **3:12** "He and his daughters" is the only mention of women among the builders.
- **3:13** "A thousand cubits" is about 450 metres. It is the longest section.
- **3:15** "Roofed" is Hebrew *yetallennu*. "The Pool of Shelah" is the Siloam pool.
- **3:16** "Nehemiah son of Azbuk" is a different man from the governor. "The tombs of David" and "the House of the Warriors" are places in the city of David.
- **3:18** "Binnui son of Henadad" follows some Hebrew manuscripts and the Syriac. The Hebrew text has "Bavvai son of Henadad". See verse 24, where Binnui son of Henadad appears. The WABV text departs from the Hebrew here and is flagged for the scholar.
- **3:20** "Zealously" follows the reading tradition. The written text may be read differently.
- **3:26** "The temple servants living on Ophel" are the *netinim* (Ezra 2:43). "The Water Gate" is on the east.
- **3:28** "Above the Horse Gate the priests repaired" says that the priests worked the section of the east wall by the temple.
- **3:31** "The Muster Gate" is Hebrew *sha'ar ha-miphqad*. The word may mean an inspection or a counting.
- **3:32** "The goldsmiths and the merchants" finish the circuit at the Sheep Gate.
- **4:1** In the Hebrew Bible 4:1-6 is numbered 3:33-38, and 4:7-23 is numbered 4:1-17. The English chapter 4 has twenty-three verses and the Hebrew chapter 4 has seventeen.
- **4:2** "The army of Samaria" is the force under Sanballat. "Will they bring the stones back to life from the heaps of rubble, burned as they are?" is a taunt that the limestone, burned by fire, could not be used again.
- **4:3** "If a fox climbed on it, it would break down their stone wall" is Tobiah's jeer at the weakness of the wall.
- **4:4** The prayer in verses 4 and 5, "turn their taunts back on their own heads and hand them over to be plundered in a land of captivity" and "do not cover their guilt", is rendered as written. It is a prayer for the LORD to judge the enemies. Similar prayers are in Psalm 109 and Jeremiah 18:23.
- **4:6** "The people had a mind to work" is Hebrew *va-yehi lev la-am la'asot*, "the people had a heart to work". The wall is built to half its height.
- **4:7** "The people of Ashdod" are the Philistine neighbours to the west.
- **4:10** "The strength of the carriers is failing" is a quotation of what people in Judah said. It may be the first sign of a work song.
- **4:12** "From every place you turn, they are against us" translates a very hard Hebrew phrase (*mikkol ha-meqomot asher tashuvu aleinu*). It may mean that the Jews living near the enemy warned that attacks could come from every direction, or it may be a call to return from every place ("you must return to us"). The WABV text follows the first sense, and flags it for the scholar.
- **4:14** "The Lord, who is great and awesome" repeats the words of the prayer in 1:5. "Fight for your relatives, your sons and daughters, your wives and your homes" is Nehemiah's call.
- **4:16** "Half of my young men" are the guard. "Body armour" translates Hebrew *shiryonim*, a coat of mail.
- **4:18** "The man who sounded the trumpet stayed beside me" is the signal that is explained in verse 20.
- **4:23** "Each kept his weapon in his right hand" follows a correction of the Hebrew. The Hebrew text has *ish shilcho ha-mayim*, "each his weapon, the water", which is not clear. The WABV text departs from the Hebrew text here and is flagged for the scholar.
- **5:1** "The people and their wives" says that the women joined in the protest. The complaint is against "their fellow Jews", the wealthy.
- **5:2** The three complaints in verses 2 to 5 are of hunger, of land mortgaged in a famine, and of loans taken to pay the king's tax.
- **5:5** "Our flesh is the same as the flesh of our relatives" is a claim of equal standing. "We are bringing our sons and daughters into slavery" refers to debt slavery, which the law limits (Exodus 21:2-11; Leviticus 25:39-43).
- **5:7** "You are charging interest, each one to his own relative" refers to the law against taking interest from a fellow Israelite (Exodus 22:25; Leviticus 25:35-37; Deuteronomy 23:19-20). "Interest" is Hebrew *neshekh*, a "bite". "The nobles and the officials" are the lenders.
- **5:8** "We ... bought back our fellow Jews who had been sold to the nations" refers to the redeeming of exiles. "They were silent and could not find a word" is the response of the nobles.
- **5:11** "The percentage" is Hebrew *me'at ha-kesef*, "the hundredth of the money". It may be a monthly rate of one per cent, which is twelve per cent a year. The sense is uncertain.
- **5:12** "They swore" repeats the oath of the nobles before the priests.
- **5:13** "I shook out the fold of my robe" is a sign of the curse. "Amen" is the assembly's answer. The curse is rendered as written.
- **5:14** "From the twentieth year to the thirty-second year of King Artaxerxes" is 445 to 433 BCE. "The governor's food allowance" is Hebrew *lechem ha-pechah*, the bread that the people owed the governor.
- **5:15** "Food and wine, besides forty shekels of silver" translates a hard Hebrew phrase (*lechem va-yayin achar kesef shekalim arba'im*). The forty shekels may be a daily or a yearly tax. "Because of the fear of God" is Nehemiah's reason for not taking it.
- **5:16** "We did not buy any land" is a contrast with the nobles who took land.
- **5:17** "A hundred and fifty Jews and officials at my table" is the number he fed at his own expense.
- **5:19** "Remember me for good, my God, for all that I have done" is the first of the memoir's prayers that ask to be remembered (see 13:14, 22, 31).
- **6:1** "Had not set up doors in the gates" says that the wall was finished but not the doors.
- **6:2** "One of the villages in the plain of Ono" is the Hebrew *bakkefirim*, which may be a place name (Hakkephirim) or "in the villages". Ono is northwest of Jerusalem, outside the province. The meeting was a trap.
- **6:5** "An open letter" was a way of making the charge public.
- **6:6** "Geshem says it too" is Hebrew *Gashmu*. The charge is that Nehemiah means to become king.
- **6:7** "You have also appointed prophets to announce about you ... 'There is a king in Judah'" is the charge of a royal proclamation. "Prophets" is Hebrew *nevi'im*. See entry H-NAVI.
- **6:9** "God, make my hands strong" is Hebrew *ve-attah chazzeq et-yadai*, "and now strengthen my hands". The Hebrew does not name God, and the Latin adds "God". The WABV text supplies it.
- **6:10** "Who was shut in at his home" translates *atsur*, which may mean confined, or under a vow of seclusion. "They are coming to kill you at night" is the trap.
- **6:11** "Would someone like me go into the temple to save his life?" refers to the rule that only priests may enter the temple (Numbers 18:7; 2 Chronicles 26:16-21). Nehemiah is not a priest.
- **6:12** "God had not sent him" is Nehemiah's test of a prophet. "Hired" says that the prophet was paid.
- **6:14** "Noadiah" is described in Hebrew as a prophetess (*ha-nevi'ah*). See entry H-NAVI. She is not otherwise known. "Remember Tobiah and Sanballat" is a prayer for judgement.
- **6:15** "The twenty-fifth day of Elul, in fifty-two days" is the end of September or the beginning of October 445 BCE. Josephus says that the work took two years and four months. The Hebrew number is followed.
- **6:16** "Lost their confidence" is Hebrew *va-yippelu me'od be-eineihem*, "fell greatly in their own eyes".
- **6:18** "Bound by oath to him, because he was the son-in-law of Shecaniah son of Arah" shows Tobiah's ties with families of Judah. "Jehohanan" is his son.
- **6:19** "They also spoke of his good deeds in my presence and reported my words to him" says that people in Jerusalem acted as his informants.
- **7:1** "The gatekeepers, the singers and the Levites had been appointed" says that the temple staff was set in place when the wall and doors were done.
- **7:2** "My brother Hanani" is the man of 1:2. "Hananiah the commander of the fortress" is Hebrew *sar ha-birah*. He "feared God more than many".
- **7:3** "The gates of Jerusalem are not to be opened until the sun is hot" orders that the gates are opened late in the morning and shut early, as a safeguard.
- **7:4** "No houses had been rebuilt" is the reason for the lots in 11:1-2, by which one in ten of the people was to live in the city.
- **7:5** "My God put it into my heart" repeats the phrase of 2:12. "The book of the family records of those who had come up at the first" is the list that follows.
- **7:6** The list (verses 6 to 73) is parallel to Ezra 2:1-70 and 1 Esdras 5:7-43. The names and numbers differ in many places. The wording follows the WABV Ezra 2 where the Hebrew is the same.
- **7:7** "Zerubbabel, Jeshua, Nehemiah, Azariah, Raamiah, Nahamani, Mordecai, Bilshan, Mispereth, Bigvai, Nehum and Baanah" are twelve leaders. Ezra 2:2 has eleven (without Nahamani) and some names are spelled differently (Seraiah for Azariah, Reelaiah for Raamiah, Mispar for Mispereth, Rehum for Nehum).
- **7:8** The families and towns of verses 8 to 38 have many numbers that differ from Ezra 2. Examples are Arah (652 here, 775 there), Pahath-moab (2,818 and 2,812), Zattu (845 and 945), Bebai (628 and 623), Azgad (2,322 and 1,222), Adonikam (667 and 666), Bigvai (2,067 and 2,056), Adin (655 and 454), Hashum (328 and 223), Bezai (324 and 323), Bethel and Ai (123 and 223), Lod, Hadid and Ono (721 and 725), and Senaah (3,930 and 3,630).
- **7:15** "Binnui" is Bani in Ezra 2:10. "Hariph" (verse 24) is Jorah in Ezra 2:18, and "Gibeon" (verse 25) is Gibbar in Ezra 2:20.
- **7:26** "The men of Bethlehem and Netophah, a hundred and eighty-eight" joins two groups that Ezra 2:21-22 gives as a hundred and twenty-three and fifty-six.
- **7:28** "Beth-azmaveth" is Azmaveth in Ezra 2:24. "The other Nebo" (verse 33) is Nebo in Ezra 2:29.
- **7:43** "Hodevah" is Hodaviah in Ezra 2:40.
- **7:44** "The singers ... a hundred and forty-eight" is the Hebrew number here. Ezra 2:41 has a hundred and twenty-eight.
- **7:45** "The gatekeepers ... a hundred and thirty-eight" is the number here. Ezra 2:42 has a hundred and thirty-nine.
- **7:46** "The temple servants" is Hebrew *netinim*, as in Ezra 2:43. Their names have small differences in spelling (Sia for Siaha, Lebana for Lebanah, Shalmai for Shamlai, and others).
- **7:59** "Amon" is Ami in Ezra 2:57.
- **7:62** "Six hundred and forty-two" is the number here. Ezra 2:60 has six hundred and fifty-two.
- **7:65** "The governor" is Hebrew *ha-tirshata*, a Persian title, as in Ezra 2:63. "The most holy food" is *qodesh haqqodashim*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **7:66** "Forty-two thousand three hundred and sixty" is the same total as in Ezra 2:64. The numbers listed here add up to about thirty-one thousand, and in Ezra to about thirty thousand, so neither list agrees with the total.
- **7:67** "Two hundred and forty-five male and female singers" is the number here. Ezra 2:65 has two hundred.
- **7:68** "Their horses were seven hundred and thirty-six" and the other animals are in some Hebrew manuscripts but not in others. See Ezra 2:66-67.
- **7:70** "The governor gave to the treasury a thousand darics of gold, fifty bowls and five hundred and thirty priests' robes" has no parallel in Ezra. Here the governor is probably Nehemiah or Zerubbabel. "Darics" is Hebrew *darkemonim*, a Persian gold coin.
- **7:72** The gifts in verses 70 to 72 total forty-one thousand darics of gold, four thousand two hundred minas of silver and five hundred and ninety-seven priests' robes. Ezra 2:69 has sixty-one thousand darics, five thousand minas and a hundred robes.
- **7:73** "When the seventh month came" joins the list to chapter 8. In the Hebrew Bible the verse is divided into 7:72 and 7:73, and the numbering of the end of the chapter differs by one or two verses from the English. This is to be checked against the Hebrew.
- **8:1** "The square in front of the Water Gate" is a large open space by the east wall of the city. "The book of the law of Moses" is Hebrew *sefer torat Mosheh*. See entry H-TORAH. It is not clear what the book contained, and it may have been the whole Torah or a part. The people asked Ezra to bring it.
- **8:2** "The first day of the seventh month" is the day of the Festival of Trumpets (Leviticus 23:23-25). "Everyone who could understand what they heard" includes children old enough to listen.
- **8:3** "From first light until midday" is about six hours of reading.
- **8:4** "A wooden platform" is Hebrew *migdal-etz*, a tower of wood. Thirteen men stood by Ezra.
- **8:5** "As he opened it all the people stood up" shows respect for the book.
- **8:6** "Amen, Amen" is the double answer to the blessing. "Lifting up their hands" and bowing with their faces to the ground are signs of worship.
- **8:7** "The Levites ... helped the people to understand the law" gives thirteen names. The people stood in their places while they taught.
- **8:8** "Making it clear and giving the sense" translates Hebrew *meforash* and *som sekhel*. *Meforash* can mean "clearly" or "translated" or "explained in sections". It is the same word as in Ezra 4:18. The verse is used to speak of the beginnings of translation and interpretation of the law.
- **8:9** "This day is holy to the LORD your God" uses *qadosh*. See entry H-QADOSH. "Nehemiah the governor" is Hebrew *ha-tirshata*. The people wept as they heard the words of the law.
- **8:10** "Eat rich food and drink sweet drinks" is Hebrew *eklu mashmannim ve-shetu mamtaqqim*. "Send portions to those who have nothing prepared" is a rule of sharing. "The joy of the LORD is your strength" is one of the best-known sentences in the book. "Our Lord" is Hebrew *adonenu*.
- **8:12** "Because they had understood the words that were made known to them" gives the reason for the joy.
- **8:13** "On the second day" the leaders study the law with Ezra. They find the rule for the Festival of Booths (Leviticus 23:33-43).
- **8:15** "Olive branches, wild olive branches, myrtle branches, palm branches and branches of leafy trees" is not the same list as in Leviticus 23:40 (fruit of good trees, palm, leafy trees and willows of the stream). The wording is from the Hebrew.
- **8:16** "Each on his own roof" shows the booths were built on flat roofs. "The Gate of Ephraim" is a gate on the north-west wall.
- **8:17** "From the days of Joshua son of Nun until that day the Israelites had not done so" is hard to place, since Ezra 3:4 says the returned exiles kept the Festival of Booths. It may mean that they had not kept it on this scale or in this way.
- **8:18** "On the eighth day there was a closing assembly" follows Leviticus 23:36 and Numbers 29:35. "He read from the book of the law of God" is Ezra's reading each day of the festival.
- **9:1** "On the twenty-fourth day of the same month" is two days after the end of the Festival of Booths (8:18). The people fast and wear sackcloth, with earth on their heads, the signs of mourning.
- **9:2** "Those of Israelite descent separated themselves from all foreigners" is Hebrew *va-yibbadelu zera Yisrael mi-kol benei nekhar*. It is a ritual separation and not a removal of people. The confession is of their own sins and the wrongs of their ancestors.
- **9:3** "A quarter of the day" is about three hours. They read for one quarter and confessed for another.
- **9:4** "The stairs of the Levites" are a raised place where the Levites stood. Eight Levites cry out. The names in verses 4 and 5 are not quite the same.
- **9:5** The Greek adds "Ezra said" before verse 6. The prayer of verses 5 to 37 may be spoken by the Levites together or by Ezra. "From everlasting to everlasting" is the call to bless the LORD.
- **9:6** The prayer of the Levites (verses 6 to 37) is a review of history from creation to the exile. It has these parts: creation (6), Abraham (7-8), the exodus (9-11), Sinai (12-15), the wilderness (16-21), the conquest (22-25), the cycles of sin and rescue (26-31), and the plea (32-37). "The host of heaven bows down to you" says that the stars are the LORD's servants and not gods.
- **9:7** "Abram ... Ur of the Chaldeans ... Abraham" repeats Genesis 11:31, 15:7 and 17:5.
- **9:8** "You found his heart faithful before you" echoes Genesis 15:6. "A covenant" is Hebrew *berit*. See entry H-BERIT. The list of six peoples follows Genesis 15:18-21. "Righteous" is Hebrew *tsaddiq*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH.
- **9:9** "The Sea of Reeds" is Hebrew *Yam Suf*, the sea of the exodus. "Heard their cry" echoes Exodus 2:23-25 and 3:7.
- **9:10** "They acted proudly" is Hebrew *zedu*, the verb for presumption. "You made a name for yourself" repeats the words of Isaiah 63:12-14 and Jeremiah 32:20.
- **9:11** "Like a stone into raging water" repeats Exodus 15:5, 10.
- **9:13** "You came down on Mount Sinai" follows Exodus 19:11, 18 and 20. "Right rules and true laws" is Hebrew *mishpatim yesharim ve-torot emet*.
- **9:14** "Your holy sabbath" is Hebrew *shabbat qodshekha*. See entries H-QADOSH and H-SHABBAT. The Sabbath is named alone among the commands.
- **9:15** "Bread from heaven ... water out of the rock" refers to Exodus 16 and 17 and Numbers 20.
- **9:16** "Stiffened their necks" is the repeated phrase for stubbornness (Exodus 32:9; Deuteronomy 9:6, 13). It is also in verses 17 and 29.
- **9:17** "To go back to their slavery in Egypt" follows many Hebrew manuscripts and the Greek, and Numbers 14:4. The Hebrew text most often read has "in their rebellion" (*be-miryam*). The two differ by one letter. The WABV text departs from the Hebrew reading and is flagged for the scholar. "A God of forgiveness, gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and rich in faithful love" is Exodus 34:6-7. "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED.
- **9:18** "A calf of cast metal" refers to Exodus 32. "Insults" is Hebrew *ne'atsot*, a word of contempt. It is also in verse 26.
- **9:20** "Your good spirit" is Hebrew *ruach-kha ha-tovah*. See entry H-RUACH. It instructs the people in the wilderness (Numbers 11:17, 25; Isaiah 63:10-14).
- **9:21** "Their clothes did not wear out and their feet did not swell" follows Deuteronomy 8:4 and 29:5.
- **9:22** "Divided them up by regions" translates a hard Hebrew phrase (*va-tachaltsem le-pe'ah*, "you divided them into corners"). The sense is uncertain. "Sihon king of Heshbon and ... Og king of Bashan" are the kings of Numbers 21.
- **9:23** "As many as the stars of heaven" echoes Genesis 15:5 and 22:17.
- **9:25** "Fortified towns ... houses full of every good thing, cisterns already dug, vineyards, olive groves" repeats the description of the land in Deuteronomy 6:10-11. "They ate and were full and grew fat" repeats Deuteronomy 32:15.
- **9:26** "They killed your prophets" refers to the killing of prophets (1 Kings 19:10; 2 Chronicles 24:21; Jeremiah 26:20-23). "Prophets" is *nevi'im*. See entry H-NAVI. "To turn them back" is Hebrew *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **9:27** "Deliverers" is Hebrew *moshi'im*, the word used of the judges (Judges 3:9, 15). The verses 27 and 28 give the cycle of Judges 2:11-19.
- **9:29** "By which a person will live if he keeps them" quotes Leviticus 18:5 (see also Ezekiel 20:11). "A stubborn shoulder" is Hebrew *katef sorerret* (see Zechariah 7:11).
- **9:30** "By your spirit through your prophets" is the way the LORD spoke to Israel (see Zechariah 7:12). "The peoples of the lands" are the empires that ruled them.
- **9:32** "The great, the mighty and the awesome God, who keeps covenant and faithful love" repeats the title of the prayer in 1:5 and Deuteronomy 7:9, and joins it to the list of leaders who suffered. "From the time of the kings of Assyria" refers to the conquest of the north from 733 BCE.
- **9:33** "You have been righteous in all that has come on us, for you have acted faithfully and we have acted wickedly" is the confession. "Righteous" is *tsaddiq*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH.
- **9:36** "Here we are today as slaves" is the Levites' complaint about the Persian rule. It is rendered as written.
- **9:37** "They rule over our bodies and over our livestock as they please" refers to the king's tax and service. "Because of our sins" ends the prayer with the confession.
- **9:38** In the Hebrew Bible this verse is 10:1, and English 10:1-39 is Hebrew 10:2-40. "A firm agreement" is Hebrew *amanah*. The written and sealed agreement is the covenant of chapter 10.
- **10:1** "Nehemiah the governor" (Hebrew *ha-tirshata*) signs first. The list of those who sealed (verses 1 to 27) has about eighty-four names: twenty-one priests (verses 1 to 8), seventeen Levites (verses 9 to 13) and forty-four leaders of the people (verses 14 to 27).
- **10:14** "The leaders of the people" are the heads of the families named in Ezra 2 and Nehemiah 7 (Parosh, Pahath-moab, Elam, Zattu and others).
- **10:28** "All who have separated themselves from the peoples of the lands to follow the law of God" includes the whole community, with wives and children who "have knowledge and understanding".
- **10:29** "A curse and an oath" is Hebrew *alah u-shevu'ah*. They bind themselves with a curse if they break the oath. "The LORD our Lord" is Hebrew *YHWH adoneinu*.
- **10:30** "We will not give our daughters to the peoples of the land or take their daughters for our sons" is the promise about marriage. It follows Deuteronomy 7:3 and is tied to Ezra 9 and 10 and Nehemiah 13:23-27. It is rendered as written.
- **10:31** "If the peoples of the land bring goods ... to sell on the sabbath day, we will not buy" is the Sabbath promise (see Nehemiah 13:15-22 and Jeremiah 17:21-27). "In the seventh year we will leave the land alone and cancel every debt" follows Exodus 23:10-11 and Deuteronomy 15:1-2.
- **10:32** "A third of a shekel" is the yearly gift for the temple. The temple tax of the law was a half shekel (Exodus 30:11-16). The difference is not explained.
- **10:33** "The bread of the Presence ... the sin offerings to make atonement for Israel" lists the regular costs of the temple. "Make atonement" is Hebrew *lekapper*. See entry H-KAPPER. "The holy things" is *ha-qodashim*.
- **10:34** "The wood offering" is the supply of wood for the altar fire. The lot decides which families bring it, at set times (see 13:31 and Leviticus 6:12).
- **10:35** "The first fruits of our ground" follows Exodus 23:19 and Deuteronomy 26:1-11.
- **10:36** "The firstborn of our sons and of our livestock" follows Exodus 13:2 and Numbers 18:15-18, where the firstborn son is redeemed.
- **10:37** "The first of our ground meal" refers to the dough offering (Numbers 15:20-21). "The tithe of our ground to the Levites" follows Numbers 18:21-24.
- **10:38** "The tithe of the tithes" is the tenth that the Levites give to the priests (Numbers 18:26-28).
- **10:39** "We will not neglect the house of our God" closes the covenant. The failure to keep it is told in 13:10-13.
- **11:1** "The holy city" is Hebrew *ir ha-qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH. The title is also used in Isaiah 48:2 and 52:1 and in verse 18. "One in ten" is chosen by lot to live in Jerusalem, in order to fill the city (7:4).
- **11:2** "The people blessed all the men who willingly offered" refers to the volunteers who moved to Jerusalem, perhaps without the lot.
- **11:3** "The leaders of the province who lived in Jerusalem" begins a list that is parallel to 1 Chronicles 9:2-17. The words in brackets, about the towns of Judah, are the same as in Ezra 2:70.
- **11:4** "Athaiah son of Uzziah" is probably the Uthai of 1 Chronicles 9:4, with a different father's name. The two lists have many differences in names and numbers. The wording follows the WABV 1 Chronicles 9 where the Hebrew is the same.
- **11:5** "Son of the Shilonite" may mean a descendant of Shelah (see 1 Chronicles 9:5).
- **11:6** "Four hundred and sixty-eight" is the number of the descendants of Perez. In 1 Chronicles 9:6 the number given for Zerah is six hundred and ninety.
- **11:8** "Nine hundred and twenty-eight" is the number of Benjamin here. In 1 Chronicles 9:9 it is nine hundred and fifty-six.
- **11:9** "Second in charge of the city" is Hebrew *mishneh al ha-ir*.
- **11:10** The list of the priests (verses 10 to 14) gives "eight hundred and twenty-two", "two hundred and forty-two" and "a hundred and twenty-eight", a total of eleven hundred and ninety-two. 1 Chronicles 9:13 gives seventeen hundred and sixty.
- **11:11** "Seraiah" is Azariah in 1 Chronicles 9:11. "The officer in charge of the house of God" is Hebrew *nagid beit ha-Elohim*.
- **11:14** "Zabdiel son of Haggedolim" may be "son of the great ones" or "one of the great men".
- **11:15** The Levites (verses 15 to 18) are 284 in all. Their names are close to 1 Chronicles 9:14-16.
- **11:17** "The leader who began the thanksgiving at prayer" is hard in the Hebrew. It may mean that Mattaniah led the praise at the hour of prayer. "Abda" is Obadiah in 1 Chronicles 9:16.
- **11:19** "A hundred and seventy-two" is the number of gatekeepers. 1 Chronicles 9:22 has two hundred and twelve, and Ezra 2:42 a hundred and thirty-nine.
- **11:21** "The temple servants lived on Ophel" repeats 3:26. "Ziha and Gishpa" are named as their overseers.
- **11:23** "A command from the king" gave the singers a daily provision. It may be the Persian king (compare Ezra 6:8-10 and 7:20-22), or David (12:45).
- **11:24** "Pethahiah ... was at the king's side in all matters concerning the people" says that he was the adviser to the Persian king for the affairs of the Jews.
- **11:25** "Kiriath-arba" is Hebron. The towns of verses 25 to 30 lie in the south of Judah, from Beersheba to the Valley of Hinnom.
- **11:31** "From Geba onwards" begins the towns of Benjamin, in the north (verses 31 to 35).
- **11:35** "The valley of the craftsmen" is called Ge-harashim in 1 Chronicles 4:14.
- **11:36** "Some divisions of the Levites in Judah were assigned to Benjamin" is hard in the Hebrew. It may mean that the Levite groups lived among both tribes.
- **12:1** The list of the priests and Levites who came up with Zerubbabel and Jeshua (verses 1 to 9) gives twenty-two heads of priestly houses. It refers to the first return of about 538 BCE.
- **12:2** "Malluch" is Malluchi in verse 14. Many of the names in verses 1 to 7 are spelled differently from the same families in verses 12 to 21 (for example Rehum and Harim, Meremoth and Meraioth, Shecaniah and Shebaniah, Mijamin and Miniamin, Maadiah and Moadiah).
- **12:8** "Mattaniah, who with his relatives was in charge of the songs of thanksgiving" is the leader of the singers. He is named in 11:17.
- **12:10** "Jeshua was the father of Joiakim, Joiakim the father of Eliashib, Eliashib the father of Joiada, Joiada the father of Jonathan, and Jonathan the father of Jaddua" gives the line of the high priests down to about 400 BCE. It shows that this part of the book was written well after the time of Nehemiah. "Jonathan" is also called Johanan (verses 22 and 23), and a letter of 407 BCE from Elephantine names a high priest Yehohanan.
- **12:12** The heads of the priestly houses in the days of Joiakim (verses 12 to 21) correspond to the twenty-two families of verses 1 to 7.
- **12:22** "Until the reign of Darius the Persian" is Darius II (423 to 404 BCE) or possibly Darius III (336 to 331 BCE). The date of the book is disputed.
- **12:23** "The Book of the Records" is Hebrew *sefer divre ha-yamim*, the name of Chronicles, here the temple records. "Johanan son of Eliashib" is the grandson of the high priest Eliashib.
- **12:24** "As David the man of God had commanded" refers to the arrangement of the Levite singers (1 Chronicles 25).
- **12:25** "Storerooms" translates a rare word (*asuppim*).
- **12:27** The dedication of the wall (verses 27 to 43) is told partly in the first person, in verses 31, 38 and 40, where Nehemiah leads one of the two groups.
- **12:28** "The descendants of the singers" came from the villages round Jerusalem to take part.
- **12:30** "The priests and the Levites made themselves clean, and they made the people clean, and the gates and the wall" is told briefly. The rite itself is not described.
- **12:31** "Two great choirs" walked on the wall in opposite directions, one to the right (south and east) and one to the left (north and east). The route is described with the names of the gates and towers.
- **12:36** "With the musical instruments of David the man of God" refers to 1 Chronicles 23:5. "Ezra the scribe went in front of them" is the only part of the dedication in which Ezra has a place.
- **12:37** "The Fountain Gate" and "the steps of the city of David" are on the south-east. The route of the first choir is clear from verse 31 to verse 37, and that of the second from verse 38 to verse 39. The place names are as in the Hebrew, and many cannot be located exactly.
- **12:39** "The Gate of the Guard" is the gate where the second choir stopped, probably near the temple. The Hebrew is *sha'ar ha-mattarah*.
- **12:40** "The two choirs ... stood in the house of God" says that the two groups met at the temple.
- **12:43** "The women and children also rejoiced, and the joy of Jerusalem was heard far away" repeats the joy of Ezra 3:13 and Nehemiah 8:12, without the weeping.
- **12:44** "On that day men were appointed over the rooms for the stores" returns to the support of the clergy (10:37-39). "Judah rejoiced over the priests and Levites who served" gives the reason. The Hebrew word is *ha-omedim*, "who stood".
- **12:45** "The charge of purification" is Hebrew *mishmeret ha-toharah*.
- **12:46** "In the days of David and Asaph there were leaders of the singers" looks back to David's arrangement.
- **12:47** "In the days of Zerubbabel and in the days of Nehemiah all Israel gave the daily portions" joins the two periods. "The Levites set apart the portion for the descendants of Aaron" follows Numbers 18:26-28.
- **13:1** "On that day" does not say which day. It may follow the dedication of the wall (12:44), or it may be a later occasion. "No Ammonite or Moabite was ever to enter the assembly of God" quotes Deuteronomy 23:3-5. The Hebrew word for "assembly" is *qahal*.
- **13:2** "Hired Balaam against them to curse them" refers to Numbers 22 to 24. "Our God turned the curse into a blessing" follows Deuteronomy 23:5.
- **13:3** "Everyone of mixed descent" translates Hebrew *kol-erev*, "all the mixture". The same word is used of the "mixed crowd" who left Egypt with Israel (Exodus 12:38). It may mean people of foreign descent in general. The text does not say who they were or what happened to them.
- **13:4** "Before this" says that the events of verses 4 to 9 took place before Nehemiah's return. "Eliashib the priest" is the high priest of 3:1. "Related to Tobiah" is Hebrew *qarov le-Toviyyah*. It is a family tie, as in 6:18.
- **13:5** "A large room where they had previously stored the grain offering ..." repeats the list of things stored in the rooms (see 10:37-39).
- **13:6** "The thirty-second year of Artaxerxes king of Babylon" is 433 BCE. "King of Babylon" is the Hebrew title here, and it is also used of Cyrus in Ezra 5:13. The text says he had gone back to the king and came again "after some time".
- **13:7** "The evil that Eliashib had done for Tobiah" is the use of a temple room as a home for a foreigner and an enemy of the work.
- **13:8** "I threw all of Tobiah's household belongings out of the room" is rendered as written.
- **13:9** "They cleansed the rooms" is a rite of purification after the room had been used for a non-sacred purpose.
- **13:10** "The Levites and the singers who did the work had gone back each to his own field" says that they left the temple to earn a living, because their portions had not been given (see 10:37-39).
- **13:11** "Why has the house of God been neglected?" is Nehemiah's question. The verb *azuv* means "left" or "abandoned".
- **13:13** "They were considered trustworthy" is the reason for the choice of the four treasurers. Their task was to distribute the portions fairly.
- **13:14** "Remember me for this, my God, and do not wipe out the good that I have done" is the first of Nehemiah's four closing prayers (verses 14, 22, 29 and 31). "The good that I have done" is literally "my acts of faithful love" (Hebrew *chasadai*).
- **13:15** "Treading winepresses on the sabbath" and the other loads refer to work and trade on the sabbath. "Sabbath" is Hebrew *shabbat*. See entry H-SHABBAT. See also Exodus 34:21, Jeremiah 17:21-27 and Amos 8:5.
- **13:16** "Men of Tyre who lived there" were traders who brought fish and goods to Jerusalem.
- **13:17** "Treating the sabbath day as common" is Hebrew *chalal*, "to profane". It is the opposite of "to make holy".
- **13:18** "Did your ancestors not do the same, so that our God brought all this disaster on us" connects the exile with the profaning of the sabbath (see Jeremiah 17:27).
- **13:19** "When it began to grow dark ... before the sabbath, I ordered that the doors be shut" says that the gates were shut at the start of the sabbath. The sabbath began at sunset.
- **13:21** "I will use force against you" is Hebrew *yad ani sholeach bakhem*, "I will lay a hand on you".
- **13:22** "To keep the sabbath day holy" is Hebrew *le-qaddesh et-yom ha-shabbat*. See entry H-QADOSH. "Your great faithful love" is *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. The Levites guard the gates because they are the holy tribe.
- **13:23** "Women from Ashdod, Ammon and Moab" are named as the foreign women. This is the same matter as Ezra 9 and 10 and the oath of Nehemiah 10:30.
- **13:24** "Half of their children spoke the language of Ashdod" is Hebrew *ashdodit*. "The language of Judah" is *yehudit*, Hebrew. The concern is for the children's language and so for their identity.
- **13:25** "I confronted them and cursed them, and beat some of them and pulled out their hair" is rendered as written, with no comment. The oath that he made them swear follows Deuteronomy 7:3.
- **13:26** "Did Solomon king of Israel not sin because of women like these?" refers to 1 Kings 11:1-8.
- **13:27** "Be unfaithful to our God" is Hebrew *ma'al*, the word used for the cause of the exile (see Ezra 9:2).
- **13:28** "One of the sons of Joiada, son of Eliashib the high priest, was a son-in-law of Sanballat the Horonite" says that a member of the high priest's family had married the daughter of the governor of Samaria. "I drove him away from me" is rendered as written. Josephus tells of a priest called Manasseh who married Sanballat's daughter and founded the temple on Mount Gerizim, but the identification is uncertain.
- **13:29** "They have made the priesthood unclean and the covenant of the priesthood and the Levites" is Hebrew *ga'alu ha-kehunnah u-vrit ha-kehunnah ve-ha-leviyyim*. "Covenant" is *berit*. See entry H-BERIT. The "covenant of the priesthood" refers to Numbers 25:12-13 and Malachi 2:4-8.
- **13:30** "I cleansed them of everything foreign" is Nehemiah's summary of the reforms. It is rendered as written.
- **13:31** "The wood offering at set times and the first fruits" repeats 10:34-35. "Remember me for good, my God" is the last line of the book.
