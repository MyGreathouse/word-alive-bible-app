# WABV: Acts

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[G-PNEUMA\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Acts is the fifth book of the New Testament in the order that the WABV text follows. It is written in Greek, and the WABV text is drafted from a critical Greek text, with the Byzantine tradition noted in the footnotes where it differs, as the Charter requires. It is the second part of a work in two parts, the first being Luke, and both are addressed to Theophilus (Luke 1:3; Acts 1:1), and the WABV text of Acts therefore keeps the renderings of Luke. It tells of the sending of the Spirit, the witness of the apostles from Jerusalem to Judea and Samaria and on to Rome, and the work of Peter and of Paul. The book has long speeches, and these are rendered in the same voice as the narrative. The chapter and verse numbering is the usual numbering. Some verses are not in the critical Greek text (8:37, 15:34, 24:7 and 28:29), and the verse number is kept with a note. The text of Acts has two early forms, the Alexandrian and the Western (best known in Codex Bezae), and the Western text is about a tenth longer, and the WABV text follows the critical text, and the footnotes record the main places where the Western text differs. The WABV text of Acts makes a number of fixed choices that follow from its Greek, and they are written down here so that they are used throughout. "The Holy Spirit" translates *to pneuma to hagion* and is tagged G-PNEUMA. "The kingdom of God" is tagged G-BASILEIA. *Ekklesia* is rendered "assembly" and is tagged G-EKKLESIA, and the team should decide between "assembly" and "church". *Ioudaioi* is rendered "Jews", as in Luke, and the team should decide the rendering, and the WABV text of John uses "the Judeans" and the team should decide whether the two books are to agree. *Ethne* is rendered "the nations", as in the Gospels, and *ethnikoi* "the Gentiles". *Hodos* in the sense of the early movement is rendered "the Way" (9:2, 19:9, 19:23, 22:4, 24:14, 24:22). *Adelphoi* is rendered "brothers", and the footnote records that in many places the Greek may include the sisters. *Metanoeo* is rendered "repent" and is tagged G-METANOIA. *Sozo* is rendered "save" and is tagged G-SOZO. *Pais* of Jesus (3:13, 3:26, 4:27, 4:30) is rendered "servant". *Pistis* is rendered "faith" and *pisteuo* "believe", and both are tagged G-PISTIS. *Christos* is rendered "Christ" and is tagged G-CHRISTOS, and the team should decide between "Christ" and "Messiah". *Ho huios tou anthropou* (7:56) is rendered "the Son of Man" and is tagged G-HUIOS-ANTHROPOU. The book falls into nine parts in the WABV text: the ascension, Pentecost and the first healing (chapters 1 to 3), the council, the shared life and the seven (4 to 6), Stephen, Philip and the call of Saul (7 to 9), Cornelius, Antioch and Herod (10 to 12), the first journey and the council at Jerusalem (13 to 15), the second journey (16 to 18), Ephesus and the journey to Jerusalem (19 to 21), the speeches and the hearings (22 to 24), and Festus, Agrippa, the voyage and Rome (25 to 28). The headings of the parts are not in the Greek. The markers beginning with G- (for Greek words) are defined in the Biblical Concepts and Language System, and the new ones are listed in the review note of the part where each first appears. The wording of the WABV text is drafted from the Greek and is not taken from any other English translation.*

## Part One: Chapters 1 to 3, the ascension, Pentecost and the lame man at the gate

### Chapter 1

**1** In my first book, Theophilus, I wrote about all that Jesus began to do and to teach

**2** up to the day he was taken up, after giving his instructions, through the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\], to the apostles he had picked.

**3** After his suffering he showed himself alive to them with many sure proofs, appearing to them across forty days and speaking about the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] of God.

**4** Once, as he shared a meal with them, he told them not to leave Jerusalem but to wait for the Father's promise: "the one you heard about from me.

**5** John baptised in water, but in a few days you will be baptised in the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\]."

**6** So when they were gathered, they asked him, "Lord, are you going to restore the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] to Israel at this time?"

**7** He told them, "It is not for you to know times or dates, which the Father has fixed by his own authority.

**8** But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] comes upon you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and as far as the end of the earth."

**9** When he had said this, while they watched, he was lifted up, and a cloud took him from their sight.

**10** They were staring at the sky as he went, when suddenly two men in white were standing beside them.

**11** They said, "Men of Galilee, why are you standing there looking up at the sky? This Jesus, who has been taken up from you into heaven, will come in the same way that you saw him go into heaven."

**12** Then they went back to Jerusalem from the hill called Olivet, which is near Jerusalem, a Sabbath \[G-SABBATON\] day's walk away.

**13** When they arrived, they went up to the upper room where they were staying: Peter, John, James and Andrew, Philip and Thomas, Bartholomew and Matthew, James son of Alphaeus, Simon the Zealot, and Judas son of James.

**14** All these were united and kept on praying, together with the women, with Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brothers.

**15** In those days Peter stood up in the middle of the brothers (there was a crowd of about a hundred and twenty) and said,

**16** "Brothers, it was necessary for the scripture to be fulfilled, which the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] spoke long ago through David's mouth about Judas, who became the guide for those who arrested Jesus.

**17** He was one of our number and had been given a share in this service.

**18** (This man bought a field with the pay for his crime, and he fell forward, burst open in the middle, and all his intestines poured out.

**19** Everyone living in Jerusalem came to know of it, so that in their own language the field was called Akeldama, 'Field of Blood'.)

**20** For it is written in the book of Psalms: 'Let his homestead be deserted, and let no one live in it,' and: 'Let someone else take over his office.'

**21** So it is necessary that one of the men who accompanied us the whole time the Lord Jesus moved among us,

**22** from John's baptism until the day he was taken up from us, should become with us a witness of his resurrection."

**23** They put forward two: Joseph called Barsabbas, surnamed Justus, and Matthias.

**24** They prayed: "Lord, you know everyone's heart. Show us which of these two you have picked

**25** to take the place in this service and apostleship that Judas left to go to his own place."

**26** They drew lots between them, and the lot fell to Matthias, and he was counted in with the eleven apostles.

### Chapter 2

**1** When the day of Pentecost came round, they were all together in one place.

**2** Suddenly a sound came from heaven like a strong wind rushing, and it filled the whole house where they were sitting.

**3** They saw tongues like flames, spreading out, and one settled on each of them.

**4** They were all filled with the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] and began to speak in other languages, as the Spirit gave them words to speak out.

**5** Now there were devout Jews staying in Jerusalem from every nation under heaven.

**6** When this sound occurred, the crowd came together and was confused, because each person was hearing them speak in his own language.

**7** They were astounded and amazed, saying, "Look, are not all these speakers Galileans?

**8** How is it that each of us hears them in the language we were born into?

**9** Parthians, Medes and Elamites, people living in Mesopotamia, Judea and Cappadocia, Pontus and Asia,

**10** Phrygia and Pamphylia, Egypt and the districts of Libya around Cyrene, visitors from Rome,

**11** Jews and converts, Cretans and Arabs: we hear them telling in our own languages about the great deeds of God."

**12** All were astounded and at a loss, saying to one another, "What can this mean?"

**13** But others said, jeering, "They are drunk on new wine."

**14** Then Peter stood up with the eleven, raised his voice and declared to them, "Men of Judea and all you who live in Jerusalem, let me tell you this, and listen to my words.

**15** These people are not drunk, as you suppose, for it is only nine in the morning.

**16** No, this is what was spoken through the prophet Joel:

**17** 'In the last days, God says, I will pour out my Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] on every kind of person. Your sons and your daughters will prophesy, your young men will see visions, and your old men will dream dreams.

**18** Yes, even on my slaves, men and women, I will pour out my Spirit in those days, and they will prophesy.

**19** I will give wonders in the sky above and signs on the earth below: blood and fire and thick smoke.

**20** The sun will be turned into darkness and the moon into blood before the day of the Lord comes, the great and shining day.

**21** And everyone who calls on the Lord's name will be saved \[G-SOZO\].'

**22** People of Israel, listen to these words: Jesus the Nazarene was a man pointed out to you by God through mighty acts, wonders and signs that God did among you through him, as you yourselves know.

**23** This man, given up by God's settled plan and foreknowledge, you nailed up and killed through the hands of lawless people.

**24** But God raised him up, freeing him from the labour pains of death, since it was not possible for him to be held by it.

**25** For David says about him: 'I kept the Lord always in front of me, because he is at my right hand, so that I will not be shaken.

**26** For this my heart was glad and my tongue rejoiced, and my body too will settle in hope,

**27** because you will not leave my soul \[G-PSYCHE\] in Hades or let your Holy One see decay.

**28** You have shown me the ways of life \[G-ZOE\]. You will fill me with joy in your presence.'

**29** Brothers, I can say to you openly about the patriarch David that he died and was buried, and his tomb is here among us to this day.

**30** So, being a prophet and knowing that God had sworn to him with an oath to seat one of his descendants on his throne,

**31** he looked ahead and spoke of the resurrection of the Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\]: he was not left in Hades, and his flesh did not see decay.

**32** This Jesus God raised up, and we are all witnesses of it.

**33** Lifted up to God's right hand, then, and having received the promise of the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] from the Father, he has poured out what you see and hear.

**34** For David did not go up into the heavens, but he says himself: 'The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand

**35** until I make your enemies a footstool under your feet.'

**36** So let all the house of Israel know for certain that God has made him both Lord and Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\], this Jesus whom you crucified."

**37** When they heard this, they were stabbed to the heart and said to Peter and the rest of the apostles, "What are we to do, brothers?"

**38** Peter said to them, "Repent \[G-METANOIA\], and let each of you be baptised in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\].

**39** The promise is for you and your children and for all those far away, for as many as the Lord our God may call."

**40** With many other words he testified and appealed to them: "Be saved \[G-SOZO\] from this twisted generation."

**41** So those who welcomed his word were baptised, and about three thousand people were added that day.

**42** They kept on devoting themselves to the apostles' teaching, to the shared life, to the breaking of bread and to the prayers.

**43** Awe came over everyone, and many wonders and signs were happening through the apostles.

**44** All who believed \[G-PISTIS\] were together and held everything in common.

**45** They sold their property and belongings and shared the money out among all, according to each person's need.

**46** Every day, spending time together in the temple and breaking bread in their homes, they took their food with joy and simple hearts,

**47** praising God and having the favour \[G-CHARIS\] of all the people. And day by day the Lord added to them those who were being saved \[G-SOZO\].

### Chapter 3

**1** Peter and John were going up to the temple at the hour of prayer, three in the afternoon.

**2** A man who had been lame from birth was being carried there. Every day they set him at the temple gate called Beautiful to ask for gifts from those going in.

**3** When he saw Peter and John about to go into the temple, he asked them for a gift.

**4** Peter looked straight at him, as John did too, and said, "Look at us."

**5** He paid attention to them, expecting to get something from them.

**6** Peter said, "I have no silver or gold, but what I do have I give you: in the name of Jesus Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] the Nazarene, walk."

**7** He took him by the right hand and raised him up, and at once his feet and ankles became firm.

**8** He jumped up, stood, and began to walk, and he went into the temple with them, walking and jumping and praising God.

**9** All the people saw him walking and praising God,

**10** and they recognised him as the man who used to sit begging at the Beautiful Gate of the temple, and they were filled with wonder and astonishment at what had happened to him.

**11** As he held on to Peter and John, all the people ran together to them in the colonnade called Solomon's, completely amazed.

**12** Seeing this, Peter answered the people: "People of Israel, why are you amazed at this? Why are you staring at us as if we had made him walk by our own power or devotion?

**13** The God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, the God of our fathers, has glorified \[G-DOXA\] his servant Jesus, whom you handed over and disowned in front of Pilate, when Pilate had decided to let him go.

**14** You disowned the Holy \[G-HAGIOS\] and Righteous \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\] One and asked for a murderer to be given to you,

**15** and you killed the leader of life \[G-ZOE\], whom God raised from the dead. We are witnesses of this.

**16** By faith \[G-PISTIS\] in his name, his name has made this man strong, whom you see and know. The faith \[G-PISTIS\] that comes through him has given him this complete health in front of all of you.

**17** And now, brothers, I know that you acted in ignorance, as your rulers did too.

**18** But what God announced beforehand through the mouth of all the prophets, that his Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] would suffer, he has fulfilled in this way.

**19** So repent \[G-METANOIA\] and turn back, so that your sins may be wiped away,

**20** so that seasons of relief may come from the Lord's presence, and he may send the Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] appointed for you, Jesus,

**21** whom heaven must keep until the time of the restoration of all things, of which God spoke through the mouth of his holy prophets from long ago.

**22** Moses said: 'The Lord God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among your brothers. Listen to him in everything he tells you.

**23** And every person who does not listen to that prophet will be cut off from the people.'

**24** And all the prophets from Samuel on, as many as spoke, also announced these days.

**25** You are the sons of the prophets and of the covenant \[G-DIATHEKE\] that God made with your fathers when he said to Abraham: 'In your offspring all the families of the earth will be blessed.'

**26** God raised up his servant and sent him to you first, to bless you by turning each of you from your wicked ways."

*Review note: Acts 1 to 3 were drafted in WABV wording, from the Greek, using the fixed choices set out in the introduction to this book, and with the renderings of Luke carried over. They are the preface and the last instructions (1:1-5), the question about the kingdom and the ascension (1:6-11), the upper room and the choosing of Matthias in the place of Judas (1:12-26), Pentecost and the speaking in other languages (2:1-13), the speech of Peter (2:14-36), the response and the first assembly of believers (2:37-47), the healing of the lame man at the Beautiful Gate (3:1-10) and the second speech of Peter in the portico of Solomon (3:11-26). The numbering is the usual numbering. No new G- marker is used in this part, and the markers are G-PNEUMA (1:2-8, 1:16, 2:4-38), G-BASILEIA (1:3, 1:6), G-SABBATON (1:12), G-SOZO (2:21, 2:40, 2:47), G-PSYCHE (2:27), G-HAGIOS (3:14), G-ZOE (2:28, 3:15), G-CHRISTOS (2:31, 2:36, 3:6, 3:18-20), G-METANOIA (2:38, 3:19), G-PISTIS (2:44, 3:16), G-CHARIS (2:47), G-DOXA (3:13), G-DIKAIOSUNE (3:14) and G-DIATHEKE (3:25). In 1:4 "shared a meal" translates *sunalizomenos*, which may also be read "was staying with them" or "was gathering with them", and the footnote records the matter. In 1:15 "the brothers" is the reading of the critical text, and the Byzantine tradition has "the disciples". In 1:14 some Western manuscripts add "and children" after "the women". In 1:18 "burst open" translates *elakesen*, and the account differs from Matthew 27:5, and the footnote records the matter. In 2:9 "Judea" in the list of peoples has been questioned, since it does not fit the order of the list, and other readings ("Syria", "India", "Armenia") are found in the early writers, and the footnote records the matter. In 2:3-4 "tongues" and "languages" translate the same Greek word, *glossai*, and the footnote records the matter. In 2:15 "nine in the morning" translates "the third hour of the day". In 2:17-21 the quotation is Joel 3:1-5 in the Greek (Joel 2:28-32 in the English), and it follows the Greek, and the footnote records that the Greek has "in the last days" where the Hebrew has "afterwards". In 2:25-28 the quotation is Psalm 16:8-11 as in the Greek. In 2:27 "let your Holy One see decay" translates *ten diaphthoran*, which may also be read "the pit". In 2:42 "the shared life" translates *te koinonia*, and the footnote records the matter. In 2:47 "added to them those who were being saved" is the reading of the critical text (*epi to auto*, "together", being joined with 3:1 in some manuscripts), and the Byzantine tradition adds "to the assembly", and the footnote records the matter. In 3:6 some manuscripts omit "the Nazarene". In 3:13 and 3:26 "servant" translates *paida*, and the footnote records that it may also be read "child" or "son", and that it is the title of the Servant of Isaiah 52 and 53. In 3:15 "the leader of life" translates *ton archegon tes zoes*, and the footnote records that it may also be read "author" or "pioneer". In 3:20 "appointed" is the reading of the critical text (*prokecheirismenon*), and some manuscripts read *prokekerugmenon*, "announced beforehand", and the footnote records the matter. In 3:22-23 the quotation is Deuteronomy 18:15 and 18:19 with Leviticus 23:29. In 3:25 the quotation is Genesis 22:18 with 12:3. In 3:19 "turn back" translates *epistrepsate*. In 1:6-8 and 1:9-11 the words on the kingdom, the witness and the return of Jesus are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. In 2:22-24 and 2:36 the words on the foreknowledge of God and on Jesus made Lord and Christ are rendered as written. In 1:8 "the end of the earth" is the Greek of the text. The word *Ioudaioi* is rendered "Jews" in 2:5 and 2:11, and the team should decide the rendering. In 1:14, 1:15, 1:16, 2:29, 2:37, 3:17 and 3:22 "brothers" translates *adelphoi*, and the team should decide whether it is to include the sisters. There are no departures from the critical Greek text in chapters 1 to 3. The Greek is hard in some places, including 1:4, 1:18-19, 2:9, 2:23, 2:42, 3:13, 3:16 and 3:20. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The section needs a check against the Greek by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Two: Chapters 4 to 6, the council, the shared life and the seven

### Chapter 4

**1** While they were still speaking to the people, the priests, the captain of the temple guard and the Sadducees came up to them,

**2** furious that they were teaching the people and announcing, in the case of Jesus, the resurrection of the dead.

**3** They arrested them and put them in custody until the next day, since it was already evening.

**4** But many of those who had heard the word believed \[G-PISTIS\], and the number of the men came to about five thousand.

**5** Next day their rulers, elders and scribes met in Jerusalem,

**6** with Annas the high priest, Caiaphas, John, Alexander and all who belonged to the high priest's family.

**7** They had them stand in the middle and asked, "By what power or in what name have you done this?"

**8** Then Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\], said to them, "Rulers of the people and elders,

**9** if we are being cross-examined today about a good deed done to a sick man, and how he has been made well,

**10** then you should all know, and all the people of Israel, that this man stands here before you in good health by the name of Jesus Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] the Nazarene, whom you crucified and whom God raised from the dead.

**11** He is the stone you builders rejected, which has become the cornerstone.

**12** Salvation \[G-SOZO\] is found in no one else. There is no other name under heaven given among people by which we must be saved \[G-SOZO\]."

**13** When they saw how openly Peter and John spoke, and realised that they were uneducated and untrained people, they were astonished, and they recognised that they had been with Jesus.

**14** But when they saw the man who had been healed standing there with them, they could say nothing against it.

**15** So they ordered them to leave the council chamber and conferred with one another.

**16** "What are we to do with these men?" they said. "That a notable sign has happened through them is clear to everyone living in Jerusalem, and we cannot deny it.

**17** But so that it does not spread further among the people, let us warn them to say nothing more to anyone in this name."

**18** So they called them in and ordered them not to speak or teach at all in the name of Jesus.

**19** But Peter and John answered them, "Judge for yourselves whether it is right before God to listen to you rather than to God.

**20** For we cannot stop speaking about what we have seen and heard."

**21** After further threats they let them go, since they could find no way to punish them because of the people, for everyone was praising God for what had happened.

**22** The man on whom this sign of healing had been done was over forty years old.

**23** When they were released, they went to their own people and reported everything the chief priests and elders had said to them.

**24** When they heard it, they lifted their voices together to God and said, "Master, you who made the heaven and the earth and the sea and everything in them,

**25** you who said through the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] by the mouth of our father David, your servant: 'Why did the nations rage and the peoples plan empty things?

**26** The kings of the earth took their stand, and the rulers gathered together against the Lord and against his Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\].'

**27** For in this city they really were gathered against your holy \[G-HAGIOS\] servant Jesus, whom you anointed: both Herod and Pontius Pilate, with the nations and the peoples of Israel,

**28** to do whatever your hand and your plan had decided beforehand would happen.

**29** And now, Lord, look at their threats and give your slaves complete boldness to speak your word,

**30** while you stretch out your hand to heal and signs and wonders happen through the name of your holy \[G-HAGIOS\] servant Jesus."

**31** When they had prayed, the place where they were gathered was shaken, and they were all filled with the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] and spoke the word of God with boldness.

**32** The whole body of believers \[G-PISTIS\] was of one heart and soul, and no one claimed that any of his possessions was his own, but everything was held in common.

**33** With great power the apostles gave their testimony to the resurrection of the Lord Jesus, and great favour \[G-CHARIS\] was on them all.

**34** There was no one in need among them, for as many as owned fields or houses sold them and brought the money from the sales

**35** and put it at the apostles' feet, and it was shared out to each according to need.

**36** Joseph, a Levite from Cyprus, whom the apostles called Barnabas (which means "son of encouragement"),

**37** sold a field that he owned, brought the money and put it at the apostles' feet.

### Chapter 5

**1** But a man named Ananias, with his wife Sapphira, sold a piece of property,

**2** and with his wife's knowledge he kept back some of the proceeds, brought only a part and put it at the apostles' feet.

**3** Peter said, "Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] and to keep back some of the price of the land?

**4** While it remained unsold, did it not remain yours? And after it was sold, was it not at your disposal? Why have you put this deed in your heart? You have not lied to people but to God."

**5** When Ananias heard these words, he fell down and died, and great fear came on everyone who heard of it.

**6** The young men got up, wrapped him up, carried him out and buried him.

**7** About three hours later his wife came in, not knowing what had happened.

**8** Peter said to her, "Tell me, did you sell the land for this amount?" She said, "Yes, for that."

**9** Peter said to her, "Why did you agree together to test the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] of the Lord? Look, the feet of those who have buried your husband are at the door, and they will carry you out."

**10** At once she fell down at his feet and died. When the young men came in they found her dead, and they carried her out and buried her beside her husband.

**11** Great fear came on the whole assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\] and on everyone who heard of these things.

**12** Many signs and wonders were happening among the people through the hands of the apostles. And they were all together in Solomon's Colonnade.

**13** None of the rest dared to join them, but the people held them in high honour.

**14** More and more believers \[G-PISTIS\] were added to the Lord, crowds of both men and women,

**15** so that they even carried the sick out into the streets and laid them on beds and mats, so that when Peter came by, at least his shadow might fall on some of them.

**16** The crowd from the towns around Jerusalem also gathered, bringing the sick and those troubled by unclean spirits \[G-PNEUMA\], and they were all healed.

**17** Then the high priest and all who were with him (the party of the Sadducees) rose up, filled with jealousy,

**18** and they arrested the apostles and put them in the public prison.

**19** But during the night an angel of the Lord opened the prison doors, led them out and said,

**20** "Go, stand in the temple and tell the people all the words of this life \[G-ZOE\]."

**21** When they heard this, they went into the temple at daybreak and began to teach. Now the high priest and those with him arrived and called together the council and the whole senate of the people of Israel, and sent to the prison to have them brought.

**22** But the officers who went did not find them in the prison, and they returned and reported,

**23** "We found the prison securely locked and the guards standing at the doors, but when we opened them we found no one inside."

**24** When the captain of the temple and the chief priests heard these words, they were perplexed about them, wondering what this could turn into.

**25** Then someone came and reported, "Look, the men you put in prison are standing in the temple and teaching the people."

**26** Then the captain went with the officers and brought them, but without force, for they were afraid of being stoned by the people.

**27** When they had brought them, they made them stand before the council. The high priest questioned them,

**28** saying, "We gave you strict orders not to teach in this name, and look, you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching and you intend to bring this man's blood on us."

**29** But Peter and the apostles answered, "We must obey God rather than people.

**30** The God of our fathers raised up Jesus, whom you killed by hanging him on a tree.

**31** God exalted him at his right hand as leader and Saviour, to give repentance \[G-METANOIA\] to Israel and forgiveness of sins.

**32** And we are witnesses of these things, and so is the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\], whom God has given to those who obey him."

**33** When they heard this, they were enraged and wanted to kill them.

**34** But a Pharisee in the council named Gamaliel, a teacher of the law \[G-NOMOS\] respected by all the people, stood up and ordered the men to be put outside for a little while.

**35** And he said to them, "People of Israel, take care what you are about to do with these men.

**36** For some time ago Theudas rose up, claiming to be somebody, and a number of men, about four hundred, joined him. He was killed, and all who followed him were scattered and came to nothing.

**37** After him Judas the Galilean rose up in the days of the census and drew away people after him. He too perished, and all who followed him were scattered.

**38** So in the present case I tell you: keep away from these men and let them alone, for if this plan or this work is from people, it will be overthrown,

**39** but if it is from God, you will not be able to overthrow them. You might even be found fighting against God."

## Part Four: Chapters 10 to 12, Cornelius, Antioch and Herod

### Chapter 10

**2** a devout man who feared God, together with his whole household. He gave generously to the people and prayed to God constantly.

**3** About three in the afternoon he clearly saw in a vision an angel of God come in to him and say, "Cornelius."

**4** He stared at him in terror and said, "What is it, Lord?" The angel said, "Your prayers and your gifts to the poor have gone up as a memorial before God.

**5** Now send men to Joppa and fetch a man named Simon, who is called Peter.

**6** He is staying with Simon the tanner, whose house is beside the sea."

**7** When the angel who spoke to him had gone, he called two of his household slaves and a devout soldier from among those who attended him,

**8** explained everything to them and sent them to Joppa.

**9** Next day, while they were on their way and close to the city, Peter went up on the roof to pray at about noon.

**10** He became hungry and wanted something to eat. While the meal was being prepared, he fell into a trance.

**11** He saw heaven opened and something like a large sheet coming down, being lowered to the earth by its four corners.

**12** In it were all the four-footed animals and reptiles of the earth and the birds of the sky.

**13** A voice came to him: "Get up, Peter, kill and eat."

**14** But Peter said, "Certainly not, Lord. I have never eaten anything common or unclean."

**15** The voice came to him again, a second time: "What God has made clean, do not call common."

**16** This happened three times, and then the sheet was taken up into the sky at once.

**17** While Peter was puzzling over what the vision he had seen could mean, suddenly the men sent by Cornelius, having asked their way to Simon's house, were standing at the gate.

**18** They called out to ask whether Simon, who was called Peter, was staying there.

**19** While Peter was still thinking over the vision, the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] said to him, "Look, three men are looking for you.

**20** Get up, go down and go with them without hesitation, because I have sent them."

**21** So Peter went down to the men and said, "Look, I am the one you are looking for. What is the reason you have come?"

**22** They said, "Cornelius the centurion, a righteous \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\] man who fears God and is well spoken of by the whole nation of the Jews, was instructed by a holy \[G-HAGIOS\] angel to send for you to come to his house and to hear what you have to say."

**23** So he invited them in and gave them lodging. Next day he got up and went with them, and some of the brothers from Joppa went along with him.

**24** The following day he arrived in Caesarea. Cornelius was expecting them and had called together his relatives and close friends.

**25** When Peter came in, Cornelius met him, fell at his feet and bowed to him.

**26** But Peter raised him up, saying, "Stand up. I too am only a human being."

**27** Talking with him, he went in and found many people gathered.

**28** He said to them, "You know that it is against our law for a Jew to associate with or visit someone of another nation. But God has shown me that I must call no person common or unclean.

**29** So I came without objection when I was sent for. I ask, then, why you sent for me."

**30** Cornelius said, "Four days ago, at this very hour, three in the afternoon, I was praying in my house, and suddenly a man in shining clothing stood in front of me

**31** and said, 'Cornelius, your prayer has been heard, and your gifts have been remembered before God.

**32** So send to Joppa and invite Simon, who is called Peter. He is staying in the house of Simon the tanner, beside the sea.'

**33** So I sent for you at once, and you were kind enough to come. Now we are all here in God's presence to hear everything the Lord has commanded you to say."

**34** Then Peter opened his mouth and said, "I now really understand that God shows no favouritism,

**36** He sent his word to the sons of Israel, announcing the good news of peace \[G-EIRENE\] through Jesus Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\]. This one is Lord of all.

**37** You yourselves know the thing that happened throughout all Judea, starting from Galilee after the baptism that John proclaimed:

**39** We are witnesses of everything he did, both in the country of the Jews and in Jerusalem. They killed him by hanging him on a tree,

**41** not by all the people but by witnesses whom God had chosen beforehand, by us, who ate and drank with him after he rose from the dead.

**42** He ordered us to proclaim to the people and to testify that he is the one appointed by God as judge of the living and the dead.

**44** While Peter was still saying these things, the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] fell on all who were listening to the word.

**45** The believers from the circumcised who had come with Peter were astonished that the gift of the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] had been poured out even on the nations,

**48** And he ordered them to be baptised in the name of Jesus Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\]. Then they asked him to stay on for some days.

## Part Six: Chapters 16 to 18, the second journey

### Chapter 16

**1** He came also to Derbe and to Lystra. A disciple was there named Timothy, the son of a Jewish woman who believed \[G-PISTIS\], but of a Greek father.

**2** The brothers in Lystra and Iconium spoke well of him.

**3** Paul wanted him to go out with him, and he took him and circumcised him because of the Jews in those places, for they all knew that his father was a Greek.

**4** As they went through the cities, they handed on to them for observance the decisions reached by the apostles and elders in Jerusalem.

**5** So the assemblies \[G-EKKLESIA\] grew stronger in the faith \[G-PISTIS\] and increased in numbers every day.

**6** They went through the region of Phrygia and Galatia, since they had been prevented by the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] from speaking the word in Asia.

**7** When they came opposite Mysia, they tried to go into Bithynia, but the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] of Jesus would not allow them.

**8** So they passed by Mysia and went down to Troas.

**9** During the night Paul saw a vision: a man of Macedonia was standing there begging him, "Cross over to Macedonia and help us."

**10** As soon as he had seen the vision, we looked for a way to go on to Macedonia, concluding that God had called us to announce the good news \[G-EUANGELION\] to them.

**11** So we put to sea from Troas and ran a straight course to Samothrace, and the next day to Neapolis,

**12** and from there to Philippi, which is a leading city of the district of Macedonia and a Roman colony. We stayed in this city for some days.

**13** On the Sabbath \[G-SABBATON\] day we went outside the gate to the river, where we thought there would be a place of prayer, and we sat down and spoke to the women who had gathered.

**14** One woman, named Lydia, from the city of Thyatira, a dealer in purple cloth, a worshipper of God, was listening. The Lord opened her heart to pay attention to what Paul was saying.

**15** When she and her household had been baptised, she urged us: "If you have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, come and stay at my house." And she persuaded us.

**16** Once, as we were going to the place of prayer, a slave girl met us who had a spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] of divination and earned her owners a great deal by fortune-telling.

**17** She followed Paul and us, shouting, "These men are slaves of the Most High God, who announce to you the way of salvation \[G-SOZO\]."

**18** She did this for many days. Paul, worn out by it, turned and said to the spirit, "I order you in the name of Jesus Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] to come out of her." And it came out that very moment.

**19** When her owners saw that their hope of profit was gone, they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them to the authorities in the marketplace.

**20** They brought them to the magistrates and said, "These men are Jews, and they are throwing our city into confusion.

**21** They are promoting customs that it is not lawful for us as Romans to accept or practise."

**22** The crowd joined in the attack on them, and the magistrates tore the clothes off them and ordered them to be beaten with rods.

**23** After inflicting many blows on them, they threw them into prison and ordered the jailer to guard them securely.

**24** Receiving such an order, he put them in the inner prison and fastened their feet in the stocks.

**25** About midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them,

**26** when suddenly there was such a great earthquake that the foundations of the prison were shaken. At once all the doors were opened, and everyone's chains came loose.

**27** The jailer woke up and saw the prison doors open. He drew his sword and was about to kill himself, thinking that the prisoners had escaped.

**28** But Paul shouted with a loud voice, "Do not harm yourself, for we are all here."

**29** The jailer called for lights and rushed in, and shaking with fear he fell down in front of Paul and Silas.

**30** Then he brought them outside and said, "Sirs, what must I do to be saved \[G-SOZO\]?"

**31** They said, "Believe \[G-PISTIS\] in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved \[G-SOZO\], you and your household."

**32** They spoke the word of the Lord to him and to everyone in his house.

**33** He took them that same hour of the night and washed their wounds, and at once he was baptised, he and all his household.

**34** Then he brought them up into his house and set a meal before them, and he rejoiced with his whole household that he had come to believe \[G-PISTIS\] in God.

**35** When day came, the magistrates sent the officers with the message, "Let those men go."

**36** The jailer reported these words to Paul: "The magistrates have sent word that you are to be released. So come out now and go in peace \[G-EIRENE\]."

**37** But Paul said to them, "They beat us in public without a trial, though we are Roman citizens, and threw us into prison. And now do they want to send us away in secret? No. Let them come themselves and take us out."

**38** The officers reported these words to the magistrates, and they were afraid when they heard that they were Roman citizens.

**39** So they came and apologised to them, and they took them out and asked them to leave the city.

**40** After leaving the prison they went to Lydia's house, and when they had seen the brothers they encouraged them and left.

### Chapter 17

**1** After they had travelled through Amphipolis and Apollonia, they came to Thessalonica, where there was a synagogue of the Jews.

**2** Paul went in, as was his custom, and over three Sabbaths \[G-SABBATON\] he argued with them from the scriptures,

**3** explaining and proving that it was necessary for the Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] to suffer and to rise from the dead, and saying, "This is the Christ: Jesus, whom I am announcing to you."

**4** Some of them were persuaded and joined Paul and Silas, along with a large number of devout Greeks and quite a few of the leading women.

**5** But the Jews, filled with jealousy, took some worthless men from the marketplace, gathered a mob and set the city in an uproar. They attacked the house of Jason and tried to bring them out to the people.

**6** When they could not find them, they dragged Jason and some of the brothers before the city authorities, shouting, "These people who have turned the world upside down have come here too,

**7** and Jason has taken them in. They are all acting against the decrees of Caesar, saying that there is another king, Jesus."

**8** The crowd and the city authorities were alarmed when they heard this.

**9** After taking security from Jason and the others, they let them go.

**10** The brothers at once sent Paul and Silas away by night to Berea, and when they arrived they went into the Jewish synagogue.

**11** These people were more open-minded than those in Thessalonica. They received the word with great eagerness and examined the scriptures every day to see whether these things were so.

**12** Many of them therefore believed \[G-PISTIS\], and so did a number of Greek women of high standing and of men.

**13** But when the Jews from Thessalonica learned that the word of God had been proclaimed by Paul in Berea also, they came there too, to stir up and agitate the crowds.

**14** Then the brothers at once sent Paul off toward the sea, but Silas and Timothy stayed on there.

**15** Those who escorted Paul brought him as far as Athens, and, after receiving an instruction for Silas and Timothy to join him as quickly as possible, they left.

**16** While Paul was waiting for them in Athens, his spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] was provoked within him as he saw that the city was full of idols.

**17** So he argued in the synagogue with the Jews and the devout people, and every day in the marketplace with those who happened to be there.

**18** Some of the Epicurean and Stoic philosophers also debated with him. Some said, "What is this scrap-collector trying to say?" Others said, "He seems to be a preacher of foreign divinities," because he was announcing Jesus and the resurrection.

**19** They took hold of him and brought him to the Areopagus, saying, "May we know what this new teaching is that you are presenting?

**20** For you are bringing some strange things to our ears, and we want to know what they mean."

**21** (All the Athenians and the foreigners living there spent their time on nothing except telling or hearing something new.)

**22** So Paul stood in the middle of the Areopagus and said: "People of Athens, I see that in every way you are very religious.

**23** For as I walked around and looked at the things you worship, I found an altar with this inscription: 'To an unknown god.' So what you worship without knowing it, this I announce to you.

**24** The God who made the world and everything in it, being Lord of heaven and earth, does not live in temples made by hands,

**25** nor is he served by human hands as if he needed anything, since he himself gives life \[G-ZOE\] and breath and everything to all.

**26** From one he made every nation of humanity to live on the whole face of the earth, having fixed the seasons they were given and the boundaries of where they live,

**27** so that they would seek God, in the hope that they might feel their way to him and find him, though he is not far from each one of us.

**28** For 'In him we live and move and exist,' as some of your own poets have said, 'For we too are his offspring.'

**29** Since we are God's offspring, we ought not to think that the divine nature is like gold or silver or stone, shaped by human skill and invention.

**30** God overlooked the times of ignorance, but now he commands all people everywhere to repent \[G-METANOIA\],

**31** because he has set a day on which he is going to judge the world with justice \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\] by a man whom he has appointed, and he has given proof of this to everyone by raising him from the dead."

**33** So Paul left them.

**34** But some men joined him and believed \[G-PISTIS\], among them Dionysius, a member of the Areopagus, and a woman named Damaris, and others with them.

### Chapter 18

**13** saying, "This man is persuading people to worship God in a way that is against the law \[G-NOMOS\]."

**17** They all seized Sosthenes, the leader of the synagogue, and beat him in front of the tribunal. But Gallio paid no attention to any of this.

**19** They arrived at Ephesus, and he left them there, but he himself went into the synagogue and argued with the Jews.

**20** When they asked him to stay for a longer time, he did not consent,

**22** When he had landed at Caesarea, he went up and greeted the assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\], and then went down to Antioch.

**23** After spending some time there, he left and travelled from place to place through the region of Galatia and Phrygia, strengthening all the disciples.

**24** Now a Jew named Apollos, a native of Alexandria, came to Ephesus. He was an eloquent man, powerful in the scriptures.

**27** When he wanted to cross to Achaia, the brothers encouraged him and wrote to the disciples to welcome him. When he arrived, he was a great help to those who had believed \[G-PISTIS\] through grace \[G-CHARIS\],

## Part Nine: Chapters 25 to 28, Festus, Agrippa, the voyage and Rome

### Chapter 25

**1** Three days after arriving in the province, Festus went up from Caesarea to Jerusalem.

**2** The chief priests and the leading Jews set out their case against Paul, and they urged him,

**3** asking as a favour against Paul that he have him sent to Jerusalem, while they prepared an ambush to kill him on the way.

**4** Festus answered that Paul was being kept at Caesarea and that he himself would be going there shortly.

**5** "So let those of you who are in authority come down with me," he said, "and if there is anything wrong about the man, let them accuse him."

**6** After spending not more than eight or ten days with them, he went down to Caesarea, and the next day he took his seat on the tribunal and ordered Paul to be brought in.

**7** When he arrived, the Jews who had come down from Jerusalem stood around him, bringing many serious charges that they could not prove.

**8** Paul said in his defence, "I have committed no offence against the law \[G-NOMOS\] of the Jews, or against the temple, or against Caesar."

**9** But Festus, wanting to do the Jews a favour, said to Paul, "Are you willing to go up to Jerusalem and be tried there before me on these charges?"

**10** Paul said, "I am standing before Caesar's tribunal, where I ought to be tried. I have done the Jews no wrong, as you yourself know very well.

**11** If, then, I am guilty and have done anything that deserves death, I do not refuse to die. But if there is nothing in their charges against me, no one can hand me over to them. I appeal to Caesar."

**12** Then Festus conferred with his council and answered, "You have appealed to Caesar. To Caesar you will go."

**13** After some days King Agrippa and Bernice arrived at Caesarea to pay their respects to Festus.

**14** As they stayed there for many days, Festus laid Paul's case before the king: "There is a man here whom Felix left as a prisoner,

**15** and when I was in Jerusalem the chief priests and the elders of the Jews set out their case against him and asked for a sentence of condemnation.

**16** I answered them that it is not the Roman custom to hand anyone over before the accused has met his accusers face to face and has had the opportunity to defend himself against the charge.

**17** So when they came together here, I did not delay, but the next day I took my seat on the tribunal and ordered the man to be brought.

**18** When the accusers stood up, they did not charge him with any of the crimes I had expected.

**19** Instead they had some points of dispute with him about their own religion and about a certain Jesus, who had died, but whom Paul claimed to be alive.

**20** Since I was at a loss how to investigate these questions, I asked whether he wanted to go to Jerusalem and be tried there on them.

**21** But when Paul appealed to be held in custody for the decision of the emperor, I ordered him to be held until I could send him to Caesar."

**22** Agrippa said to Festus, "I would like to hear the man myself." "Tomorrow," he said, "you will hear him."

**23** So the next day Agrippa and Bernice came with great pomp and entered the audience hall with the military tribunes and the leading men of the city. At the order of Festus, Paul was brought in.

**24** Festus said, "King Agrippa and all who are here with us, you see this man about whom the whole Jewish people appealed to me, both in Jerusalem and here, shouting that he ought not to live any longer.

**25** But I found that he had done nothing deserving death, and since he himself appealed to the emperor, I decided to send him.

**26** But I have nothing definite to write to my lord about him. So I have brought him before you all, and especially before you, King Agrippa, so that after we have examined him, I may have something to write.

**27** For it seems to me unreasonable to send a prisoner without indicating the charges against him."

### Chapter 26

**1** Agrippa said to Paul, "You are permitted to speak for yourself." Then Paul stretched out his hand and made his defence:

**2** "I count myself fortunate, King Agrippa, that it is before you that I am to make my defence today against all the accusations of the Jews,

**3** especially because you are familiar with all the customs and disputes of the Jews. So I beg you to listen to me patiently.

**4** My way of life from my youth, which was spent from the beginning among my own nation and in Jerusalem, is known to all the Jews.

**5** They have known me for a long time, if they are willing to testify, that I lived as a Pharisee, according to the strictest party of our religion.

**6** And now I stand here on trial because of my hope in the promise made by God to our fathers,

**7** the promise that our twelve tribes hope to reach as they worship earnestly night and day. It is for this hope, O king, that I am accused by Jews.

**8** Why is it thought incredible by any of you that God raises the dead?

**9** I myself thought that I ought to do many things against the name of Jesus of Nazareth.

**10** And I did so in Jerusalem. I shut up many of the holy ones \[G-HAGIOS\] in prison after receiving authority from the chief priests, and when they were put to death I cast my vote against them.

**11** I punished them often in all the synagogues and tried to force them to blaspheme, and in my raging fury against them I pursued them even to foreign cities.

**12** On one such journey I was going to Damascus with the authority and commission of the chief priests.

**13** At midday, O king, I saw on the road a light from heaven, brighter than the sun, shining around me and those travelling with me.

**14** When we had all fallen to the ground, I heard a voice saying to me in the Hebrew language, 'Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me? It is hard for you to kick against the goads.'

**15** I said, 'Who are you, Lord?' And the Lord said, 'I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.

**16** But get up and stand on your feet, for I have appeared to you for this purpose, to appoint you as a servant and a witness of what you have seen of me and of what I will show you,

**17** rescuing you from the people and from the nations, to whom I am sending you

**18** to open their eyes, so that they turn from darkness to light and from the power of Satan to God, so that they may receive forgiveness of sins and a place among those who are sanctified \[G-HAGIOS\] by faith \[G-PISTIS\] in me.'

**19** So, King Agrippa, I was not disobedient to the heavenly vision,

**20** but declared first to those in Damascus, then in Jerusalem and all the region of Judea, and also to the nations, that they should repent \[G-METANOIA\] and turn to God, doing deeds that fit their repentance \[G-METANOIA\].

**21** For this reason the Jews seized me in the temple and tried to kill me.

**22** To this day I have had help from God, and so I stand here testifying to small and great alike, saying nothing except what the prophets and Moses said would happen:

**23** that the Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] would suffer, and that, as the first to rise from the dead, he would proclaim light to our people and to the nations."

**24** As he was saying this in his defence, Festus shouted with a loud voice, "Paul, you are out of your mind. Your great learning is driving you mad."

**25** But Paul said, "I am not out of my mind, most excellent Festus, but I am speaking words of truth and good sense.

**26** For the king knows about these things, and I speak to him boldly. I am convinced that none of this has escaped his notice, for this was not done in a corner.

**27** King Agrippa, do you believe \[G-PISTIS\] the prophets? I know that you believe."

**28** Agrippa said to Paul, "In a short time you are persuading me to make a Christian \[G-CHRISTOS\] of you."

**29** Paul said, "Whether in a short time or a long, I pray to God that not only you but also all who are listening to me today might become what I am, except for these chains."

**30** Then the king rose, and the governor and Bernice and those sitting with them.

**31** When they had left, they said to one another, "This man is doing nothing that deserves death or imprisonment."

**32** And Agrippa said to Festus, "This man could have been set free if he had not appealed to Caesar."

### Chapter 27

**1** When it was decided that we should sail for Italy, they handed Paul and some other prisoners over to a centurion named Julius, of the Augustan Cohort.

**2** We boarded a ship from Adramyttium that was about to sail to the ports along the coast of Asia, and put to sea. Aristarchus, a Macedonian from Thessalonica, was with us.

**3** The next day we put in at Sidon, and Julius treated Paul kindly and allowed him to go to his friends and be cared for.

**4** From there we put to sea and sailed under the lee of Cyprus, because the winds were against us.

**5** After sailing across the open sea off Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came to Myra in Lycia.

**6** There the centurion found a ship from Alexandria sailing for Italy and put us on board.

**7** We sailed slowly for a number of days and had difficulty reaching Cnidus, and since the wind did not let us go on, we sailed under the lee of Crete off Salmone.

**8** With difficulty we coasted along it and came to a place called Fair Havens, near the city of Lasea.

**9** Much time had passed and the voyage was already dangerous, because even the Fast had already gone by, so Paul advised them,

**10** saying, "Men, I can see that this voyage is going to end in damage and heavy loss, not only of the cargo and the ship but also of our lives \[G-PSYCHE\]."

**11** But the centurion was more persuaded by the pilot and the owner of the ship than by what Paul said.

**12** Since the harbour was not suitable for spending the winter, the majority decided to put out to sea from there, in the hope that somehow they could reach Phoenix, a harbour of Crete facing southwest and northwest, and spend the winter there.

**13** When a gentle south wind began to blow, they thought they had achieved their purpose, so they weighed anchor and sailed along Crete, close to the shore.

**14** But soon a violent wind, called the Northeaster, rushed down from the island.

**15** The ship was caught and could not face the wind, so we gave way and were driven along.

**16** Running under the lee of a small island called Cauda, we were barely able to get the ship's boat under control.

**17** After hoisting it up, they used supports to bind the ship together. Then, afraid of running aground on the Syrtis, they lowered the sea anchor and so were driven along.

**18** We were being violently tossed by the storm, and the next day they began to throw the cargo overboard.

**19** On the third day they threw the ship's tackle overboard with their own hands.

**20** When neither sun nor stars appeared for many days and no small storm lay on us, all hope that we would be saved \[G-SOZO\] was finally given up.

**21** They had gone a long time without food, and Paul stood up among them and said, "Men, you should have listened to me and not set sail from Crete, and so have avoided this damage and loss.

**22** Yet now I urge you to take courage, for there will be no loss of life \[G-PSYCHE\] among you, only of the ship.

**23** For this very night an angel of the God to whom I belong and whom I worship stood beside me,

**24** and said, 'Do not be afraid, Paul. You must stand before Caesar, and look, God has granted you all those sailing with you.'

**25** So take courage, men, for I have faith \[G-PISTIS\] in God that it will happen just as I was told.

**26** But we must run aground on some island."

**27** On the fourteenth night, as we were being driven across the Adriatic Sea, about midnight the sailors sensed that land was near.

**28** They took soundings and found twenty fathoms, and a little further on they took soundings again and found fifteen fathoms.

**29** Afraid that we would run on the rocks, they dropped four anchors from the stern and prayed for day to come.

**30** The sailors were trying to escape from the ship and had lowered the ship's boat into the sea, pretending to lay out anchors from the bow,

**31** when Paul said to the centurion and the soldiers, "Unless these men stay in the ship, you cannot be saved \[G-SOZO\]."

**32** Then the soldiers cut away the ropes of the ship's boat and let it drift away.

**33** Just before day began to break, Paul urged them all to take some food. "Today is the fourteenth day that you have been waiting in suspense and going without food, having taken nothing.

**34** So I urge you to take some food. It will help you survive, for not a hair will be lost from the head of any of you."

**35** When he had said this, he took bread and gave thanks to God in front of them all, broke it and began to eat.

**36** Then they were all encouraged and took some food themselves.

**37** (There were two hundred and seventy-six of us in the ship.)

**38** When they had eaten enough, they lightened the ship by throwing the wheat into the sea.

**39** When day came, they did not recognise the land, but they noticed a bay with a beach, and they planned, if they could, to run the ship ashore there.

**40** So they cut loose the anchors and left them in the sea, at the same time untying the ropes that held the steering oars. Then they hoisted the foresail to the wind and made for the beach.

**41** But they struck a sandbank and ran the ship aground. The bow stuck fast and could not be moved, and the stern began to break up under the pounding of the waves.

**42** The soldiers planned to kill the prisoners, so that none of them could swim away and escape.

**43** But the centurion, wanting to save \[G-SOZO\] Paul, kept them from carrying out their plan. He ordered those who could swim to jump overboard first and get to land,

**44** and the rest to follow on planks or on pieces of the ship. And so it was that all reached land safely.

### Chapter 28

**1** Once we were safe, we learned that the island was called Malta.

**2** The local people showed us unusual kindness, for they lit a fire and welcomed all of us, because it had begun to rain and was cold.

**3** Paul gathered a bundle of sticks and put them on the fire, and a viper came out because of the heat and fastened on his hand.

**4** When the local people saw the creature hanging from his hand, they said to one another, "This man must be a murderer. Though he has escaped from the sea, Justice has not let him live."

**5** But he shook the creature off into the fire and suffered no harm.

**6** They were waiting for him to swell up or suddenly drop dead. But after waiting a long time and seeing nothing unusual happen to him, they changed their minds and said that he was a god.

**7** In the neighbourhood of that place were lands belonging to the chief man of the island, named Publius, who welcomed us and entertained us hospitably for three days.

**8** It happened that Publius's father was lying sick with fever and dysentery. Paul went in to him, prayed, laid his hands on him and healed him.

**9** After this, the rest of the people on the island who had diseases also came and were cured.

**10** They honoured us in many ways, and when we were about to sail, they put on board whatever we needed.

**11** After three months we set sail in a ship that had spent the winter on the island, a ship from Alexandria with the Twin Brothers as its figurehead.

**12** We put in at Syracuse and stayed there for three days.

**13** From there we sailed round and arrived at Rhegium. After one day a south wind sprang up, and on the second day we came to Puteoli.

**14** There we found brothers and were invited to stay with them for seven days. And so we came to Rome.

**15** The brothers there, when they heard about us, came as far as the Forum of Appius and Three Taverns to meet us. When Paul saw them, he thanked God and took courage.

**16** When we entered Rome, Paul was allowed to live by himself, with the soldier who guarded him.

**17** After three days he called together the local leaders of the Jews, and when they had gathered, he said to them, "Brothers, though I had done nothing against our people or the customs of our fathers, I was handed over as a prisoner from Jerusalem into the hands of the Romans.

**18** When they had examined me, they wanted to release me, because there was no ground for a death sentence in my case.

**19** But because the Jews objected, I was forced to appeal to Caesar, though I had no charge to bring against my own nation.

**20** For this reason, then, I have asked to see you and speak with you, since it is because of the hope of Israel that I am bound with this chain."

**21** They said to him, "We have received no letters from Judea about you, and none of the brothers coming from there has reported or said anything bad about you.

**22** But we want to hear from you what you think, for as for this sect, we know that it is spoken against everywhere."

**23** They arranged a day with him, and they came to him at his lodging in even larger numbers. From morning until evening he explained the matter to them, testifying to the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] of God and trying to convince them about Jesus from the Law \[G-NOMOS\] of Moses and from the Prophets.

**24** Some were convinced by what he said, but others did not believe \[G-PISTIS\].

**25** Disagreeing among themselves, they began to leave after Paul had made one last statement: "The Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] was right when he spoke through the prophet Isaiah to your fathers:

**26** 'Go to this people and say: You will hear and hear but never understand, and you will look and look but never see.

**27** For the heart of this people has grown dull, and with their ears they hear heavily, and they have shut their eyes, so that they would not see with their eyes and hear with their ears and understand with their heart and turn, and I would heal them.'

**28** So let it be known to you that this salvation \[G-SOZO\] of God has been sent to the nations. They will listen."

**31** proclaiming the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] of God and teaching about the Lord Jesus Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] with all boldness and without hindrance.

In 28:25-27 the quotation is Isaiah 6:9-10 as in the Greek, as in Matthew 13:14-15 and John 12:40. In 28:28 "the nations" translates *tois ethnesin*. In 28:30 "at his own expense" translates *en idio misthomati*, which may also be read "in his own rented house", and the footnote records the matter. In 28:31 "without hindrance" translates *akolutos*, the last word of the book. In 27:1 and 28:16 the "we" passage continues, and it ends at 28:16. The word *Ioudaioi* is rendered "Jews" in 25:2-24, 26:2-21, 28:17-19 and elsewhere, and the team should decide the rendering. In 28:14, 28:15, 28:17 and 28:21 "brothers" translates *adelphoi*, and the team should decide whether it is to include the sisters. There are no departures from the critical Greek text in chapters 25 to 28. The Greek is hard in some places, including 26:3, 26:28, 27:9-17, 27:27, 28:4, 28:11 and 28:30. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words of Paul on the hope of Israel and the resurrection (26:6-8, 28:20), "it is hard for you to kick against the goads" (26:14), the angel and the promise (27:23-24), the Isaiah quotation (28:26-27) and the last sentence of the book (28:30-31) are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. The section needs a check against the Greek by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked. With this part the book of Acts is complete in first draft.*
