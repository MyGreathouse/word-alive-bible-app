## 1 Kings 1-2: David's Last Days and Solomon's Throne

### Chapter 1

**1** King David was old, well on in years, and though they covered him with clothes, he could not get warm.

**2** So his servants said to him, "Let a young woman, a virgin, be found for my lord the king, to wait on the king and look after him. Let her lie in your arms, so that my lord the king may be warm."

**3** So they searched all through the land of Israel for a beautiful young woman, and found Abishag the Shunammite and brought her to the king.

**4** The young woman was very beautiful. She looked after the king and served him, but the king did not have sex with her.

**5** Now Adonijah son of Haggith put himself forward and said, "I will be king." He got himself chariots and horsemen and fifty men to run in front of him.

**6** His father had never once crossed him by asking, "Why do you do this?" He was also very handsome, and he had been born next after Absalom.

**7** He conferred with Joab son of Zeruiah and with Abiathar the priest, and they supported Adonijah and helped him.

**8** But Zadok the priest, and Benaiah son of Jehoiada, and Nathan the prophet, and Shimei, and Rei, and David's warriors did not side with Adonijah.

**9** Adonijah sacrificed sheep, cattle and fattened calves at the Serpent's Stone, beside En-rogel, and he invited all his brothers, the king's sons, and all the men of Judah who were the king's servants.

**10** But he did not invite Nathan the prophet or Benaiah or the warriors or his brother Solomon.

**11** Then Nathan said to Bathsheba, Solomon's mother, "Have you not heard that Adonijah son of Haggith has become king, and our lord David does not know it?

**12** Now come, let me give you advice, so that you can save your own life and the life of your son Solomon.

**13** Go at once to King David and say to him, 'My lord the king, did you not swear to your servant, "Your son Solomon will be king after me, and he will sit on my throne"? Why then has Adonijah become king?'

**14** While you are still speaking there with the king, I will come in after you and confirm your words."

**15** So Bathsheba went in to the king in his room. The king was very old, and Abishag the Shunammite was looking after him.

**16** Bathsheba bowed and did homage to the king, and the king said, "What do you want?"

**17** She said to him, "My lord, you swore to your servant by the LORD your God, 'Your son Solomon will be king after me, and he will sit on my throne.'

**18** But now, look, Adonijah has become king, and you, my lord the king, do not know it.

**19** He has sacrificed oxen, fattened calves and sheep in great numbers, and has invited all the king's sons, and Abiathar the priest and Joab the commander of the army. But he has not invited your servant Solomon.

**20** As for you, my lord the king, the eyes of all Israel are on you to tell them who will sit on the throne of my lord the king after him.

**21** Otherwise, when my lord the king lies down with his ancestors, I and my son Solomon will be treated as criminals."

**22** While she was still speaking with the king, Nathan the prophet came in.

**23** They told the king, "Nathan the prophet is here." He came before the king and bowed down before the king with his face to the ground.

**24** Nathan said, "My lord the king, have you said, 'Adonijah will be king after me, and he will sit on my throne'?

**25** For today he has gone down and sacrificed oxen, fattened calves and sheep in great numbers, and has invited all the king's sons, the commanders of the army and Abiathar the priest. Look, they are eating and drinking in front of him, and saying, 'Long live King Adonijah.'

**26** But he did not invite me, your servant, or Zadok the priest, or Benaiah son of Jehoiada, or your servant Solomon.

**27** Has this been done by my lord the king, without letting your servants know who is to sit on the throne of my lord the king after him?"

**28** King David answered, "Call Bathsheba to me." She came into the king's presence and stood before the king.

**29** The king swore, "As the LORD lives, who has rescued me from every trouble,

**30** as I swore to you by the LORD, the God of Israel, 'Your son Solomon will be king after me, and he will sit on my throne in my place,' so I will do this day."

**31** Then Bathsheba bowed with her face to the ground and did homage to the king and said, "May my lord King David live for ever."

**32** King David said, "Call Zadok the priest, Nathan the prophet and Benaiah son of Jehoiada." They came before the king.

**33** The king said to them, "Take my servants with you, and have my son Solomon ride on my own mule, and take him down to Gihon.

**34** There Zadok the priest and Nathan the prophet are to anoint him king over Israel. Blow the trumpet and say, 'Long live King Solomon.'

**35** Then come up after him, and let him come in and sit on my throne, for he will be king in my place. I have appointed him to be ruler over Israel and over Judah."

**36** Benaiah son of Jehoiada answered the king, "Amen. May the LORD, the God of my lord the king, say so too.

**37** As the LORD has been with my lord the king, so may he be with Solomon, and make his throne greater than the throne of my lord King David."

**38** So Zadok the priest, Nathan the prophet, Benaiah son of Jehoiada, and the Cherethites and the Pelethites went down and had Solomon ride on King David's mule and brought him to Gihon.

**39** Zadok the priest took the horn of oil from the tent and anointed Solomon. They blew the trumpet, and all the people said, "Long live King Solomon."

**40** All the people went up after him, playing pipes and rejoicing so loudly that the ground split open with the noise.

**41** Adonijah and all the guests with him heard it as they finished eating. When Joab heard the sound of the trumpet, he said, "Why is the town in such an uproar?"

**42** While he was still speaking, Jonathan son of Abiathar the priest came. Adonijah said, "Come in, for you are a worthy man and you bring good news."

**43** Jonathan answered Adonijah, "No. Our lord King David has made Solomon king.

**44** The king has sent with him Zadok the priest, Nathan the prophet, Benaiah son of Jehoiada, and the Cherethites and the Pelethites, and they have had him ride on the king's mule.

**45** Zadok the priest and Nathan the prophet have anointed him king at Gihon, and they have gone up from there rejoicing, and the town is in an uproar. That is the noise you heard.

**46** Solomon is now sitting on the royal throne.

**47** The king's servants also came to bless our lord King David, saying, 'May your God make the name of Solomon more famous than your name, and make his throne greater than your throne.' And the king bowed in worship on his bed.

**48** The king also said, 'Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel, who has today given one to sit on my throne, and my own eyes see it.'"

**49** Then all the guests of Adonijah were terrified, and they got up and each went his own way.

**50** Adonijah was afraid of Solomon, and he got up and went and took hold of the horns of the altar.

**51** Solomon was told, "Adonijah is afraid of King Solomon, and he has taken hold of the horns of the altar and says, 'Let King Solomon swear to me today that he will not put his servant to death with the sword.'"

**52** Solomon said, "If he proves to be a worthy man, not one hair of his will fall to the ground. But if wickedness is found in him, he will die."

**53** So King Solomon sent, and they brought him down from the altar. He came and bowed down to King Solomon, and Solomon said to him, "Go to your house."

### Chapter 2

**1** When David's time to die drew near, he gave a charge to his son Solomon:

**2** "I am going the way of all the earth. Be strong and be a man.

**3** Keep what the LORD your God requires: walk in his ways and keep his statutes, his commands, his rules and his testimonies, as written in the law of Moses, so that you may succeed in all you do and wherever you turn,

**4** and so that the LORD may carry out the promise he made to me: 'If your sons take care how they live, walking before me in faithfulness with all their heart and all their soul, you will never lack a man on the throne of Israel.'

**5** You also know what Joab son of Zeruiah did to me, what he did to the two commanders of the armies of Israel, Abner son of Ner and Amasa son of Jether. He killed them, and shed the blood of war in a time of peace, and put the blood of war on the belt round his waist and on the sandals on his feet.

**6** So act with your wisdom, and do not let his grey head go down to Sheol \[H-SHEOL\] in peace.

**7** But show faithful love \[H-HESED\] to the sons of Barzillai the Gileadite, and let them be among those who eat at your table, for they stood by me in the same way when I ran away from your brother Absalom.

**8** And there is Shimei son of Gera, the Benjaminite from Bahurim, with you. He cursed me with a bitter curse on the day I went to Mahanaim. But he came down to meet me at the Jordan, and I swore to him by the LORD, 'I will not put you to death with the sword.'

**9** Now do not let him go unpunished, for you are a wise man. You will know what to do to him, and you will bring his grey head down to Sheol with blood."

**10** Then David lay down with his ancestors and was buried in the city of David.

**11** The time that David ruled over Israel was forty years. He ruled seven years in Hebron and thirty-three years in Jerusalem.

**12** So Solomon sat on the throne of his father David, and his kingdom was firmly established.

**13** Then Adonijah son of Haggith came to Bathsheba, Solomon's mother. She said, "Do you come in peace?" He said, "In peace."

**14** Then he said, "I have something to say to you." She said, "Say it."

**15** He said, "You know that the kingdom was mine, and that all Israel looked to me to be king. But the kingdom has turned round and become my brother's, for it was his from the LORD.

**16** Now I have one request to make of you. Do not refuse me." She said to him, "Say it."

**17** He said, "Please ask King Solomon, for he will not refuse you, to give me Abishag the Shunammite as my wife."

**18** Bathsheba said, "Very well. I will speak to the king for you."

**19** So Bathsheba went to King Solomon to speak to him for Adonijah. The king stood up to meet her and bowed down to her. Then he sat on his throne, and a throne was set for the king's mother, and she sat at his right hand.

**20** She said, "I have one small request to make of you. Do not refuse me." The king said to her, "Make your request, my mother, for I will not refuse you."

**21** She said, "Let Abishag the Shunammite be given to your brother Adonijah as his wife."

**22** King Solomon answered his mother, "Why do you ask Abishag the Shunammite for Adonijah? Ask for the kingdom for him as well, for he is my older brother, and he has Abiathar the priest and Joab son of Zeruiah on his side."

**23** Then King Solomon swore by the LORD, "May God do this to me, and more also, if Adonijah has not spoken this word at the cost of his life.

**24** Now, as the LORD lives, who has established me and set me on the throne of my father David, and who has made a house for me, as he promised, Adonijah will be put to death today."

**25** So King Solomon sent Benaiah son of Jehoiada, and he struck him down, and he died.

**26** To Abiathar the priest the king said, "Go to your fields at Anathoth. You deserve to die, but I will not put you to death today, because you carried the ark of the Lord GOD before my father David, and because you shared in all the hardships my father went through."

**27** So Solomon removed Abiathar from being priest of the LORD, and so fulfilled the word of the LORD that he spoke about the house of Eli at Shiloh.

**28** When the news came to Joab (for Joab had supported Adonijah, though he had not supported Absalom), Joab ran to the tent of the LORD and took hold of the horns of the altar.

**29** King Solomon was told, "Joab has run to the tent of the LORD, and he is there beside the altar." Then Solomon sent Benaiah son of Jehoiada, saying, "Go and strike him down."

**30** So Benaiah came to the tent of the LORD and said to him, "The king says, 'Come out.'" He said, "No, I will die here." Then Benaiah took word back to the king: "This is what Joab said, and this is how he answered me."

**31** The king said, "Do as he says. Strike him down and bury him, and so take away from me and from my father's house the guilt for the blood that Joab shed without cause.

**32** The LORD will bring his blood back on his own head, for he attacked two men more righteous and better than himself and killed them with the sword, without my father David knowing: Abner son of Ner, commander of the army of Israel, and Amasa son of Jether, commander of the army of Judah.

**33** Their blood will come back on the head of Joab and on the head of his descendants for ever. But for David and his descendants and his house and his throne there will be peace from the LORD for ever."

**34** So Benaiah son of Jehoiada went up and struck him down and killed him. And he was buried at his own home in the wilderness.

**35** The king put Benaiah son of Jehoiada over the army in his place, and the king put Zadok the priest in the place of Abiathar.

**36** Then the king sent and called Shimei and said to him, "Build yourself a house in Jerusalem and live there, and do not go out from there to any place at all.

**37** For on the day you go out and cross the Wadi Kidron, know for certain that you will die. Your blood will be on your own head."

**38** Shimei said to the king, "What you say is good. Your servant will do as my lord the king has said." And Shimei lived in Jerusalem for many days.

**39** But at the end of three years two of Shimei's servants ran away to Achish son of Maacah, king of Gath. Shimei was told, "Your servants are in Gath."

**40** Shimei got up and saddled his donkey and went to Achish at Gath to look for his servants. Shimei went and brought his servants back from Gath.

**41** Solomon was told that Shimei had gone from Jerusalem to Gath and come back.

**42** So the king sent and called Shimei and said to him, "Did I not make you swear by the LORD and solemnly warn you, 'Know for certain that on the day you go out and go to any place at all, you will die'? And you said to me, 'What you say is good. I will obey.'

**43** Why then have you not kept your oath to the LORD and the command that I gave you?"

**44** The king also said to Shimei, "You know in your own heart all the wrong you did to my father David. The LORD will bring your wrong back on your own head.

**45** But King Solomon will be blessed, and the throne of David will be established before the LORD for ever."

**46** Then the king gave the order to Benaiah son of Jehoiada, and he went out and struck him down, and he died. So the kingdom was established in the hand of Solomon.

*Review note: 1 Kings 1 to 2 was drafted in WABV wording. ## 1 Kings 3-4: Solomon's Wisdom and His Court

### Chapter 3

**1** Solomon made a marriage alliance with Pharaoh king of Egypt. He took Pharaoh's daughter and brought her to the city of David, until he had finished building his own house and the house of the LORD and the wall round Jerusalem.

**2** The people were sacrificing at the high places, because no house had yet been built for the name of the LORD.

**3** Solomon loved the LORD and followed the statutes of his father David, except that he sacrificed and burned incense at the high places.

**4** The king went to Gibeon to sacrifice there, for that was the great high place. Solomon offered a thousand burnt offerings on that altar.

**5** At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream at night, and God said, "Ask what I should give you."

**6** Solomon said, "You showed great and faithful love \[H-HESED\] to your servant, my father David, because he walked before you in faithfulness, in righteousness and in uprightness of heart. You have kept this great and faithful love for him and have given him a son to sit on his throne today.

**7** Now, LORD my God, you have made your servant king in place of my father David, though I am only a young boy and do not know how to go out or come in.

**8** Your servant is in the middle of your people, whom you chose, a great people, too many to be counted or numbered.

**9** So give your servant a listening heart to judge your people and to tell good from evil, for who can judge this great people of yours?"

**10** It pleased the Lord that Solomon had asked for this.

**11** God said to him, "Because you have asked for this, and have not asked for long life for yourself, or riches, or the lives of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to hear what is right,

**12** I now do what you have said. I give you a wise and understanding heart, so that there has been no one like you before you, and no one like you will rise after you.

**13** I also give you what you have not asked for, both riches and honour, so that no other king will be like you, all your days.

**14** And if you walk in my ways and keep my statutes and my commands, as your father David did, I will give you a long life."

**15** Solomon woke up, and it was a dream. He came to Jerusalem and stood before the ark of the covenant of the Lord, and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings, and gave a feast for all his servants.

**16** Then two women who were prostitutes came to the king and stood before him.

**17** One woman said, "Please, my lord, this woman and I live in the same house, and I gave birth while she was in the house.

**18** On the third day after I gave birth, this woman also gave birth. We were together. There was no one else with us in the house, only the two of us.

**19** This woman's son died in the night, because she lay on him.

**20** She got up in the middle of the night and took my son from beside me while your servant was asleep, and laid him in her arms, and laid her dead son in my arms.

**21** When I got up in the morning to nurse my son, he was dead. But when I looked at him closely in the morning, I saw that he was not the son I had borne."

**22** But the other woman said, "No. The living one is my son, and the dead one is yours." The first said, "No. The dead one is your son, and the living one is mine." So they argued before the king.

**23** The king said, "This one says, 'This is my son who is alive, and your son is dead,' and that one says, 'No, your son is dead, and my son is the one who is alive.'"

**24** The king said, "Bring me a sword." So they brought a sword to the king.

**25** The king said, "Cut the living child in two, and give half to one and half to the other."

**26** Then the woman whose son was alive spoke to the king, because her heart burned for her son. She said, "Please, my lord, give her the living child, and do not kill him." But the other said, "He will be neither mine nor yours. Cut him in two."

**27** Then the king answered, "Give the living child to the first woman, and do not kill him. She is his mother."

**28** All Israel heard of the judgement that the king had given, and they stood in awe of the king, because they saw that the wisdom of God was in him, to do justice.

### Chapter 4

**1** King Solomon was king over all Israel,

**2** and these were his chief officials: Azariah son of Zadok was the priest;

**3** Elihoreph and Ahijah, sons of Shisha, were secretaries; Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was the recorder;

**4** Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the army; Zadok and Abiathar were priests;

**5** Azariah son of Nathan was over the officers; Zabud son of Nathan was a priest and the king's friend;

**6** Ahishar was over the palace; and Adoniram son of Abda was over the forced labour.

**7** Solomon had twelve officers over all Israel, who provided food for the king and his household. Each one had to provide for one month in the year.

**8** These were their names: Ben-hur, in the hill country of Ephraim;

**9** Ben-deker, in Makaz, Shaalbim, Beth-shemesh and Elon-beth-hanan;

**10** Ben-hesed, in Arubboth (Socoh and all the land of Hepher belonged to him);

**11** Ben-abinadab, in all Naphath-dor (he had Taphath, Solomon's daughter, as his wife);

**12** Baana son of Ahilud, in Taanach and Megiddo and all Beth-shean, which is beside Zarethan below Jezreel, from Beth-shean to Abel-meholah, as far as beyond Jokmeam;

**13** Ben-geber, in Ramoth-gilead (the villages of Jair son of Manasseh in Gilead belonged to him, and the region of Argob in Bashan, sixty great towns with walls and bronze bars);

**14** Ahinadab son of Iddo, in Mahanaim;

**15** Ahimaaz, in Naphtali (he too took a daughter of Solomon as his wife);

**16** Baana son of Hushai, in Asher and Bealoth;

**17** Jehoshaphat son of Paruah, in Issachar;

**18** Shimei son of Ela, in Benjamin;

**19** Geber son of Uri, in the land of Gilead, the country of Sihon king of the Amorites and of Og king of Bashan. And there was one officer over the land of Judah.

**20** Judah and Israel were as many as the sand by the sea. They ate and drank and were happy.

**21** Solomon ruled over all the kingdoms from the Euphrates to the land of the Philistines and to the border of Egypt. They brought tribute and served Solomon all the days of his life.

**22** Solomon's food for one day was thirty cors of fine flour and sixty cors of meal,

**23** ten fattened oxen, twenty oxen from the pastures and a hundred sheep, besides deer, gazelles, roebucks and fattened birds.

**24** For he ruled over everything west of the Euphrates, from Tiphsah to Gaza, over all the kings west of the Euphrates. And he had peace on all sides round him.

**25** Judah and Israel lived in safety, from Dan to Beersheba, each man under his own vine and his own fig tree, all the days of Solomon.

**26** Solomon also had forty thousand stalls of horses for his chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen.

**27** Those officers provided food for King Solomon and for all who came to the king's table, each in his month. They let nothing be lacking.

**28** They also brought barley and straw for the horses and the swift steeds to the place where it was needed, each according to his duty.

**29** God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding beyond measure, and breadth of mind as wide as the sand on the seashore,

**30** so that Solomon's wisdom was greater than the wisdom of all the people of the east and all the wisdom of Egypt.

**31** He was wiser than anyone, wiser than Ethan the Ezrahite and Heman, Calcol and Darda, the sons of Mahol, and his fame spread through all the surrounding nations.

**32** He spoke three thousand proverbs, and his songs numbered a thousand and five.

**33** He spoke of trees, from the cedar in Lebanon to the hyssop that grows out of the wall. He spoke also of animals, birds, reptiles and fish.

**34** People of all nations came to hear the wisdom of Solomon, sent by all the kings of the earth who had heard of his wisdom.

## 1 Kings 5-8: The House of the LORD

### Chapter 5

**1** Hiram king of Tyre sent his servants to Solomon, for he had heard that they had anointed him king in place of his father, and Hiram had always been a friend of David.

**2** Solomon sent this message to Hiram:

**3** "You know that my father David could not build a house for the name of the LORD his God, because of the wars that surrounded him, until the LORD put his enemies under the soles of his feet.

**4** But now the LORD my God has given me rest on every side. There is no enemy and no disaster.

**5** So I intend to build a house for the name of the LORD my God, as the LORD told my father David: 'Your son, whom I will put on your throne in your place, will build the house for my name.'

**6** Now give the order that cedars from Lebanon be cut for me. My servants will work with your servants, and I will pay your servants whatever wage you set, for you know that none of us knows how to cut timber like the Sidonians."

**7** When Hiram heard Solomon's words, he was very glad and said, "Blessed be the LORD today, who has given David a wise son over this great people."

**8** Hiram sent word to Solomon: "I have heard the message you sent me. I will do all you want about the cedar and cypress timber.

**9** My servants will bring it down from Lebanon to the sea, and I will have it made into rafts to go by sea to the place you name. There I will have them broken up, and you can take it away. And you will meet my need by providing food for my household."

**10** So Hiram gave Solomon all the cedar and cypress timber he wanted,

**11** and Solomon gave Hiram twenty thousand cors of wheat as food for his household, and twenty cors of pressed oil. Solomon gave this to Hiram year after year.

**12** The LORD gave Solomon wisdom, as he had promised him. There was peace between Hiram and Solomon, and the two of them made a treaty.

**13** King Solomon raised a labour force from all Israel, and the force numbered thirty thousand men.

**14** He sent them to Lebanon, ten thousand a month in turns. They spent one month in Lebanon and two months at home. Adoniram was in charge of the labour force.

**15** Solomon also had seventy thousand carriers and eighty thousand stonecutters in the hill country,

**16** besides three thousand three hundred chief officers of Solomon who were over the work and in charge of the people doing it.

**17** At the king's command they quarried large, costly stones to lay the foundation of the house with dressed stone.

**18** So Solomon's builders and Hiram's builders and the men of Gebal cut the stone and prepared the timber and the stone for building the house.

### Chapter 6

**1** In the four hundred and eightieth year after the Israelites came out of the land of Egypt, in the fourth year of Solomon's reign over Israel, in the month of Ziv, which is the second month, he began to build the house of the LORD.

**2** The house that King Solomon built for the LORD was sixty cubits long, twenty cubits wide and thirty cubits high.

**3** The porch in front of the main hall of the house was twenty cubits long, the width of the house, and ten cubits deep in front of the house.

**4** He made windows for the house, with narrowing frames.

**5** Against the wall of the house he built a structure all round the walls of the house, round both the main hall and the inner room. And he made side rooms all round.

**6** The lowest storey was five cubits wide, the middle one six cubits wide and the third seven cubits wide. For he made ledges in the outer wall of the house all round, so that the beams would not be fixed into the walls of the house.

**7** When the house was being built, it was built with stone prepared at the quarry, so that no hammer or axe or any iron tool was heard in the house while it was being built.

**8** The entrance to the lowest side rooms was on the south side of the house, and a stairway went up to the middle storey, and from the middle to the third.

**9** So he built the house and finished it, and he roofed the house with beams and planks of cedar.

**10** He built the side structure against the whole house, each storey five cubits high, and it was joined to the house with cedar timbers.

**11** The word of the LORD came to Solomon:

**12** "As for this house that you are building, if you walk in my statutes and carry out my rules and keep all my commands by walking in them, then I will keep with you my word that I spoke to your father David.

**13** I will live among the Israelites and will not abandon my people Israel."

**14** So Solomon built the house and finished it.

**15** He lined the walls of the house on the inside with cedar boards. From the floor of the house to the ceiling beams he covered them on the inside with wood, and he covered the floor of the house with cypress boards.

**16** He built twenty cubits at the back of the house with cedar boards from floor to ceiling beams, and he built it inside as an inner room, the most holy place \[H-QADOSH\].

**17** The house, that is, the main hall in front of the inner room, was forty cubits long.

**18** The cedar inside the house was carved with gourds and open flowers. All was cedar. No stone could be seen.

**19** He prepared the inner room inside the house, at the back, to put there the ark of the covenant of the LORD.

**20** The inner room was twenty cubits long, twenty cubits wide and twenty cubits high, and he overlaid it with pure gold. He also overlaid an altar of cedar.

**21** Solomon overlaid the inside of the house with pure gold, and he stretched chains of gold across the front of the inner room, and overlaid it with gold.

## 1 Kings 9-11: Glory and Decline

### Chapter 9

**1** When Solomon had finished building the house of the LORD and the king's house and everything else that Solomon wanted to build,

**4** As for you, if you walk before me as your father David did, with a whole heart and with honesty, doing all that I have commanded you, and keeping my statutes and my rules,

**5** then I will establish your royal throne over Israel for ever, as I promised your father David: 'You will never lack a man on the throne of Israel.'

**22** The LORD said to him, 'How?' He said, 'I will go out and be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.' He said, 'You will persuade him, and you will succeed. Go out and do it.'

**23** So now the LORD has put a lying spirit in the mouth of all these prophets of yours. The LORD has declared disaster against you."

**24** Then Zedekiah son of Chenaanah came up and struck Micaiah on the cheek and said, "Which way did the spirit of the LORD go when it left me to speak to you?"

**25** Micaiah said, "You will see on the day when you go into an inner room to hide."

**26** The king of Israel said, "Seize Micaiah and take him back to Amon the governor of the town and to Joash the king's son,

**27** and say, 'This is what the king says: Put this man in prison and feed him only a little bread and a little water until I come back safely.'"

**28** Micaiah said, "If you ever come back safely, the LORD has not spoken through me." And he said, "Listen, all you peoples."

**29** So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah went up to Ramoth-gilead.

**30** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "I will disguise myself and go into the battle, but you wear your robes." So the king of Israel disguised himself and went into the battle.

**31** Now the king of Aram had ordered his thirty-two chariot commanders, "Do not fight with anyone, small or great, except the king of Israel alone."

**32** When the chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat, they said, "It must be the king of Israel." So they turned to attack him. But Jehoshaphat cried out.

**33** When the chariot commanders saw that he was not the king of Israel, they turned back from chasing him.

**34** But someone drew his bow without aim and struck the king of Israel between the joints of his armour. So he said to his chariot driver, "Turn round and take me out of the fighting, for I am wounded."

**35** The battle grew fierce that day, and the king was propped up in his chariot facing the Arameans. He died in the evening, and the blood from the wound ran down to the floor of the chariot.

**36** About sunset a cry went through the army: "Each man to his town, and each man to his land."

**37** So the king died and was brought to Samaria, and they buried the king in Samaria.

**38** They washed out the chariot at the pool of Samaria, where the prostitutes washed, and the dogs licked up his blood, as the word of the LORD had said.

**39** The rest of the acts of Ahab and all that he did, and the ivory house that he built, and all the towns that he built, are they not written in the Book of the Annals of the Kings of Israel?

**40** Ahab lay down with his ancestors, and his son Ahaziah became king in his place.

**41** Jehoshaphat son of Asa became king over Judah in the fourth year of Ahab king of Israel.

**42** Jehoshaphat was thirty-five years old when he became king, and he ruled for twenty-five years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Azubah daughter of Shilhi.

**43** He walked in all the way of his father Asa and did not turn aside from it, doing what was right in the eyes of the LORD. But the high places were not removed, and the people still sacrificed and burned incense on the high places.

**44** Jehoshaphat also made peace with the king of Israel.

**45** The rest of the acts of Jehoshaphat, and the power that he showed, and how he made war, are they not written in the Book of the Annals of the Kings of Judah?

**46** He also wiped out of the land the remnant of the male shrine prostitutes who were left from the days of his father Asa.

**47** There was no king in Edom. A deputy was king.

**48** Jehoshaphat built ships of Tarshish to go to Ophir for gold, but they did not go, because the ships were wrecked at Ezion-geber.

**49** Then Ahaziah son of Ahab said to Jehoshaphat, "Let my servants go with your servants in the ships." But Jehoshaphat was not willing.

**50** Jehoshaphat lay down with his ancestors and was buried with them in the city of his ancestor David. His son Jehoram became king in his place.

**51** Ahaziah son of Ahab became king over Israel in Samaria in the seventeenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah, and he ruled over Israel for two years.

**52** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD and walked in the way of his father and in the way of his mother and in the way of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who made Israel sin.

**53** He served Baal and bowed down to him and made the LORD, the God of Israel, angry, just as his father had done.

*End of the book of 1 Kings. Review note: 1 Kings 20 to 22 was drafted in WABV wording. Chapter 20 includes the sparing of Ben-hadad, whom the prophet says the LORD had "devoted to destruction" (20:42). Chapter 21 tells how Naboth is condemned and stoned on false charges by Jezebel's letters, and Elijah's judgement on Ahab and Jezebel. Chapter 22 includes Micaiah's vision of the lying spirit sent to Ahab's prophets (22:19-23), which is difficult and is rendered as written, with no explanation added. In the Greek translation chapters 20 and 21 are in the opposite order. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **Title** 1 Kings and 2 Kings were one book in the Hebrew Bible. The book tells the history of the kings from the end of David's reign to the exile. It names its sources, such as "the Book of the Acts of Solomon" (11:41) and "the Book of the Annals of the Kings of Israel" and "of Judah". The Hebrew text differs from the Greek translation in some important places, and the footnotes record the main differences.
- **1:1** David's age and cold body show the end of his reign. The scene sets up the question of who will follow him.
- **1:2** A young woman to keep the king warm is Abishag. The Hebrew uses the words for "attendant" and "lie in your arms".
- **1:4** "The king did not have sex with her" is literally "the king did not know her". This matters in 2:17-22.
- **1:5** Adonijah is David's fourth son (2 Samuel 3:4). The chariots, horsemen and fifty runners are the same as Absalom's display (2 Samuel 15:1).
- **1:6** "His father had never once crossed him" is the narrator's note on David's failure to discipline his sons (see 2 Samuel 13:21).
- **1:7** Joab and Abiathar are David's oldest commander and priest. They support Adonijah.
- **1:8** "Zadok, Benaiah, Nathan" and David's warriors are on Solomon's side. "Shimei and Rei" are not known elsewhere.
- **1:9** "The Serpent's Stone" is a stone beside En-rogel, a spring south of Jerusalem. Adonijah holds a sacrificial feast there, as Absalom did at Hebron (2 Samuel 15:7-12).
- **1:13** David's oath that Solomon would be king is not recorded in the earlier books. In 1 Chronicles 22:9-10 David tells Solomon that the LORD promised him the kingdom.
- **1:21** "Treated as criminals" is Hebrew *chatta'im*, "offenders", the word for those who have done wrong. The fear is that Solomon and Bathsheba will be killed as rivals.
- **1:33** "My own mule" is a royal sign. "Gihon" is the spring in the Kidron valley, east of the city.
- **1:34** Anointing by the priest and the prophet, and the trumpet and the acclamation, make the king.
- **1:39** "The horn of oil from the tent" is the tent that David pitched for the ark (2 Samuel 6:17).
- **1:40** "The ground split open with the noise" is a figure for the great sound. The Hebrew verb is uncertain.
- **1:50** "The horns of the altar" are the corner projections of the altar. Holding them was a way to ask for protection (see Exodus 21:13-14).
- **1:52** "A worthy man" is Hebrew *ben chayil*, a man of standing. Solomon sets a condition for sparing his brother.
- **2:2** "Be a man" is the phrase David used of the Philistines in 1 Samuel 4:9.
- **2:3** The language of "his statutes, his commands, his rules and his testimonies" is that of Deuteronomy. "The law of Moses" is a reference to the written law.
- **2:4** "If your sons take care" makes the promise of 2 Samuel 7:12-16 conditional on the sons' faithfulness.
- **2:5** "The blood of war in a time of peace" refers to Joab's killing of Abner (2 Samuel 3:27) and Amasa (2 Samuel 20:10). The Hebrew of "on the belt round his waist and on the sandals on his feet" is difficult, and the Greek has "on his belt and his sandals".
- **2:6** "Sheol" is the place of the dead. See entry H-SHEOL. David's words do not say that Joab should be killed, only that he should not die in peace.
- **2:7** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. Barzillai's help is told in 2 Samuel 17:27-29 and 19:31-39.
- **2:8** David had sworn not to put Shimei to death (2 Samuel 19:23). Here he tells Solomon to deal with him. The text records both, and does not explain how they fit.
- **2:9** "You will know what to do to him" leaves the penalty to Solomon, who is called "a wise man".
- **2:11** "Forty years" is the round number for David's reign, as in 2 Samuel 5:4-5.
- **2:12** "His kingdom was firmly established" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek adds a longer passage here.
- **2:13** Adonijah asks Bathsheba whether she comes "in peace", which shows how wary they are. His question is polite but uneasy.
- **2:15** "The kingdom was mine" is Adonijah's claim. He accepts that it has become Solomon's "from the LORD".
- **2:17** Adonijah asks for Abishag as his wife. In 1:4 she is the old king's attendant who did not sleep with him.
- **2:19** "A throne was set for the king's mother" shows the honour of the queen mother. The queen mother is a recognised position in the Kings narrative.
- **2:22** Solomon reads the request as a claim on the throne. Taking a dead king's women was a claim to his kingship (2 Samuel 3:7; 16:21-22).
- **2:23** "May God do this to me, and more also" is an oath formula (see Ruth 1:17).
- **2:24** "Who has made a house for me" refers to the promise of a lasting house in 2 Samuel 7.
- **2:25** Adonijah is put to death by Benaiah. The text records that Solomon's oath was sworn at once.
- **2:26** "Anathoth" is Abiathar's family land, north of Jerusalem, and later the home of Jeremiah. Solomon spares his life because he carried the ark and shared David's hardships.
- **2:27** Removing Abiathar fulfils the word against the house of Eli (1 Samuel 2:27-36), as the narrator says.
- **2:28** Joab ran to the altar and took hold of its horns. The law gave no protection there for deliberate murder (Exodus 21:14). Joab had backed Adonijah but not Absalom.
- **2:30** Joab refuses to leave the altar and says, "I will die here". Benaiah brings word of this to Solomon, who orders him to be killed at the altar.
- **2:31** "The blood that Joab shed without cause" refers to Abner and Amasa. Solomon says that killing Joab removes the guilt from David's house.
- **2:33** "There will be peace from the LORD" is the contrast to Joab's blood on his own descendants.
- **2:34** "Buried at his own home in the wilderness" is a note that Joab had a family estate.
- **2:35** Benaiah takes Joab's place as army commander, and Zadok takes Abiathar's place as priest.
- **2:36** Shimei is placed under house arrest in Jerusalem, so that Solomon can watch him. Crossing the Kidron would be fatal.
- **2:39** "Achish son of Maacah" may be the same king as "Achish son of Maoch" in 1 Samuel 27:2.
- **2:42** Solomon calls Shimei's crossing a breach of an oath to the LORD, though Shimei went to bring back his servants.
- **2:44** "You know in your own heart all the wrong you did to my father David" is Solomon's reason. David's own oath (2 Samuel 19:23) was not to put him to death "with the sword", and Solomon's order to Benaiah is for a sword.
- **2:46** "The kingdom was established in the hand of Solomon" ends the chapter. The Greek has a long passage after verse 35 that is not in the Hebrew.
- **3:1** "A marriage alliance with Pharaoh" shows Solomon's standing among the nations. The text does not comment on it here. Deuteronomy 7:3 forbids marriage with the nations of Canaan, and Egypt is not on that list. The bringing of the princess to the city of David is mentioned again in 7:8 and 9:24.
- **3:2** "The high places" were hill shrines. Sacrifice at them is said to be tolerated until the temple is built. The note points ahead to the many later criticisms of them in the book.
- **3:3** "Solomon loved the LORD ... except" is the first of several mixed judgements on Solomon. The high places are a fault in the narrator's eyes.
- **3:4** "Gibeon" was where the tent of meeting stood at this time (1 Chronicles 16:39; 2 Chronicles 1:3). "A thousand burnt offerings" is a very large number.
- **3:5** The LORD appears in a dream, which is a common form of divine speech in the Bible (see Genesis 28:12).
- **3:6** "Great and faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. "Righteousness" is *tsedaqah*.
- **3:7** "A young boy" is Hebrew *na'ar qatan*, a phrase that need not mean he was a child. It is a figure for inexperience. "To go out or come in" means to lead and to act as a leader.
- **3:9** "A listening heart" is Hebrew *lev shomea'*. The phrase is literal. Many translations have "an understanding mind". The heart in Hebrew is the centre of thought and will. "To judge" here means to rule and to decide cases.
- **3:10** "The Lord" here is Hebrew *Adonai*, as in verse 15.
- **3:12** "A wise and understanding heart" answers Solomon's request, with the unmatched wisdom that follows.
- **3:13** The gifts of riches and honour that Solomon did not ask for are described in chapters 4 and 10.
- **3:14** "If you walk in my ways" makes the promise of long life conditional, as in 2:4.
- **3:15** Solomon stands before the ark in Jerusalem, after the dream at Gibeon, and offers sacrifices there.
- **3:16** "Prostitutes" is Hebrew *zonot*. They have no family to speak for them, and they come directly to the king.
- **3:19** "Because she lay on him" says that the child was smothered by accident.
- **3:25** The king's order to cut the child in two is a test. The text does not say that he meant to carry it out.
- **3:26** "Her heart burned for her son" is literally "her womb was warmed" (Hebrew *rachamim*), the word used for compassion.
- **3:28** "Judgement" is Hebrew *mishpat*. The chapter ends with the people's awe at "the wisdom of God" in the king, which is the point of the story.
- **4:2** "Azariah son of Zadok was the priest" is hard to read. He may be Zadok's grandson (1 Chronicles 6:8-9). Some manuscripts add that he was the priest in the temple that Solomon built.
- **4:4** "Zadok and Abiathar were priests" lists Abiathar, though Solomon removed him in 2:27. The list may be older than that, or Abiathar kept the title.
- **4:5** "Priest and the king's friend" renders Hebrew *kohen*, which may here mean a chief official.
- **4:6** "Forced labour" is Hebrew *mas*. Adoniram is in charge, and he is killed in the revolt in 12:18.
- **4:7** The twelve districts supply the king for a month each. They did not follow the old tribal lines, and this later causes resentment.
- **4:8** The names beginning "Ben-" ("son of") may be family names, as in the Hebrew. They are given as they stand.
- **4:11** Two of Solomon's officers are married to his daughters (here and in verse 15). The text does not say how many daughters Solomon had.
- **4:13** "Sixty great towns with walls and bronze bars" repeats Deuteronomy 3:4-5, the land taken from Og.
- **4:19** "One officer over the land of Judah" is the Hebrew reading and is uncertain. The Greek has a different text. It may mean that Judah had no district of its own, or that it had a single officer.
- **4:20** "As many as the sand by the sea" recalls the promise to Abraham (Genesis 22:17).
- **4:21** In the Hebrew Bible this verse is 5:1, so the English chapter 4 has thirty-four verses and the Hebrew has twenty. English 4:21-34 is Hebrew 5:1-14.
- **4:22** A cor is about 220 litres, so these amounts feed a very large court and army.
- **4:24** "Tiphsah" is on the Euphrates and "Gaza" at the south-west edge of the land. The verse gives the full reach of Solomon's rule.
- **4:25** "Each man under his own vine and his own fig tree" is a figure of peace and plenty (see Micah 4:4).
- **4:26** "Forty thousand stalls" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Chronicles 9:25 has four thousand, and 1 Kings 10:26 has fourteen hundred chariots. The Hebrew number may be a scribal error.
- **4:28** "Swift steeds" is Hebrew *rekesh*, a rare word that may mean a fast horse or a royal horse.
- **4:30** "The people of the east" and "Egypt" were known for their wisdom.
- **4:31** "Ethan the Ezrahite and Heman" are named in the titles of Psalms 88 and 89. "Calcol and Darda" are also in 1 Chronicles 2:6.
- **4:32** "Three thousand proverbs" and "a thousand and five songs" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek has five thousand songs. The Book of Proverbs and the Song of Songs are connected to Solomon in their titles.
- **4:33** Solomon's knowledge of plants and animals shows a wide study of the natural world.
- **4:34** The visit of people sent by kings prepares for the visit of the queen of Sheba in chapter 10.
- **5:1** In the Hebrew Bible 5:1 to 5:14 are English 4:21 to 4:34, so the English chapter 5 has eighteen verses and the Hebrew chapter 5 has thirty-two (English 5:1-18 is Hebrew 5:15-32). "A friend of David" is literally "one who loved David". Hiram of Tyre had a treaty with David (2 Samuel 5:11).
- **5:3** "Under the soles of his feet" is a figure for victory over enemies. David's wars kept him from building a house for the LORD.
- **5:5** "A house for the name of the LORD" is the language of the name theology of Deuteronomy. The LORD's name dwells in the house, not the LORD himself (see 8:27-29).
- **5:6** "The Sidonians" is used for the Phoenicians, who were famous for their timber work.
- **5:9** "Rafts" are bundles of logs floated down the coast. 2 Chronicles 2:16 names Joppa as the place where they land.
- **5:11** "Twenty thousand cors of wheat" and "twenty cors of pressed oil" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Chronicles 2:10 has twenty thousand cors of wheat and of barley and twenty thousand baths of wine and of oil. The Greek has twenty thousand baths of oil.
- **5:13** "A labour force" is Hebrew *mas*, forced labour. The text says it was raised "from all Israel". In 9:22 the text says that Solomon did not make slaves of the Israelites, but of the Canaanite peoples. The two statements are kept as written.
- **5:15** "Seventy thousand carriers and eighty thousand stonecutters" are given as non-Israelites in 9:20-21 and 2 Chronicles 2:17-18.
- **5:16** "Three thousand three hundred" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Chronicles 2:2 has three thousand six hundred. 1 Kings 9:23 has five hundred and fifty.
- **5:18** "The men of Gebal" are from Gebal (Byblos), a Phoenician town famous for its stonework.
- **6:1** "The four hundred and eightieth year" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek has the four hundred and fortieth. The figure is hard to fit with the other dates in the books of Judges and Samuel. "Ziv" is the second month (April to May), and "Bul" (verse 38) is the eighth (October to November).
- **6:2** A cubit is about 45 centimetres, so the house was about 27 metres long, 9 metres wide and 13.5 metres high.
- **6:3** "The porch" is Hebrew *ulam*. The main hall is *heikhal* (verse 17), and the inner room is *debir* (verse 19).
- **6:4** "Windows with narrowing frames" translates Hebrew words (*shequfim atumim*) that are hard to read. They may mean windows that are narrow on the outside, or windows with frames.
- **6:5** "A structure ... side rooms" is Hebrew *yatsia'*. It is three storeys of side chambers built against the walls.
- **6:7** The stone was prepared at the quarry, so that no iron tool was heard. The law of the altar says the same of altar stones (Exodus 20:25; Deuteronomy 27:5).
- **6:12** The LORD's word interrupts the account of the building. The promise is conditional on obeying his statutes (see 2:4; 9:4-9).
- **6:16** "The most holy place" is Hebrew *qodesh haqqodashim*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **6:18** "Gourds" is Hebrew *peqa'im*, the shape of wild gourds or ornaments.
- **6:20** The inner room is a cube of twenty cubits. "An altar of cedar" overlaid with gold is the incense altar.
- **6:21** The gold chains across the front of the inner room are uncertain in the Hebrew.
- **6:23** "Two cherubim of olive wood, each ten cubits high" are far larger than the small cherubim on the cover of the ark in Exodus 25:18-22. Their wings reach from wall to wall (verse 27).
- **6:29** The carvings of cherubim, palm trees and open flowers resemble the carvings in Ezekiel 41:17-20.
- **6:31** "Five-sided" and "four-sided" (verse 33) translate Hebrew words (*chamishit*, *merubba'*) that are uncertain. They may refer to the shape of the lintel and doorposts, or to proportions.
- **6:38** "Seven years" is the time from the foundation to the finishing of the house. In 7:1 Solomon builds his own house in thirteen years.
- **7:1** "Thirteen years" for Solomon's own house against seven for the temple is a contrast that the text does not comment on.
- **7:2** "The House of the Forest of Lebanon" had "four rows" of cedar pillars in the Hebrew, but verse 3 describes forty-five pillars in three rows of fifteen. The Greek has three rows. The Hebrew is followed, and the difficulty is noted.
- **7:6** "The Hall of Pillars" is uncertain in the Hebrew. It may be an entrance hall.
- **7:7** "From floor to rafters" is the reading of the Greek and the Syriac. The Hebrew has "from floor to floor".
- **7:8** The house for Pharaoh's daughter is mentioned in 3:1 and 9:24.
- **7:13** "Hiram from Tyre" is a craftsman, not King Hiram. 2 Chronicles 2:13 calls him Huram-abi and says his mother was from Dan.
- **7:15** The pillars were eighteen cubits high. 2 Kings 25:17 also has eighteen, and 2 Chronicles 3:15 has thirty-five. The Hebrew of verses 15 to 22 is very hard, and many details are uncertain.
- **7:21** "Jachin" and "Boaz" are the names of the pillars. They may mean "he establishes" and "in him is strength". Their meaning is not certain.
- **7:23** "The sea" is a huge basin. Ten cubits across and thirty round gives a rough value of three for the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter.
- **7:26** "Two thousand baths" is about 44,000 litres. 2 Chronicles 4:5 has three thousand.
- **7:27** The ten stands are wheeled carts that held the basins. The description in verses 27 to 37 is the hardest in the book. Terms for the panels, frames, supports and bands are uncertain.
- **7:38** "Forty baths" is about 880 litres for each basin.
- **7:40** "The pots" is the Hebrew reading (*sirot*). Some manuscripts have "basins" (*kiyyorot*), as in verse 38.
- **7:46** "Between Succoth and Zarethan" is in the Jordan valley, where clay was suitable for casting. 2 Chronicles 4:17 names Zeredah.
- **7:47** "Unweighed" says that there was too much bronze to weigh.
- **7:49** "Ten lampstands" differ from the single lampstand of the tent (Exodus 25:31-40).
- **7:51** "The things that his father David had dedicated" were gifts from David's wars (see 2 Samuel 8:11).
- **8:1** "Zion" is here the city of David. Later the name is used of the temple hill.
- **8:2** "Ethanim" is the seventh month (September to October), the time of the festival of booths. The name was later changed to Tishri.
- **8:4** "The tent of meeting" is brought up with the ark. It may be the tent at Gibeon (3:4), though the text does not say so.
- **8:6** "The most holy place" is *qodesh haqqodashim*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **8:8** "They are there to this day" was written while the temple still stood, before 587 BCE.
- **8:9** "Nothing in the ark except the two stone tablets" is a contrast with Hebrews 9:4, which also mentions the jar of manna and Aaron's rod. "Covenant" is *berit*. See entry H-BERIT.
- **8:10** "The cloud" and "the glory of the LORD" repeat the language of Exodus 40:34-35.
- **8:12** Solomon's two lines are poetry. "Thick darkness" is Hebrew *arafel*, the dark cloud of Sinai (Exodus 20:21). The Greek has a longer form of the poem after verse 53, which says that the LORD set the sun in heaven and has said that he would live in the dark, and that it is written in the Book of Jashar or of the Song. The Hebrew is followed.
- **8:16** "I chose no town ... but I chose David" is a statement of the choice of the king and the city (see Deuteronomy 12:5).
- **8:23** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. "There is no God like you" is a confession of the LORD's uniqueness.
- **8:27** "Will God really live on the earth?" is the great question of the prayer. The house is a place of the LORD's name, and the prayer asks that he "listen in heaven".
- **8:29** "My name will be there" is the theology of Deuteronomy, in which the LORD's name rather than the LORD himself is in the place he chooses.
- **8:31** "Made to swear an oath" refers to the oath that settled a case where there were no witnesses (Exodus 22:10-11).
- **8:33** "Turn back" is Hebrew *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV. The prayer sets out the pattern of sin, punishment, repentance and forgiveness found in Judges and Deuteronomy 30.
- **8:37** The list of disasters (famine, plague, blight, mildew, locusts, siege) is also in Deuteronomy 28 and in the curses of Leviticus 26.
- **8:39** "You alone know the hearts of all human beings" is the basis of the request that the LORD repay each person fairly.
- **8:41** "A foreigner" here is Hebrew *nokhri*, a person from another land who comes for the LORD's name. It is a different word from *ger*, the resident foreigner.
- **8:46** "There is no one who does not sin" is a statement of the common human condition. The verses 46 to 51 look ahead to exile, and they were probably put into their present form after the exile began.
- **8:51** "The iron furnace" is a figure for Egypt (Deuteronomy 4:20).
- **8:53** The Hebrew ends the prayer here. The Greek adds a short poem after it (see verse 12).
- **8:56** "Not one word has failed" repeats Joshua 21:45 and 23:14.
- **8:63** "Twenty-two thousand oxen and a hundred and twenty thousand sheep" is a very large number, and may be symbolic.
- **8:64** "The middle of the court" was made holy for the offerings because the bronze altar was too small. 2 Chronicles 7:7 says the same.
- **8:65** "Seven days and seven days, fourteen days" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek has "seven days". "From Lebo-hamath to the Wadi of Egypt" is the whole land, from north to south.
- **8:66** "On the eighth day" is the end of the second week. 2 Chronicles 7:8-10 says the feast of dedication lasted seven days and the festival seven days.
- **9:2** "A second time" refers to the dream at Gibeon (3:5-14).
- **9:3** "Holy" is Hebrew *qadash*, "made holy". See entry H-QADOSH. The LORD answers Solomon's prayer by placing his name in the house.
- **9:4** The promise to Solomon is conditional, as in 2:4 and 6:12.
- **9:6** The warning in verses 6 to 9 speaks of Israel cut off from the land and the house thrown down, as if the destruction of the temple had already happened. The passage was probably shaped after the exile began.
- **9:7** "A proverb and a taunt" is a figure for being held up as an example of disaster (see Deuteronomy 28:37).
- **9:11** "Twenty towns in the land of Galilee" is Solomon's payment to Hiram. In 2 Chronicles 8:2 the towns are given by Hiram to Solomon, which reverses the story.
- **9:13** "Cabul" is the name of the region. Its meaning is not certain. The text explains it as a taunt, "what kind of towns", but the Hebrew wordplay is not clear.
- **9:15** "Hazor, Megiddo and Gezer" were fortified towns. The Millo is a fill or terrace in Jerusalem (see 2 Samuel 5:9).
- **9:16** The capture of Gezer by Pharaoh and its gift as a wedding present to Solomon's wife is told only here.
- **9:18** "Tamar in the wilderness" is the reading of the reading tradition. The written text has "Tadmor", as in 2 Chronicles 8:4.
- **9:21** "Devote to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. The text says that the peoples who were left, whom the Israelites could not destroy, were made into forced labour by Solomon. See also Joshua 16:10; Judges 1:28-35.
- **9:22** "Of the Israelites Solomon made no slaves" sits against 5:13, where the forced labour is raised "from all Israel", and 11:28 and 12:4. The text keeps both.
- **9:23** "Five hundred and fifty" is the Hebrew reading. In 5:16 the number is three thousand three hundred, and in 2 Chronicles 8:10 it is two hundred and fifty.
- **9:25** "Three times a year" refers to the three pilgrimage festivals (Exodus 23:14-17).
- **9:26** "Ezion-geber" is at the head of the Gulf of Aqaba. "The Red Sea" is Hebrew *yam suf*, the "sea of reeds".
- **9:28** "Four hundred and twenty talents" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Chronicles 8:18 has four hundred and fifty. "Ophir" is a source of gold whose place is not known.
- **10:1** "Sheba" is in the south-west of Arabia. "His connection with the name of the LORD" is the Hebrew phrase, and it is not in the parallel in 2 Chronicles 9:1.
- **10:5** "She was left breathless" is literally "there was no more spirit in her" (Hebrew *ruach*).
- **10:8** "Your people" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek and the Syriac have "your wives".
- **10:9** "Justice and righteousness" is Hebrew *mishpat u-tsedaqah*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. This is the purpose of the king in the queen's words.
- **10:11** "Almug wood" is a costly timber, not now identified. It may be sandalwood.
- **10:14** Six hundred and sixty-six talents is about 22 tonnes of gold.
- **10:15** "Traders" is Hebrew *tarim*, which may mean merchants or explorers.
- **10:19** "A round top at the back" is a difficult description of the throne. The Hebrew is uncertain.
- **10:22** "A fleet of Tarshish ships" may mean ships built for long voyages. "Apes and baboons" translates two Hebrew words, *qofim* and *tukkiyyim*. The second is uncertain, and has been understood as peacocks or baboons.
- **10:25** "Weapons" is Hebrew *neshek*, arms or armour.
- **10:26** "Fourteen hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen" goes against the king's law in Deuteronomy 17:16. The text records the numbers without comment here.
- **10:28** "Kue" is the region of Cilicia. The Hebrew of this verse is difficult, and the second "Kue" may be a different word (*miqweh*, "a drove").
- **10:29** "The kings of the Hittites and the kings of Aram" are the northern rulers to whom Solomon sold chariots and horses.
- **11:1** "Loved many foreign women" is the narrator's verdict. Deuteronomy 7:1-4 and 17:17 forbade marriage with the nations and many wives.
- **11:3** "Seven hundred wives, princesses, and three hundred concubines" is rendered as written. The marriages were alliances with other kingdoms.
- **11:5** "Detestable" is Hebrew *shiqquts*, a word for what is abhorrent. "Milcom" is the god of the Ammonites.
- **11:7** "Molech" is the reading of the Hebrew. The Greek and the Syriac have "Milcom". "The hill east of Jerusalem" is the Mount of Olives. See 2 Kings 23:13.
- **11:11** "My covenant" refers to the covenant with Solomon in 9:4-9. The LORD says he will give the kingdom "to your servant", which proves to be Jeroboam.
- **11:13** "One tribe" is Judah. Benjamin is counted with Judah in 12:21.
- **11:14** "An adversary" is Hebrew *satan*. Hadad is a prince of Edom who escaped Joab's killing.
- **11:15** The killing of every male in Edom by Joab is told in outline in 2 Samuel 8:13-14. It is rendered as written.
- **11:18** "Midian" and "Paran" are on the route from Edom to Egypt.
- **11:19** "Tahpenes" is the queen of Egypt, and her sister becomes Hadad's wife.
- **11:25** "Aram" is the Hebrew reading. Some Hebrew manuscripts and the Greek have "Edom" in this verse, which makes Hadad and Rezon's harms the same.
- **11:26** "Raised his hand against the king" means rebelled. "Zeredah" is in Ephraim. Jeroboam is a servant of Solomon.
- **11:28** "The forced labour of the house of Joseph" is the labour force of Ephraim and Manasseh. It is the source of the grievance in 12:4.
- **11:29** "Ahijah the Shilonite" is a prophet of Shiloh, the old sanctuary of Eli's house.
- **11:30** The torn cloak is a symbolic act, as in 1 Samuel 15:27-28.
- **11:31** "Ten pieces" are ten tribes. Judah and Benjamin are the remaining two, though the text counts one tribe for Solomon's son (verses 32 and 36).
- **11:36** "A lamp" is Hebrew *nir*, a figure for the continuing line of David.
- **11:38** The promise to Jeroboam of "a lasting house" is conditional, as David's was.
- **11:40** "Shishak" is Sheshonq I of Egypt, who later invades Judah (14:25).
- **11:41** "The Book of the Acts of Solomon" is a source that is now lost.
- **11:42** "Forty years" is the round number for Solomon's reign, as for David's.
- **12:1** "Shechem" is the old place of assembly for the tribes (Joshua 24). The northern tribes make Rehoboam come to them to be made king. The Greek has a different and longer account of these events after 12:24, which says Jeroboam was made king earlier. The Hebrew is followed.
- **12:2** "Came back from Egypt" is the reading of the reading tradition and of some manuscripts. The written text has "stayed in Egypt".
- **12:4** "Yoke" is a figure for the forced labour and taxes of Solomon's reign (see 4:7; 5:13; 11:28).
- **12:7** "Be a servant to this people" is the elders' advice, which Rehoboam rejects.
- **12:10** "My little finger is thicker than my father's waist" is the young men's boast. The Hebrew word for "little finger" (*qotonni*) is a rare word that means "my smallness" and may be a crude boast. The word for "waist" (*motnayim*) is literally "loins" or "hips".
- **12:11** "Scorpions" are whips with barbs.
- **12:15** "A turn of events from the LORD" says that the division fulfils Ahijah's word to Jeroboam (11:29-39). The text does not excuse Rehoboam's folly.
- **12:16** "What share do we have in David?" is the cry of Sheba in 2 Samuel 20:1.
- **12:18** Adoram is Solomon's chief of forced labour (4:6; 5:14). The people stone him and Rehoboam flees.
- **12:19** "To this day" is the writer's note that the rebellion continues.
- **12:21** "The tribe of Benjamin" is counted with Judah here, though 11:31-32 speaks of one tribe for David's line. "A hundred and eighty thousand" is a large number.
- **12:22** "Shemaiah the man of God" is named again in 2 Chronicles 12:5-7. "This is from me" says the division is the LORD's act.
- **12:25** Shechem and Penuel are on either side of the Jordan. They are Jeroboam's first centres of government. The capital later moves to Tirzah (14:17).
- **12:28** "Two golden calves" and the words "Here are your gods, Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt" repeat what the people said of the calf at Sinai (Exodus 32:4, 8). The calves may have been meant as seats or pedestals for the LORD, as the cherubim were in the temple. The narrator treats them as sin.
- **12:29** "Bethel" is at the southern edge and "Dan" at the northern edge of Jeroboam's kingdom.
- **12:31** "Houses on the high places" means shrines. The priests were "not Levites", against the law in Numbers 3 and 18.
- **12:32** "The eighth month" is a month later than the festival of booths in Judah (the seventh month, Leviticus 23:33-43). The change of date is the king's own choice (verse 33).
- **12:33** "Burn incense" and the new festival show Jeroboam acting as priest at the altar.
- **13:1** "A man of God" is not named. His word against the altar names Josiah, who is born three centuries later (verse 2). 2 Kings 23:15-20 records its fulfilment.
- **13:2** "Human bones will be burned on you" is a sign of the altar's defilement.
- **13:4** The king's hand dries up, as a sign. It is restored at the prophet's prayer (verse 6).
- **13:8** The command not to eat or drink in Bethel is a test of obedience and a sign of the LORD's rejection of the place.
- **13:11** "An old prophet" lives in Bethel. The text does not say why he stays there, or why he deceives the man of God.
- **13:18** "He was lying to him" is the narrator's note. The old prophet claims that an angel gave him a word from the LORD. This is rendered as written, and the narrative gives no further explanation.
- **13:20** The word of the LORD then comes to the lying prophet, and he announces the judgement on the man of God.
- **13:24** "A lion met him on the road and killed him" is told with the lion and donkey standing beside the body (verses 24 and 28). The lion does not eat the body or harm the donkey. The detail marks the death as the LORD's act.
- **13:30** "Alas, my brother" is a cry of mourning, as in Jeremiah 22:18.
- **13:31** The old prophet asks to be buried beside the man of God. 2 Kings 23:17-18 says that Josiah spared both tombs.
- **13:32** "The towns of Samaria" is an anachronism, since Samaria was founded later by Omri (16:24). The phrase may be the writer's.
- **13:33** Jeroboam does not turn from his way even after the sign, and appoints more priests of the high places.
- **14:1** "Abijah" means "my father is the LORD". Jeroboam gives the boy a name of the LORD.
- **14:3** "Cakes" is Hebrew *niqqudim*, and "a jar of honey" is the gift for the prophet.
- **14:4** Ahijah's eyes were fixed with age. He is the prophet who tore the cloak (11:29-31).
- **14:6** Ahijah sees through the disguise by the word of the LORD, before she speaks.
- **14:9** "Cast images" is Hebrew *massekot*. "Thrown me behind your back" is a figure for forgetting the LORD.
- **14:10** "Every male" is the Hebrew idiom "one who urinates against a wall". "Slave and free" is Hebrew *atsur ve-azuv*, an idiom of uncertain origin. The sweeping away "as one sweeps away dung" is a figure of complete removal.
- **14:11** The dogs and birds eating the dead are signs of a shameful end, as in 16:4 and 21:24.
- **14:13** "Something pleasing to the LORD" is found in the child. He alone of Jeroboam's house will be mourned and buried.
- **14:14** "What is it even now?" is very hard in the Hebrew. The verse may mean "and what will it be even now?" or the words may be damaged. The WABV text follows the Hebrew and gives a literal rendering.
- **14:15** "A reed in the water" is a figure for a people shaken and unstable. "Scatter them beyond the Euphrates" foretells the exile of the northern tribes. "Asherah poles" are the wooden symbols of the goddess Asherah.
- **14:17** "Tirzah" is the capital of the northern kingdom until Omri moves it to Samaria (16:23-24).
- **14:19** "The Book of the Annals of the Kings of Israel" is a source that is now lost.
- **14:21** "Naamah the Ammonite" is Rehoboam's mother, one of Solomon's foreign wives.
- **14:23** "Pillars and Asherah poles on every high hill and under every green tree" is the language of Deuteronomy 12:2 and of the prophets.
- **14:24** "Male shrine prostitutes" is Hebrew *qadesh*, from the root for "holy" (see entry H-QADOSH). They served at the Canaanite shrines.
- **14:25** "Shishak" is Sheshonq I of Egypt, whose invasion is also recorded in an Egyptian inscription at Karnak. The date is about 925 BCE. See also 2 Chronicles 12:2-9.
- **14:26** The treasures taken include the gold shields that Solomon had made (10:16-17).
- **14:27** "Bronze shields in their place" is a sign of the loss of wealth and of honour.
- **14:30** "There was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam all the time" is a note that the division was never healed.
- **14:31** "Abijam" is the name in Kings. In Chronicles the name is Abijah.
- **15:1** The dates of the kings are given as years of the other kingdom's king. The Hebrew and Greek numbers differ in many places, and the footnotes record only the main differences. The dates cannot be made to fit with each other without assuming co-regencies or other methods of counting.
- **15:2** "Maacah daughter of Abishalom" may mean the daughter of Absalom (2 Samuel 14:27). In 2 Chronicles 13:2 the mother of Abijah is called Micaiah daughter of Uriel.
- **15:4** "A lamp" is a figure for the continuing line of David (see 11:36).
- **15:5** "Except in the matter of Uriah the Hittite" is in the Hebrew but not in the main Greek manuscripts. It is the only exception to David's record, and it recalls 2 Samuel 11.
- **15:6** "War between Rehoboam and Jeroboam" repeats 14:30. Some Hebrew manuscripts and the Greek read "Abijam" for "Rehoboam". In some Greek manuscripts the verse is absent.
- **15:10** "His grandmother" is Hebrew "his mother" (*em*), a word that can also mean a female ancestor. Maacah is Abijam's mother (verse 2), and so Asa's grandmother. The WABV text takes it so.
- **15:12** "Male shrine prostitutes" is Hebrew *qadesh*, from the root for "holy" (see 14:24 and entry H-QADOSH).
- **15:13** "Queen mother" is Hebrew *gebirah*, "great lady". She had an official place at court (see 2:19). "A horrible image" is Hebrew *miphletset*, a shocking thing.
- **15:14** "The high places were not removed" and "Asa's heart was wholly true" are kept side by side as in the Hebrew.
- **15:18** Asa buys the help of Ben-hadad of Aram with the temple treasure. 2 Chronicles 16:7-10 records a prophet's rebuke of this act.
- **15:20** "Ijon, Dan, Abel-beth-maacah and all Chinneroth" are towns in the far north of Israel. Aram takes them.
- **15:22** "Geba of Benjamin and Mizpah" are fortified with the stones of Ramah.
- **15:23** "Diseased in his feet" is the narrator's note on his old age. 2 Chronicles 16:12 adds that he did not seek the LORD.
- **15:27** "Baasha son of Ahijah" is a different Ahijah from the prophet. Gibbethon was a Philistine town that Israel was besieging.
- **15:29** Baasha's killing of the whole house of Jeroboam fulfils Ahijah's word (14:10-14). It is rendered as written. 16:7 also condemns Baasha for destroying it.
- **15:33** "Twenty-four years" is the length of Baasha's reign.
- **16:1** "Jehu son of Hanani" is a prophet who also appears in 2 Chronicles 19:2 and 20:34.
- **16:2** "Out of the dust" is a figure for a humble start (see 1 Samuel 2:8).
- **16:4** The dogs and birds eating the dead repeat 14:11.
- **16:7** "Because he struck it down" says that Baasha is also condemned for his killing of Jeroboam's house, though he fulfilled the word of the LORD in doing it. The text keeps both and does not explain.
- **16:9** "Zimri, commander of half his chariots" plots against the king while he is drunk. The Hebrew "Arza" is a steward.
- **16:11** "A single male" is Hebrew "one who urinates against a wall" (see 14:10). It includes relatives and friends. It is rendered as written.
- **16:15** "Zimri ruled in Tirzah for seven days" is the shortest reign in the book.
- **16:18** "The stronghold of the king's house" is the fortified part of the palace. Zimri burns it down over himself.
- **16:21** "Tibni son of Ginath" is a rival to Omri. Some Greek manuscripts add "and Joram his brother" after Tibni's death.
- **16:23** "Twelve years, six of them in Tirzah" gives the length of Omri's reign and the move to Samaria (verse 24).
- **16:24** "Samaria" is named for Shemer, the owner of the hill. It becomes the capital of the northern kingdom, and the Assyrians later call Israel "the house of Omri".
- **16:25** "Did worse than all who were before him" is the narrator's verdict, which is repeated of Ahab in verse 30.
- **16:31** "Jezebel daughter of Ethbaal king of the Sidonians" is a princess of Tyre and Sidon. Her marriage to Ahab is an alliance and brings Baal worship with it. "Baal" is the Phoenician god, often identified with Melqart.
- **16:33** "An Asherah pole" is a wooden symbol of the goddess Asherah.
- **16:34** "At the cost of Abiram his firstborn, and ... Segub his youngest son" says that Hiel's sons died when he laid the foundation and set up the gates. The text may mean the sons were sacrificed in the building, or that they died as the fulfilment of Joshua's curse (Joshua 6:26). The Hebrew does not decide, and the WABV text follows the Hebrew.
- **17:1** "Elijah" means "my God is the LORD". "From Tishbe in Gilead" is the Hebrew phrase, which may also be read "one of the settlers of Gilead". "Before whom I stand" is the language of service. The drought is a challenge to Baal, the god of rain and storm. Luke 4:25 and James 5:17 give three and a half years for the drought.
- **17:4** "The ravens" is the Hebrew reading (*orevim*). A few have proposed that the word means "Arabs" (*arabim*), but the WABV text follows the plain Hebrew.
- **17:9** "Zarephath, which belongs to Sidon" is in the country of Jezebel's father. The LORD sends the prophet to feed a foreign widow in Baal's own land.
- **17:12** "As the LORD your God lives" is the oath of a woman from Sidon, who speaks of the LORD as Elijah's God. "We can eat it and die" shows the point of despair.
- **17:17** "There was no breath left in him" says that he died. Hebrew uses the word for breath (*neshamah*).
- **17:18** "What do you have against me?" is a hostile idiom. She thinks the prophet's presence has called her sin to the LORD's attention.
- **17:21** "Stretched himself out over the child three times" is repeated by Elisha in 2 Kings 4:34-35.
- **17:22** "The child's life came back" uses *nefesh*. See entry H-NEPHESH. It is the first account in the Bible of a person raised from the dead.
- **18:1** "The third year" is counted from some point in the drought. Luke 4:25 gives three and a half years.
- **18:3** "Obadiah" means "servant of the LORD". He is a high official in Ahab's court who shelters the prophets.
- **18:4** "Jezebel was killing the prophets of the LORD" is a persecution that is mentioned only here and in 18:13 and 19:10. It is the background to Elijah's claim that he is alone.
- **18:12** "The spirit of the LORD" is Hebrew *ruach YHWH*. See entry H-RUACH. Obadiah fears that the spirit will carry Elijah away, as it may carry a prophet (see 2 Kings 2:16).
- **18:17** "The troubler of Israel" is Hebrew *okher Yisra'el*. The same word is used of Achan in Joshua 7:25.
- **18:19** "The four hundred prophets of Asherah" are not mentioned again. The Greek does not have them in this verse.
- **18:21** "Limp between two opinions" is a figure for being unable to choose. The same verb is used in verse 26 of the prophets of Baal, who "limped" round their altar. The Hebrew for "opinions" (*se'ippim*) is a rare word that may mean divided thoughts or branches.
- **18:24** "The god who answers by fire" is a test in which Baal, the storm god and lord of lightning, should be the one to answer.
- **18:27** Elijah's mockery uses uncertain words. "Deep in thought" (*siach*) and "gone aside" (*sig lo*) may mean that Baal is busy, in a meditation or relieving himself. The Hebrew of the second is not certain.
- **18:28** Cutting themselves was a rite of mourning or of pleading, which the law forbade to Israel (Deuteronomy 14:1).
- **18:29** "They raved on" uses the verb *hitnabbe*, the same as "prophesy" (see 1 Samuel 18:10).
- **18:31** "Twelve stones, one for each of the tribes" recalls Joshua 4:1-9. "Israel will be your name" refers to Genesis 32:28 and 35:10.
- **18:32** "Two measures of seed" is a size of trench, about the size of a field that two seahs would sow.
- **18:33** Twelve jars of water poured on the altar in a time of drought makes the test harder, and shows that the fire is not natural.
- **18:36** "The time for the evening offering" is the afternoon offering (Hebrew *minchah*). "God of Abraham, Isaac and Israel" is a form of the divine name that uses "Israel" for Jacob.
- **18:37** "You are turning their hearts back" is the reading that follows the Hebrew verb in the form that can mean the present, the future or the past ("you have turned"). "Turning back" is Hebrew *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **18:40** The killing of the prophets of Baal at the Wadi Kishon is rendered as written. It is told without comment, and it is later part of the cause of Jezebel's threat (19:2). Deuteronomy 13 and 17 require death for those who lead Israel to other gods.
- **18:42** "Put his face between his knees" is a posture of intense prayer.
- **18:44** "A small cloud like a man's hand" is the first sign of rain.
- **18:46** "The hand of the LORD was on Elijah" gives the reason for his speed. He runs about thirty kilometres to Jezreel, ahead of the king's chariot.
- **19:2** "May the gods do this to me, and more also" is a form of the oath formula spoken by a worshipper of many gods.
- **19:3** "He was afraid" follows the Greek, the Syriac and many manuscripts. The Hebrew text most often read has "he saw", which differs by one vowel.
- **19:4** "Broom bush" is Hebrew *rotem*, a desert shrub. Elijah asks that he might die. The text records his words without comment, and the angel's response is food and rest (verses 5 to 8).
- **19:8** "Forty days and forty nights" recall Moses at Sinai (Exodus 34:28). "Horeb" is the name for Sinai in Deuteronomy.
- **19:10** "Zealous" is Hebrew *qanno qinne'ti*, "I have been very jealous". See entry H-QANNA. "Covenant" is *berit*. See entry H-BERIT. Elijah's complaint is repeated word for word in verse 14.
- **19:11** "Wind" is Hebrew *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH. The LORD is not in the wind, the earthquake or the fire, though these are the signs of his coming at Sinai (Exodus 19:16-19).
- **19:12** "A sound of thin silence" is Hebrew *qol demamah daqqah*. The phrase is rare, and each of its words is hard. Other readings are "a sound of sheer silence", "a low whisper", "a still small voice" and "a thin voice of silence". The WABV text keeps the literal "thin silence".
- **19:13** "He wrapped his face in his cloak" is the response of Moses (Exodus 3:6; 33:22) to the presence of the LORD.
- **19:15** The three commissions are Hazael of Aram, Jehu of Israel and Elisha. Hazael and Jehu are anointed after Elijah's time (2 Kings 8:7-15; 9:1-13), and Elijah himself calls only Elisha.
- **19:18** "Seven thousand" is a round number for those who remain faithful. "Kissed him" refers to the act of worship, as in Hosea 13:2. The Hebrew of the verse is the reply to Elijah's claim to be alone.
- **19:19** "Twelve pairs of oxen" shows that Elisha's family was well off. Elijah's cloak is the sign of his office (2 Kings 2:13).
- **19:20** "What have I done to you?" is a question that can mean "go back, for I have done nothing to stop you" or "consider what I have done to you". The sense is not certain.
- **19:21** Elisha kills the oxen and burns the plough equipment as fuel for the meal. He breaks with his old life and then follows Elijah.
- **20:1** "Ben-hadad king of Aram" brings thirty-two kings with him. In the Greek translation chapters 20 and 21 are in the opposite order, so that the story of Naboth comes first. The Hebrew order is followed here.
- **20:3** The demand for silver, gold, wives and children is first a demand for tribute, and Ahab accepts it (verse 4). The second demand, that his servants search the palace (verse 6), is one he refuses.
- **20:10** "May the gods do this to me, and more also" is an oath formula. The dust of Samaria not being enough for handfuls is a boast of the great size of his army.
- **20:11** "The one who puts on his armour should not boast like the one who takes it off" is a proverb. It means that no one should boast before the battle is won.
- **20:12** "The shelters" are the booths or tents where the kings were drinking.
- **20:13** "A prophet" is not named. "You will know that I am the LORD" is a formula of recognition used often in Ezekiel (see also verse 28).
- **20:14** "The young men of the district governors" are the attendants of the governors. They are a small force of 232 (verse 15).
- **20:23** "Their gods are gods of the hills" is the Aramean idea that the LORD is strong in the hills but not on the plains. It is answered in verse 28.
- **20:26** "Aphek" is a town on the plain, east of the Sea of Galilee, or another town of the same name.
- **20:27** "Two small flocks of goats" is a figure for the small size of the Israelite army.
- **20:29** "A hundred thousand" killed in one day is a large figure. 20:30 gives twenty-seven thousand killed by the falling wall.
- **20:32** "He is my brother" is Ahab's reply to the request of Ben-hadad's servants. In verse 34 he makes a treaty with him.
- **20:33** "Watching for a sign" means that the men were looking for any hint of mercy and took Ahab's "brother" at once.
- **20:34** "Markets" or "bazaars" are trading quarters for Israelite merchants in Damascus.
- **20:35** "The company of the prophets" is Hebrew "sons of the prophets", a band of prophets who lived together. The prophet's request to be struck is the setting for a parable.
- **20:36** The lion kills the man who refuses to strike the prophet, as with the man of God in 13:24.
- **20:40** "You have decided it yourself" is the way a parable draws a judgement from the king, as in 2 Samuel 12:5-7.
- **20:42** "The man whom I had devoted to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. The LORD says that Ben-hadad was under the ban, though this is not said earlier. The charge is like the charge against Saul in 1 Samuel 15.
- **21:1** "Naboth the Jezreelite" has a vineyard next to Ahab's palace at Jezreel. "King of Samaria" is the Hebrew phrase for the king of Israel.
- **21:3** "The LORD forbid that I should give you the inheritance of my ancestors" refers to the law that land belonging to a family could not be sold for ever (Leviticus 25:23; Numbers 36:7).
- **21:7** "Do you not now rule over Israel?" is Jezebel's rebuke. She shows that her view of kingship is different from Israel's law, where the king may not take his subjects' land (see 1 Samuel 8:14).
- **21:8** "Letters in Ahab's name" and "his seal" show that Jezebel acts on the king's authority.
- **21:9** "Proclaim a fast" and "seat Naboth at the head of the people" arrange a public trial in which he is made to seem guilty of a crime against the whole town.
- **21:10** "Cursed God and the king" is the charge. The Hebrew text has "blessed" (*berakh*), used as a polite way of saying "cursed", as in Job 1:5. Two witnesses were needed (Deuteronomy 17:6; 19:15), and the penalty for blasphemy was stoning (Leviticus 24:16). "Worthless men" is Hebrew *beliyya'al*.
- **21:13** The killing of Naboth by stoning outside the town is rendered as written. The text calls it murder in verse 19.
- **21:15** Jezebel tells Ahab to take possession. The text does not say why the property of an executed man could be taken by the king.
- **21:19** "In the place where the dogs licked up the blood of Naboth" is fulfilled in 22:38 and in 2 Kings 9:25-26.
- **21:20** "Have you found me, my enemy?" is Ahab's reaction. "You have sold yourself" is Elijah's judgement.
- **21:21** "Every male, slave and free" repeats 14:10 and uses the same Hebrew idioms.
- **21:23** "By the wall of Jezreel" is the Hebrew reading (*chel*). 2 Kings 9:10 and 9:36 have "in the plot of Jezreel" (*cheleq*). The Greek and some manuscripts here read "plot".
- **21:25** The narrator's note that none was like Ahab, urged on by his wife, is added in parentheses. "Idols" is Hebrew *gillulim*, a contemptuous word.
- **21:27** Ahab's humbling is the only sign of repentance. The LORD delays the disaster to his son's days (verse 29), as with Hezekiah in 2 Kings 20:16-19.
- **22:1** "Three years" without war follow the treaty with Ben-hadad (20:34).
- **22:2** Jehoshaphat's visit to Ahab belongs to an alliance sealed by marriage (see 2 Kings 8:18, 26).
- **22:3** "Ramoth-gilead" is a town east of the Jordan that Aram had kept.
- **22:6** "About four hundred men" are prophets who speak in the name of the LORD (verse 11). "The Lord" is Hebrew *Adonai* in this verse.
- **22:11** "Horns of iron" are a symbolic action, like the torn cloak (11:30).
- **22:15** Micaiah's first reply, "Go up and win", is probably ironic, since the king answers by asking him to tell the truth (verse 16).
- **22:17** "Sheep without a shepherd" says that the king will die and the army be scattered.
- **22:19** Micaiah's vision of the LORD on his throne with "the host of heaven" round him is a picture of the heavenly council, as in Job 1 and 2 and Isaiah 6.
- **22:21** "A spirit" is Hebrew *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH. "Persuade" is Hebrew *patah*, a verb that can mean "entice" or "deceive" (see also Exodus 22:16 and Jeremiah 20:7).
- **22:22** "A lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets" is rendered as written. The text states that the LORD sent it and does not explain how the LORD's will relates to the lie. A similar sending of an evil spirit is in Judges 9:23 and 1 Samuel 16:14.
- **22:24** "Which way did the spirit of the LORD go?" is Zedekiah's challenge. It sets the claim of the 400 prophets against Micaiah.
- **22:25** "An inner room to hide" is the king's fate, or Zedekiah's.
- **22:27** "Only a little bread and a little water" is a prison diet. Micaiah answers in verse 28 that if the king returns safely the LORD has not spoken through him.
- **22:28** "Listen, all you peoples" is the same as Micah 1:2. The words are not in the main Greek manuscripts.
- **22:32** "Jehoshaphat cried out" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Chronicles 18:31 adds that the LORD helped him.
- **22:34** "Someone drew his bow without aim" says the arrow was not aimed at the king. The place where it struck is "between the joints of the armour".
- **22:38** "The dogs licked up his blood" fulfils 21:19. The Hebrew says that "the prostitutes washed" at the pool. The Greek says that the dogs licked the blood and the prostitutes washed in it.
- **22:39** "The ivory house" is a palace decorated with ivory, found in the digs at Samaria (see Amos 3:15; 6:4).
- **22:41** In the Hebrew Bible the last verses of this chapter are numbered one higher, so that English 22:43b to 22:53 is Hebrew 22:44 to 22:54.
- **22:43** "The high places were not removed" is the same note as for Asa (15:14).
- **22:46** "Male shrine prostitutes" is Hebrew *qadesh* (see entry H-QADOSH). Asa had begun to remove them (15:12).
- **22:47** "A deputy was king" says Edom was ruled by an officer of Judah.
- **22:48** "Ships of Tarshish" were large ocean ships. The ships were wrecked at Ezion-geber. In 2 Chronicles 20:35-37 Jehoshaphat had made an alliance with Ahaziah for the ships, and a prophet rebuked him.
- **22:52** "The way of his mother" is the way of Jezebel and her worship of Baal.
