## 1 Chronicles 1-2: From Adam to David's Line

### Chapter 1

**1** Adam, Seth, Enosh,

**2** Kenan, Mahalalel, Jared,

**3** Enoch, Methuselah, Lamech,

**4** Noah, Shem, Ham and Japheth.

**5** The sons of Japheth: Gomer, Magog, Madai, Javan, Tubal, Meshech and Tiras.

**6** The sons of Gomer: Ashkenaz, Diphath and Togarmah.

**7** The sons of Javan: Elishah, Tarshish, Kittim and Rodanim.

**8** The sons of Ham: Cush, Mizraim, Put and Canaan.

**9** The sons of Cush: Seba, Havilah, Sabta, Raamah and Sabteca. The sons of Raamah: Sheba and Dedan.

**10** Cush was the father of Nimrod, who was the first to become a mighty man on the earth.

**11** Mizraim was the father of the Ludites, Anamites, Lehabites, Naphtuhites,

**12** Pathrusites, Casluhites (from whom the Philistines came) and Caphtorites.

**13** Canaan was the father of Sidon his firstborn, and of Heth,

**14** and of the Jebusites, the Amorites, the Girgashites,

**15** the Hivites, the Arkites, the Sinites,

**16** the Arvadites, the Zemarites and the Hamathites.

**17** The sons of Shem: Elam, Asshur, Arpachshad, Lud, Aram, Uz, Hul, Gether and Meshech.

**18** Arpachshad was the father of Shelah, and Shelah was the father of Eber.

**19** Two sons were born to Eber. The name of one was Peleg, because in his days the earth was divided, and his brother's name was Joktan.

**20** Joktan was the father of Almodad, Sheleph, Hazarmaveth, Jerah,

**21** Hadoram, Uzal, Diklah,

**22** Ebal, Abimael, Sheba,

**23** Ophir, Havilah and Jobab. All these were sons of Joktan.

**24** Shem, Arpachshad, Shelah,

**25** Eber, Peleg, Reu,

**26** Serug, Nahor, Terah,

**27** Abram, that is Abraham.

**28** The sons of Abraham: Isaac and Ishmael.

**29** These are their family records. The firstborn of Ishmael was Nebaioth, then Kedar, Adbeel, Mibsam,

**30** Mishma, Dumah, Massa, Hadad, Tema,

**31** Jetur, Naphish and Kedemah. These were the sons of Ishmael.

**32** The sons of Keturah, Abraham's concubine: she bore Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak and Shuah. The sons of Jokshan: Sheba and Dedan.

**33** The sons of Midian: Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, Abida and Eldaah. All these were descendants of Keturah.

**34** Abraham was the father of Isaac. The sons of Isaac: Esau and Israel.

**35** The sons of Esau: Eliphaz, Reuel, Jeush, Jalam and Korah.

**36** The sons of Eliphaz: Teman, Omar, Zephi, Gatam, Kenaz, Timna and Amalek.

**37** The sons of Reuel: Nahath, Zerah, Shammah and Mizzah.

**38** The sons of Seir: Lotan, Shobal, Zibeon, Anah, Dishon, Ezer and Dishan.

**39** The sons of Lotan: Hori and Homam. Lotan's sister was Timna.

**40** The sons of Shobal: Alian, Manahath, Ebal, Shepho and Onam. The sons of Zibeon: Aiah and Anah.

**41** The son of Anah: Dishon. The sons of Dishon: Hamran, Eshban, Ithran and Keran.

**42** The sons of Ezer: Bilhan, Zaavan and Jaakan. The sons of Dishan: Uz and Aran.

**43** These are the kings who ruled in the land of Edom before any king ruled over the Israelites: Bela son of Beor, whose town was named Dinhabah.

**44** When Bela died, Jobab son of Zerah from Bozrah became king in his place.

**45** When Jobab died, Husham from the land of the Temanites became king in his place.

**46** When Husham died, Hadad son of Bedad, who defeated Midian in the country of Moab, became king in his place. His town was named Avith.

**47** When Hadad died, Samlah from Masrekah became king in his place.

**48** When Samlah died, Shaul from Rehoboth on the river became king in his place.

**49** When Shaul died, Baal-hanan son of Achbor became king in his place.

**50** When Baal-hanan died, Hadad became king in his place. His town was named Pai, and his wife's name was Mehetabel daughter of Matred, daughter of Me-zahab.

**51** Then Hadad died. The chiefs of Edom were: chief Timna, chief Aliah, chief Jetheth,

**52** chief Oholibamah, chief Elah, chief Pinon,

**53** chief Kenaz, chief Teman, chief Mibzar,

**54** chief Magdiel and chief Iram. These were the chiefs of Edom.

### Chapter 2

**1** These were the sons of Israel: Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun,

**2** Dan, Joseph, Benjamin, Naphtali, Gad and Asher.

**3** The sons of Judah: Er, Onan and Shelah. These three were born to him by the daughter of Shua the Canaanite. Er, Judah's firstborn, was evil in the eyes of the LORD, and he put him to death.

**4** His daughter-in-law Tamar bore him Perez and Zerah. Judah had five sons in all.

**5** The sons of Perez: Hezron and Hamul.

**6** The sons of Zerah: Zimri, Ethan, Heman, Calcol and Dara, five in all.

**7** The son of Carmi: Achar, who brought trouble on Israel by breaking faith over what was devoted to destruction \[H-CHEREM\].

**8** The son of Ethan: Azariah.

**9** The sons who were born to Hezron: Jerahmeel, Ram and Chelubai.

**10** Ram was the father of Amminadab, and Amminadab was the father of Nahshon, the leader of the people of Judah.

**11** Nahshon was the father of Salma, and Salma was the father of Boaz.

**12** Boaz was the father of Obed, and Obed was the father of Jesse.

**13** Jesse was the father of Eliab his firstborn, Abinadab the second, Shimea the third,

**14** Nethanel the fourth, Raddai the fifth,

**15** Ozem the sixth and David the seventh.

**16** Their sisters were Zeruiah and Abigail. The sons of Zeruiah were Abishai, Joab and Asahel, three.

**17** Abigail bore Amasa, and the father of Amasa was Jether the Ishmaelite.

**18** Caleb son of Hezron had children by Azubah his wife, and by Jerioth. These were her sons: Jesher, Shobab and Ardon.

**19** When Azubah died, Caleb married Ephrath, who bore him Hur.

**20** Hur was the father of Uri, and Uri was the father of Bezalel.

**21** Afterwards Hezron went in to the daughter of Machir the father of Gilead, whom he married when he was sixty years old, and she bore him Segub.

**22** Segub was the father of Jair, who had twenty-three towns in the land of Gilead.

**23** (But Geshur and Aram took from them Havvoth-jair, with Kenath and its villages, sixty towns.) All these were descendants of Machir, the father of Gilead.

**24** After the death of Hezron, Caleb went in to Ephrathah, the wife of his father Hezron, and she bore him Ashhur, the father of Tekoa.

**25** The sons of Jerahmeel, the firstborn of Hezron: Ram his firstborn, Bunah, Oren, Ozem and Ahijah.

**26** Jerahmeel had another wife, whose name was Atarah. She was the mother of Onam.

**27** The sons of Ram, the firstborn of Jerahmeel: Maaz, Jamin and Eker.

**28** The sons of Onam: Shammai and Jada. The sons of Shammai: Nadab and Abishur.

**29** The name of Abishur's wife was Abihail, and she bore him Ahban and Molid.

**30** The sons of Nadab: Seled and Appaim. Seled died without children.

**31** The son of Appaim: Ishi. The son of Ishi: Sheshan. The son of Sheshan: Ahlai.

**32** The sons of Jada, Shammai's brother: Jether and Jonathan. Jether died without children.

**33** The sons of Jonathan: Peleth and Zaza. These were the descendants of Jerahmeel.

**34** Now Sheshan had no sons, only daughters. Sheshan had an Egyptian servant whose name was Jarha.

**35** Sheshan gave his daughter in marriage to his servant Jarha, and she bore him Attai.

**36** Attai was the father of Nathan, and Nathan was the father of Zabad.

**37** Zabad was the father of Ephlal, and Ephlal was the father of Obed.

**38** Obed was the father of Jehu, and Jehu was the father of Azariah.

**39** Azariah was the father of Helez, and Helez was the father of Eleasah.

**40** Eleasah was the father of Sismai, and Sismai was the father of Shallum.

**41** Shallum was the father of Jekamiah, and Jekamiah was the father of Elishama.

**42** The sons of Caleb the brother of Jerahmeel: Mesha his firstborn, who was the father of Ziph, and the sons of Mareshah, the father of Hebron.

**43** The sons of Hebron: Korah, Tappuah, Rekem and Shema.

**44** Shema was the father of Raham, the father of Jorkeam, and Rekem was the father of Shammai.

**45** The son of Shammai was Maon, and Maon was the father of Beth-zur.

**46** Ephah, Caleb's concubine, bore Haran, Moza and Gazez. Haran was the father of Gazez.

**47** The sons of Jahdai: Regem, Jotham, Geshan, Pelet, Ephah and Shaaph.

**48** Maacah, Caleb's concubine, bore Sheber and Tirhanah.

**49** She also bore Shaaph the father of Madmannah, and Sheva the father of Machbenah and the father of Gibea. The daughter of Caleb was Achsah.

**50** These were the descendants of Caleb. The sons of Hur, the firstborn of Ephrathah: Shobal the father of Kiriath-jearim,

**51** Salma the father of Bethlehem, and Hareph the father of Beth-gader.

**52** Shobal the father of Kiriath-jearim had sons: Haroeh and half of the Menuhoth.

**53** The clans of Kiriath-jearim: the Ithrites, the Puthites, the Shumathites and the Mishraites. From these came the Zorathites and the Eshtaolites.

**54** The sons of Salma: Bethlehem, the Netophathites, Atroth-beth-joab, and half of the Manahathites, the Zorites.

**55** The clans of scribes who lived at Jabez: the Tirathites, the Shimeathites and the Sucathites. These are the Kenites who came from Hammath, the father of the house of Rechab.

*Review note: 1 Chronicles 1 and 2 was drafted in WABV wording. The chapters are genealogies, and the wording follows the parallel lists in Genesis, Numbers and Ruth where they overlap. Personal and place names are spelled as in the WABV Genesis. The Hebrew text of the lists differs from other books in many names and numbers, and the footnotes record the main differences. Several verses (for example 2:42, 2:49, 2:52-55) are very hard in the Hebrew. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

### Chapter 4

**1** The sons of Judah: Perez, Hezron, Carmi, Hur and Shobal.

**2** Reaiah son of Shobal was the father of Jahath, and Jahath was the father of Ahumai and Lahad. These were the clans of the Zorathites.

**3** These were the sons of Etam: Jezreel, Ishma and Idbash. Their sister's name was Hazzelelponi.

**4** Penuel was the father of Gedor, and Ezer the father of Hushah. These were the sons of Hur, the firstborn of Ephrathah, the father of Bethlehem.

**5** Ashhur, the father of Tekoa, had two wives, Helah and Naarah.

**6** Naarah bore him Ahuzzam, Hepher, Temeni and Haahashtari. These were the sons of Naarah.

**7** The sons of Helah: Zereth, Izhar and Ethnan.

**8** Koz was the father of Anub and Zobebah, and of the clans of Aharhel son of Harum.

**9** Jabez was more honoured than his brothers. His mother named him Jabez, saying, "I bore him in pain."

**10** Jabez called on the God of Israel: "If only you would bless me and enlarge my territory, and let your hand be with me, and keep me from harm, so that I may not be in pain." And God gave him what he asked.

**11** Chelub, the brother of Shuhah, was the father of Mehir, who was the father of Eshton.

**12** Eshton was the father of Beth-rapha, Paseah and Tehinnah the father of Ir-nahash. These were the men of Recah.

**13** The sons of Kenaz: Othniel and Seraiah. The sons of Othniel: Hathath and Meonothai.

**14** Meonothai was the father of Ophrah. Seraiah was the father of Joab, the father of Ge-harashim, so called because they were craftsmen.

**15** The sons of Caleb son of Jephunneh: Iru, Elah and Naam. The son of Elah: Kenaz.

**16** The sons of Jehallelel: Ziph, Ziphah, Tiria and Asarel.

**17** The sons of Ezrah: Jether, Mered, Epher and Jalon. These are the sons of Bithiah daughter of Pharaoh, whom Mered married. She bore Miriam, Shammai and Ishbah the father of Eshtemoa.

**18** His Judean wife bore Jered the father of Gedor, Heber the father of Soco and Jekuthiel the father of Zanoah.

**19** The sons of the wife of Hodiah, the sister of Naham: the father of Keilah the Garmite, and Eshtemoa the Maacathite.

**20** The sons of Shimon: Amnon, Rinnah, Ben-hanan and Tilon. The sons of Ishi: Zoheth and Ben-zoheth.

**21** The sons of Shelah son of Judah: Er the father of Lecah, Laadah the father of Mareshah, and the clans of the linen workers at Beth-ashbea,

**22** and Jokim, and the men of Cozeba, and Joash and Saraph, who ruled in Moab and returned to Lehem. (These records are ancient.)

**23** They were the potters and those who lived at Netaim and Gederah. They lived there in the king's service.

**24** The sons of Simeon: Nemuel, Jamin, Jarib, Zerah and Shaul.

**25** Shallum was his son, Mibsam his son, Mishma his son.

**26** The sons of Mishma: his son Hammuel, his son Zaccur, his son Shimei.

**28** They lived at Beersheba, Moladah, Hazar-shual,

**29** Bilhah, Ezem, Tolad,

**30** Bethuel, Hormah, Ziklag,

**31** Beth-marcaboth, Hazar-susim, Beth-biri and Shaaraim. These were their towns until David became king.

**32** Their villages were Etam, Ain, Rimmon, Tochen and Ashan, five towns,

**33** with all their villages round these towns as far as Baalath. These were their settlements, and they kept a record of their families.

**34** Meshobab, Jamlech, Joshah son of Amaziah,

**35** Joel, Jehu son of Joshibiah, son of Seraiah, son of Asiel,

**36** Elioenai, Jaakobah, Jeshohaiah, Asaiah, Adiel, Jesimiel, Benaiah,

**37** and Ziza son of Shiphi, son of Allon, son of Jedaiah, son of Shimri, son of Shemaiah.

**38** These listed by name were leaders in their clans, and their families grew greatly.

**39** They went to the entrance of Gedor, to the east side of the valley, to look for pasture for their flocks,

**40** and they found rich, good pasture, and the land was wide, quiet and peaceful. Some descendants of Ham had lived there before.

**41** These men, listed by name, came in the days of Hezekiah king of Judah and struck the tents of the Hamites and the Meunites who were found there, and devoted them to destruction \[H-CHEREM\], as is still the case today. They settled in their place, because there was pasture there for their flocks.

**42** Five hundred of these Simeonites went to the hill country of Seir, with Pelatiah, Neariah, Rephaiah and Uzziel, sons of Ishi, as their leaders.

**43** They struck down the remnant of the Amalekites who had escaped, and they have lived there to this day.

### Chapter 5

**1** The sons of Reuben the firstborn of Israel. (He was the firstborn, but because he defiled his father's bed, his birthright was given to the sons of Joseph son of Israel, so that he is not listed in the family records according to the birthright.

**2** Judah became strong among his brothers, and a leader came from him, but the birthright belonged to Joseph.)

**3** The sons of Reuben the firstborn of Israel: Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron and Carmi.

**4** The sons of Joel: his son Shemaiah, his son Gog, his son Shimei,

**5** his son Micah, his son Reaiah, his son Baal,

**6** his son Beerah, whom Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria took into exile. He was a leader of the Reubenites.

**7** His relatives by their clans, as listed in the family records of their generations: the chief Jeiel, Zechariah

**8** and Bela son of Azaz, son of Shema, son of Joel. They lived in Aroer, as far as Nebo and Baal-meon.

**9** To the east he lived as far as the edge of the desert that stretches to the Euphrates, because their herds had increased in the land of Gilead.

**10** In the days of Saul they made war against the Hagrites, who fell into their hands. They lived in their tents throughout the whole region east of Gilead.

**11** The sons of Gad lived opposite them in the land of Bashan as far as Salecah:

**12** Joel the chief, Shapham the second, then Janai and Shaphat in Bashan.

**13** Their relatives by their fathers' houses: Michael, Meshullam, Sheba, Jorai, Jacan, Zia and Eber, seven.

**14** These were the sons of Abihail son of Huri, son of Jaroah, son of Gilead, son of Michael, son of Jeshishai, son of Jahdo, son of Buz.

**15** Ahi son of Abdiel, son of Guni, was the head of their fathers' houses.

**16** They lived in Gilead, in Bashan and its towns, and in all the pasturelands of Sharon, as far as their borders.

**17** All of these were listed in the family records in the days of Jotham king of Judah and in the days of Jeroboam king of Israel.

**18** The Reubenites, the Gadites and the half-tribe of Manasseh had forty-four thousand seven hundred and sixty men able to bear shield and sword, to draw the bow, and trained for war, fit for service.

**19** They made war against the Hagrites, Jetur, Naphish and Nodab.

**20** They were helped against them, and the Hagrites and all who were with them were handed over to them, because they cried out to God in the battle, and he answered their plea, because they trusted in him.

**21** They took their livestock: fifty thousand camels, two hundred and fifty thousand sheep, two thousand donkeys, and a hundred thousand people.

**22** Many fell dead, because the battle was from God. They lived in their place until the exile.

**23** The members of the half-tribe of Manasseh lived in the land. They were very many, from Bashan to Baal-hermon, to Senir and Mount Hermon.

**24** These were the heads of their fathers' houses: Epher, Ishi, Eliel, Azriel, Jeremiah, Hodaviah and Jahdiel, brave warriors, famous men, heads of their fathers' houses.

**25** But they were unfaithful to the God of their ancestors, and prostituted themselves to the gods of the peoples of the land, whom God had destroyed before them.

**26** So the God of Israel stirred up the spirit \[H-RUACH\] of Pul king of Assyria, the spirit of Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, and he took them into exile, the Reubenites, the Gadites and the half-tribe of Manasseh, and brought them to Halah, Habor, Hara and the river Gozan, where they are to this day.

*Review note: 1 Chronicles 3 to 5 was drafted in WABV wording. These are rendered as written. The Hebrew text of the lists is difficult in many places, especially 3:17-24, 4:1-23 and 4:34-43, and the footnotes record the main differences. **18** and refined gold for the altar of incense, with its weight, and the plan for the chariot of gold, the cherubim that spread their wings and covered the ark of the covenant of the LORD.

**20** David said to his son Solomon, "Be strong and brave, and do it. Do not be afraid or discouraged, for the LORD God, my God, is with you. He will not fail you or abandon you until all the work for the service of the house of the LORD is finished.

**21** Here are the divisions of the priests and the Levites for all the service of the house of God. Every willing person who has skill in any kind of work will be with you for all the work, and the officials and all the people will do whatever you command."

### Chapter 29

**1** King David said to the whole assembly, "My son Solomon, the one whom God has chosen, is young and inexperienced, and the work is great, for the palace is not for a human being but for the LORD God.

**2** With all my strength I have provided for the house of my God: gold for the things of gold, silver for the things of silver, bronze for the things of bronze, iron for the things of iron and wood for the things of wood, and onyx and stones to be set, stones for mosaic, all kinds of precious stones and slabs of marble in great amounts.

**3** Besides all that I have provided for the holy house, I now give my own treasure of gold and silver to the house of my God, because of my love for the house of my God:

**4** three thousand talents of gold, of the gold of Ophir, and seven thousand talents of refined silver, to overlay the walls of the buildings,

**5** for the things of gold and the things of silver, and for all the work to be done by the craftsmen. Now who is willing to give himself today to the LORD?"

**6** Then the heads of the fathers' houses, the officers of the tribes of Israel, the commanders of thousands and of hundreds, and the officials over the king's work gave willingly.

**7** For the service of the house of God they gave five thousand talents and ten thousand darics of gold, ten thousand talents of silver, eighteen thousand talents of bronze and a hundred thousand talents of iron.

**8** Anyone who had precious stones gave them to the treasury of the house of the LORD, in the care of Jehiel the Gershonite.

**9** The people rejoiced at their willing giving, for they had given to the LORD with a whole heart. King David also rejoiced greatly.

**10** Then David blessed the LORD in the presence of the whole assembly. David said:

Blessed are you, LORD, God of our father Israel, from everlasting to everlasting.

**11** Yours, LORD, are the greatness and the power, the glory, the victory and the majesty, for everything in heaven and on earth is yours. Yours, LORD, is the kingdom, and you are lifted up as head over all.

**12** Riches and honour come from you, and you rule over all. In your hand are power and strength, and in your hand is the power to make great and to give strength to all.

**13** Now, our God, we thank you and praise your glorious name.

**14** But who am I, and who are my people, that we should be able to give so willingly? For everything comes from you, and we have given you only what is yours.

**15** We are foreigners \[H-GER\] before you, and strangers, as all our ancestors were. Our days on earth are like a shadow, and there is no hope of staying.

**16** LORD our God, all this abundance that we have provided to build you a house for your holy name comes from your hand, and all of it is yours.

**17** I know, my God, that you test the heart and take pleasure in honesty. With an honest heart I have given all these things willingly, and now I have seen with joy your people who are here giving willingly to you.

**18** LORD, God of our ancestors Abraham, Isaac and Israel, keep this for ever in the thoughts and purposes of your people's hearts, and direct their hearts towards you.

**19** Give my son Solomon a whole heart to keep your commands, your testimonies and your statutes, to do all of them, and to build the palace for which I have made provision.

**20** Then David said to the whole assembly, "Now bless the LORD your God." And the whole assembly blessed the LORD, the God of their ancestors, and bowed their heads and bowed down to the LORD and to the king.

**21** The next day they offered sacrifices to the LORD and burnt offerings to the LORD: a thousand bulls, a thousand rams and a thousand lambs, with their drink offerings, and sacrifices in great numbers for all Israel.

**22** They ate and drank before the LORD that day with great joy. They made Solomon son of David king a second time, and anointed him as leader for the LORD, and Zadok as priest.

**23** Solomon sat on the throne of the LORD as king in place of his father David. He prospered, and all Israel obeyed him.

**24** All the officials and the warriors, and all the sons of King David as well, pledged their loyalty to King Solomon.

**25** The LORD made Solomon very great in the eyes of all Israel and gave him a royal majesty such as no king before him in Israel had had.

**26** David son of Jesse ruled over all Israel.

**27** The time that he ruled over Israel was forty years. He ruled for seven years in Hebron and thirty-three years in Jerusalem.

**28** He died at a good old age, full of years, riches and honour, and his son Solomon became king in his place.

**29** The acts of King David, from first to last, are written in the records of Samuel the seer, and in the records of Nathan the prophet, and in the records of Gad the seer,

**30** with all the details of his rule and his power, and of the events that happened to him and to Israel and to all the kingdoms of the lands.

*End of the book of 1 Chronicles. Review note: 1 Chronicles 28 and 29 was drafted in WABV wording. David's prayer (29:10-19) is set as verse lines, and its wording is kept for the WABV Psalms and for 2 Chronicles. David's words to the assembly and the heavy emphasis on his giving and on Solomon's task are the Chronicler's own, and there is no parallel in Samuel or Kings. The plan given "in writing from the hand of the LORD" (28:19) is not explained in the text. The numbers of the gifts (29:4, 7) are very large. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **Title** 1 and 2 Chronicles were one book in the Hebrew Bible, placed last in the Writings. The book retells the story from Adam to the return from exile, using Samuel and Kings and other sources that it names. It was written after the exile. The Greek calls it "the things left out", but it is not a supplement. It has its own view of the history, centred on David, Solomon and the temple.
- **1:1** The list from Adam to Noah (verses 1 to 4) follows Genesis 5. Name spellings follow the WABV Genesis.
- **1:5** "The sons of Japheth" and the nations of verses 5 to 23 follow the table of the nations in Genesis 10. It lists peoples as if they were individuals.
- **1:6** "Diphath" is the reading here. Genesis 10:3 has "Riphath". The two Hebrew letters (*daleth* and *resh*) look alike.
- **1:7** "Rodanim" is the Hebrew reading here. Genesis 10:4 has "Dodanim", for the same reason.
- **1:10** "Nimrod" is called "the first to become a mighty man on the earth". The text says no more here than in Genesis 10:8-12.
- **1:12** "The Casluhites (from whom the Philistines came)" is set in a different place from Genesis 10:14. The order of words differs between the two lists.
- **1:17** "Meshech" is the Hebrew reading. Genesis 10:23 has "Mash". The list of the sons of Aram (Uz, Hul, Gether, Mash) is joined to the sons of Shem here.
- **1:19** "The earth was divided" is a play on the name Peleg (*peleg*, "division"). It may refer to the dividing of the peoples (Genesis 10:25).
- **1:24** "Shem, Arpachshad, Shelah" begins the line of Abraham, as in Genesis 11:10-26.
- **1:27** "Abram, that is Abraham" recalls the change of name in Genesis 17:5.
- **1:29** "The firstborn of Ishmael" and the list of his twelve sons follow Genesis 25:13-16.
- **1:32** "The sons of Keturah" follow Genesis 25:1-4. She is Abraham's concubine here, as in verse 32 of the Hebrew.
- **1:36** "Timna" is a son of Eliphaz here. In Genesis 36:12 Timna is a concubine of Eliphaz. The wording differs between the lists.
- **1:38** "The sons of Seir" follow Genesis 36:20-28. The names differ slightly in spelling in several places (for example Hamran for Hemdan in verse 41, and Alian for Alvan in verse 40).
- **1:43** "The kings who ruled in the land of Edom" follow Genesis 36:31-43. "Before any king ruled over the Israelites" is the writer's note.
- **1:50** "Hadad" is the Hebrew reading. Genesis 36:39 has "Hadar".
- **1:51** "Then Hadad died" is added here and is not in Genesis. The list of the chiefs of Edom follows.
- **2:1** "The sons of Israel" are listed in a different order from Genesis 35:23-26. The order here does not follow the mothers.
- **2:3** "He put him to death" refers to Genesis 38:7, where the LORD kills Er. The story of Judah and Tamar is told in Genesis 38.
- **2:6** "Ethan, Heman, Calcol and Dara" are also mentioned in 1 Kings 4:31 as men famous for their wisdom. "Zimri" is called Zabdi in Joshua 7:1. "Dara" may be an error for "Darda" in 1 Kings 4:31.
- **2:7** "Achar" is a form of the name Achan (Joshua 7). The Hebrew puns on his name: he "brought trouble" (*akhar*) on Israel. The offence is described as breaking faith over what was "devoted to destruction" (*cherem*). See entry H-CHEREM.
- **2:9** "Chelubai" is another form of the name Caleb (see verse 18).
- **2:10** "Nahshon" is the leader of Judah at the time of the wilderness (Numbers 1:7).
- **2:12** The line from Boaz to Jesse follows Ruth 4:18-22.
- **2:13** "Jesse was the father of ... David the seventh" lists seven sons. 1 Samuel 16:10-11 and 17:12 speak of eight, and one may have died without children.
- **2:16** "Zeruiah and Abigail" are called sisters of David's brothers. 2 Samuel 17:25 says Abigail was a daughter of Nahash.
- **2:17** "Jether the Ishmaelite" is the Hebrew reading here. 2 Samuel 17:25 has "Ithra the Israelite".
- **2:18** The Hebrew of verses 18 to 24 is hard, and the sense of several phrases is not certain. Caleb's wives and children are listed in a way that is difficult to follow.
- **2:20** "Bezalel" is the maker of the tent (Exodus 31:2).
- **2:21** "Machir the father of Gilead" is the son of Manasseh (Numbers 26:29). Hezron marries his daughter.
- **2:22** "Jair" is the one who took towns in Gilead (Numbers 32:41; Deuteronomy 3:14).
- **2:24** "Caleb went in to Ephrathah" is the usual reading. The Hebrew words are hard, and may mean "Caleb-ephrathah" as a place.
- **2:34** "Sheshan had an Egyptian servant" who marries his daughter, so that his line continues. The text records it without comment.
- **2:42** "The sons of Mareshah, the father of Hebron" is hard in the Hebrew, and the WABV text follows the Hebrew as it stands.
- **2:49** "The daughter of Caleb was Achsah" refers to the story in Joshua 15:16-19 and Judges 1:12-15.
- **2:52** "Haroeh and half of the Menuhoth" is hard. The words may be names of places or clans.
- **2:55** "The clans of scribes who lived at Jabez" is uncertain. The word for "scribes" (*soferim*) may also mean other kinds of officials. "The Kenites ... the house of Rechab" connects the Rechabites (2 Kings 10:15; Jeremiah 35) with the Kenites.
- **3:1** "The sons of David who were born to him at Hebron" follow 2 Samuel 3:2-5. "Daniel" is the second son here. In 2 Samuel 3:3 the name is Chileab.
- **3:2** "Maacah daughter of Talmai king of Geshur" is the mother of Absalom (2 Samuel 3:3).
- **3:4** "Seven years and six months" at Hebron and "thirty-three years" in Jerusalem are the lengths given in 2 Samuel 5:5.
- **3:5** "Bath-shua daughter of Ammiel" is Bathsheba. In 2 Samuel 11:3 she is Bathsheba daughter of Eliam. The two parts of the name are in the opposite order (*Eliam* and *Ammiel*). "Shimea" is called Shammua in 2 Samuel 5:14.
- **3:6** The list of David's sons born in Jerusalem (verses 6 to 8) says "nine" and names nine. 2 Samuel 5:14-16 names eleven and 1 Chronicles 14:4-7 names thirteen, including Elishua. The lists differ in the names and the count.
- **3:9** "Tamar" is the sister of Absalom (2 Samuel 13).
- **3:10** The line of David's descendants in verses 10 to 14 omits Queen Athaliah and several short reigns. "Azariah" in verse 12 is the king also called Uzziah.
- **3:15** "Shallum" is called Jehoahaz in 2 Kings 23:30. "Johanan" is not otherwise known as a king.
- **3:16** "His son Zedekiah" is hard to read. The last king Zedekiah was a brother of Jehoiakim (2 Kings 24:17), so this may mean a different Zedekiah, or may be an error for "his brother".
- **3:17** "Jeconiah the captive" is the king Jehoiachin, taken into exile. The Hebrew word *assir* can mean "captive" or be a name. The WABV text takes it as a description.
- **3:19** "Zerubbabel" is called the son of Pedaiah here. In Ezra 3:2 and Haggai 1:1 he is the son of Shealtiel, who was Pedaiah's brother. The text records both.
- **3:21** The Hebrew of verses 21 and 22 is hard. The list goes on for several generations after Zerubbabel, which shows that the book was written well after the exile.
- **4:1** "The sons of Judah" lists Perez, Hezron, Carmi, Hur and Shobal. These are clans as well as persons, and the list does not follow the lines of descent in chapter 2.
- **4:3** "Etam" and "Jezreel" are places in Judah. The Hebrew of this verse is uncertain.
- **4:9** "Jabez" sounds like the Hebrew word for "pain" (*otsev*). The name plays on that sound. "I bore him in pain" is the mother's explanation.
- **4:10** "Enlarge my territory" and "let your hand be with me" are the requests of Jabez. The last request, "so that I may not be in pain", plays again on his name. The Hebrew of the last clause is hard.
- **4:14** "Ge-harashim" means "valley of craftsmen" and is mentioned in Nehemiah 11:35.
- **4:17** "Bithiah daughter of Pharaoh" is an Egyptian wife of a man of Judah. The Hebrew of verses 17 and 18 is hard, and the order of the clauses may be disturbed.
- **4:21** "The clans of the linen workers" is the reading of the Hebrew. It may refer to a guild that made fine cloth.
- **4:22** "Who ruled in Moab" is the Hebrew reading and is uncertain. It may mean that they married women of Moab. "Lehem" may be Bethlehem. "These records are ancient" is a note on the source.
- **4:23** "In the king's service" says that these potters worked for the royal house.
- **4:24** "The sons of Simeon" follow Genesis 46:10 and Numbers 26:12-14, with differences in spelling. "Jarib" is Jachin there.
- **4:27** "Shimei had sixteen sons and six daughters" is a way of saying that Simeon's clans were small compared with Judah's.
- **4:28** "They lived at Beersheba ..." The towns of verses 28 to 32 are those given to Simeon in Joshua 19:2-8 and listed in Nehemiah 11:26-30.
- **4:31** "Until David became king" is the Hebrew reading. It may mean until David set up his rule over these towns.
- **4:39** "Gedor" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek has "Gerar".
- **4:41** "Struck the tents of the Hamites and the Meunites ... and devoted them to destruction" is rendered as written. "Devoted to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. The Meunites were an Arabian people. The text does not comment on the act.
- **4:43** "The remnant of the Amalekites who had escaped" refers to what was left after the wars of Saul and David (1 Samuel 15; 30).
- **5:1** "He defiled his father's bed" refers to Reuben's act in Genesis 35:22. The loss of his birthright is spoken of in Genesis 49:3-4. The birthright goes to the sons of Joseph, which means a double share for Ephraim and Manasseh.
- **5:2** "Judah became strong among his brothers, and a leader came from him" refers to David. Judah's place is leadership, and Joseph's is the birthright.
- **5:3** "The sons of Reuben" follow Genesis 46:9 and Numbers 26:5-6.
- **5:6** "Beerah ... whom Tiglath-pileser ... took into exile" refers to the exile of 733 to 732 BCE (see 2 Kings 15:29).
- **5:10** "The Hagrites" are an Arabian people (see Psalm 83:6). The war in the days of Saul is not told elsewhere.
- **5:11** "Salecah" is a town at the east end of Bashan.
- **5:17** "Jotham king of Judah and Jeroboam king of Israel" gives the time of the family records. It was in the eighth century BCE.
- **5:18** "Forty-four thousand seven hundred and sixty" is the number of fighting men of the eastern tribes.
- **5:20** "They cried out to God in the battle, and he answered their plea, because they trusted in him" is a theme of Chronicles. It is repeated in 2 Chronicles 13:14-15; 14:11-12; 18:31.
- **5:21** "A hundred thousand people" is the number of captives, given with the livestock.
- **5:22** "The battle was from God" gives the reason for the great number of dead.
- **5:25** "They were unfaithful" is Hebrew *ma'al*, to break faith. It is a key word in Chronicles. "Prostituted themselves" is the standard figure for worshipping other gods.
- **5:26** "The spirit of Pul ... the spirit of Tiglath-pileser" names one king twice. "Pul" is another name for Tiglath-pileser III (2 Kings 15:19). "Spirit" is Hebrew *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH. The LORD stirs up the king, as in Ezra 1:1.
- **6:1** In the Hebrew Bible 6:1-15 is numbered 5:27-41, and chapter 6 begins at what is 6:16 in English. The Hebrew chapter 6 therefore has sixty-six verses, ending at English 6:81. "The sons of Levi" follow Exodus 6:16-25.
- **6:3** "Aaron, Moses and Miriam" are the three children of Amram. The sons of Aaron are named in Exodus 6:23. Nadab and Abihu died without sons (Leviticus 10:1-2).
- **6:4** The line of the high priests from Eleazar to the exile (verses 4 to 15) goes through Zadok. It leaves out some names that Ezra 7:1-5 also leaves out, and it does not include Eli or Abiathar, who belonged to the line of Ithamar.
- **6:10** "It was he who served as priest in the house that Solomon built" is placed after Azariah son of Johanan. It may belong to an earlier Azariah (1 Kings 4:2), and the position is uncertain.
- **6:13** "Hilkiah" is the high priest in the time of Josiah (2 Kings 22:8). "Seraiah" (verse 14) was executed at Riblah (2 Kings 25:18-21).
- **6:15** "The LORD sent Judah and Jerusalem into exile" gives the exile as the LORD's act. "Jehozadak" was the father of Joshua the high priest (Haggai 1:1).
- **6:16** "Gershom" is another form of the name Gershon (verse 1). Both forms are in the Hebrew.
- **6:19** "Mahli and Mushi" are the sons of Merari (Exodus 6:19).
- **6:22** "Amminadab his son, Korah his son" does not agree with Exodus 6:21, where Korah is the son of Izhar. Some Greek manuscripts have "Izhar" in place of "Amminadab".
- **6:25** "Elkanah" and "Samuel" belong to a Levite line in this chapter. 1 Samuel 1:1 calls Elkanah an Ephraimite. The two may mean that he was a Levite who lived in Ephraim.
- **6:28** "Joel the firstborn" follows the Greek, the Syriac and verse 33. The Hebrew has the word *Vashni*, which is probably a corruption of "and the second". 1 Samuel 8:2 names the sons as Joel and Abijah. The WABV text departs from the Hebrew word and is flagged for the scholar. The list in verses 25 to 28 names Elkanah and then Samuel's sons, without naming Samuel in the Hebrew, and some ancient texts add "Samuel his son".
- **6:31** "David put in charge of the music" refers to the story in 1 Chronicles 15 and 16. "After the ark came to rest there" means after it was brought to Jerusalem.
- **6:32** "The tabernacle of the tent of meeting" is the tent at Gibeon (1 Chronicles 16:39), where the singers served until the temple was built.
- **6:33** "Heman the singer" is the head of the Kohathite singers. His grandfather Samuel is the prophet. Psalm 88 is attributed to Heman the Ezrahite in its title.
- **6:39** "Asaph" stands at Heman's right hand. Twelve psalms (50 and 73 to 83) are attributed to Asaph in their titles.
- **6:44** "Ethan" leads the Merarite singers on the left. He may also be the Jeduthun of 16:41 and 25:1.
- **6:48** "Their relatives the Levites" are those not of the family of Aaron. They serve in the tabernacle but not at the altar.
- **6:49** "The most holy place" is Hebrew *qodesh haqqodashim*. See entry H-QADOSH. "Make atonement" is *kapper*. See entry H-KAPPER. Only Aaron and his sons make offerings and atonement.
- **6:54** "Their dwelling places" begins the list of the Levite towns (verses 54 to 81), which is closely parallel to Joshua 21:4-39. "The first lot was theirs" says that the Aaronite Kohathites were given their towns first (Joshua 21:10).
- **6:55** "Hebron" was given to the sons of Aaron. "The fields of the town" went to Caleb (Joshua 14:13-14; 21:11-12).
- **6:57** "The towns of refuge: Hebron" are marked here. Hebron was a city of refuge (Joshua 20:7). The towns named here are eleven, but the total in verse 60 is thirteen. Joshua 21:16-17 also names Juttah and Gibeon, which are not named here.
- **6:60** "Thirteen" is the number of towns of the sons of Aaron, as in Joshua 21:19. "Alemeth" is called Almon in Joshua 21:18.
- **6:61** "Ten towns from the clans of the half-tribe of Manasseh" does not agree with Joshua 21:5, where the ten towns are from Ephraim, Dan and the half-tribe of Manasseh. The Hebrew of this verse may be damaged.
- **6:62** "Thirteen towns" for the Gershomites follow Joshua 21:6.
- **6:63** "Twelve towns" for the Merarites follow Joshua 21:7.
- **6:66** "Some of the clans of the Kohathites" had towns from Ephraim. In the list that follows, Aijalon and Gath-rimmon (verse 69) are given with the Ephraimite towns, but in Joshua 21:23-24 they come from Dan, whose list is not given here.
- **6:67** "Shechem" was a city of refuge (Joshua 20:7). "Jokmeam" (verse 68) is Kibzaim in Joshua 21:22.
- **6:70** "Aner" and "Bileam" are called Tanaach and Gath-rimmon in Joshua 21:25.
- **6:71** "Golan in Bashan and Ashtaroth" are the Gershomite towns in Manasseh east of the Jordan. "Ashtaroth" is Be-eshterah in Joshua 21:27.
- **6:72** "Kedesh" is Kishion in Joshua 21:28. "Ramoth" (verse 73) is Jarmuth and "Anem" is En-gannim in Joshua 21:29.
- **6:75** "Hukok" is Helkath in Joshua 21:31.
- **6:76** "Hammon" is Hammoth-dor and "Kiriathaim" is Kartan in Joshua 21:32.
- **6:77** "Rimmono" and "Tabor" are given in place of the names in Joshua 21:34-35. Those verses name Jokneam, Kartah, Dimnah and Nahalal, and the Hebrew of this verse may be shortened.
- **6:78** "Bezer in the wilderness" was a city of refuge for Reuben (Deuteronomy 4:43; Joshua 20:8). This verse and the next three are missing from some Hebrew manuscripts of Joshua 21:36-37.
- **6:80** "Ramoth in Gilead" was a city of refuge in Gad. "Heshbon and Jazer" end the list of the towns.
- **7:1** "The sons of Issachar" follow Genesis 46:13 and Numbers 26:23-24 with differences in spelling. "Jashub" is Iob in some texts of Genesis 46:13, and "Puah" is Puvah.
- **7:2** "Their number in the days of David was twenty-two thousand six hundred" refers to the census of David's time (1 Chronicles 21).
- **7:4** "They had many wives and sons" gives the reason for the large number of fighting men.
- **7:6** "The sons of Benjamin: Bela, Becher and Jediael, three" gives a short list. Genesis 46:21 names ten sons of Benjamin, Numbers 26:38-41 five, and chapter 8 here five. The lists differ, and the footnotes do not try to join them.
- **7:12** "Shuppim and Huppim were the sons of Ir, and Hushim the son of Aher" is very hard in the Hebrew. "Hushim" is the son of Dan in Genesis 46:23, and "Aher" may mean "another", so the verse may be a fragment of the tribe of Dan. The tribes of Dan and Zebulun are not listed in this chapter.
- **7:13** "The sons of Naphtali" follow Genesis 46:24 and Numbers 26:48-49. "Shallum" is Shillem there.
- **7:14** The Hebrew of verses 14 to 19, on the families of Manasseh, is hard, and the family relationships are uncertain. "Asriel" and "Machir" are as in Numbers 26:29-31.
- **7:15** "Zelophehad had only daughters" refers to the story in Numbers 27:1-11 and 36.
- **7:21** "The men of Gath, born in the land, killed" two sons of Ephraim. The text places this in the early days of Ephraim, and it is rendered as written.
- **7:23** "Beriah" is a play on the Hebrew *beraah*, "in disaster", from the loss of his brothers.
- **7:24** "His daughter was Sheerah, who built Lower and Upper Beth-horon" is the only woman said to have built towns in the Bible.
- **7:27** "Nun his son, Joshua his son" gives the line of Joshua the successor of Moses. It is the only place where his descent is given.
- **7:28** The towns of Ephraim and Manasseh in verses 28 and 29 follow the border towns in Joshua 16 and 17.
- **7:30** "The sons of Asher" follow Genesis 46:17 and Numbers 26:44-47, with differences in spelling.
- **7:40** "Twenty-six thousand" is the number of fighting men of Asher.
- **8:1** "Benjamin was the father of Bela his firstborn, Ashbel the second, Aharah the third, Nohah the fourth and Rapha the fifth" differs from the lists in Genesis 46:21 and Numbers 26:38-41. "Aharah" may be Ahiram (Numbers 26:38).
- **8:3** "Addar" is Ard in Numbers 26:40.
- **8:6** The Hebrew of verses 6 and 7 is very hard. The text speaks of heads of houses at Geba who were taken "into exile" to Manahath, and of someone who "took them into exile". The sense is not clear, and may refer to a move within Benjamin.
- **8:8** "Shaharaim had sons in the country of Moab" is hard. The reason for the move to Moab is not given.
- **8:12** "Ono and Lod" are Benjaminite towns in the west, mentioned again in Nehemiah 11:35.
- **8:13** "Aijalon" and "the people of Gath" refer to a battle in the border country with the Philistines. The text does not say more.
- **8:28** "They lived in Jerusalem" closes the list. Part of the tribe of Benjamin lived in Jerusalem with Judah.
- **8:29** "Jeiel the father of Gibeon" is the family from which Saul came. Verses 29 to 38 are repeated in 9:35-44, with small differences in spelling.
- **8:30** "Baal, Nadab" has no "Ner", though 9:36 has "Baal, Ner, Nadab". The name may have dropped out here.
- **8:33** "Esh-baal" is the son of Saul called Ish-bosheth in 2 Samuel 2:8. The word *baal* ("lord") was replaced by *bosheth* ("shame") in Samuel.
- **8:34** "Merib-baal" is the son of Jonathan called Mephibosheth in 2 Samuel 4:4 and 9:6.
- **8:35** "Tarea" is Tahrea in 9:41. "Ahaz" is a different man from the king of Judah.
- **8:36** "Jehoaddah" is Jarah in 9:42.
- **8:40** "A hundred and fifty" is the number of sons and grandsons of Ulam's line.
- **9:1** "The Book of the Kings of Israel" is a source. "Their unfaithfulness" is Hebrew *ma'al*, a key word in Chronicles that is used here for the reason for the exile.
- **9:2** "Temple servants" is Hebrew *netinim*, "those given", who helped the Levites (see Ezra 2:43-54). The first to live again in their property are the people who returned from exile.
- **9:3** "Judah, Benjamin, Ephraim and Manasseh" lived in Jerusalem. The parallel list in Nehemiah 11:4 names only Judah and Benjamin.
- **9:4** "Uthai" is Athaiah in Nehemiah 11:4. The lists of chapter 9 and Nehemiah 11:3-19 are close but have many differences in names and numbers.
- **9:5** "Shilonites" is the Hebrew reading. It may mean the descendants of Shelah (Numbers 26:20). Nehemiah 11:5 has "Maaseiah the Shilonite".
- **9:6** "Six hundred and ninety" is the Hebrew number. Nehemiah 11:6 gives four hundred and sixty-eight for the sons of Perez.
- **9:9** "Nine hundred and fifty-six" is the Hebrew number. Nehemiah 11:8 has nine hundred and twenty-eight.
- **9:11** "Azariah son of Hilkiah" is called Seraiah in Nehemiah 11:11. He is "the officer in charge of the house of God".
- **9:13** "One thousand seven hundred and sixty" is the number of priests. Nehemiah 11:12-14 has eleven hundred and ninety-two in total.
- **9:15** "Mattaniah son of Mica, son of Zichri, son of Asaph" is a singer. "Mica" is Micah in Nehemiah 11:17.
- **9:16** "Obadiah son of Shemaiah, son of Galal, son of Jeduthun" is called Abda in Nehemiah 11:17.
- **9:17** "The gatekeepers" are the Levite guards of the temple gates.
- **9:18** "The king's gate on the east side" is the east gate of the temple court (see Ezekiel 46:1-2).
- **9:19** "The Korahites" are descendants of Korah (Numbers 16). They were keepers of the thresholds, as their ancestors were "keepers of the entrance" to the camp.
- **9:20** "Phinehas son of Eleazar" was the officer over the gatekeepers "in the past". He is known for his act at Peor (Numbers 25:7-13).
- **9:22** "David and Samuel the seer" are said to have put the gatekeepers in their position. This appointment is not mentioned in Samuel. "Two hundred and twelve" is the Hebrew number. Nehemiah 11:19 has a hundred and seventy-two.
- **9:26** "The four chief gatekeepers" were Levites in a position of trust, in charge of the rooms and treasuries.
- **9:29** "Holy vessels" is Hebrew *kele ha-qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **9:32** "The bread of the Presence" is prepared "every sabbath" (Leviticus 24:5-9).
- **9:33** "Free from other service" says that the singers were released from other duties to be on duty day and night.
- **9:35** "Jeiel the father of Gibeon" begins the repeat of 8:29-38.
- **9:36** "Baal, Ner, Nadab" has Ner, who is missing from 8:30.
- **9:39** "Esh-baal" and "Merib-baal" (verse 40) are as in 8:33-34.
- **9:44** "Azel had six sons" ends the list. The list of Saul's family is the link to the next chapter, which tells of Saul's death.
- **10:1** The account of Saul's death in verses 1 to 12 is parallel to 1 Samuel 31 and is shorter. It leaves out the Philistines' use of the temple of Ashtaroth and the body hung on the wall of Beth-shan. It leaves out the burning of the bodies by the men of Jabesh-gilead.
- **10:4** "Uncircumcised men" is the usual word for the Philistines. Saul falls on his own sword, as in 1 Samuel 31:4. The death is told without comment.
- **10:6** "His three sons and all his house" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Samuel 31:6 has "his three sons, his armour-bearer and all his men". The "house" here means the royal line, though Ish-bosheth survived (8:33).
- **10:9** "They took his head and his armour" differs from 1 Samuel 31:9, which says they cut off his head and stripped off his armour. The news is sent "to their idols and to the people".
- **10:10** "The temple of Dagon" is where Saul's head is fastened. 1 Samuel 31:10 says the armour was put in the temple of Ashtaroth and the body fastened to the wall of Beth-shan.
- **10:12** "Under the oak in Jabesh" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Samuel 31:13 has a tamarisk tree.
- **10:13** "For his unfaithfulness" is Hebrew *ma'al*, a key word of Chronicles (see 5:25; 9:1). The verses 13 and 14 give a reason for Saul's death that is not stated in 1 Samuel 31. "Consulting a medium" refers to 1 Samuel 28.
- **10:14** "He did not ask guidance from the LORD" is the Chronicler's verdict. In 1 Samuel 28:6 Saul does ask the LORD, and receives no answer. The two accounts are kept as they are.
- **11:1** The account of David made king at Hebron (verses 1 to 3) is parallel to 2 Samuel 5:1-3. "Your own flesh and bone" is a claim of kinship.
- **11:2** "Leader" is Hebrew *nagid*, the old title for the king (1 Samuel 9:16).
- **11:3** "A covenant" is Hebrew *berit*. See entry H-BERIT. The anointing is "as the word of the LORD through Samuel said".
- **11:4** The capture of Jerusalem (verses 4 to 9) is parallel to 2 Samuel 5:6-10. "Jebus" is the name of the town before David took it. This account leaves out the taunt about "the blind and the lame" in 2 Samuel 5:6, 8.
- **11:6** "Joab son of Zeruiah went up first, and so he became chief" is not in 2 Samuel. It says that Joab became commander by this act.
- **11:10** The list of David's warriors (verses 10 to 47) is parallel to 2 Samuel 23:8-39. The Hebrew is different in many names and numbers.
- **11:11** "Jashobeam, a Hachmonite" is the name here. 2 Samuel 23:8 has "Josheb-basshebeth the Tahchemonite". "Three hundred" is the number here, and 2 Samuel 23:8 has eight hundred.
- **11:13** "Pas-dammim" is the place of the battle with the Philistines. 2 Samuel 23:9 has a different account of the place, "Lehi" in 23:11.
- **11:18** "He poured it out to the LORD" means he gave it as an offering. See 2 Samuel 23:16.
- **11:19** "The blood of these men, who risked their lives" is literally "their lives" (Hebrew *nafshotam*). See entry H-NEPHESH. The "blood" and the "lives" are the same word in the Hebrew.
- **11:22** "Two lion-like warriors of Moab" translates Hebrew *ari'el*, a word of uncertain meaning (see 2 Samuel 23:20).
- **11:23** "Five cubits tall" is not in 2 Samuel 23:21. It is about 2.2 metres.
- **11:26** "The brave warriors of the armies" begins the list of the thirty, which is close to 2 Samuel 23:24-39 but has some spelling differences (for example Shammoth for Shammah, Sibbecai for Mebunnai, Hurai for Hiddai).
- **11:41** "Uriah the Hittite" is the last of the names in 2 Samuel 23:39. Here sixteen more names follow (verses 41 to 47). The story of Uriah and Bathsheba is not told in Chronicles.
- **11:42** "Adina son of Shiza the Reubenite" and the other names after Uriah are not in Samuel.
- **12:1** "Ziklag" is the town where David stayed under the Philistine king Achish (1 Samuel 27:6). Verses 1 to 22 are not in Samuel.
- **12:2** "With either the right hand or the left" is the Hebrew description of Benjaminite skill (see Judges 20:16). They were Saul's own tribe.
- **12:8** "Faces like the faces of lions ... as swift as gazelles" is the language of David's lament (2 Samuel 1:23; 2:18).
- **12:14** "The least was a match for a hundred, and the greatest for a thousand" is a way of showing the strength of the Gadites.
- **12:15** "When it was overflowing all its banks" refers to the Jordan in the spring (see Joshua 3:15).
- **12:17** "My heart will be joined with yours" is David's test. The Benjaminites and men of Judah come to him in the stronghold.
- **12:18** "The spirit clothed Amasai" is the Hebrew idiom for being taken over by the spirit (see Judges 6:34). "Spirit" is *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH. "Peace" is *shalom*. See entry H-SHALOM. Amasai may be the same as Amasa, David's nephew.
- **12:19** "Some of the men of Manasseh went over to David" when the Philistines sent him away from the battle against Saul (1 Samuel 29:1-11).
- **12:22** "Like the camp of God" is a figure of a very large army.
- **12:23** "To turn the kingdom of Saul over to him" gives the purpose of the army that came to Hebron (see 2 Samuel 5:1-3).
- **12:24** The numbers of the divisions in verses 24 to 37 are large and total about three hundred and forty thousand. They show the support of all the tribes.
- **12:27** "Jehoiada, the leader of the house of Aaron" is a priestly leader. He is not Benaiah's father in 11:22, though the name is the same.
- **12:28** "Zadok, a brave young man" is the priest who later serves Solomon (see 1 Kings 1:39).
- **12:29** "Most of them had until then kept their loyalty to the house of Saul" means that Benjamin was slow to leave Saul's house.
- **12:32** "Men who understood the times and knew what Israel should do" is the description of the chiefs of Issachar.
- **12:33** "With undivided loyalty" is literally "not with a double heart".
- **12:35** "The Danites" are named here, though they are not in the lists of chapter 7.
- **12:38** "With a whole heart" and "of one mind" give the spirit of the people who came to make David king.
- **12:40** "There was joy in Israel" ends the chapter. The feast at Hebron is a picture of the unity of the kingdom.
- **13:1** The bringing of the ark (verses 1 to 14) is parallel to 2 Samuel 6:1-11. Chronicles adds that David consulted the whole assembly, and that the people agreed. "We did not look for it in the days of Saul" (verse 3) is not in Samuel.
- **13:5** "From the Shihor of Egypt to Lebo-hamath" is the whole land, from the south to the north (see Joshua 13:3).
- **13:6** "Baalah" is another name for Kiriath-jearim. 2 Samuel 6:2 has "Baale-judah". "Which is called by the name of the LORD" is the Chronicler's phrase for the ark.
- **13:7** "A new cart" carried the ark from Abinadab's house. "Uzzah and Ahio" drove the cart. In Hebrew *ahio* can also mean "his brother".
- **13:9** "Chidon" is the name of the threshing floor here. 2 Samuel 6:6 has "Nacon".
- **13:10** "He struck him down because he had put out his hand to the ark" is rendered as written, with no comment. 2 Samuel 6:7 adds a word of uncertain meaning (*shal*, "for his error").
- **13:11** "Perez-uzzah" means "breach of Uzzah". The name is given to the place "to this day".
- **13:13** "Obed-edom the Gittite" is probably a Levite of Gath-rimmon (Joshua 21:24). In 15:18 and 16:38 he is a Levite gatekeeper.
- **13:14** "Three months" is the time the ark stayed with Obed-edom. The LORD blessed his household.
- **14:1** Verses 1 to 17 are parallel to 2 Samuel 5:11-25. Hiram's help for David's house is told in 2 Samuel 5:11.
- **14:3** "David took more wives in Jerusalem" introduces the list of children in verses 4 to 7, which is close to 3:5-8 and 2 Samuel 5:14-16. "Beeliada" is Eliada there.
- **14:11** "Baal-perazim" means "lord of breakings through". The name comes from David's words.
- **14:12** "They were burned in the fire" says that David burned the gods of the Philistines, in keeping with Deuteronomy 7:25. 2 Samuel 5:21 says that David and his men carried them away.
- **14:14** "The balsam trees" is Hebrew *bekha'im*, an uncertain word (see 2 Samuel 5:23). It may mean balsam, mulberry or poplar trees.
- **14:16** "From Gibeon to Gezer" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Samuel 5:25 has "from Geba to Gezer".
- **15:1** "He pitched a tent for it" shows that David prepared a place for the ark in Jerusalem before the second attempt.
- **15:2** "No one may carry the ark of God except the Levites" follows Numbers 4:15 and Deuteronomy 10:8. This is the Chronicler's explanation of what went wrong the first time.
- **15:5** The six chiefs of the Levite families in verses 5 to 10 are of Kohath, Merari, Gershom, Elizaphan, Hebron and Uzziel.
- **15:11** "The priests Zadok and Abiathar" serve together at David's court (2 Samuel 15:35).
- **15:12** "Make yourselves holy" is Hebrew *hitqaddeshu*. See entry H-QADOSH. The Levites are to make themselves ritually ready.
- **15:13** "The LORD our God broke out against us" refers to Uzzah's death (13:11). "According to the rule" is Hebrew *mishpat*, the proper way. The Chronicler says that David did not ask the LORD how it should be done.
- **15:15** "On their shoulders with the poles" follows the rule for carrying the ark (Exodus 25:12-15; Numbers 4:5-6, 15; 7:9).
- **15:17** "Ethan son of Kushaiah" is called Ethan son of Kishi in 6:44. The two forms of the name are in the Hebrew.
- **15:20** "According to Alamoth" and "according to the Sheminith" (verse 21) are musical terms of uncertain meaning. *Alamoth* may mean "young women" and so a high voice or pitch, and *Sheminith* "the eighth" and so a low pitch or an eight-stringed lyre. They are also in the titles of Psalms 6, 12 and 46.
- **15:22** "Chenaniah ... was to direct the music, for he understood it" translates a Hebrew word (*massa*) that can mean "carrying" or "lifting up the voice". The sense is not certain.
- **15:25** "The ark of the covenant of the LORD" uses *berit*. See entry H-BERIT.
- **15:26** "Because God helped the Levites" refers to the safe carrying, in contrast to the first attempt. "Seven bulls and seven rams" is more than the single sacrifice in 2 Samuel 6:13.
- **15:27** "A robe of fine linen" is worn by David and the Levites. 2 Samuel 6:14 says that David wore a linen ephod.
- **15:29** "She despised him in her heart" is the only mention of Michal's attitude. Chronicles leaves out her conversation with David and her childlessness (2 Samuel 6:20-23).
- **16:1** Verses 1 to 3 are parallel to 2 Samuel 6:17-19.
- **16:4** "To call on him" translates Hebrew *lehazkir*, from the word to remember, and may mean to bring the LORD's name to remembrance in song or prayer.
- **16:7** "On that day David first appointed" is hard to read. It may mean that this was the first time the psalm was used.
- **16:8** Verses 8 to 36 are a psalm made of three psalms: 105:1-15 (verses 8 to 22), 96:1-13 (verses 23 to 33) and 106:1, 47-48 (verses 34 to 36). The wording follows the Hebrew of Chronicles and differs in small ways from the Psalms.
- **16:15** "Remember his covenant" is the Hebrew reading. Psalm 105:8 has "He remembers his covenant".
- **16:22** "My anointed ones" is Hebrew *meshichai*. See entry H-MASHIACH. The patriarchs are called anointed, and the word is plural.
- **16:26** "Nothing" translates Hebrew *elilim*, a scornful word for idols that is also close to the word for "gods".
- **16:29** "In holy splendour" is Hebrew *hadrat qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **16:30** "The world is set firm and will not be shaken" gives the reason for the earth's trembling before the LORD.
- **16:31** "The LORD reigns" is the cry of the enthronement psalms (Psalms 93, 96 to 99).
- **16:34** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. "For he is good, for his faithful love lasts for ever" is the refrain of Psalms 106, 107 and 136, and of 2 Chronicles 5:13 and 7:3.
- **16:36** "Then all the people said, 'Amen'" is the response to the last verse of the psalm. It is also the end of Psalm 106.
- **16:37** "Asaph and his relatives" serve before the ark in Jerusalem. The next verses name those who serve at Gibeon.
- **16:38** "Obed-edom son of Jeduthun" is a gatekeeper. The Hebrew of the verse is difficult, and the counts and names may be mixed.
- **16:39** "The tabernacle of the LORD at the high place at Gibeon" shows the tent of meeting still at Gibeon, with Zadok and the priests. The ark is in Jerusalem (see 1 Kings 3:4).
- **16:40** "The law of the LORD" is the written law. The morning and evening burnt offering follows Exodus 29:38-42.
- **16:41** "Jeduthun" may be the same as Ethan (see 6:44 and 15:17).
- **16:43** "To bless his household" ends the chapter. Chronicles leaves out Michal's reproach and David's answer (2 Samuel 6:20-23).
- **17:1** The promise to David (verses 1 to 15) and his prayer (verses 16 to 27) are parallel to 2 Samuel 7:1-29. "The ark of the covenant of the LORD" is the Hebrew reading here, using *berit*. See entry H-BERIT. 2 Samuel 7:2 has "the ark of God".
- **17:4** "You are not the one to build me a house" is a statement. In 2 Samuel 7:5 the LORD asks, "Are you the one to build me a house?"
- **17:5** "From tent to tent and from one dwelling to another" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Samuel 7:6 has "in a tent and a tabernacle".
- **17:6** "The judges of Israel" is the Hebrew reading here. 2 Samuel 7:7 has "the tribes of Israel". The two words (*shofete*, *shivte*) look alike in Hebrew.
- **17:7** "Leader" is Hebrew *nagid*, the old title for the king (1 Samuel 9:16).
- **17:10** "I will subdue all your enemies" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Samuel 7:11 has "I will give you rest from all your enemies". "The LORD will build you a house" means a lasting royal line.
- **17:11** "One of your own sons" is the Hebrew here. 2 Samuel 7:12 has "who will come from your own body". The promise points to Solomon (22:9-10).
- **17:13** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. "As I took it from the one who was before you" refers to Saul. This account leaves out the warning in 2 Samuel 7:14, "When he does wrong, I will punish him".
- **17:14** "I will set him over my house and my kingdom for ever" says that the kingdom is the LORD's, with David's son set over it. 2 Samuel 7:16 says "your house and your kingdom". See also 28:5 and 29:23.
- **17:17** "A man of high rank" translates a very hard Hebrew phrase (*kethor ha-adam ha-ma'alah*). It may mean "in the manner of a man of high rank" or "as one who has been raised up". The sense is uncertain.
- **17:21** "By great and awesome acts" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Samuel 7:23 has a different and difficult wording.
- **17:24** "The LORD of hosts, the God of Israel, is God over Israel" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Samuel 7:26 has "The LORD of hosts is God over Israel".
- **18:1** The wars of verses 1 to 13 are parallel to 2 Samuel 8:1-14. "Took Gath and its villages" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Samuel 8:1 has "took Metheg-ammah", a difficult phrase.
- **18:2** "Moabites became servants" leaves out the measuring and killing of the Moabite prisoners in 2 Samuel 8:2.
- **18:3** "Zobah-hamath" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Samuel 8:3 has "Zobah". "His monument" is Hebrew *yad*, "hand". It may mean a memorial or the extent of his rule.
- **18:4** "A thousand chariots, seven thousand horsemen and twenty thousand foot soldiers" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Samuel 8:4 has "a thousand seven hundred horsemen and twenty thousand foot soldiers". David "hamstrung" the chariot horses by cutting the leg tendons, which is rendered as written.
- **18:8** "Tibhath and Cun" are the places called Betah and Berothai in 2 Samuel 8:8. The bronze was used for the temple furnishings (1 Kings 7:23-47).
- **18:9** "Tou" is Toi in 2 Samuel 8:9. His son "Hadoram" is called Joram there.
- **18:11** "David dedicated these to the LORD" says that the plunder went to the future temple (see 26:26-28).
- **18:12** "Abishai son of Zeruiah struck down eighteen thousand Edomites" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Samuel 8:13 says that David made a name by striking down eighteen thousand (the Hebrew has "Aram"), and the title of Psalm 60 says that Joab struck down twelve thousand Edomites. The accounts differ.
- **18:14** "What was just and right" is Hebrew *mishpat u-tsedaqah*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH.
- **18:16** "Zadok son of Ahitub and Abimelech son of Abiathar" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Samuel 8:17 has "Ahimelech son of Abiathar". "Shavsha" is called Seraiah there.
- **18:17** "David's sons were the chief officials at the king's side" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Samuel 8:18 calls them priests.
- **19:1** The war with Ammon (verses 1 to 19) is parallel to 2 Samuel 10:1-19.
- **19:4** "Cut off their clothes in the middle, at the hips" is a way of shaming the men, as with the shaving of half the beard (2 Samuel 10:4).
- **19:6** "A thousand talents of silver" is not in 2 Samuel 10:6. "Aram-naharaim" is northern Mesopotamia.
- **19:7** "Thirty-two thousand chariots" is the Hebrew number here. 2 Samuel 10:6 gives foot soldiers: twenty thousand of Aram and Beth-rehob, a thousand of Maacah and twelve thousand of Tob. "Medeba" is in Moab, not named in Samuel.
- **19:13** "For the towns of our God" is Joab's call to his brother.
- **19:16** "Beyond the river" means beyond the Euphrates.
- **19:18** "Seven thousand of their chariot men and forty thousand foot soldiers" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Samuel 10:18 has "seven hundred chariot men and forty thousand horsemen".
- **20:1** The siege of Rabbah (verses 1 to 3) is parallel to 2 Samuel 11:1 and 12:26-31. Chronicles leaves out David's staying in Jerusalem and his sin with Bathsheba and Uriah's death, which come between verse 1 and verse 2. "But David stayed in Jerusalem" is the only trace.
- **20:2** "The crown from the head of their king" is the Hebrew reading. The same word can also be read as the name of the god Milcom (2 Samuel 12:30).
- **20:3** "He brought out the people who were in it and put them to work" follows the sense of 2 Samuel 12:31 as forced labour. The Hebrew uses a verb that can also mean "sawed", and the sense is uncertain. It is rendered as written.
- **20:4** "Sibbecai the Hushathite struck down Sippai" is parallel to 2 Samuel 21:18, where the name is Saph.
- **20:5** "Elhanan son of Jair struck down Lahmi the brother of Goliath the Gittite" differs from 2 Samuel 21:19, which says that Elhanan struck down Goliath the Gittite. Chronicles keeps the story of David and Goliath (1 Samuel 17).
- **20:7** "Jonathan son of Shimea" is the same as Jonathan son of Shimei in 2 Samuel 21:21. Shimea is David's brother (2:13).
- **21:1** The account of the census (verses 1 to 27) is parallel to 2 Samuel 24:1-25. "An adversary" is Hebrew *satan*, here without the article. 2 Samuel 24:1 says that "the anger of the LORD blazed against Israel, and he stirred up David". The word can be a common noun ("adversary") or a name, and this may be the first place where it is used as a name. See entry H-SATAN. The two verses are kept as they are in each book.
- **21:2** "From Beersheba to Dan" has the places in the opposite order to 2 Samuel 24:2.
- **21:3** "Guilt" is Hebrew *ashmah*. Joab's question is why the count should bring guilt on Israel.
- **21:5** "One million one hundred thousand" and "four hundred and seventy thousand" are the Hebrew numbers. 2 Samuel 24:9 has eight hundred thousand and five hundred thousand.
- **21:6** "He did not include Levi and Benjamin" is not in 2 Samuel. The Levites were not to be counted with the others (Numbers 1:49).
- **21:7** "This command was evil in the eyes of God, and he struck Israel" comes before David's confession here. In 2 Samuel 24:10 David's heart strikes him first.
- **21:10** "Gad" is David's seer, as in 2 Samuel 24:11.
- **21:12** "Three years of famine" is the Hebrew reading here. 2 Samuel 24:13 has "seven years" in the Hebrew. The WABV text of 2 Samuel follows the reading found here. "Swept away before your enemies" is the sense of a Hebrew word (*nispeh*) that can also mean "to be consumed".
- **21:13** "His mercy is very great" is the reason David gives for choosing the plague. See 2 Samuel 24:14.
- **21:15** "Changed his mind" is Hebrew *nicham*. See entry H-NACHAM. "Ornan the Jebusite" is called Araunah in 2 Samuel 24:16. The name appears in several forms in the Hebrew.
- **21:16** "David looked up and saw the angel of the LORD standing between earth and heaven" is not in 2 Samuel. David and the elders then fall on their faces in sackcloth.
- **21:17** "These sheep, what have they done?" is also in 2 Samuel 24:17. David asks that the punishment fall on himself and his father's house.
- **21:20** "Ornan turned and saw the angel, and his four sons ... hid themselves" is not in 2 Samuel. The threshing was of wheat.
- **21:22** "For the full price" is the Hebrew phrase here. In 2 Samuel 24:21 David says he will buy the site to build an altar.
- **21:25** "Six hundred shekels of gold by weight" is the Hebrew number here. 2 Samuel 24:24 has fifty shekels of silver for the threshing floor and oxen. The two may refer to different parts of the site.
- **21:26** "He answered him with fire from heaven" is not in 2 Samuel. Fire from heaven is also said in Leviticus 9:24, 1 Kings 18:38 and 2 Chronicles 7:1.
- **21:29** "The tabernacle of the LORD ... and the altar of burnt offering were at that time at the high place in Gibeon" explains why David offered at Jerusalem (see 16:39).
- **21:30** "He was afraid of the sword of the angel" explains why David did not go to Gibeon.
- **22:1** "Here will be the house of the LORD God" says that the site of the threshing floor is to be the place for the temple. 2 Chronicles 3:1 names it Mount Moriah.
- **22:2** "The foreigners living in the land" is Hebrew *gerim*, resident aliens. See entry H-GER. They are made to work as stonecutters. See also 1 Kings 5:15 and 9:20-21.
- **22:5** "Young and inexperienced" is Hebrew *na'ar va-rakh*, a young man and tender. The same words are used of Solomon in 29:1.
- **22:8** "You have shed a great deal of blood" is the reason given for David not building the temple. 2 Samuel 7 gives no reason. See also 28:3.
- **22:9** "A man of rest" is Hebrew *ish menuchah*. The name "Solomon" (*shelomoh*) sounds like "peace" (*shalom*), and the verse links the name with peace and quiet in his days.
- **22:10** "He will be a son to me, and I will be a father to him" repeats 17:13 and 2 Samuel 7:14.
- **22:13** "Be strong and brave. Do not be afraid or discouraged" repeats the words to Joshua (Joshua 1:6-9) and to Solomon (28:20).
- **22:14** "A hundred thousand talents of gold, a million talents of silver" is a very large amount. It is about 3,400 tonnes of gold and 34,000 tonnes of silver. "With great effort" is Hebrew *be-onyi*, which can also mean "in my affliction".
- **22:18** "Rest on every side" repeats the word in verse 9. The land is quiet.
- **22:19** "Sanctuary" is Hebrew *miqdash*, from the root for "holy". See entry H-QADOSH.
- **23:1** "He made his son Solomon king" is the Chronicler's summary. The full account of the handing over of the kingdom is in chapters 28 and 29. The arrangement in chapters 23 to 27 belongs to David's last days.
- **23:3** "Thirty years old and over" is the age of the Levites for service in Numbers 4:3. In verses 24 and 27 the age is "twenty years old and over" (see Numbers 8:24 and Ezra 3:8). The two ages are kept as they are.
- **23:4** "Twenty-four thousand ... six thousand ... four thousand ... four thousand" add up to thirty-eight thousand (verse 3).
- **23:5** "The instruments that I have made for praise" says that David made musical instruments (see Amos 6:5 and Nehemiah 12:36).
- **23:7** "Ladan and Shimei" are the sons of Gershon. "Ladan" is also called Libni (Exodus 6:17; 6:20).
- **23:9** "The sons of Shimei: Shelomoth, Haziel and Haran" belong to a different Shimei from the one in verse 10, or to the line of Ladan. The Hebrew is unclear.
- **23:11** The list in verses 10 and 11 is hard to read. Two small families, Jeush and Beriah, are counted as one father's house.
- **23:13** "Aaron was set apart" is Hebrew *hibbadel*, and "to make holy the most holy things" is *lehaqdisho qodesh qodashim*. See entry H-QADOSH. "To burn incense" and "to bless in his name" are the priestly tasks. The Hebrew of the clause about "making holy" is hard.
- **23:14** "The sons of Moses the man of God were counted as part of the tribe of Levi" says that Moses' descendants were Levites and not priests.
- **23:16** "Shebuel" is called Shubael in 24:20. "Gershom" and "Eliezer" are the sons of Moses (Exodus 18:3-4).
- **23:22** "Eleazar died without sons, only daughters, and their relatives the sons of Kish married them" follows the law in Numbers 36:6-8, so that the property stays in the tribe.
- **23:25** "The LORD has given rest to his people, and he lives in Jerusalem for ever" is the reason the Levites no longer carry the tabernacle (verse 26).
- **23:27** "By the last words of David" says that the lower age of twenty belongs to the last orders of David.
- **23:29** "Measures of quantity and size" refers to the weights and measures used in the temple.
- **24:2** "Nadab and Abihu died before their father" refers to Leviticus 10:1-2 and Numbers 3:4.
- **24:3** "Zadok" and "Ahimelech" are the priests of David's time. "Ahimelech" is the son of Abiathar (verse 6), of the line of Ithamar. See also 18:16.
- **24:4** "Sixteen ... eight" gives twenty-four fathers' houses, or groups of priests.
- **24:5** "Officials of the sanctuary and officials of God" are the high officials of the temple service. They came from both families.
- **24:7** "The first lot fell to Jehoiarib" begins the list of the twenty-four priestly groups, which are also named in Nehemiah 12:1-7, with some different spellings. Jehoiarib is the first group, and in Luke 1:5 Abijah (the eighth group) is the one to which Zechariah the father of John belonged.
- **24:20** "The rest of the descendants of Levi" begins a second list of Levite heads, which repeats the families in 23:12-23 with changes of name and order. "Shubael" is Shebuel (23:16).
- **24:26** "The sons of Jaaziah: Beno" is hard in the Hebrew. The words "Jaaziah" and "Beno" ("his son") may be a name and an addition.
- **24:31** "They also cast lots" says that the Levites cast lots for duty, as the priests did.
- **25:1** "To prophesy with lyres, harps and cymbals" uses the verb *hinnabe*, "to prophesy". See entry H-NAVI. The music of the temple is described as prophetic service. "Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun" are the leaders of the three guilds of singers (see 6:31-47).
- **25:3** "Gedaliah, Zeri, Jeshaiah, Shimei, Hashabiah and Mattithiah, six" names six sons of Jeduthun. The Hebrew text lists only five and does not name Shimei, though the count is six and Shimei appears in verse 17. The WABV text restores "Shimei" from verse 17 and the count, and the departure is flagged for the scholar.
- **25:5** "To raise up his horn" is a figure for strength and honour. The Hebrew phrase "according to the promises of God" is hard. "Heman the king's seer" is a title for a prophet attached to the court.
- **25:7** "Two hundred and eighty-eight" is twenty-four groups of twelve (verses 9 to 31).
- **25:9** "The first lot fell to Joseph, of Asaph's sons" is the Hebrew "for Asaph, for Joseph". The names of the lots follow the sons of Asaph (verses 9 to 12), Jeduthun, and Heman.
- **25:4** Some students have noticed that the last names of Heman's sons, read in order (Hananiah, Hanani, Eliathah, Giddalti, Romamti-ezer, Joshbekashah, Mallothi, Hothir, Mahazioth), form words that can be read as a prayer in Hebrew. This is not certain.
- **26:1** "Meshelemiah son of Kore, of the descendants of Asaph" is a Korahite gatekeeper. "Asaph" may be a form of Ebiasaph (9:19). He is also called Shelemiah (verse 14).
- **26:4** "Obed-edom had sons ... for God had blessed him" refers to his blessing in 13:14.
- **26:8** "Sixty-two" is the number of Obed-edom's descendants. In 16:38 the number is sixty-eight.
- **26:10** "Hosah, of the descendants of Merari" had Shimri as chief, though he was not the firstborn.
- **26:12** The twenty-four gates and duties of the gatekeepers follow the four sides of the temple area. Each gate had its watch.
- **26:16** "The Shalleketh Gate on the upper road" is on the west side. The Hebrew of verses 16 to 18 is hard, and the number of guards at each place is uncertain.
- **26:18** "The colonnade" is Hebrew *parbar*, a word of uncertain meaning that may be a structure on the west side of the temple (see 2 Kings 23:11).
- **26:20** "Ahijah" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek has "their relatives". The Hebrew of verses 20 to 22 is hard.
- **26:20** "The treasuries of the holy gifts" uses *qodashim*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **26:21** "Jehieli" is Jehiel in 23:8. "Zetham and Joel" (verse 22) are the sons of Ladan in 23:8.
- **26:24** "Shebuel son of Gershom, son of Moses" is a descendant of Moses in charge of the treasuries.
- **26:26** "The holy gifts that King David ... had dedicated" were taken from plunder (verse 27, and 18:11).
- **26:28** "Samuel the seer, Saul, Abner and Joab" are named as earlier givers. Chronicles records only here that Saul and Abner gave gifts to the sanctuary.
- **26:29** "Officials and judges" worked outside the temple, in the towns of Israel (see 23:4).
- **26:30** "West of the Jordan" and the east (verse 32) are the two districts of Levite oversight.
- **26:31** "The fortieth year of David's rule" is the last year of David's reign (29:27). "Jazer in Gilead" is a Levite town (6:81).
- **27:1** "Twenty-four thousand" in each of twelve divisions makes two hundred and eighty-eight thousand men. Each division served one month.
- **27:2** "Jashobeam" is the first of David's warriors (11:11).
- **27:4** "Dodai the Ahohite" is probably Dodo (11:12) or a form of the name.
- **27:6** "Benaiah ... over the thirty" says he led the thirty (11:25 gives him the bodyguard). "His son Ammizabad" took charge of his division.
- **27:7** "Asahel the brother of Joab" was killed by Abner early in David's reign (2 Samuel 2:23). The list may preserve an earlier arrangement, or may mean the division named for him with his son after him.
- **27:8** "Shamhuth" is Shammah or Shammoth (11:27).
- **27:15** "Heldai" is Heled or Heleb (11:30).
- **27:16** The officers of the tribes in verses 16 to 22 do not include Gad and Asher. Levi and Aaron are counted separately, and Manasseh has two halves, so that the number is still twelve.
- **27:23** "Because the LORD had promised to make Israel as many as the stars of heaven" refers to Genesis 15:5. It is the reason given for not counting those under twenty.
- **27:24** "Joab son of Zeruiah began to count but did not finish" refers to the census of chapter 21. "The records of King David" is Hebrew *divre ha-yamim*, the name that the book itself bears.
- **27:30** "Jaziz the Hagrite" is a Hagrite, from the people named in 5:10.
- **27:32** "David's uncle" is Hebrew *dod*, which can mean uncle or other close relative. "Jehiel son of Hachmoni" looked after the royal sons.
- **27:33** "Ahithophel" and "Hushai the Archite" are the counsellors of 2 Samuel 15 to 17. "The king's friend" is a court title.
- **27:34** "Jehoiada son of Benaiah, and Abiathar" followed Ahithophel. "Joab was the commander of the king's army" ends the list.
- **28:1** The assembly of the leaders in verse 1 is like the list in 27:1-34. It begins David's last address, which has no parallel in Samuel or Kings.
- **28:2** "A house of rest for the ark of the covenant" and "the footstool of our God" refer to the ark as God's footstool (Psalm 99:5; 132:7-8). "Covenant" is *berit*. See entry H-BERIT.
- **28:3** "A man of war and have shed blood" repeats 22:8 and is the reason given for David not building the temple.
- **28:4** "He chose Judah as leader" refers to Genesis 49:10. The choice of David and of his house is told as a series of choices.
- **28:5** "The throne of the kingdom of the LORD over Israel" says that the kingdom is the LORD's. See 17:14 and 29:23.
- **28:7** "If he is firm in keeping my commands" makes the promise to Solomon conditional. In 17:13-14 the promise is stated without a condition.
- **28:9** "Know the God of your father and serve him with a whole heart" begins David's charge to Solomon. "The LORD searches every heart" echoes Jeremiah 17:10. "If you abandon him, he will reject you for ever" is the warning that goes with "he will be found by you".
- **28:10** "A sanctuary" is Hebrew *miqdash*, from the root for "holy". See entry H-QADOSH.
- **28:11** "The plans" is Hebrew *tavnit*, the word used of the pattern of the tabernacle (Exodus 25:9, 40). The terms for the parts of the building (porch, buildings, storerooms, upper rooms, inner rooms and the room for the mercy seat) are listed in the order of 1 Kings 6.
- **28:12** "All that he had in mind" is literally "all that the spirit had with him" (Hebrew *ruach*). See entry H-RUACH. The phrase may mean what David had in his thought, or what the spirit had shown him.
- **28:18** "The chariot of gold, the cherubim" speaks of the cherubim over the ark as a chariot. It is the only place in Chronicles where they are called so. See Ezekiel 1 and 10.
- **28:19** "In writing from the hand of the LORD" is hard in the Hebrew. It may mean that David received the plan in written form, or that the LORD's hand was on him as he wrote. The text does not say how.
- **28:20** "Be strong and brave, and do it" repeats Joshua 1:6-9 and 22:13. "He will not fail you or abandon you" echoes Deuteronomy 31:6.
- **29:1** "Young and inexperienced" repeats 22:5. "The palace" is Hebrew *birah*, a loan-word from Persian that is used for the temple. It is also in Nehemiah 2:8.
- **29:2** "Stones for mosaic" and "slabs of marble" are uncertain terms. The Hebrew words are *puk* (a kind of cosmetic or inlay stone), and *shayish* (marble or alabaster).
- **29:4** "Three thousand talents of gold ... and seven thousand talents of refined silver" are about a hundred tonnes of gold and two hundred and forty tonnes of silver.
- **29:5** "Give himself" is literally "fill his hand", the Hebrew idiom for dedicating oneself to the LORD, as in the ordination of priests (Exodus 28:41).
- **29:7** "Darics" is Hebrew *adarkonim*, a Persian gold coin first minted about 515 BCE. It shows that the book was written after that time. The amounts given are very large.
- **29:8** "Jehiel the Gershonite" is in charge of the treasury (23:8; 26:21).
- **29:10** "David blessed the LORD" begins David's prayer (verses 10 to 19), which is set as verse lines.
- **29:11** "Greatness, power, glory, victory and majesty" is a list of the LORD's attributes. "Lifted up as head over all" is the Hebrew reading. The verse is close to the later doxology that some manuscripts add to Matthew 6:13.
- **29:14** "We have given you only what is yours" is David's response to the people's willing giving.
- **29:15** "Foreigners" is Hebrew *gerim*, resident aliens. See entry H-GER. "We are foreigners before you, and strangers" repeats the words of Leviticus 25:23 and Psalm 39:12, and they are echoed in Hebrews 11:13 and 1 Peter 2:11. "There is no hope of staying" is Hebrew *miqveh*, "expectation" or "hope".
- **29:16** "Your holy name" is Hebrew *shem qodshekha*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **29:17** "You test the heart" echoes Psalm 7:9 and Jeremiah 11:20.
- **29:19** "Give my son Solomon a whole heart" is David's prayer for Solomon. It repeats 28:9.
- **29:22** "They made Solomon son of David king a second time" refers to the first time in 23:1. The first was by David's order, and this is the public anointing. "Leader" is Hebrew *nagid*. "Zadok as priest" is named alone. Abiathar, who supported Adonijah (1 Kings 1:7), is not mentioned.
- **29:23** "The throne of the LORD" repeats 28:5. Solomon sits on the LORD's throne.
- **29:24** "All the sons of King David ... pledged their loyalty" leaves out Adonijah's attempt on the throne (1 Kings 1). Chronicles does not tell it.
- **29:27** "Forty years" repeats 1 Kings 2:11 and 2 Samuel 5:4-5.
- **29:29** "The records of Samuel the seer, ... Nathan the prophet, ... Gad the seer" are sources that are now lost. The Hebrew word for "records" is *divre*, as in the name of the book.
