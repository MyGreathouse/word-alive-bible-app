## 2 Samuel 1-4: The Lament and the House of Saul

### Chapter 1

**1** After the death of Saul, when David had come back from defeating the Amalekites, David stayed in Ziklag two days.

**2** On the third day a man came from Saul's camp, with his clothes torn and earth on his head. When he came to David, he fell to the ground and bowed down.

**3** David said to him, "Where have you come from?" He said to him, "I have escaped from the camp of Israel."

**4** David said to him, "What happened? Tell me." He said, "The people fled from the battle, and many of them fell and died. Saul and his son Jonathan are dead too."

**5** David said to the young man who told him, "How do you know that Saul and his son Jonathan are dead?"

**6** The young man who told him said, "I happened to be on Mount Gilboa, and there was Saul leaning on his spear, with the chariots and the horsemen closing in on him.

**7** He looked behind him and saw me, and called to me. I said, 'Here I am.'

**8** He said to me, 'Who are you?' I answered him, 'I am an Amalekite.'

**9** He said to me, 'Stand over me and kill me, for I am in agony, and my life still holds on.'

**10** So I stood over him and killed him, for I knew that he could not live after he had fallen. I took the crown from his head and the band from his arm, and I have brought them here to my lord."

**11** Then David took hold of his clothes and tore them, and so did all the men with him.

**12** They mourned and wept and fasted until evening for Saul and his son Jonathan, and for the people of the LORD and the house of Israel, because they had fallen by the sword.

**13** David said to the young man who had told him, "Where are you from?" He answered, "I am the son of a foreigner living among you, an Amalekite."

**14** David said to him, "How were you not afraid to raise your hand to destroy the LORD's anointed \[H-MASHIACH\]?"

**15** Then David called one of the young men and said, "Go and strike him down." So he struck him down, and he died.

**16** David said to him, "Your blood is on your own head, for your own mouth testified against you when you said, 'I have killed the LORD's anointed.'"

**17** David sang this lament over Saul and over his son Jonathan,

**18** and he ordered that the people of Judah be taught the Song of the Bow. It is written in the Book of Jashar.

**19** Your glory, Israel, lies slain on your heights. How the mighty have fallen.

**20** Do not tell it in Gath. Do not announce it in the streets of Ashkelon, or the daughters of the Philistines will rejoice, the daughters of the uncircumcised will celebrate.

**21** Mountains of Gilboa, let no dew or rain fall on you, no fields that give offerings. For there the shield of the mighty was thrown down in shame, the shield of Saul, no longer rubbed with oil.

**22** From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty, the bow of Jonathan never turned back, the sword of Saul never came home empty.

**23** Saul and Jonathan, loved and lovely, in life and in death they were not parted. They were swifter than eagles, stronger than lions.

**24** Daughters of Israel, weep for Saul, who dressed you in scarlet and fine things, who put gold ornaments on your clothes.

**25** How the mighty have fallen in the middle of the battle. Jonathan lies slain on your heights.

**26** I grieve for you, my brother Jonathan. You were very dear to me. Your love for me was wonderful, more than the love of women.

**27** How the mighty have fallen, and the weapons of war are lost.

### Chapter 2

**1** After this David asked the LORD, "Shall I go up to one of the towns of Judah?" The LORD said to him, "Go up." David said, "Where shall I go?" He said, "To Hebron."

**2** So David went up there, with his two wives, Ahinoam of Jezreel and Abigail, the widow of Nabal of Carmel.

**3** David also brought up the men who were with him, each with his household, and they settled in the towns round Hebron.

**4** The men of Judah came and anointed David there as king over the house of Judah. When David was told, "It was the people of Jabesh-gilead who buried Saul,"

**5** he sent messengers to the people of Jabesh-gilead and said to them, "May you be blessed by the LORD, because you showed this loyalty to Saul your lord and buried him.

**6** Now may the LORD show you faithful love \[H-HESED\] and truth. And I too will do good to you, because you have done this.

**7** Now be strong and be brave, for Saul your lord is dead, and the house of Judah has anointed me king over them."

**8** But Abner son of Ner, the commander of Saul's army, had taken Ish-bosheth son of Saul and brought him over to Mahanaim.

**9** He made him king over Gilead, the Ashurites, Jezreel, Ephraim, Benjamin and all Israel.

**10** Ish-bosheth son of Saul was forty years old when he became king over Israel, and he ruled for two years. But the house of Judah followed David.

**11** The time that David was king in Hebron over the house of Judah was seven years and six months.

**12** Abner son of Ner and the servants of Ish-bosheth son of Saul went out from Mahanaim to Gibeon.

**13** Joab son of Zeruiah and the servants of David went out and met them at the pool of Gibeon. They sat down, one group on one side of the pool and the other on the other side.

**14** Abner said to Joab, "Let the young men get up and compete in front of us." Joab said, "Let them get up."

**15** So they got up and were counted off, twelve for Benjamin and Ish-bosheth son of Saul, and twelve of David's servants.

**16** Each one grabbed his opponent by the head and thrust his sword into his opponent's side, and they fell down together. So that place at Gibeon was called Helkath-hazzurim.

**17** The battle that day was very fierce, and Abner and the men of Israel were beaten by David's servants.

**18** The three sons of Zeruiah were there, Joab, Abishai and Asahel. Asahel was as fast on his feet as a wild gazelle.

**19** Asahel chased Abner, and he did not turn to the right or to the left as he followed him.

**20** Abner looked behind him and said, "Is that you, Asahel?" He answered, "It is."

**21** Abner said to him, "Turn aside to your right or to your left, and seize one of the young men and take his gear." But Asahel would not turn aside from following him.

**22** Abner said again to Asahel, "Turn aside from following me. Why should I strike you to the ground? How could I look your brother Joab in the face?"

**23** But he refused to turn aside. So Abner struck him in the stomach with the butt of his spear, and the spear came out through his back. He fell there and died on the spot. Everyone who came to the place where Asahel had fallen and died stopped still.

**24** But Joab and Abishai chased Abner. As the sun was going down, they came to the hill of Ammah, which faces Giah on the road to the wilderness of Gibeon.

**25** The Benjaminites gathered behind Abner and formed one group, and took their stand on top of a hill.

**26** Abner called to Joab, "Must the sword eat for ever? Do you not know that this will end in bitterness? How long before you tell your people to stop chasing their brothers?"

**27** Joab said, "As God lives, if you had not spoken, the people would not have given up the chase of their brothers until morning."

**28** So Joab blew the trumpet, and all the people stopped. They did not chase Israel any more or fight any more.

**29** Abner and his men went all that night through the Arabah. They crossed the Jordan and marched the whole morning and came to Mahanaim.

**30** Joab came back from chasing Abner. When he had gathered all the people, nineteen of David's servants were missing, besides Asahel.

**31** But David's servants had struck down three hundred and sixty of the Benjaminites who were with Abner.

**32** They took Asahel and buried him in his father's tomb at Bethlehem. Joab and his men marched all night, and the light broke on them at Hebron.

### Chapter 3

**1** The war between the house of Saul and the house of David went on for a long time. David grew steadily stronger, and the house of Saul grew steadily weaker.

**2** Sons were born to David at Hebron. His firstborn was Amnon, by Ahinoam of Jezreel.

**3** His second was Chileab, by Abigail, the widow of Nabal of Carmel. The third was Absalom, son of Maacah daughter of Talmai king of Geshur.

**4** The fourth was Adonijah, son of Haggith. The fifth was Shephatiah, son of Abital.

**5** The sixth was Ithream, by David's wife Eglah. These were born to David at Hebron.

**6** During the war between the house of Saul and the house of David, Abner was making himself more and more powerful in the house of Saul.

**7** Now Saul had had a concubine whose name was Rizpah daughter of Aiah. Ish-bosheth said to Abner, "Why have you slept with my father's concubine?"

**8** Abner was furious at Ish-bosheth's words and said, "Am I a dog's head for Judah? All this time I have shown faithful love to the house of your father Saul, to his brothers and his friends, and have not handed you over to David. And now you charge me today with a fault over this woman.

**9** May God do this to Abner, and more also, if I do not do for David what the LORD has sworn to him:

**10** to take the kingdom from the house of Saul and set up the throne of David over Israel and over Judah, from Dan to Beersheba."

**11** Ish-bosheth could not answer Abner another word, because he was afraid of him.

**12** Abner sent messengers to David at once, saying, "Whose is the land? Make a covenant with me, and I will help you bring all Israel over to you."

**13** David said, "Good. I will make a covenant with you. But I ask one thing of you: you will not see my face unless you bring Michal, Saul's daughter, when you come to see me."

**14** Then David sent messengers to Ish-bosheth son of Saul, saying, "Give me my wife Michal, whom I won for a hundred Philistine foreskins."

**15** Ish-bosheth sent and took her from her husband Paltiel son of Laish.

**16** Her husband went with her, weeping behind her all the way to Bahurim. Then Abner said to him, "Go back." And he went back.

**17** Abner had talked with the elders of Israel and said, "For some time now you have wanted David as your king.

**18** Now do it, for the LORD has said of David, 'By the hand of my servant David I will save my people Israel from the hand of the Philistines and from the hand of all their enemies.'"

**19** Abner also spoke to the Benjaminites. Then Abner went to tell David at Hebron all that Israel and the whole house of Benjamin were willing to do.

**20** When Abner came to David at Hebron with twenty men, David gave a feast for Abner and the men with him.

**21** Abner said to David, "Let me go and gather all Israel to my lord the king, so that they may make a covenant with you, and you may rule over all that your heart desires." So David sent Abner away, and he went in peace.

**22** Just then David's servants and Joab came back from a raid, bringing a great deal of plunder with them. But Abner was no longer with David at Hebron, for he had sent him away, and he had gone in peace.

**23** When Joab and all the army with him arrived, Joab was told, "Abner son of Ner came to the king, and he sent him away, and he has gone in peace."

**24** Then Joab went to the king and said, "What have you done? Look, Abner came to you. Why did you send him away? Now he is gone.

**25** You know Abner son of Ner. He came to deceive you, to learn your movements and everything you are doing."

**26** When Joab left David, he sent messengers after Abner, and they brought him back from the cistern of Sirah. But David did not know it.

**27** When Abner came back to Hebron, Joab took him aside into the middle of the gateway to speak to him privately, and there he stabbed him in the stomach, and he died, to pay for the blood of his brother Asahel.

**28** Afterwards, when David heard of it, he said, "I and my kingdom are for ever innocent before the LORD of the blood of Abner son of Ner.

**29** May it fall on the head of Joab and on all his father's house. May the house of Joab never be without someone with a discharge, or a skin disease, or one who holds a spindle, or one who falls by the sword, or one who lacks bread."

**30** So Joab and his brother Abishai killed Abner, because he had killed their brother Asahel in the battle at Gibeon.

**31** David said to Joab and to all the people with him, "Tear your clothes, put on sackcloth and mourn in front of Abner." And King David himself walked behind the bier.

**32** They buried Abner at Hebron, and the king raised his voice and wept at Abner's grave, and all the people wept.

**33** The king sang a lament for Abner:

"Should Abner have died as a fool dies?

**34** Your hands were not bound, your feet were not put in chains. As one falls before wicked men, you fell."

And all the people wept over him again.

**35** Then all the people came to urge David to eat something while it was still day. But David swore, "May God do this to me, and more also, if I taste bread or anything else before the sun goes down."

**36** All the people took note of it, and it pleased them, as everything the king did pleased them.

**37** So on that day all the people and all Israel understood that it had not been the king's will to kill Abner son of Ner.

**38** The king said to his servants, "Do you not know that a commander, a great man, has fallen today in Israel?

**39** And today I am weak, though anointed king. These men, the sons of Zeruiah, are too hard for me. May the LORD repay the one who does evil according to his evil."

### Chapter 4

**1** When Saul's son heard that Abner had died at Hebron, his courage failed, and all Israel was dismayed.

**2** Saul's son had two men who were leaders of raiding bands. One was named Baanah and the other Rechab, sons of Rimmon of Beeroth, a Benjaminite. (Beeroth is also counted as part of Benjamin,

**3** and the people of Beeroth had fled to Gittaim, where they have lived as foreigners to this day.)

**4** Jonathan son of Saul had a son who was crippled in both feet. He was five years old when the news about Saul and Jonathan came from Jezreel. His nurse picked him up and ran away, and as she hurried to flee, he fell and was left lame. His name was Mephibosheth.

**5** The sons of Rimmon of Beeroth, Rechab and Baanah, set out and came to the house of Ish-bosheth at the hottest part of the day, while he was taking his midday rest.

**6** They went into the middle of the house, as if to fetch wheat, and stabbed him in the stomach. Then Rechab and his brother Baanah slipped away.

**7** They had come into the house while he was lying on his bed in his bedroom, and they struck him down and killed him and cut off his head. They took his head and travelled all night by the way of the Arabah,

**8** and brought the head of Ish-bosheth to David at Hebron. They said to the king, "Here is the head of Ish-bosheth son of Saul, your enemy, who tried to take your life. Today the LORD has avenged my lord the king on Saul and his descendants."

**9** But David answered Rechab and his brother Baanah, the sons of Rimmon of Beeroth, "As the LORD lives, who has rescued me from every trouble,

**10** when someone told me, 'Saul is dead,' and thought he was bringing good news, I seized him and killed him at Ziklag. That was the reward I gave him for his news.

**11** How much more, when wicked men have killed an innocent man in his own house, on his own bed, will I not demand his blood from your hands and wipe you from the earth?"

**12** David gave the order to his young men, and they killed them and cut off their hands and feet and hung them beside the pool at Hebron. But they took the head of Ish-bosheth and buried it in the tomb of Abner at Hebron.

*Review note: 2 Samuel 1 to 4 was drafted in WABV wording. The Hebrew text of Samuel is difficult in many places and in this section is notably damaged at 1:18-27 (the Song of the Bow, in which several lines are uncertain), 2:12-32, 3:12 and 4:6. The Greek text and the Dead Sea Scrolls differ in places, and the footnotes record the main differences. The Amalekite's story in 1:6-10 differs from the account in 1 Samuel 31:3-5, and the footnotes note this without choosing between them. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## 2 Samuel 5-8: Jerusalem, the Ark and the Promise

### Chapter 5

**1** All the tribes of Israel came to David at Hebron and said, "Look, we are your own flesh and bone.

**2** In the past, when Saul was king over us, it was you who led Israel out and brought it back. And the LORD said to you, 'You will shepherd my people Israel, and you will be ruler over Israel.'"

**3** So all the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron, and King David made a covenant \[H-BERIT\] with them at Hebron before the LORD, and they anointed David king over Israel.

**4** David was thirty years old when he became king, and he ruled for forty years.

**5** In Hebron he ruled over Judah for seven years and six months, and in Jerusalem he ruled over all Israel and Judah for thirty-three years.

**6** The king and his men marched to Jerusalem against the Jebusites who lived there. They said to David, "You will not get in here. Even the blind and the lame can turn you back," thinking, "David cannot get in here."

**7** But David captured the stronghold of Zion, that is, the city of David.

**8** On that day David said, "Whoever strikes down the Jebusites, let him go up the water shaft and strike those lame and blind enemies of David." That is why people say, "The blind and the lame may not enter the house."

**9** David lived in the stronghold and called it the city of David. He built all round it, from the Millo inwards.

**10** David grew greater and greater, for the LORD, the God of hosts, was with him.

**11** Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, with cedar trees and carpenters and stonemasons, and they built a house for David.

**12** David knew that the LORD had established him as king over Israel, and that he had raised his kingdom high for the sake of his people Israel.

**13** After he came from Hebron, David took more concubines and wives in Jerusalem, and more sons and daughters were born to him.

**14** These are the names of the children born to him in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, Solomon,

**15** Ibhar, Elishua, Nepheg, Japhia,

**16** Elishama, Eliada and Eliphelet.

**17** When the Philistines heard that David had been anointed king over Israel, they all went up to search for David. But David heard of it and went down to the stronghold.

**18** The Philistines had come and spread out in the Valley of Rephaim.

**19** David asked the LORD, "Shall I go up against the Philistines? Will you hand them over to me?" The LORD said to David, "Go up, for I will certainly hand the Philistines over to you."

**20** So David came to Baal-perazim, and David defeated them there. He said, "The LORD has broken through my enemies before me like a bursting flood." So that place is called Baal-perazim.

**21** The Philistines left their idols there, and David and his men carried them off.

**22** The Philistines came up again and spread out in the Valley of Rephaim.

**23** When David asked the LORD, he said, "Do not go straight up. Circle round behind them and come at them opposite the balsam trees.

**24** When you hear the sound of marching in the tops of the balsam trees, then move quickly, for the LORD has gone out ahead of you to strike down the Philistine army."

**25** David did as the LORD commanded him, and struck down the Philistines from Geba to Gezer.

### Chapter 6

**1** David again gathered all the chosen men of Israel, thirty thousand.

**2** David and all the people with him set out from Baale-judah to bring up from there the ark of God, which is called by the name of the LORD of hosts, who sits enthroned on the cherubim.

**3** They put the ark of God on a new cart and brought it out of Abinadab's house on the hill. Uzzah and Ahio, sons of Abinadab, were guiding the new cart,

**4** with the ark of God on it, and Ahio walked in front of the ark.

**5** David and the whole house of Israel were celebrating before the LORD with all their strength, with songs and with lyres, harps, tambourines, rattles and cymbals.

**6** When they came to the threshing floor of Nacon, Uzzah reached out his hand to the ark of God and took hold of it, because the oxen had stumbled.

**7** The anger of the LORD blazed against Uzzah, and God struck him down there for his error, and he died there beside the ark of God.

**8** David was angry because the LORD had burst out against Uzzah. That place is called Perez-uzzah to this day.

**9** David was afraid of the LORD that day, and he said, "How can the ark of the LORD come to me?"

**10** So David was not willing to take the ark of the LORD into the city of David. David took it aside to the house of Obed-edom the Gittite.

**11** The ark of the LORD stayed in the house of Obed-edom the Gittite for three months, and the LORD blessed Obed-edom and all his household.

**12** It was told to King David, "The LORD has blessed the household of Obed-edom and everything he has, because of the ark of God." So David went and brought up the ark of God from the house of Obed-edom to the city of David with rejoicing.

**13** When those who carried the ark of the LORD had gone six steps, he sacrificed an ox and a fattened animal.

**14** David danced before the LORD with all his strength. David was wearing a linen ephod.

**15** So David and the whole house of Israel brought up the ark of the LORD with shouting and the sound of the ram's horn.

**16** As the ark of the LORD came into the city of David, Michal daughter of Saul looked out of the window and saw King David leaping and dancing before the LORD, and she despised him in her heart.

**17** They brought in the ark of the LORD and set it in its place, inside the tent that David had pitched for it. David offered burnt offerings and peace offerings before the LORD.

**18** When David had finished offering the burnt offerings and peace offerings, he blessed the people in the name of the LORD of hosts,

**19** and gave to all the people, the whole crowd of Israel, men and women alike, a loaf of bread, a portion of meat and a cake of raisins to each. Then all the people went home, each to his house.

**20** David went home to bless his household. But Michal daughter of Saul came out to meet David and said, "How the king of Israel honoured himself today, uncovering himself in front of his servants' maidservants, as one of the vulgar men shamelessly uncovers himself."

**21** David said to Michal, "It was before the LORD, who chose me over your father and over all his house, to appoint me ruler over Israel, the LORD's people. I will celebrate before the LORD.

**22** I will make myself even more lowly than this, and I will be humble in my own eyes. But with the maidservants of whom you have spoken, with them I will be held in honour."

**23** And Michal daughter of Saul had no child to the day of her death.

### Chapter 7

**1** When the king was settled in his house, and the LORD had given him rest from all his enemies around him,

**2** the king said to Nathan the prophet, "Look, I am living in a house of cedar, while the ark of God stays in a tent."

**3** Nathan said to the king, "Go and do whatever is in your heart, for the LORD is with you."

**4** But that same night the word of the LORD came to Nathan:

**5** "Go and tell my servant David, 'This is what the LORD says: Are you the one to build me a house to live in?

**6** I have not lived in a house from the day I brought the Israelites up out of Egypt to this day. I have been moving about in a tent as my dwelling.

**7** In all the places where I moved about among all the Israelites, did I ever say a word to any of the leaders of Israel, whom I commanded to shepherd my people Israel: "Why have you not built me a house of cedar?"'

**8** Now say this to my servant David: 'This is what the LORD of hosts says: I took you from the pasture, from following the sheep, to be ruler over my people Israel.

**9** I have been with you wherever you went, and I have cut off all your enemies from in front of you. I will make you a great name, like the names of the greatest in the earth.

**10** I will set a place for my people Israel and plant them, so that they can live in their own place and not be disturbed again. Violent people will not oppress them any more, as they did at first,

**11** from the day I appointed judges over my people Israel. I will give you rest from all your enemies. And the LORD tells you that the LORD will make a house for you.

**12** When your days are over and you lie down with your ancestors, I will raise up your offspring after you, who will come from your own body, and I will establish his kingdom.

**13** He will build a house for my name, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom for ever.

**14** I will be a father to him, and he will be a son to me. When he does wrong, I will correct him with a human rod and with human blows.

**15** But my faithful love \[H-HESED\] will not leave him, as I took it from Saul, whom I removed from before you.

**16** Your house and your kingdom will be secure for ever before me. Your throne will be established for ever.'"

**17** Nathan spoke to David in keeping with all these words and with all this vision.

**18** Then King David went in and sat before the LORD and said, "Who am I, Lord GOD, and what is my house, that you have brought me this far?

**19** And yet this was a small thing in your eyes, Lord GOD, for you have also spoken about your servant's house for a long time to come. And this is the way of a human being, Lord GOD.

**20** What more can David say to you? You know your servant, Lord GOD.

**21** For the sake of your word, and according to your own heart, you have done all this greatness and made it known to your servant.

**22** So you are great, LORD God. There is no one like you, and there is no God but you, in everything we have heard with our own ears.

**23** And who is like your people Israel, one nation on earth whom God went out to redeem \[H-PADAH\] as his people, to make a name for himself and to do great and awesome things for them, driving out nations and their gods before your people, whom you redeemed for yourself from Egypt?

**24** You made your people Israel your own people for ever, and you, LORD, became their God.

**25** And now, LORD God, keep for ever the word that you have spoken about your servant and his house, and do as you have said.

**26** Then your name will be great for ever, and people will say, 'The LORD of hosts is God over Israel.' And the house of your servant David will be established before you.

**27** For you, LORD of hosts, God of Israel, have uncovered the ear of your servant, saying, 'I will build you a house.' That is why your servant has found the courage to pray this prayer to you.

**28** Now, Lord GOD, you are God, and your words are true, and you have promised this good thing to your servant.

**29** So now, be pleased to bless the house of your servant, so that it may continue for ever before you. For you, Lord GOD, have spoken, and by your blessing the house of your servant will be blessed for ever."

### Chapter 8

**1** After this David defeated the Philistines and brought them under his rule, and David took Metheg-ammah from the Philistines.

**2** He also defeated Moab. He made them lie on the ground and measured them with a line. He measured two lengths to be put to death, and one full length to be kept alive. So the Moabites became David's servants and brought tribute.

**3** David also defeated Hadadezer son of Rehob, king of Zobah, as he went to restore his power at the River Euphrates.

**4** David captured from him seventeen hundred horsemen and twenty thousand foot soldiers. David hamstrung all the chariot horses but kept enough for a hundred chariots.

**5** When the Arameans of Damascus came to help Hadadezer king of Zobah, David struck down twenty-two thousand of the Arameans.

**6** Then David set garrisons in Aram of Damascus, and the Arameans became David's servants and brought tribute. The LORD gave David victory wherever he went.

**7** David took the gold shields that belonged to Hadadezer's servants and brought them to Jerusalem.

**8** From Betah and Berothai, towns of Hadadezer, King David took a great deal of bronze.

**9** When Toi king of Hamath heard that David had defeated the whole army of Hadadezer,

**10** Toi sent his son Joram to King David to greet him and bless him, because he had fought against Hadadezer and defeated him, for Hadadezer had often been at war with Toi. Joram brought with him objects of silver, gold and bronze.

**11** King David also dedicated these to the LORD, along with the silver and gold that he had dedicated from all the nations he had conquered:

**12** from Edom, Moab, the Ammonites, the Philistines and Amalek, and from the plunder of Hadadezer son of Rehob, king of Zobah.

**13** David made a name for himself when he returned from striking down eighteen thousand Edomites in the Valley of Salt.

**14** He set garrisons in Edom, throughout Edom, and all the Edomites became David's servants. The LORD gave David victory wherever he went.

**15** David ruled over all Israel, and David did what was just and right for all his people.

**16** Joab son of Zeruiah was over the army, Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was the recorder,

**17** Zadok son of Ahitub and Ahimelech son of Abiathar were priests, Seraiah was the secretary,

**18** Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and the Pelethites, and David's sons were priests.

## 2 Samuel 16-19: Rebellion and Return

## 2 Samuel 20-24: Sheba, the Gibeonites, the Songs and the Census

### Chapter 20

**3** David came to his house in Jerusalem. The king took the ten concubines whom he had left to look after the house and put them in a house under guard. He provided for them but did not go in to them. So they were shut up until the day of their death, living as widows.

**17** He came near her, and the woman said, "Are you Joab?" He said, "I am." She said to him, "Listen to the words of your servant." He said, "I am listening."

**18** Then she said, "In the old days they used to say, 'Ask at Abel,' and so they settled a matter.

**20** Joab answered, "Far be it, far be it from me, that I should swallow up or destroy.

**21** That is not the case. A man from the hill country of Ephraim, named Sheba son of Bichri, has raised his hand against King David. Hand him over, him alone, and I will withdraw from the town." The woman said to Joab, "His head will be thrown to you over the wall."

### Chapter 21

**1** There was a famine in the days of David for three years, year after year. David sought the face of the LORD, and the LORD said, "There is bloodguilt on Saul and on his house, because he put the Gibeonites to death."

**2** So the king called the Gibeonites and spoke to them. (The Gibeonites were not Israelites but a remnant of the Amorites. The Israelites had sworn an oath to them, but Saul had tried to wipe them out in his zeal for the people of Israel and Judah.)

**3** David said to the Gibeonites, "What shall I do for you? How can I make amends, so that you may bless the inheritance of the LORD?"

**4** The Gibeonites said to him, "It is not a matter of silver or gold between us and Saul or his house, and it is not for us to put anyone in Israel to death." He said, "Then what do you say I should do for you?"

**5** They said to the king, "The man who finished us off and planned to destroy us, so that we have no place anywhere within the borders of Israel,

Chapter 21 records the handing over and hanging of seven of Saul's descendants to the Gibeonites to end a famine, a passage that raises hard questions that the text itself does not answer. Chapters 22 and 23:1-7 are poems, and chapter 22 is also Psalm 18, with differences recorded in the footnotes. Chapter 24 says that the LORD stirred David up to count the people, and then sent a plague because he did so; 1 Chronicles 21 names Satan in this role. The Hebrew text of this section is difficult in many places. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **Title** 2 Samuel continues 1 Samuel, and the two were one book in the Hebrew Bible. The Hebrew text of Samuel is damaged in many places and often differs from the Greek translation and the Dead Sea Scrolls, and the footnotes record the main differences.
- **1:1** The Amalekites had raided Ziklag in 1 Samuel 30, which is why David has just come back from fighting them.
- **1:6** The Amalekite's account of Saul's death differs from 1 Samuel 31:3-5, where Saul falls on his own sword. The text does not say which is true. The man may have made up his story in order to win a reward. David's judgement on him in verse 16 rests on his own words.
- **1:10** "The crown" is Hebrew *nezer*, a diadem or a headband, and "the band" is *etsadah*, an armlet. They are signs of kingship.
- **1:14** "The LORD's anointed" is Hebrew *mashiach YHWH*. See entry H-MASHIACH. David holds to the principle he had in 1 Samuel 24:6 and 26:9.
- **1:16** "Your blood is on your own head" means that the man is responsible for his own death.
- **1:18** The Hebrew has only "the bow". "The Song of the Bow" is a title taken from the content, and it may be a title of the poem or of the book from which it comes. The Book of Jashar is also cited in Joshua 10:13. The Hebrew of this verse is difficult.
- **1:19** "Your glory" is Hebrew *tsevi*, which means both "glory" or "beauty" and "gazelle". The line may mean "Your gazelle, Israel", a figure for Saul or for Jonathan. "Your heights" are the hills of Gilboa.
- **1:20** Gath and Ashkelon are Philistine cities. The women of the Philistines sing the victory songs, as the Israelite women did in 1 Samuel 18:6.
- **1:21** "No fields that give offerings" is Hebrew *sedei terumot*, which is hard to read. The curse on Gilboa is that nothing will grow there to give offerings. "No longer rubbed with oil" may mean that the shield was no longer polished for battle, or that Saul, the one anointed with oil, lay dead.
- **1:22** The bow of Jonathan and the sword of Saul never came back unused.
- **1:23** "In life and in death they were not parted" is a statement that does not fit the quarrels of 1 Samuel 18 to 20, and the poem takes a generous view of both men. "Swifter than eagles, stronger than lions" is a figure for speed and strength.
- **1:24** "Scarlet" is a rich dye and a mark of luxury. Saul is praised for the wealth he brought to Israel.
- **1:26** "Your love for me was wonderful, more than the love of women" is rendered as written. The poem states David's love for Jonathan, and the text gives no more.
- **1:27** The refrain "How the mighty have fallen" closes the poem as it opened it (verses 19 and 25).
- **2:1** David asks the LORD, as he did by the ephod in 1 Samuel 30:7-8. Hebron is the old town of Caleb and of the patriarchs.
- **2:4** This is David's second anointing. The first was private (1 Samuel 16:13), and the third is over all Israel (5:3).
- **2:6** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED.
- **2:8** "Ish-bosheth" means "man of shame". In 1 Chronicles 8:33 the name is Esh-baal, "man of Baal". The word *bosheth* ("shame") replaces *ba'al* in some names (see Judges 6:32).
- **2:9** "The Ashurites" is the Hebrew reading and is uncertain. It may mean the Asherites, or the Geshurites.
- **2:10** "Forty years old ... ruled two years" is hard to match with the seven and a half years of David's reign in Hebron (verse 11). Ish-bosheth may have ruled over all Israel for two years only after a period of war.
- **2:14** "Compete" is Hebrew *sachaq*, to play or to laugh. It is a contest, and it becomes a deadly one.
- **2:16** "Helkath-hazzurim" is Hebrew *chelqat hatstsurim*, which may mean "field of flints" or "field of sides".
- **2:18** "A wild gazelle" is the same word as *tsevi* in 1:19.
- **2:21** "His gear" is the equipment of the dead man. Abner offers Asahel an easier prize.
- **2:23** "The butt of his spear" is the pointed end at the base of the shaft. Abner kills Asahel in the chase, which sets up the blood feud in chapter 3.
- **2:26** "Must the sword eat for ever?" is Abner's appeal to Joab to end the pursuit.
- **2:31** "Three hundred and sixty" and "nineteen" (verse 30) are the casualty counts.
- **3:2** David's six sons at Hebron are listed again in 1 Chronicles 3:1-4. "Chileab" is called "Daniel" there.
- **3:3** "Absalom" is the son of Maacah, daughter of the king of Geshur. This marriage is part of David's alliances.
- **3:7** Taking a dead king's concubine was a claim to his throne (see 16:21-22 and 1 Kings 2:13-22). Ish-bosheth takes Abner's act as treason.
- **3:8** "A dog's head for Judah" is hard to read. The Hebrew may mean "a dog's head, which belongs to Judah", or "a traitor to Judah". The meaning is that Abner is not David's creature.
- **3:12** "At once" translates a Hebrew word (*tachtav*) that is hard to read. It may mean "in his place" or "on his own behalf". The Greek has "to Hebron".
- **3:14** "A hundred Philistine foreskins" is the price named by Saul in 1 Samuel 18:25. David brought two hundred (1 Samuel 18:27). The Hebrew here has "a hundred".
- **3:15** "Paltiel" is the same as "Palti" in 1 Samuel 25:44.
- **3:27** Joab kills Abner "in the middle of the gateway", and the text gives his reason: the blood of Asahel. Joab acts as the avenger of the blood (see entry H-GAAL), though Abner had killed Asahel in battle and in self-defence (2:22-23), and so the death is murder by the standards of the law of the cities of refuge (Numbers 35).
- **3:29** "A skin disease" is Hebrew *tsara'at*. See entry H-TSARAAT. "One who holds a spindle" may mean a weak man or one who does a woman's work. David's curse on Joab's house is a string of misfortunes.
- **3:31** David walks behind the bier. This is the sign of his honour of Abner and of his own innocence.
- **3:33** The lament for Abner is a short poem. "As a fool dies" means a death without cause or defence.
- **3:35** "God do so to me, and more also" is an oath formula (see Ruth 1:17).
- **3:39** "The sons of Zeruiah are too hard for me" is David's admission of Joab's power. It is a theme of the next chapters.
- **4:2** "Beeroth" is a town of the Gibeonites that was counted as Benjaminite. The people of Beeroth fled to Gittaim, perhaps after the killing of the Gibeonites by Saul (see 21:1-2).
- **4:4** "Mephibosheth" is the grandson of Saul. His name is Merib-baal in 1 Chronicles 8:34. The note about his lameness prepares for chapter 9.
- **4:6** The Hebrew of this verse is damaged. The Greek has a different account, in which a woman at the door who was cleaning wheat fell asleep, and the two men slipped past. The WABV text follows the Hebrew with "as if to fetch wheat".
- **4:8** The brothers bring Ish-bosheth's head to David, as the Amalekite brought Saul's crown (1:10). David judges them as he did him.
- **4:12** "Cut off their hands and feet and hung them" is rendered as written. It is a public display of the punishment. The head of Ish-bosheth is buried in Abner's tomb, as a mark of respect.
- **5:1** "Your own flesh and bone" is the claim of kinship that the northern tribes make, as Abner had done (3:17-19).
- **5:2** "Shepherd" is the image of a king as a guide and protector. "Ruler" is Hebrew *nagid*, the word used of Saul in 1 Samuel 9:16.
- **5:3** "Covenant" is Hebrew *berit*. See entry H-BERIT. This is David's third anointing (see 2:4), and the covenant is made "before the LORD", between the king and the elders.
- **5:5** David's years at Hebron (seven and a half) and at Jerusalem (thirty-three) add up to forty years and six months, and verse 4 gives the round figure.
- **5:6** "The blind and the lame" is the Jebusites' boast that even weak defenders could hold the city. The Hebrew can also mean that the Jebusites placed blind and lame people on the walls.
- **5:8** "The water shaft" is Hebrew *tsinnor*, a rare word that may mean a channel, a pipe or a hook. The way David's men entered the city is debated. "The blind and the lame may not enter the house" is the saying that is traced to this event, and its sense is also uncertain.
- **5:9** "The Millo" is Hebrew *millo*, "the fill", probably terraces on the slope of the city. "The city of David" is the old Jebusite town.
- **5:11** Hiram of Tyre sends cedar and craftsmen. The relationship with Tyre continues into Solomon's reign (1 Kings 5).
- **5:13** David's taking of more wives is against Deuteronomy 17:17, and the text does not comment.
- **5:14** The sons born in Jerusalem are also listed in 1 Chronicles 3:5-8 and 14:4-7. Solomon is named fourth.
- **5:20** "Baal-perazim" means "lord of breakthroughs". "Broken through" is Hebrew *parats*, the same verb as for Uzzah in 6:8.
- **5:21** David and his men carry off the Philistine idols. In 1 Chronicles 14:12 they are burned.
- **5:23** "Balsam trees" is Hebrew *bekha'im*, a tree of uncertain kind. It may be mulberries or poplars, or trees that weep.
- **5:24** "The sound of marching" is a rustling in the treetops, which is the sign that the LORD has gone out ahead.
- **5:25** "From Geba to Gezer" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Chronicles 14:16 and the Greek have "Gibeon".
- **6:1** "Thirty thousand" is the Hebrew number. The Dead Sea Scrolls have seventy thousand.
- **6:2** "Baale-judah" is another name for Kiriath-jearim (see 1 Samuel 7:1). The Hebrew of the second half of the verse, "which is called by the name", is difficult.
- **6:3** A new cart was used by the Philistines (1 Samuel 6:7). The law required the ark to be carried on poles by the Levites (Numbers 4:15; 7:9). David does not follow the law, and the story shows the result.
- **6:6** "The threshing floor of Nacon" is called Chidon in 1 Chronicles 13:9.
- **6:7** "For his error" is Hebrew *shal*, a word that is hard to read and is not in some Greek manuscripts. The text does not say what the error was. The anger of the LORD against Uzzah is told with no further comment.
- **6:8** "Perez-uzzah" means "the outburst against Uzzah". The verb *parats* is the same as in 5:20.
- **6:10** "Obed-edom the Gittite" may be a man from Gath, or a Levite of that name (1 Chronicles 15:18).
- **6:14** "A linen ephod" is a priest's garment. David is dressed as a priest and not as a king.
- **6:16** Michal is called "daughter of Saul" here, not "wife of David", which shows the distance between them.
- **6:17** "The tent that David had pitched" is a new tent in Jerusalem. The old tent of meeting was at Gibeon (1 Chronicles 16:39).
- **6:20** "Uncovering himself" refers to David's dancing in a short garment. Michal's scorn is sharp.
- **6:21** David says the LORD chose him "over your father and over all his house".
- **6:23** "No child to the day of her death" is the narrator's note. In 21:8 the Hebrew names Michal as the mother of five sons, though many take that to be an error for Merab.
- **7:2** "A house of cedar" is David's palace, built by Hiram's craftsmen (5:11). He is troubled that the ark is in a tent.
- **7:5** "A house" is the word on which the chapter turns. David wishes to build the LORD a house (a temple). The LORD says he will build David a house (a dynasty, verse 11). The same word, *bayit*, is used for both.
- **7:6** "I have been moving about in a tent" says that the LORD's dwelling has always moved with the people.
- **7:7** "Leaders" is the Hebrew *shivtei*, which can mean "tribes" or "rods" or "leaders". 1 Chronicles 17:6 has "judges".
- **7:12** "Your offspring, who will come from your own body" is the next king. The promise is first about Solomon, and then about David's line.
- **7:13** "A house for my name" is the temple. "For ever" is a word that recurs in the chapter.
- **7:14** "I will be a father to him, and he will be a son to me" is the language of adoption, used of the king. Hebrews 1:5 quotes it. "A human rod" means a human punishment. 1 Chronicles 17 omits the clause "when he does wrong".
- **7:15** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. The LORD's love will not be taken from David's son as it was from Saul.
- **7:19** "This is the way of a human being" is Hebrew *torat ha-adam*, which is very hard to read. It may mean "this is a teaching for humanity", or "is this the way people deal with one another?", or "this is the law of mankind". 1 Chronicles 17:17 reads differently.
- **7:23** "Redeem" is Hebrew *padah*. See entry H-PADAH. The Hebrew has a plural verb with "God" (*elohim*), which is unusual. The Greek and 1 Chronicles read a singular.
- **7:27** "Uncovered the ear" is a Hebrew idiom for revealing something.
- **8:1** "Metheg-ammah" is Hebrew for "the bridle of the mother city". In 1 Chronicles 18:1 it is Gath.
- **8:2** "Measured them with a line" is a way of choosing prisoners for death. Two-thirds are put to death and one-third spared. This is rendered as written.
- **8:3** "Hadadezer" is a king of Zobah, in Syria. He is called Hadarezer in Chronicles.
- **8:4** "Seventeen hundred horsemen" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Chronicles 18:4 has seven thousand. "Hamstrung" means to cut the tendons of the horses, as in Joshua 11:6.
- **8:8** "Betah and Berothai" are towns of Hadadezer. In 1 Chronicles 18:8 they are Tibhath and Cun.
- **8:10** "Joram" is called Hadoram in 1 Chronicles 18:10.
- **8:13** The Hebrew reads "when he returned from striking down Aram, eighteen thousand in the Valley of Salt". The Greek, the Syriac and Psalm 60 (title) point to Edom. The WABV text follows "Edomites", which fits verse 14. This departs from the Hebrew and is flagged for the scholar.
- **8:15** "Just and right" is Hebrew *mishpat u-tsedaqah*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH.
- **8:17** "Ahimelech son of Abiathar" reverses the names in 1 Samuel 22:20, where Abiathar is the son of Ahimelech. One of the two is probably an error.
- **8:18** "David's sons were priests" is the Hebrew reading. The word may be used here of high officials, as in 1 Chronicles 18:17 ("chief officials at the king's side").
- **9:1** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. David keeps the promise he made to Jonathan in 1 Samuel 20:14-15 and 20:42.
- **9:3** "The kindness of God" is faithful love (*hesed*) that is given as God gives it, without a claim.
- **9:4** "Lo-debar" is a town east of the Jordan, in Gilead. Machir is a man of means who has sheltered the boy since 4:4.
- **9:6** "Mephibosheth" is the name that appears here. It is Merib-baal in 1 Chronicles 8:34. The element *bosheth* ("shame") replaces *ba'al* (see 2:8).
- **9:8** "A dead dog" is a figure of low worth, as in 1 Samuel 24:14.
- **9:10** "Ziba had fifteen sons and twenty servants" shows his standing. Ziba later gives a different report of Mephibosheth (16:1-4; 19:24-30).
- **9:13** The note that Mephibosheth was lame in both feet repeats 4:4 and ends the chapter.
- **10:2** "Faithful love" again is *hesed*. Nahash king of the Ammonites is the king who besieged Jabesh-gilead in 1 Samuel 11. He seems to have helped David at some time, but the text does not say how.
- **10:4** "Shaved off half of each man's beard" and cutting their clothes "at the hips" was a deep public shaming. The Hebrew says "to the buttocks".
- **10:6** "A stench" means that they were loathed, as in 1 Samuel 13:4 and 27:12. "Beth-rehob, Zobah, Maacah and Tob" are Aramean states north of Israel. The numbers differ in 1 Chronicles 19:6-7.
- **10:12** Joab's words are a call to courage, with the outcome left to the LORD.
- **10:16** "The River" is the Euphrates. "Helam" is a place east of the Jordan.
- **10:18** "Seven hundred chariots" and "forty thousand horsemen" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Chronicles 19:18 has seven thousand chariots and forty thousand foot soldiers.
- **11:1** "The time when kings go out to war" is the spring. David "stayed in Jerusalem" and does not go with the army, which is the setting for what follows.
- **11:2** "One evening" is literally "at the time of the evening". David sees the woman from the roof. The Hebrew does not say that she meant to be seen.
- **11:3** "Bathsheba daughter of Eliam" may be the granddaughter of Ahithophel, David's counsellor, since an Eliam son of Ahithophel is named in 23:34. "Uriah the Hittite" is one of David's mighty men (23:39), a foreigner who lives as an Israelite and swears by the LORD.
- **11:4** "Took her" is the Hebrew verb *laqach*, and "she came to him" and "he lay with her" is all that is said of the act. The text does not describe what Bathsheba felt or whether she was able to refuse, and the king had the power to summon a subject. Nathan's parable (12:1-4) describes the act as a taking from a weaker person by a stronger. The parenthesis says that she had just finished her monthly period of impurity (Leviticus 15:19-30), so the child that follows must be David's.
- **11:8** "Wash your feet" is a polite way of telling a man to go home and rest. "Feet" is also a euphemism in Hebrew for the body.
- **11:11** "The ark and Israel and Judah are staying in tents" is Uriah's reason. Soldiers on campaign kept from sex (see 1 Samuel 21:5). Uriah's loyalty puts David's plan in doubt, and shows the contrast with David.
- **11:13** David makes Uriah drunk to try to send him to his house. It fails.
- **11:15** David's letter to Joab, which Uriah carries himself, is an order to have him killed in battle.
- **11:21** "Abimelech son of Jerubbesheth" is Abimelech son of Gideon. "Jerubbesheth" is the form of Jerubbaal with *bosheth* (see Judges 6:32; 9:53).
- **11:25** "The sword eats one as well as another" is David's reply, which treats Uriah's death as a chance loss of war.
- **11:27** "Evil in the eyes of the LORD" is the narrator's only comment on what David did.
- **12:1** "The LORD sent Nathan" and the parable that follows is the way the LORD confronts the king. A parable of this sort is told to get a judgement from the king himself (see 14:1-20).
- **12:3** "A small ewe lamb" that "slept in his arms, like a daughter to him" is a figure for Bathsheba and also for Uriah's loss.
- **12:6** "Four times over" is the penalty for stealing a sheep in Exodus 22:1.
- **12:7** "You are the man" is the center of the chapter.
- **12:8** "Your master's wives" are the wives of Saul. The earlier text does not record that David took them. The phrase may be a figure for succession.
- **12:10** "The sword will never leave your house" is fulfilled in the deaths of Amnon, Absalom and Adonijah (chapters 13, 18 and 1 Kings 2).
- **12:11** The punishment is fulfilled when Absalom lies with David's concubines on the roof (16:22).
- **12:13** "The LORD has also taken away your sin" is Hebrew *he'evir*, "passed over". Psalm 51 is linked in its title to this event.
- **12:14** The Hebrew has "you have utterly scorned the enemies of the LORD". Scribes seem to have added the words "enemies of" so as not to say that the LORD himself was scorned. The WABV text follows the sense without the added words.
- **12:16** David's fasting and lying on the ground are a plea for the child's life.
- **12:23** "I will go to him" means David will join the child in death, in Sheol (see entry H-SHEOL). It is not said that the child is anywhere in particular.
- **12:24** "Solomon" is Hebrew *Shelomoh*, from the root *shalom* (see entry H-SHALOM). "The LORD loved him" says the LORD chose him from the start.
- **12:25** "Jedidiah" means "beloved of the LORD".
- **12:27** "The water supply of the city" is Hebrew *'ir hammayim*, "city of waters". It may mean the lower city or the water supply.
- **12:30** "The crown of their king" is Hebrew *malkam*, which may also be read "Milcom", the god of the Ammonites, so that the crown was taken from an idol. A talent of gold is about 34 kilograms.
- **12:31** The Hebrew of this verse is uncertain. It can be read as setting the people to work with saws, picks and axes and at the brick kilns (forced labour), or as putting them to death with them. The WABV text follows the first reading, and 1 Chronicles 20:3 can be read in either way.
- **13:1** Tamar is Absalom's full sister and Amnon's half-sister, by a different mother. Amnon is David's firstborn (3:2). The Hebrew word "loved" (*ahev*) is used of Amnon's desire. Verse 15 shows that it was not love in the sense of care.
- **13:3** "Clever" is Hebrew *chakham*, "wise", which is used here for cunning. Jonadab is David's nephew, and he helps Amnon's plan.
- **13:12** "Do not force me" is Hebrew *te'anneni*, a verb of humbling or violating. "This is not done in Israel" and "shameful thing" (*nevalah*) are the words used of the crimes at Shechem (Genesis 34:7) and at Gibeah (Judges 19:23; 20:6).
- **13:13** "He will not keep me from you" is Tamar's appeal. The law forbids marriage with a half-sister (Leviticus 18:9, 11; 20:17). Her words may be a desperate attempt to stop him, or they may reflect an older custom. The text does not explain.
- **13:14** "He overpowered her and raped her" translates two Hebrew verbs, *vaye'anneha vayishkav otah*, "he humbled her and lay with her". The text says that he was stronger than she was and that he forced her. The WABV text uses the plain word for what the Hebrew describes.
- **13:15** "Hated her with a very great hatred" is the narrator's note on Amnon. The hatred was greater than the "love" (verse 1).
- **13:16** The Hebrew of Tamar's reply is hard to read. The Greek has "Do not send me away, for this wrong is greater than the other." The meaning is that sending her out of the house after what he has done is a worse wrong. She is left with no hope of marriage, and no protection.
- **13:18** "A long robe with sleeves" is Hebrew *ketonet passim*, the same words as Joseph's coat in Genesis 37:3. It was a mark of a king's daughter who was a virgin.
- **13:19** Ashes on the head, the torn robe and the hand on the head are signs of grief and shame. She cries aloud, which is a protest.
- **13:20** Absalom tells her to "keep quiet" and "not take it to heart". The word "desolate" is Hebrew *shomemah*. She lives in Absalom's house, as a woman with no future.
- **13:21** The Hebrew says only that David was very angry. The Dead Sea Scrolls and the Greek add: "but he did not punish Amnon his son, because he loved him, since he was his firstborn." The WABV text follows the Hebrew, and records the longer text.
- **13:23** Baal-hazor is a place in Ephraim, north of Jerusalem. Sheep-shearing is again a festival (see 1 Samuel 25:2-8).
- **13:27** The Greek and the Dead Sea Scrolls add: "Absalom made a feast like a king's feast." The Hebrew does not.
- **13:32** "This has been Absalom's plan from the day Amnon raped his sister Tamar" is Jonadab's comment, which shows that he had known of the planned revenge.
- **13:37** Talmai king of Geshur is Absalom's mother's father (3:3), so Absalom has family to go to.
- **13:39** "The king's spirit longed to go out to Absalom" translates a hard Hebrew verb (*kalah*), which can mean "to be spent", "to long" or "to cease". The subject can be David or his spirit. The meaning may be that David's anger passed, or that he longed for his son.
- **14:1** "Turned towards Absalom" is Hebrew *'al Absalom*, which can mean "towards" or "against" Absalom. The Hebrew can be read to mean that the king's heart was against Absalom. The WABV text takes it as "towards", which suits Joab's plan.
- **14:2** Tekoa is a town south of Bethlehem. The wise woman is one of the women of Tekoa, and she is the first of a group of wise women in Samuel (see 20:16).
- **14:5** The woman's story is a parable, as Nathan's was (12:1-4). It is told to win a judgement from the king. In her story the family wants to put a murderer to death.
- **14:7** "The avenger of blood" is Hebrew *go'el haddam*. See entry H-GAAL. "My last ember" means the last hope of her husband's line.
- **14:11** "Not one hair of your son will fall" echoes 1 Samuel 14:45.
- **14:13** "Why then have you planned such a thing against the people of God?" turns the story on David: if he spares her son, he should recall his own banished son.
- **14:14** "We all must die" and "water spilled on the ground" are the woman's reasons for mercy. "God does not take away life" is the Hebrew reading, which is hard. It may mean "God does not take away a life" or "God does not lift up a soul". The sense is that God devises ways for the banished to return.
- **14:17** "The angel of God" is a figure for wisdom (see also verse 20).
- **14:19** "The hand of Joab" shows that the king sees the plan behind the woman's story.
- **14:24** Absalom may return, but he may not see the king. This half-measure sets up the conflict.
- **14:25** Absalom is praised for his beauty, as Saul was (1 Samuel 9:2) and David (1 Samuel 16:12). The praise is a sign of the danger he is.
- **14:26** "Two hundred shekels" of hair is about two and a half kilograms, a large weight. Some Greek manuscripts have one hundred.
- **14:27** "Three sons and one daughter whose name was Tamar" are given to Absalom. In 18:18 Absalom says that he has no son to keep his name. The two verses are kept as written.
- **14:30** Setting fire to Joab's field is a rash act by Absalom to get Joab's attention.
- **14:33** "The king kissed Absalom" ends the quarrel, but the story shows that it is not repaired.
- **15:1** A chariot, horses and fifty runners are a royal display, like Adonijah's in 1 Kings 1:5.
- **15:2** "The road to the gate" is the way to the place of judgement. Absalom uses it to win hearts.
- **15:6** "Stole the hearts" is a figure for winning people by false means.
- **15:7** "Four years" follows the Syriac, the Greek manuscripts and Josephus. The Hebrew text reads "forty years", which cannot be right. The WABV text departs from the Hebrew, and the choice is flagged for the scholar.
- **15:8** The vow Absalom claims to have made at Geshur is a pretext for going to Hebron, where he was born (3:2-3) and where David was first made king.
- **15:10** "The sound of the ram's horn" is the signal for acclaiming a king (see 1 Kings 1:34).
- **15:11** "Two hundred men ... in their innocence" were taken to give the plot an air of legitimacy.
- **15:12** "Ahithophel the Gilonite" is David's counsellor. He may be the grandfather of Bathsheba (see 11:3; 23:34), which would explain his break with David.
- **15:16** "Ten concubines" are left to look after the house. This leads to 16:21-22.
- **15:18** "The Cherethites and the Pelethites" are David's bodyguard (8:18). "The six hundred Gittites" are men from Gath who came with David and follow him.
- **15:19** "Ittai the Gittite" is a foreigner whom David tries to send back. Ittai's reply shows loyalty beyond what was owed.
- **15:20** "Faithful love and truth" is *hesed ve-emet* (see entry H-HESED). It is David's blessing on Ittai.
- **15:23** "The Wadi Kidron" runs east of Jerusalem. David crosses it going out to the wilderness.
- **15:24** "Abiathar went up" is a hard Hebrew clause. It may mean that Abiathar offered sacrifices, or that he went up from the city.
- **15:25** "His dwelling place" is the sanctuary where the ark is kept. David sends the ark back, and leaves his fate to the LORD.
- **15:27** "Are you not a seer?" is the Hebrew reading. It can also be read "Do you see?" The sense is the same, that Zadok understands and can serve as a source of information.
- **15:30** "Weeping, with his head covered and his feet bare" are signs of mourning and of shame. The Mount of Olives is east of Jerusalem.
- **15:31** David's prayer that Ahithophel's advice be turned into foolishness is answered in 17:14.
- **15:32** "Where God was worshipped" is a shrine on the top of the hill. "Hushai the Archite" becomes David's agent in Absalom's court.
- **16:1** Ziba, the servant of Mephibosheth (9:2-12), brings gifts for the king's company. His help is the opening of a second story about Mephibosheth.
- **16:3** Ziba's report is that Mephibosheth hopes to be given back the kingdom of his grandfather. In 19:24-30 Mephibosheth gives a very different account. The two accounts are kept as they stand, and the text does not say which is true.
- **16:4** David gives Ziba all that belonged to Mephibosheth, on one man's word and without hearing the other.
- **16:5** Bahurim is a village east of Jerusalem, in Benjamin. Shimei is of Saul's clan, and he speaks for those who resent David.
- **16:7** "Man of blood" is Hebrew *ish damim*, and "worthless man" is *beliyya'al*.
- **16:8** "All the blood of the house of Saul" refers to the deaths of Saul's family. The text elsewhere says that David did not kill Saul or Abner or Ish-bosheth (1 Samuel 24 and 26; 2 Samuel 3:28-39; 4:9-12). The text leaves Shimei's charge without an answer here. See also 21:1-9.
- **16:10** David accepts that the curse may come from the LORD, and will not have Shimei killed.
- **16:12** "My distress" is the reading tradition (Hebrew *'onyi*). The written text has "my wrong" (*'avoni*). The WABV text follows the reading tradition, which suits David's words about "good" in return for the curse.
- **16:16** "Long live the king" is Hushai's greeting. It can be heard as meant for Absalom or for David.
- **16:21** Ahithophel advises Absalom to sleep with his father's concubines, to make the break with David final. In this world the act is a claim to the throne (see 3:7 and 1 Kings 2:22).
- **16:22** The tent on the roof is where David first saw Bathsheba (11:2). This fulfils Nathan's word in 12:11-12.
- **16:23** "Like the word of God" says how highly Ahithophel's advice was valued.
- **17:3** "As a bride comes back to her husband" is a figure for the people returning to Absalom.
- **17:8** "A bear robbed of her cubs" is a figure for a fierce fighter, as in Hosea 13:8.
- **17:11** "From Dan to Beersheba" is the whole land. Hushai's advice is to gather a vast army and lead it himself, which wastes time.
- **17:13** "Ropes" to drag a town into the valley is a figure of total destruction.
- **17:14** "The LORD had ordained that the good advice of Ahithophel be defeated" is the narrator's comment on the plan, and answers David's prayer in 15:31.
- **17:17** "En-rogel" is a spring south of Jerusalem. A servant woman carries messages so that the priests' sons are not seen in the town.
- **17:19** The woman covers the well with a cloth and grain to hide the two men.
- **17:23** Ahithophel puts his house in order and takes his own life. The text records this without comment, and says he was buried in his father's tomb.
- **17:24** Mahanaim is where Ish-bosheth's court was (2:8). David now holds it.
- **17:25** "Ithra the Israelite" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Chronicles 2:17 has "Jether the Ishmaelite", and the Greek has "Jezreelite". Amasa is Joab's cousin. He is said to be the son of "Abigal daughter of Nahash, sister of Zeruiah". In 1 Chronicles 2:16 Zeruiah and Abigail are daughters of Jesse. The difference is not resolved.
- **17:27** "Shobi son of Nahash from Rabbah" is an Ammonite who helps David, possibly the brother of the Hanun of chapter 10. "Machir" is the man who sheltered Mephibosheth (9:4). "Barzillai" is a wealthy Gileadite.
- **18:3** "You are worth ten thousand of us" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek has "now there are ten thousand like us". The army will not let the king risk himself.
- **18:5** "Be gentle with the young man Absalom" is the king's order, which the soldiers heard.
- **18:8** "The forest swallowed up more people than the sword" is a figure for the dangers of the thick woods.
- **18:9** The Hebrew says that Absalom's head was caught in the oak. It does not say that it was his hair. The story of his hair is in 14:26.
- **18:12** The soldier's refusal shows that Joab acted against the king's explicit order.
- **18:14** "Three spears" is Hebrew *shevatim*, which can mean rods, darts or spears. Joab thrusts them into Absalom's heart while he hangs. Ten armour-bearers then finish him. The killing is rendered as written.
- **18:17** "A large pit" and "a very great heap of stones" is a shameful burial, like that of Achan (Joshua 7:26) and the king of Ai (Joshua 8:29).
- **18:18** "I have no son to keep my name alive" sits uneasily with 14:27, where three sons are born to Absalom. The sons may have died. The pillar is called "Absalom's Monument" and is in the King's Valley.
- **18:21** "The Cushite" is a man from Cush, in Africa. He is probably a foreign servant of Joab.
- **18:28** "Peace" is Hebrew *shalom*. See entry H-SHALOM.
- **18:29** Ahimaaz avoids the question, which the Cushite then answers.
- **18:32** "May the enemies of my lord the king be like that young man" is the Cushite's veiled reply that Absalom is dead.
- **18:33** In the Hebrew Bible this verse is 19:1, so the English chapter 19 has forty-three verses and the Hebrew has forty-four. David's cry is rendered plainly, as in the Hebrew.
- **19:1** "The king is weeping" shows the people the cost of the victory.
- **19:5** Joab's rebuke is harsh and direct, and it is the plain speech of the commander who has just killed the king's son.
- **19:7** "I swear by the LORD" is Joab's oath that the army will leave David if he does not come out.
- **19:8** "The gateway" is the place where the king hears the people.
- **19:11** The elders of Judah delay in bringing back the king, and David presses them through the priests.
- **19:13** David appoints Amasa commander in place of Joab. This sets up the struggle in 20:4-13.
- **19:16** Shimei is first among the Benjaminites to meet David.
- **19:21** "The LORD's anointed" is the principle from 1 Samuel 24 and 26. Abishai wants Shimei put to death for cursing him.
- **19:22** "Adversaries" is Hebrew *satan*, "opponents". David refuses to start his return with an execution. He charges Solomon to deal with Shimei in 1 Kings 2:8-9.
- **19:24** Mephibosheth's neglect of his feet, moustache and clothes are signs of mourning.
- **19:26** "My servant deceived me" is Mephibosheth's accusation of Ziba, in contrast to Ziba's in 16:3.
- **19:29** "You and Ziba will divide the land" is David's compromise. It does not settle who is telling the truth.
- **19:30** "Let him take it all" is Mephibosheth's reply, which shows he cares more for David's return than the land.
- **19:32** "Barzillai" is eighty years old and a very rich man who fed David at Mahanaim.
- **19:35** Barzillai's words about taste and hearing show the limits of old age.
- **19:37** "Chimham" goes in his place. He may be Barzillai's son. See also 1 Kings 2:7 and Jeremiah 41:17.
- **19:41** The quarrel between Judah and the other tribes shows the first sign of the split of the kingdom (see chapter 20 and 1 Kings 12).
- **19:43** "We have ten shares in the king" refers to the ten tribes of the north, against Judah's one. "The words of the men of Judah were harsher" is the narrator's judgement.
- **20:1** "Sheba son of Bichri" is a Benjaminite who leads the northern tribes away from David. "We have no share in David" is the cry that is used again at the split of the kingdom (1 Kings 12:16).
- **20:3** The ten concubines whom Absalom took (16:21-22) are shut up for life as "widows". They are not put to death, and David does not go in to them. The text records this without comment.
- **20:5** Amasa is slow in calling out Judah. The text does not say why.
- **20:9** "Is it well with you, my brother?" is the greeting, and Joab's kiss is the cover for the killing. His left-handed sword is the weapon. Amasa is Joab's cousin (17:25). Joab kills in the same way as Abner (3:27).
- **20:11** "Whoever favours Joab, and whoever is for David" shows how Joab links himself with the king.
- **20:14** "Abel of Beth-maacah" is a town in the far north. The "Bichrites" are Sheba's clan.
- **20:18** "Ask at Abel" is a saying about the town's wisdom. The Hebrew text is uncertain and some readers take the verse to say "let them ask at Abel, and so settle it".
- **20:19** "A mother in Israel" is a title of honour for a town (as Deborah was in Judges 5:7). "The inheritance of the LORD" is the land.
- **20:22** The woman's wisdom saves the town by the death of one man. Sheba's head is thrown over the wall.
- **20:23** The list of David's officers is like the one in 8:15-18. "Adoram" is in charge of forced labour, which later causes the revolt (1 Kings 12:18).
- **21:1** "David sought the face of the LORD" is the Hebrew idiom for asking the LORD. The famine and the bloodguilt are linked. The text does not say when Saul killed the Gibeonites, since this is not told in the earlier books.
- **21:2** "The Gibeonites" are the people who made a treaty with Joshua (Joshua 9), who swore an oath to them. Saul's attempt to wipe them out broke that oath.
- **21:6** "We will hang them before the LORD" is the Gibeonites' request. The Hebrew word *yaqa'* ("hang" or "expose") is rare. The text does not say whether it was hanging or impaling. "The chosen one of the LORD" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek has a different reading.
- **21:7** David spares Mephibosheth because of his oath to Jonathan (1 Samuel 20:14-17, 42).
- **21:8** "The five sons of Merab" is the reading of some Hebrew manuscripts, the Greek and the Syriac. The Hebrew text has "the five sons of Michal", who is not recorded as having children (6:23), while Merab was married to Adriel (1 Samuel 18:19). The WABV text departs from the Hebrew here, and the choice is flagged for the scholar. The deaths of seven of Saul's descendants for Saul's guilt are rendered as written. The law says that children are not to be put to death for their parents' sin (Deuteronomy 24:16).
- **21:10** "Rizpah" is the woman Abner was accused of taking (3:7). Her vigil of guarding the bodies from birds and animals is told with great restraint. It lasts from the beginning of the harvest until the autumn rains.
- **21:12** David then collects the bones of Saul and Jonathan, from Jabesh-gilead, and buries them in the family tomb. The story of how the Philistines hung their bodies is in 1 Samuel 31:10-13.
- **21:14** "God answered prayers for the land" means the famine ended. It is only said after the burials, and not after the hanging.
- **21:16** "Descendants of the giants" is Hebrew *Rapha*, the *repha'im*. Their names and the sizes of their weapons are given as in the list.
- **21:17** "The lamp of Israel" is a figure for the king, whose life must not be risked.
- **21:19** The Hebrew says that Elhanan killed "Goliath the Gittite", while 1 Samuel 17 says David did. 1 Chronicles 20:5 says that Elhanan killed "Lahmi the brother of Goliath". The Hebrew is followed here, and the difference is not resolved. "Jaare-oregim" ("forests of weavers") is odd, since *oregim* ("weavers") also ends the verse.
- **21:20** "Six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot" is a detail that the text gives in full, and it is repeated in 1 Chronicles 20:6.
- **22:1** This song is also Psalm 18, with many small differences. The footnotes record the main ones.
- **22:2** "Crag" and "fortress" are figures of safety. The first lines of Psalm 18 have a different opening ("I love you, LORD").
- **22:3** "Rock" is Hebrew *tsur*. See entry H-TSUR. "The horn of my safety" is a figure for strength.
- **22:6** "Sheol" is the place of the dead. See entry H-SHEOL.
- **22:7** "From his temple" means from his dwelling in heaven.
- **22:8** The verses 8 to 16 describe the LORD coming in a storm to rescue the king. They use the language of the earthquake, fire, thunder and lightning.
- **22:11** "He appeared" is the reading in this text (Hebrew *vayera*). Psalm 18:10 has "he swooped" (*vayeda*).
- **22:21** "Righteousness" is Hebrew *tsedaqah*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. David's claim to be blameless (verses 21 to 25) sits beside the story of Bathsheba and Uriah, and the text does not comment. It is also read as the claim of a king who is faithful to the covenant in his life as a whole.
- **22:26** "With the faithful you show yourself faithful" is Hebrew *hasid*. The line says God answers each person as they are.
- **22:33** "He makes my way safe" is the reading of this text. Psalm 18:32 has "he makes my way blameless".
- **22:36** "Your help makes me great" is the Hebrew reading of this text.
- **22:43** "Mud in the streets" is a figure for total defeat.
- **22:44** "The quarrels of my people" refers to the revolts of Absalom and Sheba.
- **22:47** "The LORD lives" is a shout of praise, and a common oath.
- **22:51** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. "His anointed" is *mashiach*. See entry H-MASHIACH. The first word of the verse is "tower" in this text and "he gives great victories" in Psalm 18:50. The WABV text follows the second, which fits the sense.
- **23:1** "The saying of David" is Hebrew *ne'um*, the word of a prophetic oracle. "Raised on high" and "the anointed of the God of Jacob" are David's titles. "The sweet singer of Israel" may mean "the one dear to the songs of Israel".
- **23:2** "The spirit of the LORD" is Hebrew *ruach YHWH*. See entry H-RUACH.
- **23:3** "The Rock of Israel" is the LORD. The saying about the just ruler is the centre of the poem.
- **23:5** "An everlasting covenant" is Hebrew *berit olam*. See entry H-BERIT. The Hebrew of this verse is hard, and the meaning is uncertain.
- **23:6** "The worthless" is Hebrew *beliyya'al*. They are compared to thorns that cannot be handled.
- **23:8** "Josheb-basshebeth the Tahchemonite" is called Jashobeam in 1 Chronicles 11:11. The Hebrew number of those he killed is eight hundred, and Chronicles has three hundred.
- **23:10** "His hand stuck to the sword" is a figure for fighting until he could not let go.
- **23:13** "The cave of Adullam" and the three warriors who bring water from Bethlehem is a story that is also in 1 Chronicles 11:15-19.
- **23:16** "He poured it out to the LORD" means he gave it as an offering, since the water was bought with the risk of men's lives.
- **23:20** "Two lion-like warriors of Moab" translates Hebrew *ari'el*, a word of uncertain meaning. It may mean "lion of God", or "hearth of God", or a title of Moabite heroes.
- **23:39** "Uriah the Hittite" closes the list. The count of thirty-seven is the count of those named, including the three and Abishai and Benaiah.
- **24:1** "He stirred up David" says that the LORD himself moved David to take the census. 1 Chronicles 21:1 says that "Satan" stirred David up. The Hebrew text of Samuel is followed here, and the footnote records both. The text does not explain why a census was wrong.
- **24:3** Joab protests, and he is overruled. A census was linked with a plague in Exodus 30:12.
- **24:6** "Kadesh in the land of the Hittites" follows an ancient Greek reading. The Hebrew text has "the land of Tahtim-hodshi", a place that is not otherwise known. The WABV text departs from the Hebrew here, and the choice is flagged for the scholar.
- **24:9** "Eight hundred thousand" and "five hundred thousand" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Chronicles 21:5 gives 1,100,000 for Israel and 470,000 for Judah.
- **24:10** "David's heart struck him" is the same idiom as in 1 Samuel 24:5. He confesses at once.
- **24:13** "Three years of famine" follows the Greek and 1 Chronicles 21:12. The Hebrew text of 2 Samuel reads "seven years". The WABV text departs from the Hebrew here, and the choice is flagged for the scholar. The other two choices, three months and three days, fit a set of threes.
- **24:14** David chooses the plague, in the hand of the LORD, because his mercy is great.
- **24:15** "Seventy thousand" is the number of the dead.
- **24:16** "The LORD changed his mind" is Hebrew *nacham*, the same verb as in 1 Samuel 15:11, 29, 35. "The angel of the LORD" is at the threshing floor of Araunah, the place of the later temple (2 Chronicles 3:1).
- **24:17** David says that he is the one who has sinned, and asks that the punishment fall on him and his father's house. The people are "sheep".
- **24:24** "I will not offer burnt offerings to the LORD my God that cost me nothing" is David's reason for buying the site. The price is fifty shekels of silver here and six hundred shekels of gold in 1 Chronicles 21:25.
- **24:25** "The LORD answered the plea for the land" is repeated from 21:14, which closes both the famine story and this one.
