# WABV: Ruth

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-QADOSH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end.*

## Ruth 1-2: Loss, Loyalty and the Barley Fields

### Chapter 1

**1** In the days when the judges ruled, there was a famine in the land, and a man from Bethlehem in Judah went to live in the country of Moab, he and his wife and his two sons.

**2** The man's name was Elimelech, his wife's name was Naomi, and the names of his two sons were Mahlon and Chilion. They were Ephrathites from Bethlehem in Judah. They came to the country of Moab and stayed there.

**3** Elimelech, Naomi's husband, died, and she was left with her two sons.

**4** They took Moabite women as wives, one named Orpah and the other named Ruth. They lived there for about ten years,

**5** and then both Mahlon and Chilion died, so the woman was left without her two sons and without her husband.

**6** Then she got up with her daughters-in-law to come back from the country of Moab, for in the country of Moab she had heard that the LORD had turned his attention to his people and given them food.

**7** So she left the place where she had been living, and her two daughters-in-law with her, and they set out on the road to go back to the land of Judah.

**8** Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, "Go back, each of you, to your mother's house. May the LORD show faithful love \[H-HESED\] to you, as you have shown to the dead and to me.

**9** May the LORD grant that each of you finds a home of security in the house of a husband." Then she kissed them, and they raised their voices and wept.

**10** They said to her, "No, we will go back with you to your people."

**11** But Naomi said, "Turn back, my daughters. Why would you go with me? Do I still have sons in my womb, who could become your husbands?

**12** Turn back, my daughters, go, for I am too old to have a husband. Even if I thought there was hope for me, even if I had a husband tonight and bore sons,

**13** would you wait for them until they were grown? Would you shut yourselves away, and not marry? No, my daughters. My bitterness is greater than yours, because the hand of the LORD has gone out against me."

**14** They raised their voices and wept again. Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth held on to her.

**15** Naomi said, "Look, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people and to her gods. Go back after your sister-in-law."

**16** But Ruth said:

"Do not urge me to leave you, or to turn back from following you. Wherever you go, I will go, and wherever you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my people, and your God will be my God.

**17** Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May the LORD do this to me, and more as well, if anything but death separates you and me."

**18** When Naomi saw that she was determined to go with her, she said no more.

**19** So the two of them went on until they came to Bethlehem. When they came to Bethlehem, the whole town was stirred because of them, and the women said, "Is this Naomi?"

**20** She said to them, "Do not call me Naomi. Call me Mara, for the Almighty has made my life very bitter.

**21** I went away full, but the LORD has brought me back empty. Why call me Naomi, when the LORD has testified against me, and the Almighty has brought trouble on me?"

**22** So Naomi came back, and with her Ruth the Moabite, her daughter-in-law, who returned from the country of Moab. They came to Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest.

### Chapter 2

**1** Now Naomi had a relative on her husband's side, a man of standing, from the clan of Elimelech, whose name was Boaz.

**2** Ruth the Moabite said to Naomi, "Let me go to the fields and glean among the ears of grain behind someone in whose eyes I may find favour." She said to her, "Go, my daughter."

**3** So she went and came and gleaned in the field behind the reapers. As it happened, she came to the part of the field that belonged to Boaz, who was of the clan of Elimelech.

**4** Just then Boaz came from Bethlehem and said to the reapers, "The LORD be with you." They answered, "The LORD bless you."

**5** Boaz said to his servant who was in charge of the reapers, "Whose young woman is this?"

**6** The servant in charge of the reapers answered, "She is the young Moabite woman who came back with Naomi from the country of Moab.

**7** She said, 'Please let me glean and gather among the sheaves behind the reapers.' She came, and she has been on her feet from morning until now, except for a short rest in the shelter."

**8** Then Boaz said to Ruth, "Listen, my daughter. Do not go to glean in another field, and do not leave this one, but stay here close to my young women.

**9** Keep your eyes on the field they are reaping, and follow behind them. I have ordered the young men not to touch you. When you are thirsty, go to the water jars and drink from what the young men have drawn."

**10** Then she fell on her face, bowing to the ground, and said to him, "Why have I found favour in your eyes, that you should notice me, when I am a foreigner?"

**11** Boaz answered her, "I have been told all that you have done for your mother-in-law since the death of your husband, how you left your father and mother and the land of your birth and came to a people you did not know before.

**12** May the LORD repay you for what you have done, and may you have a full reward from the LORD, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge."

**13** She said, "May I continue to find favour in your eyes, my lord, for you have comforted me and spoken kindly to your servant, though I am not even one of your servants."

**14** At mealtime Boaz said to her, "Come over here and eat some of the bread, and dip your piece in the vinegar." So she sat beside the reapers, and he passed her roasted grain. She ate until she was satisfied, and had some left over.

**15** When she got up to glean, Boaz ordered his young men, "Let her glean even among the sheaves, and do not shame her.

**16** Pull out some from the bundles for her, and leave it for her to glean, and do not rebuke her."

**17** So she gleaned in the field until evening. Then she beat out what she had gleaned, and it came to about an ephah of barley.

**18** She picked it up and went into the town. Her mother-in-law saw what she had gleaned. She also took out what she had left over after she was satisfied, and gave it to her.

**19** Her mother-in-law said to her, "Where did you glean today? Where did you work? Blessed be the one who took notice of you." So she told her mother-in-law whom she had worked with, and said, "The name of the man I worked with today is Boaz."

**20** Naomi said to her daughter-in-law, "May he be blessed by the LORD, whose faithful love has not abandoned the living or the dead." Naomi also said to her, "The man is a close relative of ours. He is one of our redeemers \[H-GAAL\]."

**21** Ruth the Moabite said, "He also said to me, 'Stay close to my young people until they have finished all my harvest.'"

**22** Naomi said to Ruth her daughter-in-law, "It is good, my daughter, that you go out with his young women, so that no one will harm you in another field."

**23** So she stayed close to the young women of Boaz, gleaning until the end of the barley and wheat harvests. And she lived with her mother-in-law.

## Ruth 3-4: The Threshing Floor and the Gate

### Chapter 3

**1** Naomi her mother-in-law said to her, "My daughter, should I not look for a home for you, so that it may go well with you?

**2** Now, is not Boaz our relative, with whose young women you were? Look, he is winnowing barley tonight at the threshing floor.

**3** So wash and anoint yourself, and put on your best clothes and go down to the threshing floor. But do not make yourself known to the man until he has finished eating and drinking.

**4** When he lies down, notice the place where he lies. Then go and uncover his feet and lie down, and he will tell you what to do."

**5** She said to her, "I will do everything you say."

**6** So she went down to the threshing floor and did just as her mother-in-law had told her.

**7** When Boaz had eaten and drunk and his heart was glad, he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain. Then she came quietly and uncovered his feet and lay down.

**8** In the middle of the night the man was startled and turned over, and there was a woman lying at his feet.

**9** He said, "Who are you?" She answered, "I am Ruth, your servant. Spread your wing over your servant, for you are a redeemer."

**10** He said, "May you be blessed by the LORD, my daughter. This last act of faithful love \[H-HESED\] of yours is better than the first, because you have not gone after young men, whether poor or rich.

**11** Now, my daughter, do not be afraid. I will do for you all that you ask, for everyone in the gate of my people knows that you are a woman of worth.

**12** It is true that I am a redeemer, but there is a redeemer closer than I.

**13** Stay here tonight, and in the morning, if he will redeem you, good, let him redeem you. But if he does not want to redeem you, then, as the LORD lives, I will redeem you. Lie down until morning."

**14** So she lay at his feet until morning, but got up before one person could recognise another. He said, "Let it not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor."

**15** He said, "Bring the cloak you are wearing and hold it out." She held it, and he measured out six measures of barley and put it on her. Then she went into the town.

**16** When she came to her mother-in-law, she said, "How did it go, my daughter?" Then she told her everything the man had done for her,

**17** and said, "He gave me these six measures of barley, for he said, 'You must not go back to your mother-in-law empty-handed.'"

**18** She said, "Wait, my daughter, until you know how the matter turns out, for the man will not rest until he has settled it today."

### Chapter 4

**1** Boaz went up to the gate and sat down there. Just then the redeemer of whom Boaz had spoken came by. Boaz said, "Come over here, friend, and sit down." So he came over and sat down.

**2** Boaz took ten of the elders of the town and said, "Sit down here." So they sat down.

**3** He said to the redeemer, "Naomi, who has come back from the country of Moab, is selling the piece of land that belonged to our relative Elimelech.

**4** I thought I should tell you, and say: Buy it in the presence of those sitting here and in the presence of the elders of my people. If you will redeem it, redeem it. But if you will not, tell me, so that I may know, for there is no one but you to redeem it, and I come after you." He said, "I will redeem it."

**5** Then Boaz said, "On the day you buy the field from Naomi, you also acquire Ruth the Moabite, the widow of the dead man, to keep the name of the dead man alive on his inheritance."

**6** The redeemer said, "I cannot redeem it for myself, or I would damage my own inheritance. Take my right of redemption yourself, for I cannot redeem it."

**7** Now this was the custom in Israel in earlier times concerning redeeming and exchanging, to make any matter binding: one man took off his sandal and gave it to the other. This was the way of confirming a matter in Israel.

**8** So when the redeemer said to Boaz, "Buy it for yourself," he took off his sandal.

**9** Then Boaz said to the elders and all the people, "You are witnesses today that I have bought from Naomi everything that belonged to Elimelech and everything that belonged to Chilion and Mahlon.

**10** I have also acquired Ruth the Moabite, the widow of Mahlon, to be my wife, to keep the name of the dead man alive on his inheritance, so that the name of the dead man is not cut off from among his kinsmen and from the gate of his town. You are witnesses today."

**11** All the people at the gate and the elders said, "We are witnesses. May the LORD make the woman who is coming into your house like Rachel and Leah, who together built up the house of Israel. May you prosper in Ephrathah and have a name in Bethlehem,

**12** and may your house be like the house of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah, through the children that the LORD will give you by this young woman."

**13** So Boaz took Ruth, and she became his wife. He went in to her, and the LORD enabled her to conceive, and she bore a son.

**14** The women said to Naomi, "Blessed be the LORD, who has not left you today without a redeemer. May his name be renowned in Israel.

**15** He will renew your life and sustain you in your old age, for your daughter-in-law who loves you, who is better to you than seven sons, has given birth to him."

**16** Naomi took the child and laid him on her lap, and she became his nurse.

**17** The women of the neighbourhood gave him a name, saying, "A son has been born to Naomi." They named him Obed. He was the father of Jesse, the father of David.

**18** These are the generations of Perez: Perez was the father of Hezron,

**19** Hezron was the father of Ram, and Ram was the father of Amminadab,

**20** Amminadab was the father of Nahshon, and Nahshon was the father of Salmon,

**21** Salmon was the father of Boaz, and Boaz was the father of Obed,

**22** Obed was the father of Jesse, and Jesse was the father of David.

*End of the book of Ruth. Review note: Ruth was drafted in WABV wording. The book is short, and the best-known passages, especially Ruth's words to Naomi (1:16-17), are very familiar, and some wording may sit close to existing translations. The footnotes record the points where the Hebrew is difficult (for example "uncover his feet" in 3:4 and 3:7, and the purchase of the field and of Ruth in 4:5 and 4:10), and where legal customs of the time are involved. The book needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **1:1** "In the days when the judges ruled" places the story in the time of the book of Judges. "Bethlehem" means "house of bread", so the famine in Bethlehem is a stark irony. Moab lies east of the Dead Sea. Moabites were barred from the assembly in Deuteronomy 23:3.
- **1:2** "Elimelech" means "my God is king" and "Naomi" means "pleasant". "Mahlon" and "Chilion" are names that suggest illness and wasting. "Ephrathites" are from the clan of Ephrath, the area around Bethlehem.
- **1:4** Marrying Moabite women is not forbidden in the text of the book. The ten years are a round number.
- **1:6** "Turned his attention to his people" is Hebrew *paqad*, to visit or take notice, which can mean to help.
- **1:8** "Your mother's house" is unusual. A woman usually returned to her father's house. "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. Naomi prays that the LORD will show to the young women the loyalty they have shown to the dead and to her.
- **1:11** Naomi's question about sons in her womb recalls the custom of the brother of a dead man marrying his widow (Deuteronomy 25:5-10; Genesis 38).
- **1:15** "Her gods" are the gods of Moab, such as Chemosh (see Judges 11:24).
- **1:16** Ruth's words are poetry in the Hebrew. They are a vow of loyalty to Naomi, to her people and to her God.
- **1:17** "May the LORD do this to me, and more as well" is an oath formula, found also in 1 Samuel 3:17 and 2 Samuel 3:9. The wording "if anything but death separates you and me" is a firm promise.
- **1:20** "Mara" means "bitter". "The Almighty" is Hebrew *Shaddai*. Naomi blames the LORD for her loss.
- **1:21** "Full" and "empty" is a contrast with the famine and the loss of her husband and sons. "Testified against me" is a legal figure.
- **1:22** "The beginning of the barley harvest" is in spring, around April or May. The time is a first sign of hope.
- **2:1** "A man of standing" is Hebrew *gibbor chayil*, a phrase that means strong, wealthy and respected. The same Hebrew phrase is used of Ruth in 3:11 ("woman of worth").
- **2:2** To glean is to gather what the reapers leave. The poor, the foreigner, the orphan and the widow have the right to do this (Leviticus 19:9-10; 23:22; Deuteronomy 24:19-21).
- **2:3** "As it happened" is Hebrew *miqreha*, "her chance". The text implies that this was not chance but the LORD's care.
- **2:9** "Not to touch you" means to protect her from harassment in the field.
- **2:10** "A foreigner" is Hebrew *nokhriyah*, a woman from another people.
- **2:12** "Under whose wings you have come to take refuge" is a figure for the LORD's protection. In 3:9 Ruth asks Boaz to "spread his wing" over her.
- **2:14** "The vinegar" is sour wine or wine vinegar, used as a dip.
- **2:17** An ephah is about twenty-two litres, so Ruth gleans a large amount, about thirteen kilograms of barley.
- **2:20** "Redeemer" is Hebrew *go'el*. See entry H-GAAL. The redeemer is a close kinsman who acts to protect a family's land, person or name (Leviticus 25:25-55). "The living or the dead" says that the LORD's faithful love has shown itself in both Naomi's and the dead husband's family.
- **2:23** The barley and wheat harvests last about seven weeks, from spring to the Festival of Weeks.
- **3:1** "A home" is Hebrew *manoach*, a place of rest, as in 1:9.
- **3:4** "Uncover his feet" and "lie down" (verses 4, 7 and 8) are discussed by readers. The words may refer to uncovering his feet and lying at them as a sign of a request for protection and marriage. Some have seen a sexual meaning. The text does not say that anything happened beyond this, and Boaz protects her reputation (verse 14).
- **3:9** "Spread your wing" is Hebrew *kanaf*, which means both "wing" and "the edge of a garment". It is a figure for marriage (see Ezekiel 16:8). It echoes Boaz's blessing in 2:12, and Ruth asks him to be the answer to his own prayer.
- **3:10** "This last act of faithful love is better than the first" says that Ruth's earlier loyalty to Naomi was great, and that this act, in seeking the family's redeemer and not a young man, is greater.
- **3:11** "A woman of worth" is Hebrew *eshet chayil*, the same phrase as in Proverbs 31:10.
- **3:12** A nearer redeemer exists, and Boaz is bound by the order of kinship.
- **3:15** "Six measures" does not name the measure. It may be six *se'ah*, or six smaller units. The gift is large.
- **4:1** The gate is the place of the town court. Ten elders witness the matter, as a full legal panel.
- **4:3** Naomi "is selling" the land of Elimelech. The Hebrew can mean that she has sold it, or is about to. A redeemer may buy back family land that has been sold (Leviticus 25:25).
- **4:5** The Hebrew text has a note on this verse. The written text reads "I acquire Ruth" and the reading tradition reads "you acquire Ruth". The WABV text follows "you acquire", and records the other reading.
- **4:6** "I would damage my own inheritance" means that the land and Ruth's son would divide the redeemer's estate. His reason is not a lack of kindness but the loss to his own heirs.
- **4:7** "A sandal" confirmed a transaction. This differs from the sandal removed in Deuteronomy 25:9-10, which is a shaming of a brother who refuses to marry the widow.
- **4:10** "To keep the name of the dead man alive" is the purpose of the marriage. The text does not call it a levirate marriage, since Boaz is not Mahlon's brother, but it follows the same aim.
- **4:11** "Rachel and Leah" are the wives of Jacob, mothers of the tribes of Israel. "Ephrathah" is the area of Bethlehem.
- **4:12** Perez was the son of Judah and Tamar (Genesis 38). Tamar was also a widow who secured a child for her dead husband's line.
- **4:13** "The LORD enabled her to conceive" is one of only two places in the book where the LORD is the direct agent in an event of the plot. The other is 1:6.
- **4:15** "Better to you than seven sons" says Ruth's value to Naomi is complete, since seven sons is a number of fullness.
- **4:16** "She became his nurse" says Naomi cares for the child. The Hebrew may mean she became his foster-mother.
- **4:17** The women name the child, "Obed", which means "one who serves". The statement that Obed is the father of Jesse and grandfather of David places the story in the line of the king.
- **4:18** The genealogy from Perez to David has ten generations. It is also given in 1 Chronicles 2:5-15, and Matthew 1:3-6 uses it.
