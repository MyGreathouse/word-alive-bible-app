# WABV: Daniel

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-RUACH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Daniel is in two languages. The Hebrew text has 1:1 to 2:4a and 8:1 to 12:13, and the Aramaic text has 2:4b to 7:28. The WABV text does not mark the change in the verses, and the footnotes at 2:4 and 8:1 record it. The WABV text follows the Hebrew and Aramaic text and the Protestant order of the books, which places Daniel after Ezekiel. In the Hebrew Bible it stands among the Writings. The Greek text has long additions that are not in the Hebrew and Aramaic text (the Prayer of Azariah and the Song of the Three Young Men after 3:23, Susanna, and Bel and the Dragon), and they are not part of this translation. The Greek and the Latin also differ from the Hebrew and Aramaic in many smaller places, and the footnotes record the matter where it affects the draft. The chapter and verse numbering is the English numbering. In the Aramaic text, English 4:1-3 is Aramaic 3:31-33, so English 4:4 is Aramaic 4:1, and English 5:31 is Aramaic 6:1, so English 6:1-28 is Aramaic 6:2-29. The book falls into four parts in the WABV text: Daniel and his friends at the court of Babylon (chapters 1 to 3), the dreams and the writing on the wall and the lions' den (4 to 6), the visions of the beasts and the seventy sevens (7 to 9) and the last vision (10 to 12). The headings of the parts are not in the Hebrew. The WABV text does not decide on questions of the date and the growth of the book, and the footnotes record only the language. The names of God are given as in the earlier books: "the LORD" for YHWH, "Lord" for Adonai, and "the God of heaven" and "the Most High" for the Aramaic and Hebrew titles. The Aramaic and Hebrew phrase for "one like a son of man" (7:13) is given as written, and the footnote records the matter, together with the Hebrew phrase ben adam in Ezekiel and in 8:17.*

## Part One: Chapters 1 to 3, Daniel and his friends at court

### Chapter 1

**1** In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon marched against Jerusalem and laid siege to it.

**2** The Lord handed over to him Jehoiakim king of Judah, together with some of the vessels of the house of God. He carried them off to the land of Shinar, to the temple of his god, and placed the vessels in the treasury of his god.

**3** The king told Ashpenaz, the chief of his eunuchs, to bring in some of the Israelites, from the royal line and from the nobility,

**4** young men with no physical defect, handsome, trained in every branch of wisdom, rich in knowledge, quick to understand, and fit to serve in the king's palace. He was to teach them the writings and the language of the Chaldeans.

**5** The king appointed for them a daily share of the royal food and of the wine he drank. They were to be trained for three years, and after that they were to enter the king's service.

**6** Among them were Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah, from the tribe of Judah.

**7** The chief of the eunuchs gave them new names. He called Daniel Belteshazzar, Hananiah Shadrach, Mishael Meshach and Azariah Abednego.

**8** But Daniel made up his mind not to defile himself with the royal food or with the wine the king drank, and he asked the chief of the eunuchs for permission not to defile himself.

**9** God had given Daniel favour and compassion \[H-HESED\] in the eyes of the chief of the eunuchs,

**10** but the chief of the eunuchs said to Daniel, "I am afraid of my lord the king, who has fixed your food and drink. Why should he see you looking worse than the young men of your own age? You would put my head in danger with the king."

**11** Then Daniel said to the guard whom the chief of the eunuchs had put over Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah,

**12** "Please test your servants for ten days. Let us be given vegetables to eat and water to drink.

**13** Then compare our appearance with that of the young men who eat the royal food, and deal with your servants according to what you see."

**14** He agreed to this and tested them for ten days.

**15** At the end of the ten days they looked healthier and better fed than all the young men who ate the royal food.

**16** So the guard kept taking away their food and the wine they were to drink, and gave them vegetables.

**17** God gave these four young men knowledge and skill in every kind of writing and wisdom, and Daniel could understand every vision and dream.

**18** When the time that the king had set was over, the chief of the eunuchs brought them before Nebuchadnezzar.

**19** The king spoke with them, and among them all he found none like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah. So they entered the king's service.

**20** In every matter that called for wisdom and understanding, when the king questioned them, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters in his whole kingdom.

**21** And Daniel remained there until the first year of King Cyrus.

### Chapter 2

**1** In the second year of his reign Nebuchadnezzar had dreams, and his spirit \[H-RUACH\] was troubled, and he could not sleep.

**2** The king ordered that the magicians, the enchanters, the sorcerers and the Chaldeans be summoned to tell him what he had dreamed. They came and stood before the king.

**3** The king said to them, "I have had a dream, and my spirit \[H-RUACH\] is troubled until I know what the dream was."

**4** The Chaldeans said to the king in Aramaic, "May the king live for ever. Tell your servants the dream, and we will give the interpretation."

**5** The king answered the Chaldeans, "My word is settled: if you do not make known to me the dream and its interpretation, you will be cut in pieces and your houses made a rubbish heap.

**6** But if you tell me the dream and its interpretation, you will receive from me gifts, rewards and great honour. So tell me the dream and its interpretation."

**7** They answered a second time, "Let the king tell his servants the dream, and we will give its interpretation."

**8** The king answered, "I know for certain that you are playing for time, because you see that my word is settled.

**9** If you do not make the dream known to me, there is only one verdict for you. You have agreed together to speak false and misleading words to me until the situation changes. So tell me the dream, and I will know that you can also give me its interpretation."

**10** The Chaldeans answered the king, "There is no one on earth who can do what the king demands. No king, however great or powerful, has ever asked such a thing of any magician, enchanter or Chaldean.

**11** What the king asks is too hard, and no one can show it to the king except the gods, whose home is not among mortals."

**12** At this the king was angry and furious, and he gave orders to destroy all the wise men of Babylon.

**13** The decree went out that the wise men were to be killed, and they looked for Daniel and his friends to kill them.

**14** Then Daniel answered Arioch, the captain of the king's guard, who had gone out to kill the wise men of Babylon, with tact and good sense.

**15** He asked Arioch, the king's officer, "Why is the king's decree so harsh?" Arioch explained the matter to Daniel.

**16** Daniel went in and asked the king to give him time, so that he could tell the king the interpretation.

**17** Then Daniel went home and told the matter to his friends Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah,

**18** and asked them to seek mercy from the God of heaven about this mystery, so that Daniel and his friends would not be destroyed with the rest of the wise men of Babylon.

**19** Then the mystery was revealed to Daniel in a vision in the night, and Daniel blessed the God of heaven.

**20** Daniel said: "Blessed be the name of God for ever and ever, for wisdom and power are his.

**21** He changes times and seasons. He removes kings and sets up kings. He gives wisdom to the wise and knowledge to those who understand.

**22** He uncovers deep and hidden things. He knows what is in the darkness, and light lives with him.

**23** To you, God of my fathers, I give thanks and praise, for you have given me wisdom and strength, and now you have shown me what we asked of you. You have made known to us the king's matter."

**24** Then Daniel went in to Arioch, whom the king had appointed to destroy the wise men of Babylon, and said to him, "Do not destroy the wise men of Babylon. Take me before the king, and I will give the king the interpretation."

**25** Arioch quickly brought Daniel before the king and said to him, "I have found among the exiles of Judah a man who can tell the king the interpretation."

**26** The king asked Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, "Are you able to tell me the dream I saw, and its interpretation?"

**27** Daniel answered the king, "The mystery the king is asking about cannot be shown to the king by wise men, enchanters, magicians or diviners,

**28** but there is a God in heaven who reveals mysteries, and he has made known to King Nebuchadnezzar what will happen in the days to come. This was your dream, and the visions in your head as you lay on your bed:

**29** As you lay on your bed, O king, your thoughts turned to what would come after this, and the one who reveals mysteries has shown you what will happen.

**30** As for me, this mystery has been revealed to me, not because I have more wisdom than anyone alive, but so that the interpretation may be made known to the king and you may understand the thoughts of your heart.

**31** You looked, O king, and there was a great statue. This statue was huge, and its brightness was extraordinary. It stood before you, and it was terrifying to look at.

**32** The head of the statue was of fine gold, its chest and arms of silver, its belly and thighs of bronze,

**33** its legs of iron, and its feet partly of iron and partly of clay.

**34** While you were watching, a stone was cut out, but not by hands, and it struck the statue on its feet of iron and clay and crushed them.

**35** Then the iron, the clay, the bronze, the silver and the gold were crushed together and became like the chaff on a summer threshing floor. The wind carried them away, and no trace of them was found. But the stone that struck the statue became a great mountain and filled the whole earth.

**36** This was the dream. Now we will tell the king its interpretation.

**37** You, O king, are the king of kings, to whom the God of heaven has given the kingdom, the power, the strength and the glory,

**38** and into whose hand he has placed human beings, wherever they live, and the animals of the field and the birds of the sky, so that you rule over them all. You are the head of gold.

**39** After you another kingdom will rise, lower than yours, and then a third kingdom, of bronze, which will rule over the whole earth.

**40** And there will be a fourth kingdom, as strong as iron, because iron breaks and shatters everything. Like iron that smashes, it will break and crush all the others.

**41** You saw that the feet and toes were partly potter's clay and partly iron. That means it will be a divided kingdom, though some of the strength of iron will be in it, as you saw iron mixed with wet clay.

**42** The toes of the feet were partly iron and partly clay, so the kingdom will be partly strong and partly brittle.

**43** You saw iron mixed with wet clay. They will mix themselves through marriage, but they will not hold together, just as iron does not mix with clay.

**44** In the days of those kings the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that will never be destroyed, and its rule will not be left to another people. It will crush all these kingdoms and bring them to an end, and it will stand for ever.

**45** That is the meaning of the stone you saw cut from the mountain, but not by hands, which crushed the iron, the bronze, the clay, the silver and the gold. The great God has made known to the king what will happen after this. The dream is certain, and its interpretation is sure."

**46** Then King Nebuchadnezzar fell on his face and bowed down to Daniel, and ordered that an offering and incense be presented to him.

**47** The king said to Daniel, "Truly your God is the God of gods and the Lord of kings and the revealer of mysteries, since you have been able to reveal this mystery."

**48** Then the king raised Daniel to high position and gave him many great gifts. He made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon and chief of all the wise men of Babylon.

**49** At Daniel's request the king put Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego in charge of the province of Babylon. But Daniel stayed at the king's court.

### Chapter 3

**1** King Nebuchadnezzar made a gold statue sixty cubits high and six cubits wide, and set it up on the plain of Dura in the province of Babylon.

**2** He sent word to assemble the satraps, the prefects, the governors, the advisers, the treasurers, the judges, the magistrates and all the officials of the provinces to attend the dedication of the statue he had set up.

**3** So the satraps, prefects, governors, advisers, treasurers, judges, magistrates and all the officials of the provinces assembled for the dedication of the statue that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up, and they stood before it.

**4** A herald cried out loudly, "To you it is commanded, peoples, nations and languages:

**5** the moment you hear the sound of the horn, flute, lyre, trigon, harp, pipes and every kind of instrument, you must fall down and worship the gold statue that King Nebuchadnezzar has set up.

**6** Whoever does not fall down and worship will at once be thrown into a blazing furnace of fire."

**7** So when all the peoples heard the sound of the horn, flute, lyre, trigon, harp and every kind of instrument, all the peoples, nations and languages fell down and worshipped the gold statue that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

**8** At that time some Chaldeans came forward and denounced the Jews.

**9** They said to King Nebuchadnezzar, "May the king live for ever.

**10** You, O king, issued a decree that everyone who hears the sound of the horn, flute, lyre, trigon, harp, pipes and every kind of instrument must fall down and worship the gold statue,

**11** and that whoever does not fall down and worship will be thrown into a blazing furnace of fire.

**12** There are some Jews whom you put in charge of the province of Babylon, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego. These men, O king, have paid no attention to you. They do not serve your gods, and they do not worship the gold statue you have set up."

**13** Nebuchadnezzar, in a rage of fury, ordered Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego to be brought. So these men were brought before the king.

**14** Nebuchadnezzar said to them, "Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, is it true that you do not serve my gods and do not worship the gold statue I have set up?

**15** Now, if you are ready, when you hear the sound of the horn, flute, lyre, trigon, harp, pipes and every kind of instrument, to fall down and worship the statue I have made, good. But if you do not worship, you will at once be thrown into a blazing furnace of fire. And what god is there who can rescue you from my hands?"

**16** Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego answered the king, "Nebuchadnezzar, we have no need to give you an answer to this.

**17** If it must be, our God whom we serve is able to rescue us from the blazing furnace of fire, and he will rescue us from your hand, O king.

**18** But even if he does not, let it be known to you, O king, that we will not serve your gods or worship the gold statue you have set up."

**19** Then Nebuchadnezzar was full of fury, and the look on his face changed toward Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego. He ordered that the furnace be heated seven times hotter than usual,

**20** and he ordered some of the strongest men in his army to bind Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego and throw them into the blazing furnace of fire.

**21** So these men were bound in their trousers, their tunics, their head coverings and their other clothes, and were thrown into the blazing furnace of fire.

**22** Because the king's command was urgent and the furnace was extremely hot, the flames of the fire killed the men who carried up Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego.

**23** And these three men, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, fell, bound, into the blazing furnace of fire.

**24** Then King Nebuchadnezzar was astounded and got up quickly. He said to his advisers, "Did we not throw three men, bound, into the fire?" They answered the king, "Certainly, O king."

**25** He said, "But I see four men, unbound, walking about in the fire, and they are not harmed. And the fourth looks like a son of the gods."

**26** Then Nebuchadnezzar went near to the opening of the blazing furnace of fire and called, "Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, servants of the Most High God, come out and come here." So Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego came out of the fire.

**27** The satraps, prefects, governors and the king's advisers gathered round and saw that the fire had had no power over the bodies of these men. The hair of their heads was not singed, their trousers were not affected, and the smell of fire had not touched them.

**28** Nebuchadnezzar said, "Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, who sent his angel and rescued his servants who trusted in him. They disobeyed the king's command and gave up their bodies rather than serve or worship any god except their own God.

**29** So I now issue a decree: anyone of any people, nation or language who speaks against the God of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego will be cut in pieces and their houses made a rubbish heap, because there is no other god who can rescue in this way."

**30** Then the king promoted Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego in the province of Babylon.

*Review note: Daniel 1 to 3 were drafted in WABV wording. They are the taking of Daniel and his three friends to Babylon and their training (1:1-21), Nebuchadnezzar's dream of the statue and Daniel's interpretation (2:1-49), and the golden statue and the fiery furnace (3:1-30). The Aramaic text begins in 2:4, where the Chaldeans speak, and it continues to the end of chapter 7, and the footnote at 2:4 records the matter. Chapters 1 to 3 are prose, with the prayer of Daniel in 2:20-23 set in verse lines. The dates are the third year of Jehoiakim (1:1, 605 before Christ) and the second year of Nebuchadnezzar (2:1, 603 before Christ), and the footnotes record that these are not the same as the count in Jeremiah 25:1 and 46:2, and that the three years of training in 1:5 and the second year in 2:1 do not fit together without comment. In 1:9 "favour and compassion" translates chesed ve-rachamim and is tagged H-HESED. In 2:1 and 2:3 "spirit" translates the Aramaic ruach and is tagged H-RUACH. There are no departures from the Hebrew or the Aramaic in chapters 1 to 3. The Greek additions after 3:23, the Prayer of Azariah and the Song of the Three Young Men, are not part of this translation. The words of 2:5 ("you will be cut in pieces and your houses made a rubbish heap") and of 3:29 are the same in the Aramaic and are given the same wording. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The section needs a check against the Hebrew and the Aramaic by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Two: Chapters 4 to 6, the tree, the writing on the wall, and the lions' den

### Chapter 4

**1** King Nebuchadnezzar, to all the peoples, nations and languages that live in all the earth: May your peace \[H-SHALOM\] be great.

**2** It has seemed good to me to make known the signs and wonders that the Most High God has done for me.

**3** How great are his signs, and how mighty his wonders. His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and his rule lasts from generation to generation.

**4** I, Nebuchadnezzar, was at ease in my house and flourishing in my palace.

**5** I saw a dream that frightened me. As I lay on my bed, the thoughts and the visions in my head terrified me.

**6** So I gave an order that all the wise men of Babylon be brought before me, to make known to me the interpretation of the dream.

**7** Then the magicians, the enchanters, the Chaldeans and the diviners came in, and I told them the dream, but they could not make known its interpretation to me.

**8** At last Daniel came in before me, whose name is Belteshazzar after the name of my god, and in whom is the spirit \[H-RUACH\] of the holy \[H-QADOSH\] gods. I told him the dream:

**9** "Belteshazzar, chief of the magicians, I know that the spirit \[H-RUACH\] of the holy \[H-QADOSH\] gods is in you and that no mystery is too hard for you. Tell me the visions of my dream that I saw, and its interpretation.

**10** These were the visions in my head as I lay on my bed. I was looking, and there was a tree in the middle of the earth, and it was very tall.

**11** The tree grew large and strong, and its top reached to heaven, and it could be seen to the ends of the whole earth.

**12** Its leaves were beautiful and its fruit abundant, and there was food in it for everyone. Under it the animals of the field found shade, and in its branches the birds of the sky lived, and from it every creature was fed.

**13** I was looking in the visions in my head as I lay on my bed, and there was a watcher, a holy \[H-QADOSH\] one, coming down from heaven.

**14** He called out loudly and said: Cut down the tree and chop off its branches. Strip off its leaves and scatter its fruit. Let the animals flee from under it and the birds from its branches.

**15** But leave the stump of its roots in the ground, bound with a band of iron and bronze, in the new grass of the field. Let him be wet with the dew of heaven, and let his share be with the animals in the grass of the earth.

**16** Let his mind be changed from a human mind, and let the mind of an animal be given to him, and let seven times pass over him.

**17** The sentence is by the decree of the watchers, the demand by the word of the holy \[H-QADOSH\] ones, so that the living may know that the Most High rules the kingdom of mankind, gives it to whom he wishes, and sets over it the lowliest of people.

**18** This is the dream that I, King Nebuchadnezzar, saw. And you, Belteshazzar, tell me its interpretation, for none of the wise men of my kingdom can make known to me the interpretation, but you can, because the spirit \[H-RUACH\] of the holy \[H-QADOSH\] gods is in you."

**19** Then Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, was dismayed for a moment, and his thoughts alarmed him. The king said, "Belteshazzar, do not let the dream or its interpretation alarm you." Belteshazzar answered, "My lord, may the dream be for those who hate you and its interpretation for your enemies.

**20** The tree that you saw, which grew large and strong, whose top reached to heaven and which could be seen by the whole earth,

**21** whose leaves were beautiful and its fruit abundant, with food in it for everyone, under which the animals of the field lived and in whose branches the birds of the sky dwelt,

**22** it is you, O king, who have grown large and strong. Your greatness has grown and reaches to heaven, and your rule to the ends of the earth.

**23** And the king saw a watcher, a holy \[H-QADOSH\] one, coming down from heaven and saying, Cut down the tree and destroy it, but leave the stump of its roots in the ground, bound with a band of iron and bronze, in the new grass of the field. Let him be wet with the dew of heaven, and let his share be with the animals of the field, until seven times pass over him.

**24** This is the interpretation, O king, and this is the decree of the Most High that has come on my lord the king:

**25** You will be driven away from human beings, and your home will be with the animals of the field. You will be fed grass like oxen, and you will be wet with the dew of heaven, and seven times will pass over you, until you know that the Most High rules the kingdom of mankind and gives it to whom he wishes.

**26** And as it was said to leave the stump of the tree's roots, your kingdom will be kept for you from the time you know that Heaven rules.

**27** Therefore, O king, may my advice be acceptable to you. Break off your sins by righteousness \[H-TSEDAQAH\], and your iniquities by showing mercy to the poor, in case there may be a lengthening of your prosperity."

**28** All this came on King Nebuchadnezzar.

**29** At the end of twelve months he was walking on the roof of the royal palace of Babylon,

**30** and the king said, "Is this not great Babylon, which I have built by my mighty power as a royal residence, for the glory of my majesty?"

**31** While the words were still in the king's mouth, a voice fell from heaven: "King Nebuchadnezzar, to you it is declared: the kingdom has departed from you,

**32** and you will be driven away from human beings, and your home will be with the animals of the field. You will be fed grass like oxen, and seven times will pass over you, until you know that the Most High rules the kingdom of mankind and gives it to whom he wishes."

**33** At that moment the word was fulfilled against Nebuchadnezzar. He was driven away from human beings and ate grass like oxen, and his body was wet with the dew of heaven, until his hair grew like eagles' feathers and his nails like birds' claws.

**34** At the end of the days I, Nebuchadnezzar, lifted my eyes to heaven, and my understanding returned \[H-SHUV\] to me, and I blessed the Most High and praised and honoured him who lives for ever, for his rule is an everlasting rule, and his kingdom lasts from generation to generation.

**35** All the inhabitants of the earth are counted as nothing, and he does as he wills with the host of heaven and the inhabitants of the earth. There is no one who can strike his hand or say to him, "What are you doing?"

**36** At that time my understanding returned \[H-SHUV\] to me, and my majesty and splendour returned \[H-SHUV\] to me for the glory of my kingdom. My counsellors and my nobles sought me out, and I was established in my kingdom, and even greater majesty was added to me.

**37** Now I, Nebuchadnezzar, praise and exalt and honour the King of heaven, for all his works are true and his ways are just, and he is able to humble those who walk in pride.

### Chapter 5

**1** King Belshazzar gave a great feast for a thousand of his nobles, and he drank wine in front of the thousand.

**2** Under the influence of the wine, Belshazzar gave orders to bring in the gold and silver vessels that his father Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the temple in Jerusalem, so that the king and his nobles, his wives and his concubines might drink from them.

**3** So they brought in the gold vessels that had been taken from the temple, the house of God in Jerusalem, and the king and his nobles, his wives and his concubines drank from them.

**4** They drank wine and praised the gods of gold and silver, of bronze, iron, wood and stone.

**5** At that moment the fingers of a human hand appeared and wrote on the plaster of the wall of the king's palace, opposite the lampstand, and the king saw the back of the hand as it wrote.

**6** Then the king's face turned pale, and his thoughts alarmed him. His hips gave way, and his knees knocked together.

**7** The king called loudly for the enchanters, the Chaldeans and the diviners to be brought in. The king said to the wise men of Babylon, "Whoever reads this writing and tells me its interpretation will be clothed in purple, with a chain of gold around his neck, and will rule as third in the kingdom."

**8** Then all the king's wise men came in, but they could not read the writing or make known its interpretation to the king.

**9** Then King Belshazzar was greatly alarmed, and his face turned pale, and his nobles were bewildered.

**10** The queen, because of the words of the king and his nobles, came into the banquet hall. The queen said, "O king, live for ever. Do not let your thoughts alarm you or your face turn pale.

**11** There is a man in your kingdom in whom is the spirit \[H-RUACH\] of the holy \[H-QADOSH\] gods. In the days of your father, light, understanding and wisdom like the wisdom of the gods were found in him, and King Nebuchadnezzar your father, your father the king, made him chief of the magicians, enchanters, Chaldeans and diviners,

**12** because an extraordinary spirit \[H-RUACH\], knowledge and understanding, the interpreting of dreams, the solving of riddles and the untying of knots were found in this Daniel, whom the king named Belteshazzar. Now let Daniel be called, and he will give the interpretation."

**13** Then Daniel was brought in before the king. The king said to Daniel, "Are you that Daniel, one of the exiles of Judah whom my father the king brought from Judah?

**14** I have heard of you that the spirit \[H-RUACH\] of the gods is in you, and that light, understanding and extraordinary wisdom were found in you.

**15** Now the wise men and the enchanters were brought before me to read this writing and make known its interpretation to me, but they could not show the interpretation of the matter.

**16** But I have heard that you can give interpretations and untie knots. Now if you can read the writing and make known its interpretation to me, you will be clothed in purple, with a chain of gold around your neck, and will rule as third in the kingdom."

**17** Then Daniel answered the king, "Keep your gifts for yourself, and give your rewards to someone else. Yet I will read the writing to the king and make known the interpretation to him.

**18** O king, the Most High God gave Nebuchadnezzar your father the kingdom, greatness, glory and majesty.

**19** Because of the greatness that he gave him, all peoples, nations and languages trembled and feared before him. Whom he wished he killed, and whom he wished he kept alive. Whom he wished he raised up, and whom he wished he brought low.

**20** But when his heart was lifted up and his spirit \[H-RUACH\] became hard so that he acted proudly, he was removed from the throne of his kingdom, and his glory was taken from him.

**21** He was driven away from human beings, and his mind was made like that of an animal, and his home was with the wild donkeys. He was fed grass like oxen, and his body was wet with the dew of heaven, until he knew that the Most High God rules the kingdom of mankind and sets over it whom he will.

**3** Then a mighty king will arise, who will rule with great dominion and do as he pleases.

**4** And as soon as he has arisen, his kingdom will be broken and divided toward the four winds \[H-RUACH\] of heaven, but not to his descendants, and not with the dominion that he ruled, for his kingdom will be uprooted and given to others besides these.

**5** Then the king of the south will grow strong, but one of his princes will grow stronger than he and rule, and his rule will be a great rule.

**6** After some years they will make an alliance, and the daughter of the king of the south will come to the king of the north to make an agreement. But she will not keep her power, and he and his offspring will not stand. She will be given up, she and those who brought her, and her child, and the one who supported her in those times.

**7** And from a branch from her roots one will arise in his place. He will come against the army and enter the stronghold of the king of the north, and he will deal with them and prevail.

**8** He will also carry off to Egypt their gods with their metal images and their precious vessels of silver and gold, and for some years he will refrain from attacking the king of the north.

**9** Then the latter will come into the realm of the king of the south but will go back \[H-SHUV\] to his own land.

**10** His sons will wage war and assemble a multitude of great forces, which will keep coming and overflow and pass through, and will again wage war as far as his fortress.

**11** Then the king of the south, enraged, will come out and fight against the king of the north. And he will raise a great multitude, but that multitude will be given into his hand.

**12** And when the multitude is carried off, his heart will be lifted up, and he will cast down tens of thousands, but he will not prevail.

**13** For the king of the north will again raise a multitude, greater than the first. And after some years he will come on with a great army and abundant supplies.

**14** In those times many will rise against the king of the south, and violent people among your own people will lift themselves up to fulfil the vision, but they will fail.

**15** Then the king of the north will come and throw up siege mounds and take a well-fortified city. And the forces of the south will not stand, or even his best troops, for there will be no strength to stand.

**16** But the one who comes against him will do as he pleases, and no one will stand before him. And he will stand in the beautiful land, with destruction in his hand.

**17** He will set his face to come with the strength of his whole kingdom, and he will make an agreement with him. He will give him a woman in marriage in order to destroy the kingdom, but it will not stand or serve him.

**18** Then he will turn his face to the coastlands and capture many of them, but a commander will put an end to his insolence. Indeed, he will turn his insolence back \[H-SHUV\] on him.

**19** Then he will turn his face back \[H-SHUV\] toward the fortresses of his own land, but he will stumble and fall, and will not be found.

**20** Then one will arise in his place who will send an exactor of tribute through the glory of the kingdom. But within a few days he will be broken, though not in anger or in battle.

**21** In his place a contemptible person will arise, on whom the royal majesty has not been conferred. He will come in without warning and seize the kingdom by intrigue.

**22** Armies will be utterly swept away before him and broken, and also the prince of the covenant \[H-BERIT\].

**23** And from the time that an alliance is made with him he will act deceitfully, and he will become strong with a small people.

**24** Without warning he will come into the richest parts of the province, and he will do what neither his fathers nor his fathers' fathers did, scattering plunder, spoil and goods among them. He will devise plans against strongholds, but only for a time.

**25** And he will stir up his power and his heart against the king of the south with a great army. And the king of the south will wage war with an exceedingly great and mighty army, but he will not stand, for plots will be devised against him.

**26** Even those who eat his food will break him. His army will be swept away, and many will fall down slain.

**27** And as for the two kings, their hearts will be bent on evil. They will speak lies at one table, but to no avail, for the end is still to come at the appointed time.

**28** And he will go back \[H-SHUV\] to his land with great wealth, but his heart will be set against the holy \[H-QADOSH\] covenant \[H-BERIT\]. And he will act and go back \[H-SHUV\] to his own land.

**29** At the appointed time he will go back \[H-SHUV\] and come into the south, but it will not be this time as it was before.

**30** For ships of Kittim will come against him, and he will lose heart. He will turn back \[H-SHUV\] and rage against the holy \[H-QADOSH\] covenant \[H-BERIT\] and act. He will go back \[H-SHUV\] and give heed to those who forsake the holy \[H-QADOSH\] covenant \[H-BERIT\].

**31** Forces from him will appear and profane the sanctuary \[H-QADOSH\], the fortress, and they will take away the regular offering. And they will set up the abomination that makes desolate.

**32** He will seduce with flattery those who violate the covenant \[H-BERIT\], but the people who know their God will stand firm and take action.

**33** And the wise among the people will make many understand, though for some days they will stumble by sword and flame, by captivity and plunder.

**34** When they stumble, they will receive a little help, and many will join themselves to them with flattery.

**35** And some of the wise will stumble, so that they may be refined, purified and made white, until the time of the end, for it still awaits the appointed time.

**36** Then the king will do as he pleases. He will exalt himself and magnify himself above every god, and he will speak astonishing things against the God of gods. He will prosper until the indignation is accomplished, for what is decreed will be done.

**37** He will pay no attention to the gods of his fathers, or to the one beloved of women, or to any other god, for he will magnify himself above all.

**38** He will honour the god of fortresses instead of these. A god whom his fathers did not know he will honour with gold and silver, with precious stones and costly gifts.

**39** He will deal with the strongest fortresses with the help of a foreign god. Those who acknowledge him he will load with honour. He will make them rulers over many and divide the land for a price.

**40** At the time of the end, the king of the south will attack him, and the king of the north will rush on him like a whirlwind, with chariots and horsemen and many ships. And he will come into the countries and overflow and pass through.

**41** He will come into the beautiful land, and tens of thousands will fall, but these will be delivered out of his hand: Edom and Moab and the main part of the Ammonites.

**42** He will stretch out his hand against the countries, and the land of Egypt will not escape.

**43** He will become ruler of the treasures of gold and silver, and all the precious things of Egypt, and the Libyans and the Cushites will follow in his train.

**44** But news from the east and the north will alarm him, and he will go out with great fury to destroy and devote many to destruction \[H-CHEREM\].

**45** And he will pitch his palatial tents between the sea and the beautiful holy \[H-QADOSH\] mountain. Yet he will come to his end, with none to help him.

### Chapter 12

**1** At that time Michael will arise, the great prince who stands guard over your people. There will be a time of distress such as has never been since there was a nation until that time. But at that time your people will be rescued, everyone who is found written in the book.

**2** Many of those who sleep in the dust of the earth will wake, some to lasting life, and some to shame and lasting contempt.

**3** Those who are wise will shine like the brightness of the sky, and those who lead many to righteousness \[H-TSEDAQAH\] like the stars for ever and ever.

**4** But you, Daniel, close up the words and seal the book until the time of the end. Many will run to and fro, and knowledge will increase."

**5** Then I, Daniel, looked, and there were two others standing, one on this bank of the stream and one on that bank of the stream.

**6** One said to the man dressed in linen, who was above the waters of the stream, "How long will it be until the end of these wonders?"

**7** I heard the man dressed in linen, who was above the waters of the stream. He raised his right hand and his left hand to heaven and swore by the one who lives for ever that it would be for a time, times and half a time, and that when the shattering of the power of the holy \[H-QADOSH\] people comes to an end, all these things would be finished.

**8** I heard, but I did not understand. So I said, "My lord, what will be the outcome of these things?"

**9** He said, "Go on your way, Daniel, for the words are closed up and sealed until the time of the end.

**10** Many will be purified, made white and refined, but the wicked will act wickedly, and none of the wicked will understand, but those who are wise will understand.

**11** From the time that the regular offering is taken away and the abomination that makes desolate is set up, there will be one thousand two hundred and ninety days.

**12** Blessed is the one who waits and reaches the one thousand three hundred and thirty-five days.

**13** But as for you, go on to the end. You will rest, and you will rise to your portion at the end of the days."

*Review note: Daniel 10 to 12 were drafted in WABV wording. They are the vision by the Tigris and the word of the man in linen (10:1-21), the history of the kings of the north and the south and of the king who exalts himself (11:1-45), and the time of the end, the rising of the many who sleep in the dust and the sealing of the words (12:1-13). The whole of these chapters is in the Hebrew text. The vision is dated to the third year of Cyrus (10:1, 536 before Christ), and the footnote records the matter. The chapters are prose, with the words on the rising of the dead and the wise (12:2-3) set in verse lines. In 10:19 "peace" translates shalom and is tagged H-SHALOM. In 11:4 "winds" translates ruchot and is tagged H-RUACH. In 10:20, 11:9, 11:18, 11:19, 11:28 (twice), 11:29 and 11:30 (twice) forms of shuv ("go back", "turn back", "turn his insolence back") are tagged H-SHUV, and "again" in 11:10 and 11:13 translates shuv used as an adverb and is not tagged. In 11:22, 11:28, 11:30 and 11:32 "covenant" is tagged H-BERIT. In 11:28, 11:30, 11:31, 11:45 and 12:7 "holy covenant", "sanctuary", "holy mountain" and "holy people" are tagged H-QADOSH. In 11:44 "devote to destruction" translates le-hacharim and is tagged H-CHEREM. In 12:3 "righteousness" translates the Hebrew of matsdiqei ha-rabbim and is tagged H-TSEDAQAH. The footnotes do not name the kings of the north and the south or the king of 11:36-45, and they record only the language. Michael (10:13, 10:21; 12:1) is the great prince who stands guard over the people, and the words on the princes of Persia and of Greece (10:13, 10:20-21) are rendered as written, with no comment. The words on the rising of the many (12:2), the numbers of days (12:11-12) and the sealed words (12:4, 12:9) are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapters 10 to 12, and there are none in the whole book, in the Hebrew and the Aramaic. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 10:1, 10:13, 11:1, 11:6, 11:10, 11:14-20, 11:24, 11:31, 11:35, 11:37-39, 11:45, 12:2, 12:7, 12:11 and 12:12. The Greek differs from the Hebrew in many places in these chapters, and the footnotes record the main differences. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Footnotes

- **1:1** "The third year of the reign of Jehoiakim" is 605 before Christ, counted in the Babylonian way. Jeremiah 25:1 and 46:2 give the same year as the fourth year of Jehoiakim, counted in the Judean way. "Nebuchadnezzar" translates *Nevukhadnetstsar*, the spelling of this book.
- **1:2** "The land of Shinar" is Babylonia (Genesis 10:10; 11:2). "The vessels of the house of God" are taken again in 5:2. See also 2 Chronicles 36:7.
- **1:3** "The chief of his eunuchs" translates *rav sarisav*.
- **1:4** "No physical defect" translates *ein-bahem kol-me'um*, and "the writings and the language of the Chaldeans" translates *sefer u-lashon Kasdim*. The verse is hard in the choice of terms.
- **1:5** "A daily share" translates *devar-yom be-yomo*, "to be trained" translates *le-gaddelam*, literally "to raise them", and "to enter the king's service" translates *ya'amdu lifnei ha-melekh*, literally "to stand before the king".
- **1:7** The Hebrew names contain El and Yah (Daniel, "God is my judge"; Hananiah, "the LORD is gracious"; Mishael; Azariah, "the LORD has helped"). The Babylonian names Belteshazzar, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego are of uncertain meaning.
- **1:8** "Defile himself" translates *yitga'al*.
- **1:9** "Favour and compassion" translates *chesed ve-rachamim*. See entry H-HESED.
- **1:10** "You would put my head in danger" translates *ve-chiyyavtem et-roshi la-melekh*.
- **1:12** "Vegetables" translates *zero'im*, literally "things sown", and may include grain.
- **1:21** "Until the first year of King Cyrus" is 539 or 538 before Christ. The verse does not say that Daniel's service ended then (see 10:1).
- **2:1** "The second year of his reign" is about 603 before Christ, and it does not fit easily with the three years of training in 1:5. "His spirit was troubled" translates *va-titpa'em ruchō*. See entry H-RUACH. "He could not sleep" translates *ve-shenato nihyetah alav*, literally "his sleep was over him", which is hard.
- **2:2** "The magicians", "the enchanters", "the sorcerers" and "the Chaldeans" translate *chartummim*, *ashshafim*, *mekhashshefim* and *Kasdim*. The exact sense of the first three terms is uncertain, and "Chaldeans" here is a class of learned men.
- **2:4** "In Aramaic" translates *Aramit*. The Aramaic text begins here and runs to the end of chapter 7. "May the king live for ever" translates *malka le-alemin chayi*.
- **2:5** "My word is settled" translates *millet minni azda*, in which *azda* is a Persian word of uncertain sense. Another reading is "the matter has gone from me" (the king has forgotten the dream). "You will be cut in pieces and your houses made a rubbish heap" translates *haddamin tit'avedun u-vattekhon nevaluh yitsimun*, and the same words are in 3:29.
- **2:8** "You are playing for time" translates *zeman antun zabbenin*, and in verse 9 "until the situation changes" translates *ad di zeman yishtanne*.
- **2:11** "The gods, whose home is not among mortals" translates *elahin di mador-hon im bisera la havah*, literally "whose dwelling is not with flesh".
- **2:14** "The captain of the king's guard" translates *rav tabbachayya*, literally "the chief of the slaughterers".
- **2:18** "Mercy" translates *rachamin*.
- **2:19** "In a vision in the night" translates *be-chezva di-lelya*.
- **2:20** The verses 20 to 23 are a prayer of praise and are set in lines. "Wisdom and power" translates *chokhmeta u-gevurta*.
- **2:28** "In the days to come" translates *be-acharit yomayya*.
- **2:31** "A statue" translates *tselem*.
- **2:34** "But not by hands" translates *di la bidayin*, and the same is said in verse 45.
- **2:37** "King of kings" translates *melekh malkayya*, as in Ezekiel 26:7 and Ezra 7:12.
- **2:38** "Human beings" translates *benei enasha*.

- **8:8** "The four winds of heaven" translates *arba ruchot ha-shamayim*. See entry H-RUACH.
- **8:9** "The beautiful land" translates *ha-tsevi*, which is usually taken to be the land of Israel.
- **8:11** "The commander of the host" translates *sar ha-tsava*, "the regular offering" translates *ha-tamid*, and "his sanctuary" translates *miqdasho*. See entry H-QADOSH. "Was thrown down" translates *hushlakh*. The verses 9 to 14 are hard.
- **8:12** "Through transgression" translates *be-fasha*, which is hard.
- **8:13** "A holy one" translates *qadosh*, and the same is said of the second speaker. See entry H-QADOSH. "The transgression that makes desolate" translates *ha-pesha shomem*, and "the sanctuary" translates *qodesh*.
- **8:14** "Two thousand three hundred evenings and mornings" translates *erev va-voqer alpayim u-shlosh me'ot*. It may be read as 2,300 days or as 1,150 days (two offerings each day), and some Greek manuscripts have 2,400. "Will be restored" translates *nitsdaq*, literally "will be put right". See entry H-TSEDAQAH.
- **8:15** "Someone who looked like a man" translates *kemar'eh gaver*.
- **8:16** "Gabriel" translates *Gavri'el*, the first angel named in the Bible. See also 9:21.
- **8:17** "Son of man" translates *ben adam*, as in Ezekiel (see the footnote at Ezekiel 2:1), and the team should decide the rendering.
- **8:19** "The indignation" translates *za'am*, and "the appointed time of the end" translates *le-mo'ed qets*.
- **8:21** "The shaggy goat" translates *ha-tsafir ha-sa'ir*, and "the king of Greece" translates *melekh Yavan*.
- **8:23** "Hard of face" translates *az panim*.
- **8:24** "The people of the holy ones" translates *am qedoshim*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **8:25** "The Prince of princes" translates *sar sarim*, and "not by human hand" translates *be-efes yad*.
- **8:26** "Seal up" translates *setom*.
- **8:27** "Was overcome and lay sick" translates *nihyeti ve-nechleti*, and the words "the king's business" translate *melekhet ha-melekh*.
- **9:1** "Darius the son of Ahasuerus" translates *Daryavesh ben-Achashverosh*, and he is not known outside this book. "Was made king" translates *homlakh*. See also 5:31.
- **9:2** "The books" translates *ha-sefarim*, and "seventy years" translates *shiv'im shanah*. See Jeremiah 25:11-12 and 29:10.
- **9:3** "I set my face" translates *va-attenah et-panai*.
- **9:4** "Who keeps covenant and faithful love" translates *shomer ha-berit ve-hachesed*. See entries H-BERIT and H-HESED. The verses 4 to 19 are Daniel's prayer.
- **9:7** "Righteousness belongs to you" translates *lekha Adonai ha-tsedaqah*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. "Open shame" translates *boshet ha-panim*, literally "shame of faces".
- **9:9** "Mercy and forgiveness" translates *ha-rachamim ve-ha-selichot*.
- **9:10** "His teachings" translates *be-torotav*. See entry H-TORAH.
- **9:11** "Your teaching" translates *toratekha*, and "the teaching of Moses" translates *be-torat Mosheh*. See entry H-TORAH. "The curse and the oath" translates *ha-alah ve-ha-shevu'ah*. See Deuteronomy 28 to 30.
- **9:13** "Turning back" translates *la-shuv*. See entry H-SHUV. "Gaining insight into your truth" translates *le-haskil be-amittekha*.
- **9:14** "Kept the disaster ready" translates *va-yishqod al-ha-ra'ah*, literally "watched over the disaster".
- **9:16** "Turn away" translates *yashov*. See entry H-SHUV. "Your righteous acts" translates *tsidqotekha*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. "Your holy mountain" translates *har qodshekha*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **9:17** "Your sanctuary" translates *miqdashekha*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **9:20** "The holy mountain of my God" translates *har qodesh Elohai*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **9:21** "In swift flight" translates *mu'af bi-ya'af*, which is hard. "The evening offering" translates *minchat ereb*.
- **9:23** "Greatly loved" translates *chamudot*.
- **9:24** "Seventy sevens" translates *shavu'im shiv'im*, which are usually taken to be sevens of years. "To seal up sins" follows the reading tradition (*lachtom*), and the written text has "to finish" (*lehatem*). "To atone for iniquity" translates *le-khapper avon*. See entry H-KAPPER. "Lasting righteousness" translates *tsedeq olamim*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. "To anoint a most holy place" translates *limshoach qodesh qodashim*, which may also be read "to anoint a most holy one". See entry H-QADOSH.
- **9:25** "To restore and rebuild" translates *le-hashiv ve-livnot*. See entry H-SHUV. "An anointed one, a leader" translates *mashiach nagid*. See entry H-MASHIACH. "Seven sevens and sixty-two sevens" follows the Hebrew accents, which separate the two numbers. Some join them as sixty-nine. "Square and trench" translates *rechov ve-charuts*, and "distress of the times" translates *be-tsoq ha-ittim*. The verses 24 to 27 are very hard, and the footnote does not choose among the readings.
- **9:26** "An anointed one will be cut off" translates *yikkaret mashiach*. See entry H-MASHIACH. "And will have nothing" translates *ve-ein lo*, which is hard. "The people of a leader who is to come" translates *am nagid ha-ba*, and "the sanctuary" translates *ha-qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH. "With a flood" translates *ba-shetef*, and "desolations are decreed" translates *necheretset shomamot*.
- **9:27** "He will make a strong covenant" translates *higbir berit*. See entry H-BERIT. The subject of "he" may be the leader, the anointed one or God, and the footnote does not decide. "For one seven" translates *shavua echad*, and "on the wing of abominations" translates *al kenaf shiqqutsim*, which is hard. "One who makes desolate" translates *meshomem*.
- **10:1** "The third year of Cyrus king of Persia" is 536 before Christ. "A great conflict" translates *tsava gadol*, in which *tsava* can mean war, service or host.
- **10:2** "Three weeks" translates *shelosha shavu'im yamim*, "three weeks of days".
- **10:4** "The twenty-fourth day of the first month" is April 536 before Christ. "The great river, the Tigris" translates *ha-nahar ha-gadol hu Chiddeqel*.
- **10:5** "A man dressed in linen" translates *ish echad lavush baddim*, and "Uphaz" translates *Ufaz*, a place of gold that is not known (see also Jeremiah 10:9).
- **10:6** "Beryl" translates *tarshish*, and "burnished bronze" translates *nechoshet qalal*.
- **10:11** "Greatly loved" translates *chamudot*.
- **10:13** "The prince of the kingdom of Persia" translates *sar malkhut Paras*, "Michael, one of the chief princes" translates *Mikha'el achad ha-sarim ha-rishonim*, and "I was left there beside the kings of Persia" translates *ve-ani notarti sham el melekhei Paras*, which is hard. The words on the princes are rendered as written, with no comment.
- **10:16** "One in the likeness of the sons of men" translates *kidmut benei adam*.
- **10:17** "Breath" translates *neshamah*, and is not *ruach*.
- **10:19** "Peace be with you" translates *shalom lakh*. See entry H-SHALOM.
- **10:20** "I will go back" translates *ashuv*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **10:21** "The book of truth" translates *ketav emet*. The speech of the man in linen runs from 10:20 to 12:4, and the chapter division comes in the middle of it.
- **11:1** "As for me" is the man in linen who speaks. The verse belongs with 10:21.
- **11:2** "Three more kings" translates *shelosha melakhim*. The text does not name the kings here or in the rest of the chapter, and the footnotes do not identify them.
- **11:4** "The four winds of heaven" translates *arba ruchot ha-shamayim*. See entry H-RUACH.
- **11:5** "The king of the south" and "the king of the north" translate *melekh ha-negev* and *melekh ha-tsafon*, and they stand in this chapter for the rulers of the south and the north.
- **11:6** "An alliance" translates *mesharim*, and "her child" translates *ha-yoldah*, which is uncertain. Some read "the one who begot her". "She will be given up" translates *titten*.
- **11:7** "A branch from her roots" translates *netser mi-shorashehah*.
- **11:9** "Will go back" translates *ve-shav*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **11:10** "Again" translates *ve-shav*, and *shuv* is used as an adverb here and in verse 13. The text is not tagged.
- **11:14** "Violent people among your own people" translates *benei parits ammekha*, which is hard.
- **11:16** "The beautiful land" translates *erets ha-tsevi*, as in 8:9.
- **11:17** "A woman in marriage" translates *bat ha-nashim*, literally "the daughter of women", which is hard.
- **11:18** "The coastlands" translates *iyyim*, and "he will turn his insolence back on him" translates *yashiv lo*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **11:19** "He will turn his face back" translates *ve-yashev panav*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **11:20** "An exactor of tribute" translates *nogesh*, and "the glory of the kingdom" translates *hadar malkhut*.
- **11:21** "A contemptible person" translates *nivzeh*.
- **11:22** "The prince of the covenant" translates *nagid berit*, and the footnote does not decide who is meant. See entry H-BERIT.
- **11:24** "The richest parts of the province" translates *mishmanne medinah*.
- **11:28** "The holy covenant" translates *berit qodesh*. See entries H-QADOSH and H-BERIT. "He will go back" translates *ve-shav*, and it is used twice. See entry H-SHUV.
- **11:29** "He will go back" translates *yashuv*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **11:30** "Ships of Kittim" translates *tsiyyim Kittim*, in which *Kittim* is Cyprus or the peoples of the west. "He will lose heart" translates *ve-nichah*, and the two forms of "turn back" and "go back" translate *ve-shav*. See entry H-SHUV. "The holy covenant" is as in verse 28.
- **11:31** "The sanctuary, the fortress" translates *ha-miqdash ha-ma'oz*. See entry H-QADOSH. "The regular offering" translates *ha-tamid*, and "the abomination that makes desolate" translates *ha-shiqquts meshomem*.
- **11:32** "Those who violate the covenant" translates *marshi'ei berit*. See entry H-BERIT. "The people who know their God" translates *am yod'ei Eloheihem*.
- **11:33** "The wise" translates *maskilim*.
- **11:35** "Refined, purified and made white" translates *litsrof u-lebarer ve-lalben*.
- **11:36** "The God of gods" translates *El elim*, and "the indignation" translates *za'am*.
- **11:37** "The one beloved of women" translates *chemdat nashim*, which is not known. The verses 37 to 39 are hard.
- **11:38** "The god of fortresses" translates *elo'ah ma'uzzim*.
- **11:43** "The Libyans and the Cushites" translates *Luvim ve-Kushim*.
- **11:44** "Devote many to destruction" translates *le-hacharim rabbim*, from *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM.
- **11:45** "Palatial tents" translates *aholei appadno*, and "the beautiful holy mountain" translates *har-tsevi-qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **12:1** "Michael" translates *Mikha'el*, "the great prince who stands guard over your people" translates *ha-sar ha-gadol ha-omed al benei ammekha*, and "found written in the book" translates *ha-nimtsa katuv ba-sefer*.
- **12:2** "Sleep in the dust of the earth" translates *yeshenei admat-afar*, "lasting life" translates *chayyei olam*, and "shame and lasting contempt" translates *charafot le-dir'on olam*. See also Isaiah 66:24. The footnote does not comment on how the verse is read.
- **12:3** "The wise" translates *ha-maskilim*, and "those who lead many to righteousness" translates *matsdiqei ha-rabbim*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH.
- **12:4** "Run to and fro" translates *yeshotetu*, and "knowledge" translates *ha-da'at*.
- **12:7** "A time, times and half a time" translates *mo'ed mo'adim va-chetsi*, as in 7:25. "The power of the holy people" translates *yad-am-qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **12:9** "Closed up and sealed" translates *setumim ve-chatumim*.
- **12:11** "One thousand two hundred and ninety days" and, in verse 12, "one thousand three hundred and thirty-five days", are given as the Hebrew has them, and the text does not explain the difference.
- **12:13** "Your portion" translates *le-goralekha*.
