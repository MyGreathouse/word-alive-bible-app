## Leviticus 1-10: The Offerings and the Priesthood

### Chapter 1

**1** The LORD called to Moses and spoke to him from the tent of meeting:

**2** "Speak to the Israelites and tell them: When any of you brings an offering to the LORD, you are to bring your offering from the livestock, from the herd or from the flock.

**3** If his offering is a burnt offering \[H-OLAH\] from the herd, he is to offer a male without blemish. He is to bring it to the entrance of the tent of meeting, so that it may be accepted before the LORD.

**4** He is to lay his hand on the head of the burnt offering, and it will be accepted on his behalf, to make atonement \[H-KAPPER\] for him.

**5** He is to slaughter the bull before the LORD, and Aaron's sons, the priests, are to bring the blood and splash it against the sides of the altar at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**6** He is to skin the burnt offering and cut it into pieces.

**7** The sons of Aaron the priest are to put fire on the altar and arrange wood on the fire.

**8** Then Aaron's sons, the priests, are to arrange the pieces, the head and the fat on the wood that is on the fire on the altar.

**9** He is to wash the entrails and the legs with water, and the priest is to burn all of it on the altar. It is a burnt offering, a food offering, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**10** If his offering for a burnt offering is from the flock, from the sheep or the goats, he is to offer a male without blemish.

**11** He is to slaughter it on the north side of the altar before the LORD, and Aaron's sons, the priests, are to splash its blood against the sides of the altar.

**12** He is to cut it into pieces, with its head and its fat, and the priest is to arrange them on the wood that is on the fire on the altar.

**13** The entrails and the legs he is to wash with water. The priest is to bring all of it and burn it on the altar. It is a burnt offering, a food offering, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**14** If his offering to the LORD is a burnt offering of birds, he is to bring his offering from the turtledoves or young pigeons.

**15** The priest is to bring it to the altar, wring off its head and burn it on the altar. Its blood is to be drained out on the side of the altar.

**16** He is to remove its crop with its contents and throw it beside the altar on the east side, in the place for ashes.

**17** He is to tear it open by its wings, without dividing it. The priest is to burn it on the altar, on the wood that is on the fire. It is a burnt offering, a food offering, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

### Chapter 2

**1** When anyone brings a grain offering to the LORD, his offering is to be of fine flour. He is to pour oil on it and put frankincense on it,

**2** and bring it to Aaron's sons, the priests. The priest is to take from it a handful of the fine flour and oil, with all its frankincense, and burn this as its memorial portion on the altar, a food offering, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**3** What is left of the grain offering belongs to Aaron and his sons. It is a most holy \[H-QADOSH\] portion of the food offerings to the LORD.

**4** When you bring a grain offering baked in an oven, it is to be of fine flour: unleavened loaves mixed with oil, or unleavened wafers spread with oil.

**5** If your offering is a grain offering made on a griddle, it is to be of fine flour mixed with oil, unleavened.

**6** Break it into pieces and pour oil on it. It is a grain offering.

**7** If your offering is a grain offering made in a pan, it is to be made of fine flour with oil.

**8** You are to bring the grain offering made of these things to the LORD, and present it to the priest, who will bring it to the altar.

**9** The priest is to take from the grain offering its memorial portion and burn it on the altar, a food offering, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**10** What is left of the grain offering belongs to Aaron and his sons. It is a most holy portion of the food offerings to the LORD.

**11** No grain offering that you bring to the LORD is to be made with leaven, for you are not to burn any leaven or any honey as a food offering to the LORD.

**12** You may bring them to the LORD as an offering of first produce, but they are not to be offered on the altar as a pleasing aroma.

**13** Season every grain offering with salt. Do not leave the salt of the covenant of your God out of your grain offering. With all your offerings you are to offer salt.

**14** If you bring a grain offering of first ripe produce to the LORD, you are to bring, as your grain offering of first ripe produce, new grain roasted on the fire, ground from fresh ears.

**15** You are to put oil on it and lay frankincense on it. It is a grain offering.

**16** The priest is to burn as its memorial portion some of the ground grain and oil, with all its frankincense. It is a food offering to the LORD.

### Chapter 3

**1** If his offering is a peace offering, and he offers an animal from the herd, whether male or female, he is to offer it without blemish before the LORD.

**2** He is to lay his hand on the head of his offering and slaughter it at the entrance of the tent of meeting, and Aaron's sons, the priests, are to splash the blood against the sides of the altar.

**3** From the peace offering he is to present, as a food offering to the LORD, the fat that covers the entrails and all the fat that is on the entrails,

**4** and the two kidneys with the fat that is on them at the loins, and the long lobe of the liver, which he is to remove with the kidneys.

**5** Aaron's sons are to burn it on the altar on top of the burnt offering, which is on the wood on the fire. It is a food offering, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**6** If his offering for a peace offering to the LORD is from the flock, male or female, he is to offer it without blemish.

**7** If he offers a lamb as his offering, he is to offer it before the LORD,

**8** lay his hand on the head of his offering and slaughter it in front of the tent of meeting, and Aaron's sons are to splash its blood against the sides of the altar.

**9** From the peace offering he is to present, as a food offering to the LORD, its fat, the whole fat tail, cut off close to the backbone, and the fat that covers the entrails and all the fat that is on the entrails,

**10** and the two kidneys with the fat on them at the loins, and the long lobe of the liver, which he is to remove with the kidneys.

**11** The priest is to burn it on the altar as food, a food offering to the LORD.

**12** If his offering is a goat, he is to offer it before the LORD,

**13** lay his hand on its head and slaughter it in front of the tent of meeting, and the sons of Aaron are to splash its blood against the sides of the altar.

**14** From it he is to present his offering, as a food offering to the LORD, the fat that covers the entrails and all the fat that is on the entrails,

**15** and the two kidneys with the fat on them at the loins, and the long lobe of the liver, which he is to remove with the kidneys.

**16** The priest is to burn them on the altar as food, a food offering with a pleasing aroma. All fat belongs to the LORD.

**17** It is a permanent rule through your generations, in all your dwelling places: you are not to eat any fat or any blood."

### Chapter 4

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**2** "Tell the Israelites: Suppose someone does wrong without meaning to, by breaking any of the LORD's commands about what must not be done, and does any one of them.

**3** If it is the anointed priest who does wrong, bringing guilt on the people, he is to present to the LORD a young bull without blemish as a sin offering \[H-CHATTAT\] for the wrong he has done.

**4** He is to bring the bull to the entrance of the tent of meeting, before the LORD, lay his hand on its head and slaughter it before the LORD.

**5** The anointed priest is to take some of the bull's blood and bring it into the tent of meeting.

**6** He is to dip his finger in the blood and sprinkle some of it seven times before the LORD, towards the curtain of the sanctuary.

**7** The priest is to put some of the blood on the horns of the altar of fragrant incense that stands before the LORD in the tent of meeting. All the rest of the bull's blood he is to pour out at the base of the altar of burnt offering, at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**8** He is to take all the fat from the sin offering bull: the fat covering the entrails and all the fat on them,

**9** the two kidneys with the fat on them by the loins, and the long lobe of the liver, which he is to remove with the kidneys,

**10** as these are taken from the ox of the peace offering. The priest is to burn them on the altar of burnt offering.

**11** But the bull's hide and all its flesh, with its head, legs, entrails and dung,

**12** the entire remains of the bull, he is to carry outside the camp to a clean place, where the ashes are dumped, and burn it on a wood fire. It is to be burned where the ashes are dumped.

**13** If the whole community of Israel does wrong without knowing it, and the matter is hidden from the assembly, and they break any of the LORD's commands about what must not be done, and become guilty,

**14** then when the wrong they have done becomes known, the assembly is to offer a young bull as a sin offering and bring it before the tent of meeting.

**15** The elders of the community are to lay their hands on the bull's head before the LORD, and the bull is to be slaughtered before the LORD.

**16** The anointed priest is to bring some of the bull's blood into the tent of meeting,

**17** dip his finger in the blood and sprinkle it seven times before the LORD, towards the curtain.

**18** He is to put some of the blood on the horns of the altar that stands before the LORD in the tent of meeting, and pour out all the rest of the blood at the base of the altar of burnt offering, at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**19** He is to take all its fat and burn it on the altar.

**20** He is to do with this bull what he did with the sin offering bull. In this way the priest will make atonement for them, and they will be forgiven.

**21** He is to carry the bull outside the camp and burn it as he burned the first bull. It is the sin offering for the assembly.

**22** When a leader does wrong, breaking unintentionally any one of the commands of the LORD his God about what must not be done, and is guilty,

**23** or if he is made aware of the wrong he has done, he is to bring as his offering a male goat without blemish.

**24** He is to lay his hand on the goat's head and slaughter it at the place where the burnt offering is slaughtered before the LORD. It is a sin offering.

**25** The priest is to take some of the blood of the sin offering on his finger, put it on the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and pour out the rest of its blood at the base of the altar of burnt offering.

**26** All its fat he is to burn on the altar, like the fat of the peace offering. In this way the priest will make atonement for him for his wrong, and he will be forgiven.

**27** If an ordinary member of the people does wrong without meaning to, breaking any of the LORD's commands about what must not be done, and is guilty,

**28** or if he is made aware of the wrong he has done, he is to bring as his offering a female goat without blemish for the wrong he has done.

**29** He is to lay his hand on the head of the sin offering and slaughter it at the place of the burnt offering.

**30** The priest is to take some of its blood on his finger, put it on the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and pour out all the rest of its blood at the base of the altar.

**31** He is to take away all its fat, as the fat is taken from the peace offering, and the priest is to burn it on the altar as a pleasing aroma to the LORD. In this way the priest will make atonement for him, and he will be forgiven.

**32** If he brings a lamb as his sin offering, he is to bring a female without blemish.

**33** He is to lay his hand on the head of the sin offering and slaughter it as a sin offering at the place where the burnt offering is slaughtered.

**34** The priest is to take some of the blood of the sin offering on his finger, put it on the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and pour out all the rest of its blood at the base of the altar.

**35** He is to take away all its fat, as the lamb's fat is taken from the peace offering, and the priest is to burn it on the altar, on top of the food offerings to the LORD. In this way the priest will make atonement for him for the wrong he has done, and he will be forgiven.

### Chapter 5

**1** If anyone does wrong by failing to speak when he has heard a public summons to testify and is a witness, having seen or learned of the matter, he will bear the consequences of his guilt.

**2** Or if anyone touches something unclean, whether the carcass of an unclean wild animal, or of unclean livestock, or of unclean swarming creatures, and he does not realise it, he has become unclean and is guilty.

**3** Or if he touches human uncleanness, whatever kind of uncleanness it may be, and he does not realise it, then when he comes to know it, he is guilty.

**4** Or if anyone swears thoughtlessly with his lips to do harm or to do good, in any way that a person may swear thoughtlessly, and he does not realise it, then when he comes to know it, he is guilty in any of these matters.

**5** When he is guilty in any of these ways, he is to confess the wrong he has done,

**6** and bring to the LORD as his compensation for the wrong he has done a female from the flock, a lamb or a goat, as a sin offering. The priest is to make atonement for him for his wrong.

**7** If he cannot afford a lamb, he is to bring to the LORD, as compensation for his wrong, two turtledoves or two young pigeons, one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering.

**8** He is to bring them to the priest, who is to offer the sin offering first. He is to wring its head from its neck, but not tear it off.

**9** He is to sprinkle some of the blood of the sin offering on the side of the altar, and the rest of the blood is to be drained out at the base of the altar. It is a sin offering.

**10** The second he is to offer as a burnt offering according to the rule. In this way the priest will make atonement for him for the wrong he has done, and he will be forgiven.

**11** If he cannot afford two turtledoves or two young pigeons, he is to bring as his offering for the wrong he has done a tenth of an ephah of fine flour as a sin offering. He is not to put oil or frankincense on it, because it is a sin offering.

**12** He is to bring it to the priest, and the priest is to take a handful of it as its memorial portion and burn it on the altar, on top of the food offerings to the LORD. It is a sin offering.

**13** In this way the priest will make atonement for him for any of these wrongs he has done, and he will be forgiven. The rest will belong to the priest, as with the grain offering."

**14** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**15** "If anyone is unfaithful and does wrong without meaning to, in matters concerning the LORD's holy things, he is to bring to the LORD as his compensation a ram without blemish from the flock, valued by you in silver shekels, by the shekel of the sanctuary, as a guilt offering \[H-ASHAM\].

**16** He is to make good what he has taken from the holy things, adding a fifth to it, and give it to the priest. The priest is to make atonement for him with the ram of the guilt offering, and he will be forgiven.

**17** If anyone does wrong, breaking any of the LORD's commands about what must not be done, though he did not know it, he is guilty and will bear the consequences.

**18** He is to bring to the priest a ram without blemish from the flock, valued as a guilt offering. The priest is to make atonement for him for the wrong he did without knowing it, and he will be forgiven.

**19** It is a guilt offering. He was certainly guilty before the LORD."

### Chapter 6

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**2** "If anyone does wrong and is unfaithful to the LORD by deceiving his neighbour about something entrusted to him, or left in his keeping, or stolen, or by cheating his neighbour,

**3** or has found a lost article and lied about it, or swears falsely about any of the things people do that are wrong,

**4** then, since he has done wrong and is guilty, he is to give back what he took by theft or by cheating, or what was entrusted to him, or the lost article he found,

**5** or whatever else he swore falsely about. He is to repay it in full, adding a fifth to it, and give it to its owner on the day he admits his guilt.

**6** He is to bring to the priest, as his compensation to the LORD, a ram without blemish from the flock, valued as a guilt offering.

**7** The priest is to make atonement for him before the LORD, and he will be forgiven for anything he did that made him guilty."

**8** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**9** "Command Aaron and his sons: This is the teaching about the burnt offering. The burnt offering is to stay on the hearth of the altar all night until morning, and the altar fire is to be kept burning on it.

**10** The priest is to put on his linen robe, with linen undergarments next to his body, and take up the ashes of the burnt offering that the fire has consumed on the altar, and place them beside the altar.

**11** Then he is to take off his garments, put on other garments and carry the ashes outside the camp to a clean place.

**12** The fire on the altar is to be kept burning on it. It must not go out. The priest is to burn wood on it every morning, arrange the burnt offering on it and burn the fat of the peace offerings on it.

**13** A fire is to be kept burning on the altar continually. It must not go out.

**14** This is the teaching about the grain offering: Aaron's sons are to present it before the LORD, in front of the altar.

**15** One of them is to take from it a handful of the fine flour and oil of the grain offering, with all the frankincense that is on the grain offering, and burn this as its memorial portion on the altar, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**16** Aaron and his sons are to eat what is left of it. It is to be eaten as unleavened bread in a holy place. They are to eat it in the courtyard of the tent of meeting.

**17** It must not be baked with leaven. I have given it as their share of my food offerings. It is most holy, like the sin offering and the guilt offering.

**18** Every male among Aaron's descendants may eat it, as a permanent share through your generations from the food offerings to the LORD. Whatever touches them will become holy."

**19** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**20** "This is the offering that Aaron and his sons are to present to the LORD on the day he is anointed: a tenth of an ephah of fine flour as a regular grain offering, half of it in the morning and half in the evening.

**21** It is to be made with oil on a griddle. You are to bring it well mixed, and present the grain offering in baked pieces, as a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**22** The anointed priest from among his sons who succeeds him is to prepare it. It is a permanent rule: the whole of it is to be burned to the LORD.

**23** Every grain offering of a priest is to be burned completely. It is not to be eaten."

**24** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**25** "Tell Aaron and his sons: This is the teaching about the sin offering. At the place where the burnt offering is slaughtered, the sin offering is to be slaughtered before the LORD. It is most holy.

**26** The priest who offers it for sin is to eat it. It is to be eaten in a holy place, in the courtyard of the tent of meeting.

**27** Whatever touches its flesh will become holy, and if any of its blood is spattered on a garment, you are to wash the garment on which it was spattered in a holy place.

**28** The clay pot in which it was boiled is to be broken. If it was boiled in a bronze pot, that pot is to be scoured and rinsed with water.

**29** Every male among the priests may eat it. It is most holy.

**30** But no sin offering whose blood is brought into the tent of meeting to make atonement in the holy place may be eaten. It is to be burned with fire.

### Chapter 7

**1** This is the teaching about the guilt offering. It is most holy.

**2** At the place where the burnt offering is slaughtered they are to slaughter the guilt offering, and its blood is to be splashed against the sides of the altar.

**3** All its fat is to be offered: the fat tail, the fat covering the entrails,

**4** the two kidneys with the fat on them at the loins, and the long lobe of the liver, which he is to remove with the kidneys.

**5** The priest is to burn them on the altar as a food offering to the LORD. It is a guilt offering.

**6** Every male among the priests may eat it. It is to be eaten in a holy place. It is most holy.

**7** The guilt offering is like the sin offering. There is one rule for both. The priest who makes atonement with it will have it.

**8** The priest who presents anyone's burnt offering will have for himself the skin of the burnt offering he has presented.

**9** Every grain offering baked in an oven, and every one made in a pan or on a griddle, belongs to the priest who presents it.

**10** Every grain offering, whether mixed with oil or dry, belongs to all the sons of Aaron, equally.

**11** This is the teaching about the sacrifice of peace offerings that a person may present to the LORD.

**12** If he presents it as a thank offering, he is to present with the thank offering unleavened loaves mixed with oil, unleavened wafers spread with oil, and loaves of fine flour well mixed with oil.

**13** With his thank offering of peace offerings he is to present his offering with loaves of leavened bread.

**14** From each kind of offering he is to present one portion as a contribution to the LORD. It belongs to the priest who splashes the blood of the peace offerings.

**15** The flesh of his thank offering is to be eaten on the day it is presented. He is not to leave any of it until morning.

**16** If his sacrifice is a vow or a freewill offering, it is to be eaten on the day he presents his sacrifice, and what is left of it may be eaten the next day.

**17** But what is left of the flesh of the sacrifice on the third day is to be burned with fire.

**18** If any of the flesh of his peace offering is eaten on the third day, it will not be accepted. It will not count for the one who presented it. It will be foul, and the person who eats of it will bear the consequences of his guilt.

**19** Flesh that touches anything unclean must not be eaten. It is to be burned with fire. As for other flesh, anyone who is clean may eat it,

**20** but if anyone eats flesh from the LORD's peace offering while he is unclean, that person will be cut off from his people.

**21** If anyone touches something unclean, whether human uncleanness or an unclean animal or any unclean detestable creature, and then eats flesh from the LORD's peace offering, that person will be cut off from his people."

**22** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**23** "Tell the Israelites: You are not to eat any fat of an ox, a sheep or a goat.

**24** The fat of an animal that has died naturally or been torn by wild animals may be used for any other purpose, but you must not eat it.

**25** Anyone who eats the fat of an animal from which a food offering may be presented to the LORD will be cut off from his people.

**26** Wherever you live, you are not to eat any blood, whether of bird or of animal.

**27** Anyone who eats any blood will be cut off from his people."

**28** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**29** "Tell the Israelites: Whoever presents a peace offering to the LORD is to bring his offering to the LORD from the peace offering itself.

**30** With his own hands he is to bring the LORD's food offerings. He is to bring the fat with the breast, so that the breast may be lifted up as an elevation offering before the LORD.

**31** The priest is to burn the fat on the altar, but the breast will belong to Aaron and his sons.

**32** You are to give the right thigh to the priest as a contribution from your peace offerings.

**33** The son of Aaron who presents the blood of the peace offerings and the fat will have the right thigh as his share.

**34** For I have taken the breast that is lifted up and the thigh that is contributed from the Israelites, from their peace offerings, and have given them to Aaron the priest and his sons as a permanent due from the Israelites."

**35** This is the portion of Aaron and of his sons from the food offerings to the LORD, from the day they were presented to serve the LORD as priests.

**36** The LORD commanded that the Israelites give this to them from the day he anointed them, as a permanent due through their generations.

**37** This is the teaching about the burnt offering, the grain offering, the sin offering, the guilt offering, the installation offering and the peace offering,

**38** which the LORD commanded Moses on Mount Sinai, on the day he commanded the Israelites to bring their offerings to the LORD in the wilderness of Sinai.

*Review note: Leviticus 1 to 7 was drafted in WABV wording. The offering terms (burnt, grain, peace, sin and guilt offerings) are standard and recur throughout, so key terms were kept consistent. Much of the wording follows familiar translation phrasing, especially in the repeated ritual formulas, and the section needs a check against the Hebrew and an originality check before it is locked.*

### Chapter 8

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**2** "Take Aaron and his sons with him, and the garments, the anointing oil, the bull of the sin offering, the two rams and the basket of unleavened bread,

**3** and gather the whole community at the entrance of the tent of meeting."

**4** Moses did as the LORD commanded him, and the community gathered at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**5** Moses said to the community, "This is what the LORD has commanded to be done."

**6** Moses brought Aaron and his sons forward and washed them with water.

**7** He put the tunic on Aaron and wrapped the sash around him, clothed him with the robe and put the ephod on him. He fastened the ephod on him with its woven waistband, tying it to him.

**8** He placed the breastpiece on him and put the Urim and the Thummim in the breastpiece.

**9** He set the turban on his head, and on the turban, at the front, he set the gold plate, the sacred diadem, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**10** Then Moses took the anointing oil and anointed the tabernacle and everything in it, and so consecrated them.

**11** He sprinkled some of the oil on the altar seven times, and anointed the altar and all its utensils, and the basin and its stand, to consecrate them.

**12** He poured some of the anointing oil on Aaron's head and anointed him, to consecrate him.

**13** Moses brought Aaron's sons forward, clothed them with tunics, wrapped sashes around them and tied headbands on them, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**14** Then he brought the bull of the sin offering, and Aaron and his sons laid their hands on its head.

**15** Moses slaughtered it, took the blood and with his finger put it on the horns of the altar all round, to purify the altar. He poured out the rest of the blood at the base of the altar. In this way he consecrated it, to make atonement for it.

**16** He took all the fat that was on the entrails, the long lobe of the liver and the two kidneys with their fat, and Moses burned them on the altar.

**17** But the bull, with its hide, its flesh and its dung, he burned with fire outside the camp, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**18** Then he presented the ram of the burnt offering, and Aaron and his sons laid their hands on its head.

**19** Moses slaughtered it and splashed the blood against the sides of the altar.

**20** He cut the ram into pieces, and Moses burned the head, the pieces and the fat.

**21** He washed the entrails and the legs with water, and Moses burned the whole ram on the altar. It was a burnt offering, a pleasing aroma, a food offering to the LORD, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**22** Then he presented the second ram, the ram of installation, and Aaron and his sons laid their hands on its head.

**23** Moses slaughtered it, took some of its blood and put it on the lobe of Aaron's right ear, on the thumb of his right hand and on the big toe of his right foot.

**24** Moses also brought Aaron's sons forward and put some of the blood on the lobes of their right ears, on the thumbs of their right hands and on the big toes of their right feet. Then Moses splashed the blood against the sides of the altar.

**25** He took the fat, the fat tail, all the fat on the entrails, the long lobe of the liver, the two kidneys with their fat, and the right thigh.

**26** From the basket of unleavened bread that was before the LORD he took one unleavened loaf, one loaf of oiled bread and one wafer, and placed them on the fat and on the right thigh.

**27** He put all these in the hands of Aaron and of his sons, and lifted them up as an elevation offering before the LORD.

**28** Then Moses took them from their hands and burned them on the altar on top of the burnt offering. This was an installation offering, a pleasing aroma, a food offering to the LORD.

**29** Moses took the breast and lifted it up as an elevation offering before the LORD. It was Moses' portion of the ram of installation, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**30** Then Moses took some of the anointing oil and some of the blood that was on the altar and sprinkled it on Aaron and his garments, and on his sons and their garments. So he consecrated Aaron and his garments, and his sons and their garments with him.

**31** Moses said to Aaron and his sons, "Boil the flesh at the entrance of the tent of meeting, and eat it there with the bread that is in the basket of installation offerings, as I was commanded: 'Aaron and his sons are to eat it.'

**32** What is left of the flesh and the bread you are to burn with fire.

**33** You are not to go out from the entrance of the tent of meeting for seven days, until the days of your installation are completed, for it will take seven days to install you.

**34** What has been done today the LORD has commanded to be done, to make atonement for you.

**35** You are to stay at the entrance of the tent of meeting day and night for seven days and keep the charge of the LORD, so that you do not die, for so I have been commanded."

**36** So Aaron and his sons did everything the LORD had commanded through Moses.

### Chapter 9

**1** On the eighth day Moses summoned Aaron and his sons and the elders of Israel.

**2** He said to Aaron, "Take a bull calf for your sin offering and a ram for your burnt offering, both without blemish, and present them before the LORD.

**3** Then say to the Israelites: Take a male goat for a sin offering, a calf and a lamb, both a year old and without blemish, for a burnt offering,

**4** and an ox and a ram for peace offerings to sacrifice before the LORD, and a grain offering mixed with oil. For today the LORD will appear to you."

**5** They brought what Moses commanded to the front of the tent of meeting, and the whole community drew near and stood before the LORD.

**6** Moses said, "This is what the LORD has commanded you to do, so that the glory of the LORD may appear to you."

**14** He is to take some of the bull's blood and sprinkle it with his finger on the front of the cover of atonement, and in front of the cover he is to sprinkle some of the blood with his finger seven times.

**15** Then he is to slaughter the goat of the sin offering for the people and bring its blood inside the curtain. He is to do with its blood as he did with the bull's blood, sprinkling it on the cover of atonement and in front of it.

**21** Aaron is to lay both his hands on the head of the live goat and confess over it all the guilt of the Israelites, all their rebellions, all their wrongs. He is to place them on the goat's head and send it away into the wilderness by the hand of a man appointed for the task.

**24** He is to bathe his body in water in a holy place, put on his own garments and come out, and offer his burnt offering and the burnt offering of the people, and make atonement for himself and for the people.

**25** The fat of the sin offering he is to burn on the altar.

**26** The man who released the goat to Azazel is to wash his clothes and bathe his body in water, and afterwards he may come into the camp.

**31** It is a Sabbath \[H-SHABBAT\] of complete rest for you, and you are to humble yourselves. It is a permanent rule.

**32** The priest who is anointed and installed to serve as priest in place of his father is to make atonement. He is to put on the linen garments, the sacred garments.

**20** You are not to present anything with a defect, because it will not be accepted for you.

**21** When someone presents a peace offering to the LORD from the herd or the flock, in fulfilment of a special vow or as a freewill offering, it must be without defect to be accepted. There must be no blemish in it.

**23** You may present as a freewill offering an ox or a sheep that has a limb too long or too short, but it will not be accepted for a vow.

**24** You are not to present to the LORD any animal whose testicles are bruised, crushed, torn or cut. You are not to do this in your land,

**28** You are not to slaughter an ox or a sheep and its young on the same day.

**29** When you sacrifice a thank offering to the LORD, sacrifice it so that it is accepted for you.

**30** It is to be eaten on the same day. You are not to leave any of it until morning. I am the LORD.

**31** Keep my commands and do them. I am the LORD.

**32** Do not profane my holy name, so that I may be treated as holy among the Israelites. I am the LORD who makes you holy,

**33** who brought you out of the land of Egypt to be your God. I am the LORD."

## Leviticus 23-25: Appointed Times and the Jubilee

**3** For six days work may be done, but the seventh day is a Sabbath \[H-SHABBAT\] of complete rest, a sacred assembly. You are not to do any work. It is a Sabbath to the LORD in all your homes.

**4** These are the LORD's appointed festivals, the sacred assemblies you are to proclaim at their appointed times:

**5** The LORD's Passover begins at twilight on the fourteenth day of the first month.

**6** On the fifteenth day of that month the LORD's Festival of Unleavened Bread begins. For seven days you are to eat bread made without yeast.

**7** On the first day you are to hold a sacred assembly. You are not to do any ordinary work.

**8** For seven days you are to present a food offering to the LORD. On the seventh day there is to be a sacred assembly. You are not to do any ordinary work."

**10** "Speak to the Israelites and tell them: When you enter the land that I am giving you and reap its harvest, bring to the priest a sheaf of the first of your harvest.

**11** He is to lift the sheaf before the LORD so that it may be accepted for you. The priest is to lift it on the day after the Sabbath.

**12** On the day you lift the sheaf you are to offer a year-old male lamb without defect as a burnt offering to the LORD,

**13** with its grain offering of two-tenths of an ephah of fine flour mixed with oil, a food offering to the LORD, a pleasing aroma, and its drink offering of a quarter of a hin of wine.

**14** You are not to eat any bread or roasted grain or fresh grain until the very day you bring this offering to your God. This is a permanent rule through all your generations, in all your homes.

**15** From the day after the Sabbath, from the day you bring the sheaf of the elevation offering, you are to count seven full weeks.

**16** You are to count fifty days, up to the day after the seventh Sabbath, and then present an offering of new grain to the LORD.

**17** From your homes you are to bring two loaves to be lifted up, made of two-tenths of an ephah of fine flour, baked with yeast, as first produce for the LORD.

**18** With the bread you are to present seven year-old male lambs without defect, one young bull and two rams. They are to be a burnt offering to the LORD, with their grain offerings and drink offerings, a food offering, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**19** Then you are to offer one male goat as a sin offering and two year-old male lambs as a peace offering.

**20** The priest is to lift them up with the bread of the first produce as an elevation offering before the LORD, together with the two lambs. They are to be holy to the LORD for the priest.

**21** On that same day you are to proclaim a sacred assembly. You are not to do any ordinary work. This is a permanent rule through all your generations, in all your homes.

**24** "Tell the Israelites: On the first day of the seventh month you are to have a day of rest, a remembrance marked by trumpet blasts, a sacred assembly.

**25** You are not to do any ordinary work, but you are to present a food offering to the LORD."

**27** "The tenth day of this seventh month is the Day of Atonement. You are to hold a sacred assembly and humble yourselves, and present a food offering to the LORD.

**28** You are not to do any work on that day, because it is a Day of Atonement, to make atonement for you before the LORD your God.

**29** Anyone who does not humble himself on that day is to be cut off from his people.

**30** Anyone who does any work on that day, I will destroy from among his people.

**31** You are not to do any work at all. This is a permanent rule through all your generations, in all your homes.

**32** It is a Sabbath of complete rest for you, and you are to humble yourselves. From the evening of the ninth day of the month until the following evening you are to keep your Sabbath."

**33** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**34** "Tell the Israelites: On the fifteenth day of this seventh month the LORD's Festival of Booths begins, and it lasts for seven days.

**35** The first day is a sacred assembly. You are not to do any ordinary work.

**36** For seven days you are to present food offerings to the LORD. On the eighth day you are to hold a sacred assembly and present a food offering to the LORD. It is a closing assembly. You are not to do any ordinary work.

**37** These are the LORD's appointed festivals, which you are to proclaim as sacred assemblies, to present food offerings to the LORD: burnt offerings, grain offerings, sacrifices and drink offerings, each on its own day,

**38** in addition to the LORD's Sabbaths, and in addition to your gifts, and in addition to all your vows and all your freewill offerings that you give to the LORD.

**39** On the fifteenth day of the seventh month, when you have gathered in the produce of the land, you are to keep the festival of the LORD for seven days. The first day is a day of complete rest, and the eighth day is a day of complete rest.

**40** On the first day you are to take the fruit of splendid trees, palm branches, boughs of leafy trees and willows of the stream, and you are to rejoice before the LORD your God for seven days.

**41** You are to keep it as a festival to the LORD for seven days in the year. It is a permanent rule through all your generations. You are to keep it in the seventh month.

**42** You are to live in booths for seven days. All the native-born in Israel are to live in booths,

**43** so that your descendants may know that I made the Israelites live in booths when I brought them out of the land of Egypt. I am the LORD your God."

**44** So Moses declared to the Israelites the appointed festivals of the LORD.

### Chapter 25

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses on Mount Sinai:

**2** "Speak to the Israelites and tell them: When you enter the land that I am giving you, the land itself is to keep a Sabbath for the LORD.

**3** For six years you may sow your field, and for six years you may prune your vineyard and gather its produce.

**4** But in the seventh year the land is to have a Sabbath of complete rest, a Sabbath for the LORD. You are not to sow your field or prune your vineyard.

**5** You are not to reap what grows by itself from your harvest or gather the grapes of your untended vines. It is to be a year of complete rest for the land.

**6** Whatever the land yields during its Sabbath will be food for you, for yourself, for your male and female servants, for your hired worker and the resident foreigner who lives with you,

**7** and for your livestock and the wild animals in your land. All its produce may be eaten.

**8** You are to count off seven Sabbath years, seven times seven years, so that the seven Sabbath years add up to forty-nine years.

**9** Then you are to sound the ram's horn loudly on the tenth day of the seventh month. On the Day of Atonement you are to sound the horn throughout your land.

**10** You are to consecrate the fiftieth year and proclaim release \[H-DEROR\] throughout the land for all who live in it. It is to be a Jubilee \[H-YOVEL\] for you. Each of you is to return to his own holding, and each to his own clan.

**11** The fiftieth year is to be a Jubilee for you. You are not to sow, or reap what grows by itself, or gather the grapes of the untended vines.

**12** For it is a Jubilee. It is to be holy to you. You may eat only what the fields produce.

**13** In this Year of Jubilee each of you is to return to his own holding.

**14** If you sell anything to your neighbour or buy anything from him, you are not to wrong one another.

**15** You are to buy from your neighbour according to the number of years since the Jubilee, and he is to sell to you according to the number of years of crops that remain.

**16** If the years are many, you are to increase the price, and if the years are few, you are to reduce the price, because what he is selling you is a number of harvests.

**17** You are not to wrong one another, but fear your God. I am the LORD your God.

**18** Keep my statutes and my rulings and do them, and you will live securely in the land.

**19** The land will give its fruit, and you will eat your fill and live there securely.

**20** If you ask, 'What will we eat in the seventh year, if we do not sow or gather our produce?'

**21** I will command my blessing on you in the sixth year, so that it yields a crop for three years.

**22** In the eighth year you will sow, and you will eat from the old crop until the ninth year, until its produce comes in. You will eat the old crop until then.

**23** The land is not to be sold permanently, for the land is mine. You are only foreigners and tenants with me.

**24** In all the land you hold, you are to allow redemption of the land.

**25** If your brother becomes poor and sells part of his holding, his nearest relative is to come and redeem \[H-GAAL\] what his brother has sold.

**26** If a man has no one to redeem it, but he prospers and gains enough to redeem it himself,

**27** he is to count the years since the sale, repay the balance to the man to whom he sold it, and return to his holding.

**28** But if he does not gain enough to repay him, what he sold will stay in the hands of the buyer until the Year of Jubilee. It will be released in the Jubilee, and he may return to his holding.

**29** If a man sells a dwelling house in a walled city, he has the right of redemption until a full year after its sale. For a full year he has the right of redemption.

**30** If it is not redeemed within a full year, the house in the walled city will pass permanently to the buyer and his descendants. It will not be released in the Jubilee.

**31** But the houses of villages that have no surrounding wall are to be treated as part of the open country. They may be redeemed, and they are released in the Jubilee.

**32** As for the cities of the Levites, the Levites always have the right of redemption of the houses in the cities they hold.

**33** Whatever one of the Levites redeems, a house sold in a city that they hold, will be released in the Jubilee, because the houses in the cities of the Levites are their holding among the Israelites.

**34** But the open pasture land around their cities is not to be sold, for it is their permanent possession.

**35** If your brother becomes poor and cannot support himself among you, you are to support him, as you would a foreigner or a resident, so that he can live among you.

**36** Do not take interest or profit from him, but fear your God, so that your brother may live among you.

**37** You are not to lend him your money at interest, or give him your food for profit.

**38** I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt to give you the land of Canaan and to be your God.

**39** If your brother becomes poor beside you and sells himself to you, you are not to make him work as a slave.

**40** He is to be with you as a hired worker and a resident. He is to serve you until the Year of Jubilee.

**41** Then he and his children with him are to go free from you, and he will return to his clan and to the holding of his ancestors.

**42** For they are my servants, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt. They are not to be sold as slaves.

**43** You are not to rule over him harshly, but fear your God.

**44** As for your male and female slaves whom you may have, you may buy male and female slaves from the nations around you.

**45** You may also buy from the children of the foreign residents who live with you, and from their families who are with you, who have been born in your land. They may become your property.

**46** You may leave them to your children after you as a holding, to inherit as property. You may make them work for you permanently. But over your brothers, the Israelites, you are not to rule one over another harshly.

**47** If a foreigner or resident who lives with you grows rich, and your brother beside him becomes poor and sells himself to the foreigner who lives with you, or to a member of the foreigner's clan,

**48** he has the right of redemption after he has sold himself. One of his brothers may redeem him,

**49** or his uncle, or his uncle's son may redeem him, or any close relative from his clan may redeem him. Or if he prospers, he may redeem himself.

**50** He is to reckon with the one who bought him, from the year he sold himself to him until the Year of Jubilee. The price of his sale is to be set according to the number of years, as the days of a hired worker are with him.

**51** If there are many years left, he is to pay back part of his purchase price in proportion to them as his redemption.

**52** If there are only a few years left until the Year of Jubilee, he is to reckon with him, and pay back the price of his redemption in proportion to his years.

**53** He is to be with him like a worker hired year by year. He is not to rule over him harshly in your sight.

**54** If he is not redeemed in any of these ways, he and his children with him are to go free in the Year of Jubilee,

**55** for the Israelites are my servants. They are my servants, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt. I am the LORD your God."

## Leviticus 26-27: Blessings, Curses and Vows

### Chapter 26

**1** "You are not to make idols for yourselves, or set up a carved image or a standing stone for yourselves, or place a stone carved with figures in your land to bow down before it. I am the LORD your God.

**2** Keep my Sabbaths and revere my sanctuary. I am the LORD.

**3** If you walk by my statutes and keep my commands and do them,

**4** I will give you your rains in their season, and the land will yield its produce, and the trees of the field will yield their fruit.

**5** Your threshing will last until the grape harvest, and the grape harvest will last until the time of sowing. You will eat your bread until you are full and live securely in your land.

**6** I will give peace in the land, and you will lie down with no one to make you afraid. I will remove wild animals from the land, and no sword will pass through your land.

**7** You will chase your enemies, and they will fall before you by the sword.

**8** Five of you will chase a hundred, and a hundred of you will chase ten thousand, and your enemies will fall before you by the sword.

**10** You will be eating the old harvest, stored long, and you will have to clear out the old to make room for the new.

**11** I will place my dwelling among you, and I will not reject you.

**12** I will walk among you, and I will be your God, and you will be my people.

**13** I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, so that you would no longer be their slaves. I broke the bars of your yoke and made you walk upright.

**15** if you reject my statutes, and if you despise my rulings so that you do not do all my commands, and so break my covenant,

**17** I will set my face against you, and you will be struck down before your enemies. Those who hate you will rule over you, and you will flee when no one is chasing you.

**19** I will break your proud strength, and I will make the sky above you like iron and the earth beneath you like bronze.

**26** When I cut off your supply of bread, ten women will bake your bread in one oven, and they will bring your bread back by weight. You will eat, but you will not be satisfied.

**29** You will eat the flesh of your sons, and you will eat the flesh of your daughters.

**30** I will destroy your high places and cut down your incense stands, and I will heap your corpses on the lifeless bodies of your idols, and I will loathe you.

**31** I will turn your cities into ruins and lay waste your sanctuaries, and I will not smell your pleasing aromas.

**33** I will scatter you among the nations, and I will draw a sword after you. Your land will be a desolation, and your cities will be ruins.

### Chapter 27

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**2** "Speak to the Israelites and tell them: When someone makes a special vow, the persons are to be the LORD's according to your valuation.

**5** If the person is from five years old up to twenty years old, your valuation for a male is to be twenty shekels, and for a female ten shekels.

**6** If the person is from one month old up to five years old, your valuation for a male is to be five shekels of silver, and for a female your valuation is to be three shekels of silver.

**7** If the person is sixty years old or more, your valuation for a male is to be fifteen shekels, and for a female ten shekels.

**8** If the one who made the vow is too poor to pay your valuation, he is to be brought before the priest, and the priest is to value him. The priest is to set the valuation according to what the one who made the vow can afford.

**9** If it is an animal that may be presented as an offering to the LORD, everything that any such person gives to the LORD is holy.

**10** He is not to replace it or exchange it, a good one for a bad one, or a bad one for a good one. If he does exchange one animal for another, then both it and its substitute will be holy.

**11** If it is any unclean animal that may not be presented as an offering to the LORD, he is to bring the animal before the priest,

**12** and the priest is to value it, whether it is good or bad. As the priest values it, so it will be.

**13** If he wishes to redeem it, he is to add a fifth to your valuation.

**14** When a man dedicates his house as holy to the LORD, the priest is to value it, whether it is good or bad. As the priest values it, so it will stand.

**15** If the one who dedicated it wishes to redeem his house, he is to add a fifth to your valuation, and it will be his.

**16** If a man dedicates to the LORD part of the field that is his holding, your valuation is to be according to the seed needed for it: a homer of barley seed is to be valued at fifty shekels of silver.

**17** If he dedicates his field from the Year of Jubilee, your valuation is to stand.

**18** But if he dedicates his field after the Jubilee, the priest is to reckon the price according to the years that remain until the next Year of Jubilee, and it is to be deducted from your valuation.

**19** If the one who dedicated the field wishes to redeem it, he is to add a fifth to your valuation, and it will be his.

**20** But if he does not redeem the field, or if he has sold the field to another man, it may not be redeemed any more.

**21** When the field is released in the Jubilee, it will be holy to the LORD, like a field devoted. It will become the holding of the priest.

**22** If he dedicates to the LORD a field that he has bought, which is not part of his own holding,

**23** the priest is to reckon the amount of your valuation up to the Year of Jubilee, and the man is to pay your valuation on that day as something holy to the LORD.

**24** In the Year of Jubilee the field is to return to the one from whom it was bought, to the one whose holding the land was.

**25** Every valuation is to be by the shekel of the sanctuary: twenty gerahs to the shekel.

**26** But a firstborn of the animals, which as a firstborn belongs to the LORD, no one may dedicate. Whether ox or sheep, it is the LORD's.

**27** If it is an unclean animal, he may buy it back at your valuation, and add a fifth to it. If it is not redeemed, it is to be sold at your valuation.

**28** But nothing that a man devotes \[H-CHEREM\] to the LORD, of all that he has, whether man or animal or field of his holding, may be sold or redeemed. Everything devoted is most holy to the LORD.

**29** No person devoted, who is to be devoted to destruction from among mankind, may be ransomed. He is to be put to death.

**30** All the tithe of the land, whether of the grain of the land or of the fruit of the trees, is the LORD's. It is holy to the LORD.

**31** If a man wishes to redeem some of his tithe, he is to add a fifth to it.

**32** Every tithe of herd and flock, every tenth animal that passes under the rod, is to be holy to the LORD.

**33** He is not to look to see whether it is good or bad, and he is not to exchange it. If he does exchange it, both it and its substitute will be holy. They may not be redeemed."

**34** These are the commands that the LORD gave to Moses for the Israelites on Mount Sinai.

*End of the book of Leviticus. Review note: Leviticus 26 and 27 was drafted in WABV wording. Chapter 26 is a blessing and curse sequence in a solemn, repeated pattern (the "seven times" refrain), and chapter 27 is a technical list of valuations. Verse 26:29, about eating the flesh of one's children, and verse 27:29 on those "devoted to destruction" are rendered as written. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **1:1** The book's English name comes from the Levites, the tribe of the priests. Its Hebrew name is *Vayyiqra*, "and he called", from the first word.
- **1:2** "Offering" is Hebrew *qorban*, "something brought near".
- **1:3** "Burnt offering" is Hebrew *olah*, "that which goes up". The whole animal was burned. See entry H-OLAH.
- **1:4** Laying a hand on the animal identified the offerer with it. "Atonement" is Hebrew *kipper*, to cover or cleanse. See entry H-KAPPER.
- **1:9** "Pleasing aroma" is Hebrew *reach nichoach*, "a soothing smell" that expresses acceptance. "Food offering" is Hebrew *ishsheh*, an offering made by fire.
- **1:16** The Hebrew word translated "crop" is uncertain. It can also mean "feathers" or "contents".
- **2:1** "Grain offering" is Hebrew *minchah*, a gift or tribute. It is a presentation, usually of flour, oil and frankincense.
- **2:2** "Memorial portion" is Hebrew *azkarah*, the part burned to bring the offerer to God's remembrance.
- **2:3** "Most holy" is Hebrew *qodesh qodashim*, the highest degree of holiness.
- **2:11** Leaven and honey were both forms of fermentation, and neither was burned on the altar.
- **2:13** "The salt of the covenant" speaks of permanence. Salt preserves, and sharing salt sealed an agreement.
- **3:1** "Peace offering" is Hebrew *shelamim*, from *shalom*. It is also called a fellowship or well-being offering. The worshipper shared in the meal.
- **3:9** "The fat tail" refers to the broad tail of the sheep common in the region.
- **3:17** The fat and the blood belong to the LORD alone. See Leviticus 17:10-14.
- **4:2** "Without meaning to" is Hebrew *bishgagah*, which includes error, ignorance and carelessness, but not deliberate defiance.
- **4:3** "Sin offering" is Hebrew *chatta't*, which cleanses a person or the sanctuary from the effects of wrongdoing. See entry H-CHATTAT. "The anointed priest" is the high priest.
- **4:6** The seven-fold sprinkling is directed towards the curtain that screens the ark.
- **4:12** Burning the remains outside the camp marks them as unfit for the altar, because they carry the impurity of the sin.
- **4:22** "Leader" is Hebrew *nasi*, a tribal head or prince.
- **5:1** The public summons is an oath of adjuration, which required a witness to speak.
- **5:6** "Compensation" is Hebrew *asham*, which also names the guilt offering.
- **5:15** "Guilt offering" is Hebrew *asham*. It covers an offence against the LORD's holy things and wrongs that need restitution. See entry H-ASHAM. "Unfaithful" is Hebrew *ma'al*, a breach of trust.
- **5:16** Adding a fifth means paying a twenty per cent penalty.
- **6:1** Chapter 6 verses 1-7 are numbered as 5:20-26 in the Hebrew Bible, so the Hebrew chapter 6 begins at the English verse 8.
- **6:9** "Teaching" is Hebrew *torah*. The word means instruction or direction.
- **6:10** The priests wore linen garments for the altar duties, and changed when carrying the ashes outside.
- **6:16** The courtyard of the tent of meeting is the enclosure around the tabernacle.
- **6:20** The grain offering for the high priest was offered daily and burned completely.
- **6:27** "Whatever touches ... will become holy" means holiness is transferred by contact, and calls for care.
- **6:28** A clay pot absorbs the meat, so it must be broken. Bronze can be scoured.
- **7:12** "Thank offering" is Hebrew *todah*, offered with praise and gratitude.
- **7:13** Leavened bread was allowed with the thank offering because it was eaten, but not burned on the altar.
- **7:18** "Foul" is Hebrew *piggul*, meat that has become unfit.
- **7:20** "Cut off" is Hebrew *karet*. It may mean exclusion from the community or divine judgement. Its exact sense is debated.
- **7:30** "Elevation offering" is Hebrew *tenufah*, often rendered "wave offering". The act is raising or presenting the portion before the LORD.
- **7:34** "Contribution" is Hebrew *terumah*, a portion set apart for the priests.
- **7:37** "Installation offering" is Hebrew *millu'im*. It refers to the offering used to install the priests (Leviticus 8).
- **8:2** The installation of the priests carries out the instructions given in Exodus 29. See Exodus 28 and 29.
- **8:8** The Urim and the Thummim are objects used to receive God's answers. Their form and use are unknown. See entry H-URIM.
- **8:15** "Purify" means to cleanse the altar, as in Exodus 29:36. The altar itself had to be made fit for use.
- **8:23** The blood on the right ear, thumb and big toe may mark the whole person as set apart, in hearing, action and walking.
- **8:27** "Elevation offering" is Hebrew *tenufah*, often rendered "wave offering".
- **8:33** The seven days of installation recall the seven days of creation and the seven-day pattern of the festivals.
- **9:1** The eighth day follows the seven days of installation. The priests begin their service on this day.
- **9:6** "Glory" is Hebrew *kavod*, the visible splendour of God.
- **9:22** The words of the priestly blessing are not given here. See Numbers 6:24-26.
- **9:24** "Fire came out from before the LORD" marks God's acceptance of the first offering. It is also the same sort of fire as in 10:2.
- **10:1** "Unauthorised fire" is literally "strange fire" (Hebrew *esh zarah*). The text does not say exactly what was wrong. It may have been the wrong fire, the wrong time or the wrong place, but the key point is that the LORD "had not commanded" it.
- **10:3** "Those who are near me" means the priests, who approach God. Aaron's silence is a sign of grief and submission.
- **10:6** Loose hair and torn clothes were signs of mourning. They were forbidden to the priests on duty, as in 21:10.
- **10:9** The ban on wine and strong drink applies to the priests when on duty, to keep them clear-headed to teach.
- **10:16** The goat of the sin offering was burned rather than eaten, so Moses questions Eleazar and Ithamar.
- **10:17** "To carry the guilt" means the priests, by eating, took on the task of dealing with the people's offence. See Hebrew *nasa avon*.
- **10:19** Aaron's answer is brief, and it may mean that after the loss of his sons he was unfit to eat the holy portion.
- **10:20** "He was satisfied" is literally "it was good in his eyes".
- **11:2** "Clean" and "unclean" are Hebrew *tahor* and *tamei*. They describe a ritual status that decides who may approach the sanctuary or share in worship. They are not a judgement about moral character. See entry H-TAMEH.
- **11:5** "Hyrax" is Hebrew *shafan*, a small rock-dwelling mammal. Older translations use "rock badger" or "coney".
- **11:6** The hare is said to "bring up the cud" because of its chewing movement. Modern classification differs.
- **11:10** "Detestable" is Hebrew *sheqets*, something to be kept away from and not eaten.
- **11:13-19** The identification of many of these birds is uncertain, and ancient and modern lists differ. The bat is included here because it flies, although it is not a bird in modern classification.
- **11:22** The four kinds of locust and grasshopper are uncertain. The exact insects cannot be identified with confidence.
- **11:29-30** The names of the swarming creatures, mostly small reptiles and rodents, are uncertain.
- **11:32** Washing in water removes the uncleanness at evening. The rule shows that uncleanness could be removed, and was usually temporary.
- **11:44** "Holy" is Hebrew *qadosh*. See entry H-QADOSH. The command "be holy, for I am holy" is the heart of the food laws and recurs in chapters 19 and 20.
- **12:2** Uncleanness after birth is a ritual status. It is not a statement that birth or motherhood is sinful.
- **12:3** Circumcision on the eighth day repeats the instruction given to Abraham (Genesis 17:12).
- **12:5** The reason for the doubled period after the birth of a girl is not given.
- **12:6** The sin offering was for cleansing, not for confession of wrongdoing. See Luke 2:22-24.
- **13:2** "Skin disease" is Hebrew *tsara'at*. It is not the same as the modern disease called leprosy (Hansen's disease), and it covers a range of skin conditions and also mould on cloth and houses. See entry H-TSARAAT. "Swelling", "scab" and "bright spot" are Hebrew *se'et*, *sappachat* and *baheret*, terms whose exact meaning is uncertain.
- **13:4** "Keep apart" is Hebrew *sagar*, "to shut up".
- **13:12-13** A person wholly covered in white is declared clean, probably because the condition was no longer active.
- **13:30** "Scall" is Hebrew *netek*, a skin disease of the head or beard.
- **13:45** Torn clothes, loose hair and the covered lip were signs of mourning. The cry "Unclean!" warned others to keep away.
- **13:46** Living outside the camp was meant to protect the holiness of the camp where God dwelt.
- **13:47** "Mould" renders the same word *tsara'at* when it affects cloth or leather. It is probably a mildew or fungus.
- **14:4** Cedar, crimson yarn and hyssop were used in cleansing rites. See also Numbers 19:6.
- **14:7** The release of the live bird shows the disease being carried away.
- **14:10** A log is about 0.3 litres.
- **14:14** The blood on the right ear, thumb and big toe repeats the rite used to install the priests in 8:23.
- **14:34** The rules for mould in houses apply only after the entry into the land of Canaan, when Israel would live in houses.
- **14:53** Cleansing the house is called "atonement" for it, which shows the word means cleansing as well as forgiveness.
- **15:2** "Discharge" is Hebrew *zov*, a flow from the body, probably from the genitals. The exact condition is not specified.
- **15:19** The monthly uncleanness is Hebrew *niddah*, a ritual state during menstruation.
- **15:25** The text distinguishes the normal period from a longer abnormal flow of blood.
- **15:31** Uncleanness endangered the sanctuary if an unclean person approached it. The rules protect the holiness of the tabernacle.
- **16:1** The chapter looks back to the death of Nadab and Abihu in 10:1-2, and explains how the high priest may safely approach God's presence.
- **16:2** "The Holy Place, inside the curtain" is the innermost room, also called the Most Holy Place. "Cover of atonement" is Hebrew *kapporet*, the lid of the ark. See entry H-KAPPORET.
- **16:3** The rite described here is the Day of Atonement, in Hebrew *Yom Kippur*, "day of covering".
- **16:4** On this day the high priest wears plain linen garments, not the richly woven garments of chapter 28. This may express humility.
- **16:8** "Azazel" is a Hebrew name of uncertain meaning. It may be the name of a place or of a desert power, or it may mean "the goat that goes away". Casting lots left the choice of the goats to the LORD. See entry H-AZAZEL.
- **16:10** The live goat is not offered as a sacrifice. It is sent away, carrying the people's guilt out of the camp.
- **16:16** The sanctuary itself needs cleansing because the people's uncleanness and rebellion have polluted it.
- **16:21** Laying both hands on the goat's head and confessing over it transfers the guilt to the goat.
- **16:22** "A barren land" is Hebrew *eretz gezerah*, a land cut off.
- **16:29** "Humble yourselves" is Hebrew *innah nefesh*, "afflict the self", understood as fasting. The seventh month is Tishri, in September or October.
- **16:31** "A Sabbath of complete rest" is Hebrew *shabbat shabbaton*. See entry H-SHABBAT.
- **17:1** Chapters 17 to 26 are often called the Holiness Code, because of their stress on the call to be holy.
- **17:4** Killing an animal for meat away from the sanctuary was treated as shedding blood, because every animal's life belonged to God.
- **17:7** "Goat-demons" is Hebrew *se'irim*, "hairy ones", spirit beings thought to take the form of goats. Some read the word simply as "goats".
- **17:10** "Set my face against" means to turn against someone in judgement.
- **17:11** "Life" is Hebrew *nefesh*. See entry H-NEPHESH. "Blood" is Hebrew *dam*. See entry H-DAM. The last clause can also be read "for the blood atones by means of life".
- **17:13** Covering the blood with earth treated it with respect, even in hunting.
- **17:15** The rule about eating a carcass is for the ordinary Israelite. The priests are not permitted it at all (22:8).
- **18:3** The chapter sets Israel's way of life against the customs of Egypt, where they had lived, and of Canaan, where they were going.
- **18:5** "Will live by them" means that following the LORD's statutes is the path to life.
- **18:6** "Uncover nakedness" is a Hebrew idiom for sexual relations. "Close relative" is literally "flesh of his flesh".
- **18:9** "Born in the same house or elsewhere" is a Hebrew way of covering full and half sisters, whether born into the household or outside it.
- **18:17** "Shameful act" is Hebrew *zimmah*, a word for a serious, deliberate wrong.
- **18:18** The wording is difficult. It forbids marrying two sisters at the same time, so that they become rivals.
- **18:21** "Molek" is the name of a god to whom children were offered. The meaning of "passed over" is debated, and may refer to a rite by fire. See also 20:2-5.
- **18:22** The Hebrew is literally "with a male you are not to lie the lyings of a woman". "Abhorrent" is Hebrew *to'evah*, something the LORD finds repugnant. The exact scope of the command is discussed by scholars, and the text addresses men and does not mention women.
- **18:23** "Perversion" is Hebrew *tevel*, a mixing of what should be kept separate.
- **18:25** The land is spoken of as a living thing that "spews out" its inhabitants, a figure for judgement.
- **19:2** "Holy" is Hebrew *qadosh*. See entry H-QADOSH. This is the heart of the Holiness Code.
- **19:3** "Revere" is Hebrew *yare*, the word usually rendered "fear". It is used here of parents and in verse 14 of God.
- **19:9** The commands to leave the edges of the field and fallen grapes made provision for the poor and the foreigner. See Ruth 2.
- **19:16** "Stand by while your neighbour's life is at stake" is literally "do not stand on the blood of your neighbour".
- **19:17** "Reason frankly" is Hebrew *yakach*, to argue a case or to rebuke. The aim is to put things right without hatred.
- **19:18** "Love" is Hebrew *ahav*. See entry H-AHAV. "Neighbour" is Hebrew *rea*, a fellow member of the people. "As yourself" may also be read "for he is like you".
- **19:19** The ban on mixing kinds may express the order of creation and the need to keep things distinct.
- **19:20** This verse is difficult. The Hebrew word translated "inquiry" (*biqqoret*) is uncertain and may mean "compensation".
- **19:23** "Forbidden" is literally "uncircumcised", used figuratively of fruit that may not be used.
- **19:27** The hair and beard customs were probably linked to mourning rites of other peoples.
- **19:31** "Spirit-consulters" is Hebrew *yidde'onim*, those who claimed to know hidden things through spirits.
- **19:34** "Foreigner" is Hebrew *ger*, a resident alien. See entry H-GER. This is the second "love as yourself" command in the chapter.
- **20:2** The death penalty for giving a child to Molek shows how serious the offence was.
- **20:13** The wording matches 18:22, with a penalty added. See the note on that verse.
- **20:14** Burning is a harsh penalty used rarely. The shameful act is described as *zimmah*.
- **20:17** "Disgrace" is Hebrew *chesed*, a word that elsewhere means faithful love (see entry H-HESED). Here it has the opposite sense, "shame".
- **20:20** "They will die childless" may mean they will have no surviving children.
- **20:25** The distinction of clean and unclean animals is a sign of Israel's being set apart. See chapter 11.
- **20:27** "Medium" is Hebrew *ov*. See also Exodus 22:18 and 1 Samuel 28.
- **21:1** Contact with a dead body caused uncleanness (see Numbers 19). Priests had to avoid it because they served at the sanctuary, except for the closest relatives.
- **21:4** The Hebrew of this verse is very difficult and its meaning is uncertain. It may refer to a priest being made unclean for relatives by marriage, or to his being made unclean as a husband or a person of standing among his people.
- **21:5** The shaving and cutting were mourning customs of other peoples. See Leviticus 19:27-28.
- **21:6** "Holy" is Hebrew *qadosh* (see entry H-QADOSH). "Profane" is Hebrew *chalal*, "to make common" (see entry H-CHALAL), the opposite of making holy.
- **21:7** The marriage rules for priests were stricter than for other Israelites, and stricter again for the high priest (verses 13-14).
- **21:9** The penalty of burning is severe. It may have been applied after execution. The offence of a priest's daughter reflects on her father, who represents the holy.
- **21:10** The rules for the high priest repeat those from 10:6-7 about mourning and avoiding the dead.
- **21:12** "He is not to leave the sanctuary" probably means he is not to abandon his duties because of a death.
- **21:17** "Defect" is Hebrew *mum*, "blemish". The same word is used of animals for offering.
- **21:18-20** The terms for the physical conditions are uncertain. Several words occur only here, and the translation of each is tentative.
- **21:22** A priest with a defect may not serve at the altar, but he may still eat the holy food. He remains a full member of the priestly family.
- **21:23** "Sanctuaries" is plural in the Hebrew, and refers to the holy places of the tabernacle.
- **22:2** "Holy gifts" are the portions given to God and to the priests. They must be handled with care.
- **22:3** "Cut off from before me" means removed from God's presence.
- **22:4** The same conditions as in chapters 13 and 15 bar a priest from the holy food until he is clean.
- **22:10** "Outsider" is Hebrew *zar*, someone not of the priest's household.
- **22:11** "Buys a person" refers to a slave in the priest's household, who may share the food.
- **22:13** A widowed or divorced daughter without children returns to her father's household and may eat again.
- **22:19** The male animal without defect was the standard for burnt offerings.
- **22:23** An animal with a limb too long or short may be given as a freewill gift, but not to fulfil a vow.
- **22:24** The ban on offering injured or castrated animals reflects the requirement of wholeness.
- **22:27** The eighth day for a newborn animal parallels the eighth day for a newborn child in 12:3.
- **22:28** The command not to kill an animal and its young on the same day expresses care for animals. See also Deuteronomy 22:6-7.
- **22:32** "Treated as holy" means honoured in the way the LORD's name deserves. The command to the people closes with the LORD's own claim, "I am the LORD who makes you holy".
- **23:2** "Appointed festivals" is Hebrew *mo'adim*, set times. "Sacred assemblies" is Hebrew *miqra qodesh*, a gathering called by God.
- **23:3** The Sabbath is the first of the appointed times, before the yearly festivals. See entry H-SHABBAT.
- **23:5** Passover falls on the fourteenth day of the first month, called Abib (Nisan), in March or April.
- **23:10** The sheaf of first grain marks the start of the barley harvest. Nothing from the new harvest may be eaten until it is offered.
- **23:11** "The day after the Sabbath" is debated. It may mean the day after the first day of Unleavened Bread, or the day after the weekly Sabbath in that week. This affects the count to the Festival of Weeks.
- **23:16** "Fifty days" gives the later name "Pentecost", from the Greek for "fiftieth". The Festival of Weeks marks the wheat harvest.
- **23:17** The two loaves for the Festival of Weeks are baked with yeast. This is an exception to the rule that no yeast goes on the altar.
- **23:24** "A remembrance marked by trumpet blasts" is later known as the festival of the new year.
- **23:27** "Humble yourselves" is Hebrew *anah nefesh*, "afflict the self", understood as fasting. See Leviticus 16.
- **23:34** "Booths" is Hebrew *sukkot*. It is the harvest festival at the end of the year.
- **23:40** "The fruit of splendid trees" is a difficult phrase. Later Jewish tradition took it as the citron, together with palm, myrtle and willow.
- **24:2** The lamp oil rule repeats Exodus 27:20-21.
- **24:5** The twelve loaves stand for the twelve tribes. The Hebrew name is "bread of the Presence" (Exodus 25:30).
- **24:11** "Blasphemed the Name" refers to the LORD's own name. The Hebrew verb can mean "pierced" or "pronounced", and some read it as speaking the name wrongly.
- **24:14** Laying hands on the offender's head may have passed the guilt of the curse back to him, and made the witnesses part of the judgement.
- **24:19** The law of equal retaliation limits punishment to match the harm. See also Exodus 21:23-25.
- **24:22** "One rule for the foreigner and the native-born" makes the law the same for both groups.
- **25:2** "The land itself" keeping a Sabbath is the origin of the sabbatical year, or *shemitta*.
- **25:9** "Ram's horn" is Hebrew *shofar*. The Jubilee begins on the Day of Atonement.
- **25:10** "Release" is Hebrew *deror*, a word for freedom, as in setting free a captive. See entry H-DEROR. "Jubilee" is Hebrew *yovel*, from the word for a ram's horn. See entry H-YOVEL.
- **25:23** "The land is mine" is the basis of the whole system. The people are "foreigners and tenants", not owners.
- **25:25** "Redeem" is Hebrew *ga'al*, to buy back what has been lost. The nearest relative is the *go'el*, the kinsman-redeemer. See entry H-GAAL.
- **25:29** The special rule for houses in walled cities treats them as ordinary property, not as part of the family land.
- **25:35** "Support" is literally "make strong". The foreigner and resident were protected members of the community.
- **25:36** "Interest" and "profit" translate two Hebrew words, *neshekh* and *tarbit*.
- **25:39** "Sells himself" describes an Israelite who has fallen into debt and enters service. He is to be treated as a hired worker and freed in the Jubilee.
- **25:42** "They are my servants" is the reason Israelites cannot be sold as slaves. They already belong to God.
- **25:44-46** The rule for slaves from other nations differs from the rule for Israelites. It allows them to be held permanently and inherited. The contrast is plain in the text.
- **25:47-55** The chapter ends with the right to redeem Israelites who have sold themselves to a foreigner, and returns to the reason in verse 55.
- **26:1** "Standing stone" is Hebrew *matstsevah*, a pillar used in worship. The "stone carved with figures" is *maskit*, an uncertain term.
- **26:3** Chapter 26 follows the shape of a covenant: blessings for obedience, curses for disobedience, and a promise of restoration. Compare Deuteronomy 28.
- **26:9** "Covenant" is Hebrew *berit*. See entry H-BERIT.
- **26:11** "My dwelling" is Hebrew *mishkani*, related to the word for the tabernacle.
- **26:12** "I will walk among you" recalls God walking in the garden in Genesis 3:8.
- **26:13** "Made you walk upright" is literally "walk with raised head", the opposite of a bent yoke.
- **26:16** "Sudden terror" is Hebrew *behalah*. "Wasting disease" and "fever" are *shachefet* and *qaddachat*, whose exact meaning is uncertain.
- **26:18** "Seven times" is a refrain that grows in force at each stage (verses 18, 21, 24, 28). Seven suggests completeness.
- **26:19** Sky like iron and earth like bronze describes a drought, with the sky hard and giving no rain.
- **26:21** "Walk in opposition" translates Hebrew *qeri*, a rare word that occurs in this chapter (verses 21-41). Its meaning is uncertain. It has been read as "opposition", "hostility", "in a haphazard way" or "by chance".
- **26:25** "The vengeance of the covenant" refers to the penalties that go with breaking the covenant.
- **26:26** Ten women baking in one oven shows how little bread there will be.
- **26:29** Eating the flesh of one's own children describes the horror of a siege. See Deuteronomy 28:53-57 and 2 Kings 6:28-29.
- **26:30** "Incense stands" is Hebrew *chammanim*, objects used in worship at high places.
- **26:34** The land "enjoying its Sabbaths" recalls the sabbatical year in chapter 25. See 2 Chronicles 36:21.
- **26:36** The sound of a driven leaf shows how fear will make even slight noises terrifying.
- **26:41** "Uncircumcised hearts" means hearts that are closed and unresponsive.
- **26:42** The covenants are listed in the unusual order Jacob, Isaac, Abraham, with Abraham last.
- **26:44** Even in judgement, the LORD promises not to destroy his people completely.
- **27:2** "A special vow" is Hebrew *neder pele*. "Valuation" is Hebrew *erekh*, the sum set by the priest to redeem a person dedicated to God by a vow.
- **27:3** The sums are in shekels of silver. The shekel is about 11 grams.
- **27:9** An animal that is vowed becomes holy, and cannot be exchanged.
- **27:16** A homer is about 220 litres.
- **27:21** A field that is not redeemed goes to the priests, in place of the original owner.
- **27:26** A firstborn animal already belongs to the LORD, so it cannot also be vowed.
- **27:28** "Devoted" is Hebrew *cherem*, something given over to God completely and permanently, and not available for ordinary use. See entry H-CHEREM.
- **27:29** The rule about a person devoted to destruction is not explained. It may refer to a person under the ban in war or after a judgement. The text gives no details.
- **27:30** "Tithe" is Hebrew *ma'aser*, "a tenth".
- **27:32** "Passes under the rod" describes counting sheep one by one as they pass the shepherd's staff.
- **27:34** The closing sentence ties the whole book to Mount Sinai.
