# WABV: 1 Timothy

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[G-EKKLESIA\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. First Timothy is the fifteenth book of the New Testament in the order that the WABV text follows, and the tenth of the letters, and the first of the three called the pastoral letters (with 2 Timothy and Titus). It is written in Greek, and the WABV text is drafted from a critical Greek text, with the Byzantine tradition noted in the footnotes where it differs, as the Charter requires. It is a letter from Paul to Timothy, left at Ephesus (1:3) while Paul goes on to Macedonia, about the teaching of the assembly, its worship, its leaders and its members. The three pastoral letters share a vocabulary that is not found in the other letters of Paul (*eusebeia*, "godliness"; *pistos ho logos*, "the saying is trustworthy"; *hugiainousa didaskalia*, "sound teaching"; *episkopos*, "overseer"; *presbuteros*, "elder"; *soter*, "Saviour", of God and of Christ), and their authorship is debated: some scholars hold them to be Paul, and some a close follower of Paul writing after his death, and the WABV text does not decide. Several passages are among the most debated in the New Testament, and they are rendered as written, with the Greek and the main alternatives in the footnotes and no comment in the text: the words on women in the worship of the assembly (2:8-15), the qualifications of overseers and servants (3:1-13), the widows (5:3-16) and the slaves (6:1-2). The chapter and verse numbering is the usual numbering. The WABV text of 1 Timothy is drafted with the voice standard (Part 46 of the review package), and it sets the hymn of 3:16 in lines. Where the text of a critical edition is not certain, the draft says so in the footnote and asks the team to confirm the reading. It makes a number of fixed choices that follow from its Greek, and they are written down here so that they are used throughout. *Ekklesia* is rendered "assembly" and is tagged G-EKKLESIA, and the team should decide between "assembly" and "church". *Episkopos* is rendered "overseer", *diakonos* "servant" and *presbuteros* "elder", and the footnote records "bishop", "deacon" and "presbyter". *Eusebeia* is rendered "godliness". *Soter* is rendered "Saviour". *Hagios* is rendered "holy", and is tagged G-HAGIOS. *Pistis* is rendered "faith" and *pisteuo* "believe", and both are tagged G-PISTIS. *Agape* is rendered "love" and is tagged G-AGAPE. *Charis* is rendered "grace" and is tagged G-CHARIS. *Eleos* is rendered "mercy" and is tagged G-ELEOS. *Eirene* is rendered "peace" and is tagged G-EIRENE. *Euangelion* is rendered "good news" and is tagged G-EUANGELION. *Doxa* is rendered "glory" and is tagged G-DOXA. *Sozo* is rendered "save" and is tagged G-SOZO. *Zoe* is rendered "life" and is tagged G-ZOE. *Lutron* and *antilutron* (2:6) are rendered "ransom" and are tagged G-LYTRON. *Nomos* is rendered "law" and is tagged G-NOMOS. *Aletheia* is rendered "truth" and is tagged G-ALETHEIA. *Pneuma* is rendered "Spirit" or "spirit" and is tagged G-PNEUMA. *Anthropos* is rendered "person", "people" or "human being". *Adelphoi* is rendered "brothers", and the footnote records that in many places it may include the sisters, and the team should decide. *Christos* is rendered "Christ" and is tagged G-CHRISTOS. The headings of the parts are not in the Greek. The markers beginning with G- (for Greek words) are defined in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. The wording of the WABV text is drafted from the Greek and is not taken from any other English translation.*

## Part One: Chapters 1 to 3, the charge, prayer, worship, overseers and servants

### Chapter 1

**1** Paul, an apostle of Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] Jesus by the command of God our Saviour and of Christ Jesus our hope,

**2** to Timothy, my true child in the faith \[G-PISTIS\]: grace \[G-CHARIS\], mercy \[G-ELEOS\] and peace \[G-EIRENE\] from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord.

**3** As I urged you when I was going to Macedonia, remain at Ephesus so that you may charge certain persons not to teach any different doctrine,

**4** nor to devote themselves to myths and endless genealogies, which promote speculations rather than the stewardship from God that is by faith.

**5** The aim of our charge is love \[G-AGAPE\] that issues from a pure heart and a good conscience and a sincere faith \[G-PISTIS\].

**6** Certain persons, by swerving from these, have wandered away into vain discussion,

**7** desiring to be teachers of the law \[G-NOMOS\], without understanding either what they are saying or the things about which they make confident assertions.

**8** Now we know that the law is good, if one uses it lawfully,

**9** understanding this, that the law is not laid down for the just but for the lawless and disobedient, for the ungodly and sinners, for the unholy and profane, for those who strike their fathers and mothers, for murderers,

**10** the sexually immoral, men who lie with men, enslavers, liars, perjurers, and whatever else is contrary to sound teaching,

**11** in accordance with the glorious \[G-DOXA\] good news \[G-EUANGELION\] of the blessed God with which I have been entrusted.

**12** I thank him who has given me strength, Christ Jesus our Lord, because he judged me faithful, appointing me to his service,

**13** though formerly I was a blasphemer, a persecutor, and a violent opponent. But I received mercy \[G-ELEOS\] because I had acted ignorantly in unbelief,

**14** and the grace \[G-CHARIS\] of our Lord overflowed for me with the faith \[G-PISTIS\] and love \[G-AGAPE\] that are in Christ Jesus.

**15** The saying is trustworthy and deserves full acceptance, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save \[G-SOZO\] sinners, of whom I am the foremost.

**16** But I received mercy \[G-ELEOS\] for this reason, that in me, as the foremost, Jesus Christ might display his perfect patience as an example to those who were to believe \[G-PISTIS\] in him for eternal life \[G-ZOE\].

**17** To the King of the ages, immortal, invisible, the only God, be honour and glory \[G-DOXA\] for ever and ever. Amen.

**18** This charge I entrust to you, Timothy, my child, in accordance with the prophecies previously made about you, that by them you may wage the good warfare,

**19** holding faith \[G-PISTIS\] and a good conscience. By rejecting this, some have made shipwreck of their faith,

**20** among whom are Hymenaeus and Alexander, whom I have handed over to Satan that they may learn not to blaspheme.

### Chapter 2

**1** First of all, then, I urge that supplications, prayers, intercessions, and thanksgivings be made for all people,

**2** for kings and all who are in high positions, that we may lead a peaceful and quiet life, godly and dignified in every way.

**3** This is good, and it is pleasing in the sight of God our Saviour,

**4** who desires all people to be saved \[G-SOZO\] and to come to the knowledge of the truth \[G-ALETHEIA\].

**5** For there is one God, and there is one mediator between God and people, the human being Christ Jesus,

**6** who gave himself as a ransom \[G-LYTRON\] for all, the testimony given at the proper time.

**7** For this I was appointed a preacher and an apostle (I am telling the truth, I am not lying), a teacher of the nations in faith \[G-PISTIS\] and truth \[G-ALETHEIA\].

**8** I desire then that in every place the men should pray, lifting holy \[G-HAGIOS\] hands without anger or quarrelling;

**9** likewise also that women should adorn themselves in respectable apparel, with modesty and self-control, not with braided hair and gold or pearls or costly attire,

**10** but with what is proper for women who profess godliness, with good works.

**11** Let a woman learn quietly with all submissiveness.

**12** I do not permit a woman to teach or to exercise authority over a man; rather, she is to remain quiet.

**13** For Adam was formed first, then Eve;

**14** and Adam was not deceived, but the woman was deceived and became a transgressor.

**15** Yet she will be saved \[G-SOZO\] through childbearing, if they continue in faith \[G-PISTIS\] and love \[G-AGAPE\] and holiness \[G-HAGIOS\], with self-control.

### Chapter 3

**1** The saying is trustworthy: if anyone aspires to the office of overseer, he desires a noble task.

**2** Therefore an overseer must be above reproach, the husband of one wife, sober-minded, self-controlled, respectable, hospitable, able to teach,

**3** not a drunkard, not violent but gentle, not quarrelsome, not a lover of money.

**4** He must manage his own household well, with all dignity keeping his children submissive,

**5** for if someone does not know how to manage his own household, how will he care for God's assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\]?

**6** He must not be a recent convert, or he may become puffed up with conceit and fall into the condemnation of the devil.

**7** Moreover, he must be well thought of by outsiders, so that he may not fall into disgrace, into a snare of the devil.

**8** Servants likewise must be dignified, not double-tongued, not addicted to much wine, not greedy for dishonest gain.

**9** They must hold the mystery of the faith \[G-PISTIS\] with a clear conscience.

**10** And let them also be tested first. Then let them serve as servants if they prove themselves blameless.

**11** Women likewise must be dignified, not slanderers, but sober-minded, faithful in all things.

**12** Let servants each be the husband of one wife, managing their children and their own households well.

**13** For those who serve well as servants gain a good standing for themselves and also great confidence in the faith \[G-PISTIS\] that is in Christ Jesus.

**14** I hope to come to you soon, but I am writing these things to you so that,

**15** if I delay, you may know how one ought to behave in the household of God, which is the assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\] of the living God, a pillar and buttress of the truth \[G-ALETHEIA\].

**16** Great indeed, we confess, is the mystery of godliness:\
He was manifested in the flesh,\
vindicated by the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\],\
seen by angels,\
proclaimed among the nations,\
believed \[G-PISTIS\] on in the world,\
taken up in glory \[G-DOXA\].

## Part Two: Chapters 4 to 6, false teaching, widows, elders, slaves and money

### Chapter 4

**1** Now the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] expressly says that in later times some will depart from the faith \[G-PISTIS\] by devoting themselves to deceitful spirits and teachings of demons,

**2** through the insincerity of liars whose consciences are seared,

**3** who forbid marriage and require abstinence from foods that God created to be received with thanksgiving by those who believe \[G-PISTIS\] and know the truth \[G-ALETHEIA\].

**4** For everything created by God is good, and nothing is to be rejected if it is received with thanksgiving,

**5** for it is made holy \[G-HAGIOS\] by the word of God and prayer.

**6** If you put these things before the brothers, you will be a good servant of Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] Jesus, being trained in the words of the faith \[G-PISTIS\] and of the good teaching that you have followed.

**7** Have nothing to do with irreverent, silly myths. Rather train yourself for godliness;

**8** for while bodily training is of some value, godliness is of value in every way, as it holds promise for the present life \[G-ZOE\] and also for the life to come.

**9** The saying is trustworthy and deserves full acceptance.

**10** For to this end we toil and strive, because we have our hope set on the living God, who is the Saviour of all people, especially of those who believe \[G-PISTIS\].

**11** Command and teach these things.

**12** Let no one despise you for your youth, but set the believers an example in speech, in conduct, in love \[G-AGAPE\], in faith \[G-PISTIS\], in purity.

**13** Until I come, devote yourself to the public reading of scripture, to exhortation, to teaching.

**14** Do not neglect the gift \[G-CHARIS\] you have, which was given you by prophecy when the council of elders laid their hands on you.

**15** Practise these things, devote yourself to them, so that all may see your progress.

**16** Keep a close watch on yourself and on the teaching. Persist in this, for by so doing you will save \[G-SOZO\] both yourself and your hearers.

### Chapter 5

**1** Do not rebuke an older man but encourage him as you would a father, younger men as brothers,

**2** older women as mothers, younger women as sisters, in all purity.

**3** Honour widows who are truly widows.

**4** If a widow has children or grandchildren, let them first learn to show godliness to their own household and to make some return to their parents, for this is pleasing in the sight of God.

**5** She who is truly a widow, left all alone, has set her hope on God and continues in supplications and prayers night and day,

**6** but she who is self-indulgent is dead even while she lives.

**7** Command these things as well, so that they may be without reproach.

**8** But if anyone does not provide for his relatives, and especially for members of his household, he has denied the faith \[G-PISTIS\] and is worse than an unbeliever.

**9** Let a widow be enrolled if she is not less than sixty years of age, having been the wife of one husband,

**10** and having a reputation for good works: if she has brought up children, has shown hospitality, has washed the feet of the holy ones \[G-HAGIOS\], has cared for the afflicted, and has devoted herself to every good work.

**11** But refuse to enroll younger widows, for when their passions draw them away from Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\], they desire to marry

**12** and so incur condemnation for having abandoned their former faith \[G-PISTIS\].

**13** Besides that, they learn to be idlers, going about from house to house, and not only idlers, but also gossips and busybodies, saying what they should not.

**14** So I would have younger widows marry, bear children, manage their households, and give the adversary no occasion for slander.

**15** For some have already strayed after Satan.

**16** If any believing woman has relatives who are widows, let her care for them. Let the assembly \[G-EKKLESIA\] not be burdened, so that it may care for those who are truly widows.

**17** Let the elders who rule well be considered worthy of double honour, especially those who labour in preaching and teaching.

**18** For the scripture says, "You shall not muzzle an ox when it treads out the grain," and, "The labourer deserves his wages."

**19** Do not admit a charge against an elder except on the evidence of two or three witnesses.

**20** As for those who persist in sin, rebuke them in the presence of all, so that the rest may stand in fear.

**21** In the presence of God and of Christ Jesus and of the elect angels I charge you to keep these rules without prejudging, doing nothing from partiality.

**22** Do not be hasty in the laying on of hands, nor take part in the sins of others. Keep yourself pure.

**23** (No longer drink only water, but use a little wine for the sake of your stomach and your frequent ailments.)

**24** The sins of some people are conspicuous, going before them to judgement, but the sins of others appear later.

**25** So also good works are conspicuous, and even those that are not cannot remain hidden.

### Chapter 6

**1** Let all who are under a yoke as slaves regard their own masters as worthy of all honour, so that the name of God and the teaching may not be reviled.

**2** Those who have believing masters must not be disrespectful on the ground that they are brothers. Rather they must serve all the better, since those who benefit by their good service are believers and loved \[G-AGAPE\]. Teach and urge these things.

**3** If anyone teaches a different doctrine and does not agree with the sound words of our Lord Jesus Christ and the teaching that accords with godliness,

**4** he is puffed up with conceit and understands nothing. He has an unhealthy craving for controversy and for quarrels about words, which produce envy, dissension, slander, evil suspicions,

**5** and constant friction among people who are depraved in mind and deprived of the truth \[G-ALETHEIA\], imagining that godliness is a means of gain.

**6** But godliness with contentment is great gain,

**7** for we brought nothing into the world, and we cannot take anything out of the world.

**8** But if we have food and clothing, with these we will be content.

**9** But those who desire to be rich fall into temptation, into a snare, into many senseless and harmful desires that plunge people into ruin and destruction.

**10** For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evils. It is through this craving that some have wandered away from the faith \[G-PISTIS\] and pierced themselves with many pangs.

**11** But as for you, O person of God, flee these things. Pursue righteousness \[G-DIKAIOSUNE\], godliness, faith \[G-PISTIS\], love \[G-AGAPE\], steadfastness, gentleness.

**12** Fight the good fight of the faith \[G-PISTIS\]. Take hold of the eternal life \[G-ZOE\] to which you were called and about which you made the good confession in the presence of many witnesses.

**13** I charge you in the presence of God, who gives life to all things, and of Christ Jesus, who in his testimony before Pontius Pilate made the good confession,

**14** to keep the commandment unstained and free from reproach until the appearing of our Lord Jesus Christ,

**15** which he will display at the proper time, he who is the blessed and only Sovereign, the King of kings and Lord of lords,

**16** who alone has immortality, who dwells in unapproachable light, whom no one has ever seen or can see. To him be honour and eternal dominion. Amen.

**17** As for the rich in this present age, charge them not to be haughty, nor to set their hopes on the uncertainty of riches, but on God, who richly provides us with everything to enjoy.

**18** They are to do good, to be rich in good works, to be generous and ready to share,

**19** thus storing up for themselves the treasure of a good foundation for the future, so that they may take hold of that which is truly life \[G-ZOE\].

**20** O Timothy, guard what has been entrusted to you. Avoid the irreverent babble and contradictions of what is falsely called knowledge,

**21** for by professing it some have swerved from the faith \[G-PISTIS\]. Grace \[G-CHARIS\] be with you.

In 5:11-12 "abandoned their former faith" translates *ten proten pistin ethetesan*, which may also be read "broken their first pledge", and the footnote records the matter. In 5:13 "gossips and busybodies" is rendered as written. In 5:14 "the adversary" is rendered as written. In 5:16 "any believing woman" is the reading of the critical text, and some manuscripts read "any believing man or woman", and the footnote records the matter. In 5:17 "the elders who rule well" and "double honour" are rendered as written, and the footnote records that "honour" (*times*) may mean respect or pay. In 5:18 the quotations are Deuteronomy 25:4 and Luke 10:7 (called "scripture"), and the footnote records the matter. In 5:19 "two or three witnesses" is as in Deuteronomy 19:15. In 5:21 "the elect angels" is rendered as written. In 5:22 "the laying on of hands" is rendered as written, and the footnote records that it may refer to ordination or to the restoring of those who had sinned. In 5:23 "use a little wine for the sake of your stomach" is rendered as written. In 6:1-2 "slaves" translates *douloi*, and "masters" translates *despotas*, and the passage is rendered as written, with no comment on how it is read, and the footnote records that it presupposes the institution of slavery and does not address it, and that "brothers" translates *adelphoi*. In 6:2 "those who benefit by their good service are believers and loved" is rendered as written, and the Greek is hard. In 6:3 "the sound words of our Lord Jesus Christ" is rendered as written. In 6:5 "deprived of the truth" is rendered as written, and the Byzantine tradition adds "From such withdraw yourself" at the end of the verse, and the footnote records the matter. In 6:7 "for we brought nothing into the world, and we cannot take anything out of the world" is the reading of the critical text, and the Byzantine tradition adds "it is certain that" before "we cannot", and the footnote records the matter. In 6:10 "the love of money is a root of all kinds of evils" translates *riza gar panton ton kakon estin he philarguria*, and the footnote records that it may be read "the root of all evils" or "a root of all kinds of evil", since the Greek has no article. In 6:11 "O person of God" translates *o anthrope theou*, and it is the voice standard's rendering of *anthropos*. In 6:12 "Fight the good fight of the faith" and "the good confession in the presence of many witnesses" are rendered as written, and the footnote records that the confession may be that of baptism or ordination. In 6:13 "Christ Jesus, who in his testimony before Pontius Pilate made the good confession" is rendered as written. In 6:14 "until the appearing of our Lord Jesus Christ" translates *mechri tes epiphaneias*. In 6:15-16 the doxology is rendered as written, and the footnote records that it may be a hymn, and that "King of kings and Lord of lords" is as in Revelation 19:16. In 6:17-19 "the rich in this present age" is rendered as written. In 6:20 "guard what has been entrusted to you" translates *ten parathekin phulaxon*, and "what is falsely called knowledge" translates *pseudonumou gnoseos*, and the footnote records that it is the only place where *gnosis* is used in the pastoral letters of the teaching that is opposed. In 6:21 "be with you" is the reading of the critical text (the plural *meth' humon*, "with you all"), and some manuscripts add "Amen", and the footnote records the matter. In 4:6 and 6:2 "brothers" translates *adelphoi*, and the team should decide whether it is to include the sisters. There are no departures from the critical Greek text in chapters 4 to 6. The Greek is hard in some places, including 4:1-5, 4:14, 5:3-16, 5:17-18, 6:2, 6:5 and 6:10. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the Spirit and the later times (4:1-5), the gift and the laying on of hands (4:14), the widows (5:3-16), the elders and the double honour (5:17-18), the slaves (6:1-2), the love of money (6:10), the good confession (6:12-13) and the doxology (6:15-16) are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. The section needs a check against the Greek by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked. With this the book of 1 Timothy is complete in first draft.*

## Appendix A: Footnotes

- **1:1** "God our Saviour" is used of God in the pastoral letters (1:1, 2:3, 4:10, and Titus 1:3, 2:10, 3:4) and of Christ (2 Timothy 1:10, Titus 1:4, 2:13, 3:6). See entry G-CHRISTOS.
- **1:2** "My true child in the faith" is rendered as written. See entries G-PISTIS, G-CHARIS, G-ELEOS and G-EIRENE.
- **1:4** "The stewardship from God" is the reading of the critical text (*oikonomian*), and some manuscripts read "building up" (*oikodomen*).
- **1:5** "Love" translates *agape*. See entry G-AGAPE.
- **1:7** "The law" translates *nomodidaskaloi* and *nomos*. See entry G-NOMOS.
- **1:9** The list of those for whom the law is laid down in verses 9 and 10 is rendered as written, and "men who lie with men" translates *arsenokoitai*, and "enslavers" translates *andrapodistais*. The sense of *arsenokoitai* is debated, as at 1 Corinthians 6:9, and the rendering agrees with that verse.
- **1:11** "The glorious good news of the blessed God" is rendered as written. See entries G-DOXA and G-EUANGELION.
- **1:13** "I received mercy because I had acted ignorantly in unbelief" is rendered as written. See entry G-ELEOS.
- **1:15** "The saying is trustworthy and deserves full acceptance" translates *pistos ho logos kai pases apodoches axios*, and it is one of five trustworthy sayings in the pastoral letters (1:15, 3:1, 4:9, 2 Timothy 2:11, Titus 3:8). "Of whom I am the foremost" is rendered as written. See entry G-SOZO.
- **1:16** "Eternal life" translates *zoen aionion*. See entry G-ZOE.
- **1:17** "The King of the ages, immortal, invisible, the only God" is the reading of the critical text, and the Byzantine tradition adds "wise" before "God".
- **1:20** "Whom I have handed over to Satan" is rendered as written, as in 1 Corinthians 5:5.
- **2:1** "For all people" and "for kings and all who are in high positions" are rendered as written.
- **2:4** "Who desires all people to be saved" is rendered as written. See entry G-ALETHEIA.
- **2:5** "One mediator between God and people, the human being Christ Jesus" is rendered as written, and "the human being" translates *anthropos*, and the sentence is probably a confession.
- **2:6** "Gave himself as a ransom for all" translates *antilutron huper panton*, and *antilutron* is from the same root as *lutron*. See Mark 10:45, and entry G-LYTRON.
- **2:7** "A teacher of the nations" is rendered as written.
- **2:8** The passage in verses 8 to 15 on men and women in the worship of the assembly is rendered as written, and it is one of the most debated passages in the New Testament, and the Greek has several uncertain words, and the WABV text does not decide. "The men" translates *tous andras*, the males, and "without anger or quarrelling" may be read "without doubting" (*dialogismou*). See entry G-HAGIOS.
- **2:9** "Women" and "adorn themselves" are rendered as written.
- **2:11** "Let a woman learn quietly with all submissiveness" is rendered as written.
- **2:12** "To exercise authority" translates *authentein*, a word used only here in the New Testament, which may also be read "to domineer", "to assume authority" or "to dominate". "A woman" and "a man" may be read "a wife" and "a husband".
- **2:13** "Adam was formed first, then Eve" and "the woman was deceived and became a transgressor" (verse 14) are rendered as written.
- **2:15** "She will be saved through childbearing, if they continue in faith and love and holiness, with self-control" is rendered as written, and the Greek has a change from the singular "she" to the plural "they", and may be read "saved through the childbearing" (the birth of Christ) or "saved in childbirth". See entries G-SOZO, G-PISTIS, G-AGAPE and G-HAGIOS.
- **3:1** "The saying is trustworthy" may be read with the end of chapter 2 or with the beginning of chapter 3. "Overseer" translates *episkopes*, and may be read "bishop", and the office is not yet fixed, and overseers and elders may be the same.
- **3:2** "The husband of one wife" translates *mias gunaikos andra*, and it is rendered as written, and may be read "married only once", "faithful to his wife" or "not a polygamist". The WABV text does not decide.
- **3:5** "God's assembly" may be read "the assembly of God". See entry G-EKKLESIA.
- **3:8** "Servants" translates *diakonous*, and may be read "deacons".
- **3:9** "The mystery of the faith" is rendered as written. See entry G-PISTIS.
- **3:11** "Women" translates *gunaikas*, which may be read "their wives" (the wives of the servants) or "women servants" (women who serve as deacons, as Phoebe in Romans 16:1), and the rendering "women" keeps both readings. The WABV text does not decide.
- **3:12** "Servants each the husband of one wife" is rendered as written.
- **3:13** "Gain a good standing for themselves" is rendered as written.
- **3:15** "The household of God, which is the assembly of the living God, a pillar and buttress of the truth" is rendered as written. See entries G-EKKLESIA and G-ALETHEIA.
- **3:16** The hymn is set in lines, and it may be a hymn that is quoted, and it has six lines in three pairs. "He was manifested in the flesh" is the reading of the critical text (*hos*, "who"), and the Byzantine tradition reads "God" (*theos*). "Vindicated by the Spirit" is rendered as written. See entries G-PNEUMA and G-DOXA.
- **4:1** "The Spirit expressly says" is rendered as written, and "deceitful spirits and teachings of demons" and "who forbid marriage and require abstinence from foods" (verse 3) are rendered as written. See entries G-PNEUMA and G-PISTIS.

- **4:5** "It is made holy by the word of God and prayer" is rendered as written. See entry G-HAGIOS.
- **4:6** "Servant" translates *diakonos*, and "the brothers" translates *adelphois*, which may include the sisters. See entry G-CHRISTOS.
- **4:7** "Train yourself for godliness" and "bodily training is of some value" (verse 8) are rendered as written.
- **4:9** "The saying is trustworthy and deserves full acceptance" is the fourth of the trustworthy sayings, and it may refer to verse 8 or to verse 10.
- **4:10** "We toil and strive" is the reading of the critical text (*agonizometha*), and some manuscripts read "we suffer reproach" (*oneidizometha*). "The Saviour of all people, especially of those who believe" is rendered as written.
- **4:13** "The public reading of scripture" is rendered as written.
- **4:14** "The gift" translates *charismatos*, and "the council of elders" translates *presbuteriou*, and may be read "the elders" or "the body of elders". The laying on of hands by Paul is in 2 Timothy 1:6. See entry G-CHARIS.
- **4:16** "You will save both yourself and your hearers" is rendered as written. See entry G-SOZO.
- **5:3** The passage on the widows in verses 3 to 16 is rendered as written, and the widows may be the women who are supported by the assembly or a group who serve, and the details (the age of sixty, "the wife of one husband", the washing of feet) are debated. The WABV text does not decide.
- **5:4** "To show godliness to their own household" is rendered as written.
- **5:6** "Dead even while she lives" is rendered as written.
