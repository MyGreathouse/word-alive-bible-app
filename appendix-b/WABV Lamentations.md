# WABV: Lamentations

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-HESED\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Lamentations is five poems in the Hebrew, and the whole book is poetry, set in verse lines. The Hebrew name of the book is Eikhah ("How"), the first word of chapters 1, 2 and 4. Chapters 1, 2 and 4 are acrostics: each of their twenty-two verses begins with a letter of the Hebrew alphabet, in order. Chapter 3 has sixty-six verses, in twenty-two groups of three, and each line of a group begins with the same letter. Chapter 5 has twenty-two verses and is not an acrostic. The English cannot keep the letters, and the footnotes record the order of the letters where it matters. In chapters 2, 3 and 4 the letter pe comes before ayin, and the footnotes record the matter. The WABV text follows the Hebrew text. The Greek text begins with a short prose introduction that is not in the Hebrew, and the footnotes record the matter. The chapter and verse numbering is the English numbering, and it is the same as the Hebrew. The speakers are not marked in the Hebrew, and the WABV text does not add labels. In chapter 3 a man speaks, and the WABV text does not decide who he is. The book falls into three parts in the WABV text: the city in mourning (chapters 1 and 2), the man who has seen affliction (chapter 3) and the siege remembered and the prayer for restoration (chapters 4 and 5). The headings of the parts are not in the Hebrew. The WABV text does not decide on questions of the author or the date of the book, and the footnotes record only the language. The names of God are given as in the earlier books: "the LORD" for YHWH, "Lord" for Adonai, and "the LORD of hosts" for YHWH tseva'ot. In this book Adonai is more frequent than YHWH, and the footnotes do not mark each place.*

## Part One: The City in Mourning (Chapters 1 and 2)

### Chapter 1

**1** How lonely sits the city that was full of people. She has become like a widow, she who was great among the nations. The princess among the provinces has become a forced labourer.

**2** She weeps bitterly in the night, and her tears are on her cheek. She has no one to comfort \[H-NACHAM\] her among all her lovers. All her friends have dealt treacherously with her. They have become her enemies.

**3** Judah has gone into exile after affliction and harsh servitude. She lives among the nations. She finds no resting place. All her pursuers have overtaken her in the midst of the narrow places.

**4** The roads to Zion mourn, for no one comes to the festival. All her gates are desolate. Her priests groan. Her young women are grieved, and she herself is bitter.

**5** Her foes have become the head. Her enemies prosper, for the LORD has afflicted her because of the multitude of her transgressions. Her children have gone away, captives before the foe.

**6** From the daughter of Zion all her splendour has departed. Her princes have become like deer that find no pasture. They have fled without strength before the pursuer.

**7** Jerusalem remembers, in the days of her affliction and wandering, all the precious things that were hers from days of old. When her people fell into the hand of the foe, and there was none to help her, her foes saw her and laughed at her downfall.

**8** Jerusalem sinned grievously. Therefore she has become a thing unclean. All who honoured her despise her, for they have seen her nakedness. She herself groans and turns \[H-SHUV\] her face away.

**9** Her uncleanness was in her skirts. She did not remember her end. Therefore her fall was terrible. She has no comforter \[H-NACHAM\]. "See, LORD, my affliction, for the enemy has triumphed."

**10** The foe has stretched out his hand over all her precious things. For she has seen the nations enter her sanctuary \[H-QADOSH\], those whom you forbade to enter your congregation.

**11** All her people groan as they seek bread. They trade their treasures for food to revive their lives \[H-NEPHESH\]. "See, LORD, and look, for I have become despised."

**12** "Is it nothing to you, all you who pass by? Look and see if there is any sorrow like my sorrow, which was brought on me, which the LORD inflicted on the day of his fierce anger.

**13** From on high he sent fire. Into my bones he made it descend. He spread a net for my feet. He turned me back \[H-SHUV\]. He has made me desolate, faint all the day.

**14** The yoke of my transgressions is bound by his hand. They are knit together and laid on my neck. He has made my strength fail. The Lord has given me into the hands of those I cannot withstand.

**15** The Lord has rejected all my mighty ones in my midst. He summoned an assembly against me to crush my young men. The Lord has trodden as in a winepress the young daughter Judah.

**16** For these things I weep. My eyes flow with tears, for a comforter \[H-NACHAM\] is far from me, one to revive my life \[H-NEPHESH\]. My children are desolate, for the enemy has prevailed."

**17** Zion stretches out her hands, but there is none to comfort \[H-NACHAM\] her. The LORD has commanded against Jacob that his neighbours should be his foes. Jerusalem has become a thing unclean among them.

**18** "The LORD is in the right \[H-TSEDAQAH\], for I have rebelled against his word. But hear, all you peoples, and see my suffering. My young women and my young men have gone into captivity.

**19** I called to my lovers, but they deceived me. My priests and my elders perished in the city while they sought food for themselves to revive their lives \[H-NEPHESH\].

**20** See, LORD, for I am in distress. My stomach churns. My heart is wrung within me, because I have been very rebellious. In the street the sword bereaves. In the house it is like death.

**21** They heard that I groan, with none to comfort \[H-NACHAM\] me. All my enemies have heard of my trouble. They are glad that you have done it. You will bring the day you have announced, and they will be like me.

**22** Let all their evil come before you, and deal with them as you have dealt with me because of all my transgressions. For my groans are many, and my heart is faint."

### Chapter 2

**1** How the Lord in his anger has set the daughter of Zion under a cloud. He has cast down from heaven to earth the splendour of Israel. He has not remembered his footstool in the day of his anger.

**2** The Lord has swallowed up without pity all the habitations of Jacob. In his wrath he has broken down the strongholds of the daughter of Judah. He has brought them down to the ground. He has profaned the kingdom and its rulers.

**3** He has cut down in fierce anger all the horn of Israel. He has withdrawn his right hand from before the foe. He has burned in Jacob like a flaming fire consuming all around.

**4** He has bent his bow like an enemy, with his right hand set like a foe. He has killed all who were delightful in the tent of the daughter of Zion. He has poured out his fury like fire.

**5** The Lord has become like an enemy. He has swallowed up Israel. He has swallowed up all its palaces. He has laid in ruins its strongholds, and he has multiplied in the daughter of Judah mourning and lamentation.

**6** He has laid waste his booth like a garden. He has destroyed his meeting place. The LORD has made Zion forget festival and Sabbath, and in his fierce indignation has spurned king and priest.

**7** The Lord has scorned his altar. He has disowned his sanctuary \[H-QADOSH\]. He has given into the hand of the enemy the walls of her palaces. They raised a clamour in the house of the LORD as on the day of festival.

**8** The LORD determined to lay in ruins the wall of the daughter of Zion. He stretched out the measuring line. He did not restrain his hand from destroying. He caused rampart and wall to lament. Together they languish.

**9** Her gates have sunk into the ground. He has ruined and broken her bars. Her king and princes are among the nations. The teaching \[H-TORAH\] is no more, and her prophets find no vision from the LORD.

**10** The elders of the daughter of Zion sit on the ground in silence. They have thrown dust on their heads and put on sackcloth. The young women of Jerusalem have bowed their heads to the ground.

**11** My eyes are spent with weeping. My stomach churns. My heart is poured out on the ground because of the destruction of the daughter of my people, because infants and babies faint in the streets of the city.

**12** They cry to their mothers, "Where is bread and wine?" as they faint like the wounded in the streets of the city, as their life \[H-NEPHESH\] is poured out on their mothers' bosom.

**13** What can I say for you? To what compare you, daughter of Jerusalem? To what can I liken you, that I may comfort \[H-NACHAM\] you, young daughter Zion? For your ruin is vast as the sea. Who can heal you?

**14** Your prophets have seen for you false and deceptive visions. They did not expose your iniquity to restore your fortunes \[H-SHUV\], but have seen for you oracles that are false and misleading.

**15** All who pass along the way clap their hands at you. They hiss and wag their heads at the daughter of Jerusalem: "Is this the city that was called the perfection of beauty, the joy of all the earth?"

**16** All your enemies rail against you. They hiss, they gnash their teeth. They cry, "We have swallowed her. Surely this is the day we longed for. We have found it. We have seen it."

**17** The LORD has done what he planned. He has carried out his word, which he commanded long ago. He has thrown down without pity. He has made the enemy rejoice over you and exalted the might of your foes.

**18** Their heart cried out to the Lord. Wall of the daughter of Zion, let tears run down like a torrent day and night. Give yourself no rest. Let your eyes have no respite.

**19** Arise, cry out in the night, at the beginning of the watches. Pour out your heart like water before the face of the Lord. Lift your hands to him for the lives \[H-NEPHESH\] of your children, who faint for hunger at the head of every street.

**20** See, LORD, and consider. To whom have you done this? Should women eat the fruit of their womb, the children they have held? Should priest and prophet be killed in the sanctuary of the Lord?

**21** On the ground in the streets lie the young and the old. My young women and my young men have fallen by the sword. You killed them in the day of your anger. You slaughtered them without pity.

**22** You summoned as on a festival day my terrors on every side. On the day of the anger of the LORD no one escaped or survived. Those whom I held and raised my enemy destroyed.

*Review note: Lamentations 1 and 2 were drafted in WABV wording. They are the first two poems of the book: the city that sits alone and the voice of the city that speaks of its sorrow (1:1-22), and the anger of the Lord that has torn down the city, the temple and the festival, and the cry for the children (2:1-22). Both are acrostics, with twenty-two verses of one letter each, and the footnotes record the order of the letters, with the letter pe before ayin in chapter 2. The WABV text does not mark the speakers, and the footnote on 1:9 and 1:12 records where the voice changes in the Hebrew. In 1:2, 1:9, 1:16, 1:17, 1:21 and 2:13 "comfort" and "comforter" translate forms of *nacham* and are tagged H-NACHAM. In 1:8, 1:13 and 2:14 forms of *shuv* ("turns", "turned me back", "restore your fortunes") are tagged H-SHUV. In 1:10 and 2:7 "sanctuary" translates *miqdash* and is tagged H-QADOSH. In 1:11, 1:16, 1:19, 2:12 and 2:19 "lives" and "life" translate *nefesh* and are tagged H-NEPHESH. In 1:18 "The LORD is in the right" translates *tsaddiq hu* and is tagged H-TSEDAQAH. In 2:9 "the teaching" is tagged H-TORAH. In 1:15 and 2:13 *betulah* is given as "young daughter", and the rendering is open to the team's decision. In 2:22 "my terrors on every side" is as in Jeremiah 6:25, 20:3 and 49:29, and the wording is matched. The prayer against the enemies (1:21-22) and the words on women who eat their children (2:20) are rendered as written, with no comment on the ethics. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapters 1 and 2. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 1:3, 1:8, 1:9, 1:12, 1:14, 1:20, 2:1, 2:6 and 2:18. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Two: The Man Who Has Seen Affliction (Chapter 3)

### Chapter 3

**1** I am the man who has seen affliction under the rod of his wrath.

**2** He has driven me and made me walk in darkness and not in light.

**3** Surely against me he turns his hand again and again all the day.

**4** He has made my flesh and my skin waste away. He has broken my bones.

**5** He has besieged me and surrounded me with bitterness and hardship.

**6** He has made me live in dark places like those long dead.

**7** He has walled me about so that I cannot escape. He has made my chain heavy.

**8** Though I call and cry for help, he shuts out my prayer.

**9** He has blocked my ways with hewn stones. He has made my paths crooked.

**10** He is a bear lying in wait for me, a lion in hiding.

**11** He turned aside my ways and tore me to pieces. He has made me desolate.

**12** He bent his bow and set me as a target for his arrow.

**13** He drove into my kidneys the arrows of his quiver.

**14** I have become the laughingstock of all my people, the object of their mocking song all the day.

**15** He has filled me with bitterness. He has sated me with wormwood.

**16** He has made my teeth grind on gravel. He has made me cower in ashes.

**17** My soul is bereft of peace \[H-SHALOM\]. I have forgotten what happiness is.

**18** So I say, "My endurance has perished, and my hope from the LORD."

**19** Remember my affliction and my wanderings, the wormwood and the poison.

**20** My soul \[H-NEPHESH\] continually remembers it and is bowed down within me.

**21** But this I call to mind, and therefore I have hope:

**22** The steadfast love \[H-HESED\] of the LORD never ceases. His mercies never come to an end.

**23** They are new every morning. Great is your faithfulness.

**24** "The LORD is my portion," says my soul \[H-NEPHESH\], "therefore I will hope in him."

**25** The LORD is good to those who wait for him, to the soul \[H-NEPHESH\] who seeks him.

**26** It is good to wait quietly for the salvation of the LORD.

**27** It is good for a man to bear the yoke in his youth.

**28** Let him sit alone in silence, for he has laid it on him.

**29** Let him put his mouth in the dust. Perhaps there is hope.

**30** Let him offer his cheek to the one who strikes him. Let him be filled with insults.

**31** For the Lord will not cast off for ever.

**32** Though he cause grief, he will have compassion according to the abundance of his steadfast love \[H-HESED\].

**33** For he does not afflict from his heart or grieve the children of humankind.

**34** To crush underfoot all the prisoners of the earth,

**35** to deny a person his right before the face of the Most High,

**36** to subvert a person in his lawsuit, the Lord does not approve.

**37** Who has spoken and it came to pass, unless the Lord has commanded it?

**38** Is it not from the mouth of the Most High that calamities and good come?

**39** Why should a living person complain, a man, about the punishment of his sins?

**40** Let us test and examine our ways, and return \[H-SHUV\] to the LORD.

**41** Let us lift up our hearts and hands to God in heaven:

**42** "We have transgressed and rebelled, and you have not forgiven.

**43** You have wrapped yourself in anger and pursued us, killing without pity.

**44** You have wrapped yourself with a cloud so that no prayer can pass through.

**45** You have made us scum and garbage among the peoples."

**46** All our enemies open their mouths against us.

**47** Panic and pit have come on us, devastation and ruin.

**48** My eyes flow with rivers of water because of the destruction of the daughter of my people.

**49** My eyes will flow without ceasing, without respite,

**50** until the LORD from heaven looks down and sees.

**51** My eyes bring pain to my soul \[H-NEPHESH\] for all the daughters of my city.

**52** Those who were my enemies without cause have hunted me like a bird.

**53** They ended my life in a pit and cast stones on me.

**54** Water closed over my head. I said, "I am cut off."

**55** I called on your name, LORD, from the depths of the pit.

**56** You heard my plea: "Do not close your ear to my cry for help."

**57** You came near when I called on you. You said, "Do not fear."

**58** You have pleaded the cause of my soul \[H-NEPHESH\], Lord. You have redeemed \[H-GOEL\] my life.

**59** You have seen, LORD, the wrong done to me. Judge my cause.

**60** You have seen all their vengeance, all their plots against me.

**61** You have heard their taunts, LORD, all their plots against me,

**62** the lips of my assailants and their muttering against me all the day.

**63** See their sitting and their rising. I am the object of their mocking song.

**64** You will repay them, LORD, according to the work of their hands.

**65** You will give them dullness of heart. Your curse will be on them.

**66** You will pursue them in anger and destroy them from under the heavens of the LORD.

*Review note: Lamentations 3 was drafted in WABV wording. It is the third poem of the book, and the middle one: a man who has seen affliction describes it (3:1-20), the poem turns to hope in the steadfast love of the LORD (3:21-33), a general reflection and a call to return follow (3:34-47), the man weeps for the city (3:48-51), and he remembers the pit and the deliverance and prays that the LORD will judge his cause (3:52-66). The chapter is an acrostic of twenty-two groups of three verses, and each of the three verses of a group begins with the same letter, with the letter pe before ayin (see the footnote on 2:1). The WABV text does not decide who the man is. In 3:17 "peace" is tagged H-SHALOM. In 3:20, 3:24, 3:25, 3:51 and 3:58 "soul" translates nefesh and is tagged H-NEPHESH. In 3:22 and 3:32 "steadfast love" is tagged H-HESED. In 3:40 "return" is tagged H-SHUV. In 3:58 "redeemed" translates ga'alta and is tagged H-GOEL. In 3:38 "calamities and good" translates ha-ra'ot ve-ha-tov, and the rendering of ra'ot is open to the team's decision, with Isaiah 45:7 and Amos 3:6. At 3:22 the WABV text gives "never ceases", following the Greek, the Syriac and the Latin (lo tamu), where the Hebrew text has lo tamnu, "we have not come to an end", and it departs from the Hebrew; it is the one departure in chapter 3, and it is flagged for the scholar. The words on the enemies at 3:64-66 are rendered as written, with no comment. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 3:5, 3:16, 3:34-36, 3:53, 3:56 and 3:65. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked, and the originality check comes first for 3:22-26.*

## Part Three: The Siege Remembered and the Prayer (Chapters 4 and 5)

### Chapter 4

**1** How the gold has grown dim, how the fine gold is changed. The holy stones \[H-QADOSH\] lie scattered at the head of every street.

**2** The precious sons of Zion, worth their weight in fine gold, how they are counted as earthen pots, the work of a potter's hands.

**3** Even jackals offer the breast and nurse their young, but the daughter of my people has become cruel, like the ostriches in the wilderness.

**4** The tongue of the nursing child sticks to the roof of its mouth for thirst. The children beg for bread, but no one gives to them.

**5** Those who feasted on delicacies perish in the streets. Those who were brought up in scarlet embrace ash heaps.

**6** For the punishment of the daughter of my people is greater than the punishment of Sodom, which was overthrown in a moment, though no hands were wrung for her.

**7** Her princes were purer than snow, whiter than milk. Their bodies were more ruddy than coral, their appearance like sapphire.

**8** Now their faces are blacker than soot. They are not recognised in the streets. Their skin has shrivelled on their bones. It has become dry as wood.

**9** Those slain by the sword are better off than those slain by hunger, who waste away, pierced for lack of the fruits of the field.

**10** The hands of compassionate women have boiled their own children. They became their food in the destruction of the daughter of my people.

**11** The LORD gave full vent to his wrath. He poured out his hot anger, and he kindled a fire in Zion that consumed its foundations.

**12** The kings of the earth did not believe, nor any of the inhabitants of the world, that foe or enemy could enter the gates of Jerusalem.

**13** It was for the sins of her prophets and the iniquities of her priests, who shed in the midst of her the blood of the righteous \[H-TSEDAQAH\].

**14** They wandered, blind, through the streets. They were so defiled with blood that no one could touch their garments.

**15** "Away. Unclean," people cried at them. "Away. Away. Do not touch." So they became fugitives and wanderers. People said among the nations, "They will stay with us no longer."

**16** The anger of the LORD has scattered them. He will regard them no more. No honour was shown to the priests, no favour to the elders.

**17** Our eyes failed, ever watching vainly for help. In our watching we watched for a nation that could not save.

**18** They hunted our steps so that we could not walk in our streets. Our end drew near. Our days were numbered, for our end had come.

**19** Our pursuers were swifter than the eagles in the heavens. They chased us on the mountains. They lay in wait for us in the wilderness.

**20** The breath \[H-RUACH\] of our nostrils, the LORD's anointed \[H-MASHIACH\], was captured in their pits, he of whom we said, "Under his shadow we will live among the nations."

**21** Rejoice and be glad, daughter of Edom, you who live in the land of Uz. But to you also the cup will pass. You will become drunk and strip yourself bare.

**22** The punishment of your iniquity, daughter of Zion, is complete. He will keep you in exile no longer. But your iniquity, daughter of Edom, he will punish. He will uncover your sins.

### Chapter 5

**1** Remember, LORD, what has come on us. Look and see our disgrace.

**2** Our inheritance has been turned over to strangers, our homes to foreigners.

**3** We have become orphans, fatherless. Our mothers are like widows.

**4** We must pay for the water we drink. Our wood comes at a price.

**5** Our pursuers are at our necks. We are weary. We are given no rest.

**6** We have given the hand to Egypt and to Assyria to get enough bread.

**7** Our fathers sinned and are no more, and we bear their iniquities.

**8** Slaves rule over us. There is none to deliver us from their hand.

**9** We get our bread at the peril of our lives \[H-NEPHESH\], because of the sword in the wilderness.

**10** Our skin is hot as an oven with the burning heat of famine.

**11** Women are raped in Zion, young women in the towns of Judah.

**12** Princes are hanged by their hands. No respect is shown to the elders.

**13** Young men are made to grind at the mill, and boys stagger under loads of wood.

**14** The elders have left the city gate, the young men their music.

**15** The joy of our hearts has ceased. Our dancing has turned to mourning.

**16** The crown has fallen from our head. Woe to us, for we have sinned.

**17** For this our heart is sick. For these things our eyes have grown dim,

**18** for Mount Zion, which lies desolate. Jackals prowl over it.

**19** But you, LORD, reign for ever. Your throne endures to all generations.

**20** Why do you forget us for ever, forsake us for so many days?

**21** Bring us back \[H-SHUV\] to yourself, LORD, that we may be restored \[H-SHUV\]. Renew our days as of old,

**22** unless you have utterly rejected us and are angry with us beyond measure.

*Review note: Lamentations 4 and 5 were drafted in WABV wording. In 4:1 "holy stones" translates avnei-qodesh and is tagged H-QADOSH. In 4:13 "the righteous" translates tsaddiqim and is tagged H-TSEDAQAH. In 4:17 "vainly" translates hevel, and the rendering is open to the team's decision with 2:5 and 10:3 of Jeremiah. The words on the women who boiled their children (4:10) and on the rape of women (5:11) are rendered as written, with no comment. The fall of Edom (4:21-22) is as the Hebrew has it. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 4:3, 4:7, 4:9, 4:14, 4:17, 4:20 and 5:22. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. ## Appendix A: Footnotes

- **1:1** "How" translates *eikhah*, the first word of the book in the Hebrew. The twenty-two verses of the chapter begin with the twenty-two letters of the Hebrew alphabet in order, from aleph to tav. The Greek has a short prose introduction before this verse that is not in the Hebrew. "A forced labourer" translates *la-mas*, a worker under forced labour. "A widow" translates *almanah*.
- **1:2** "To comfort her" translates *menachem lah*. See entry H-NACHAM. "Her lovers" translates *me'ahaveha*.
- **1:3** "Affliction" translates *oni*, "harsh servitude" translates *rov avodah*, and "the narrow places" translates *ben ha-metsarim*, which is hard.
- **1:4** "The festival" translates *mo'ed*.
- **1:5** "Transgressions" translates *peshaeha*.
- **1:6** "Splendour" translates *hadarah*.
- **1:7** "Her wandering" translates *merudeha*, and "her downfall" translates *mishbatteha*, which is hard and may be read "her Sabbaths".
- **1:8** "A thing unclean" translates *le-nidah*, which is hard. "Turns her face away" translates *ve-tashov achor*, from *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **1:9** "Her uncleanness" translates *tum'atah*. "A comforter" translates *menachem*. See entry H-NACHAM. The last line is spoken by the city, and the Hebrew does not mark the change of voice.
- **1:10** "Her sanctuary" translates *miqdashah*. See entry H-QADOSH. "Your congregation" translates *qahal*. See also Deuteronomy 23:3.
- **1:11** "To revive their lives" translates *lehashiv nafesh*, in which the verb is *shuv* and the noun is *nefesh*. See entry H-NEPHESH. The text is tagged for *nefesh* only. The last lines are spoken by the city.
- **1:12** "Is it nothing to you" translates *lo aleikhem*, which is hard and may be read "may it not come to you". The verses 12 to 16 are the words of the city.
- **1:13** "He turned me back" translates *heshivani achor*. See entry H-SHUV. "A net" translates *reshet*.
- **1:14** "The yoke of my transgressions is bound by his hand" translates *nisqad ol peshai be-yado*, in which the verb *nisqad* is rare and the line is hard.
- **1:15** "My mighty ones" translates *abbirai*, "an assembly" translates *mo'ed*, and "the young daughter Judah" translates *betulat bat-Yehudah*. The rendering of *betulah* is open to the team's decision.
- **1:16** "One to revive my life" translates *meshiv nafshi*. See entry H-NEPHESH. The verb is *shuv*.
- **1:17** "A thing unclean" translates *le-nidah*, as in 1:8.
- **1:18** "The LORD is in the right" translates *tsaddiq hu YHWH*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. "I have rebelled" translates *marithi*. The verses 18 to 22 are the words of the city.
- **1:19** "To revive their lives" translates *ve-yashivu et-nafsham*. See entries H-SHUV and H-NEPHESH.
- **1:20** "My stomach churns" translates *me'ai chomarmaru*, and "my heart is wrung within me" translates *nehpakh libbi be-qirbi*. "Very rebellious" translates *maro marithi*. "Bereaves" translates *teshakkel*.
- **1:21** "The day you have announced" translates *yom qarata*.
