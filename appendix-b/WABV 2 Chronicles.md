# WABV: 2 Chronicles

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-QADOSH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end.*

## 2 Chronicles 1-4: Solomon and the Building of the Temple

### Chapter 1

**1** Solomon son of David was firmly established in his kingdom, for the LORD his God was with him and made him very great.

**2** Solomon spoke to all Israel, to the commanders of thousands and of hundreds, to the judges and to all the leaders in all Israel, the heads of the fathers' houses.

**3** Then Solomon and the whole assembly with him went to the high place at Gibeon, for the tent of meeting of God, which Moses the servant of the LORD had made in the wilderness, was there.

**4** (David had brought up the ark of God from Kiriath-jearim to the place he had prepared for it, for he had pitched a tent for it in Jerusalem.)

**5** The bronze altar that Bezalel son of Uri, son of Hur, had made was also there, in front of the tabernacle of the LORD. Solomon and the assembly came to it.

**6** Solomon went up to the bronze altar before the LORD at the tent of meeting and offered a thousand burnt offerings on it.

**7** That night God appeared to Solomon and said to him, "Ask for whatever you want me to give you."

**8** Solomon said to God, "You have shown great and faithful love \[H-HESED\] to my father David, and have made me king in his place.

**9** Now, LORD God, let your word to my father David be fulfilled, for you have made me king over a people as many as the dust of the earth.

**10** Give me now wisdom and knowledge, so that I may go out and come in before this people, for who can judge this great people of yours?"

**11** God said to Solomon, "Because this was in your heart, and you did not ask for possessions, wealth or honour, or for the lives of those who hate you, and did not even ask for long life, but asked for wisdom and knowledge for yourself so that you can judge my people over whom I have made you king,

**12** wisdom and knowledge are given to you. I will also give you riches, possessions and honour, such as none of the kings before you had, and none after you will have."

**13** So Solomon came from the high place at Gibeon, from the tent of meeting, to Jerusalem. And he ruled over Israel.

**14** Solomon gathered chariots and horsemen. He had fourteen hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen, whom he stationed in the chariot towns and with the king in Jerusalem.

**15** The king made silver and gold as common in Jerusalem as stones, and cedar as plentiful as the sycamore fig trees in the lowland.

**16** Solomon's horses were imported from Egypt and from Kue, and the king's traders bought them from Kue at a price.

**17** A chariot imported from Egypt cost six hundred shekels of silver and a horse a hundred and fifty, and they were exported through the king's traders to all the kings of the Hittites and the kings of Aram.

### Chapter 2

**1** Solomon decided to build a house for the name of the LORD, and a royal palace for himself.

**2** Solomon assigned seventy thousand men to carry loads, eighty thousand to cut stone in the hill country, and three thousand six hundred to supervise them.

**3** Solomon sent word to Hiram king of Tyre: "Do for me as you did for my father David, when you sent him cedar to build a house to live in.

**4** I am about to build a house for the name of the LORD my God and to make it holy \[H-QADOSH\] to him, for burning fragrant incense before him, for the regular arrangement of the bread of the Presence, and for burnt offerings morning and evening, on the sabbaths and the new moons and the appointed festivals of the LORD our God, as ordained for ever in Israel.

**5** The house that I am to build will be great, for our God is greater than all gods.

**6** But who can build him a house, since heaven, even the highest heaven, cannot contain him? Who am I to build a house for him, except as a place to burn offerings before him?

**7** So now send me a man skilled to work in gold, silver, bronze and iron, and in purple, crimson and blue cloth, and trained in engraving, to work with the skilled men who are with me in Judah and Jerusalem, whom my father David provided.

**8** Also send me cedar, cypress and algum timber from Lebanon, for I know that your servants know how to cut timber in Lebanon. My servants will work with yours

**9** to prepare a large amount of timber for me, for the house I am to build will be great and wonderful.

**10** I will give your servants, the woodcutters who cut the timber, twenty thousand cors of ground wheat, twenty thousand cors of barley, twenty thousand baths of wine and twenty thousand baths of oil."

**11** Hiram king of Tyre answered in a letter that he sent to Solomon: "Because the LORD loves his people, he has made you king over them."

**12** Hiram also said, "Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel, who made heaven and earth, who has given King David a wise son, with insight and understanding, who will build a house for the LORD and a royal palace for himself.

**13** I am now sending a skilled man, who has understanding, Huram-abi,

**14** the son of a woman from the daughters of Dan, whose father was from Tyre. He is trained to work in gold, silver, bronze, iron, stone and wood, and in purple, blue and crimson cloth and fine linen, and to do any kind of engraving and carry out any design given to him, with your craftsmen and the craftsmen of my lord David your father.

**15** Now let my lord send to his servants the wheat and the barley, the oil and the wine that he has promised.

**16** We will cut all the timber you need from Lebanon and bring it to you in rafts by sea to Joppa, and you can take it up to Jerusalem."

**17** Solomon counted all the foreigners \[H-GER\] who were in the land of Israel, following the count that his father David had made, and there were a hundred and fifty-three thousand six hundred.

**18** He assigned seventy thousand of them to carry loads, eighty thousand to cut stone in the hill country, and three thousand six hundred to be overseers to make the people work.

### Chapter 3

**1** Solomon began to build the house of the LORD in Jerusalem on Mount Moriah, where the LORD had appeared to his father David, at the place David had prepared, on the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite.

**2** He began to build in the second month of the fourth year of his reign.

**3** These are the measurements Solomon laid down for building the house of God. The length, in cubits of the old standard, was sixty cubits, and the width twenty cubits.

**4** The porch in front of the main hall was twenty cubits long, the width of the house, and its height was a hundred and twenty. He overlaid the inside with pure gold.

**5** He lined the main hall with cypress and covered it with fine gold, and put palm trees and chains on it.

**6** He decorated the house with precious stones. The gold was gold from Parvaim.

**7** He covered the house with gold, its beams, thresholds, walls and doors, and he carved cherubim on the walls.

**8** He made the most holy place \[H-QADOSH\]. Its length, matching the width of the house, was twenty cubits, and its width was twenty cubits. He covered it with six hundred talents of fine gold.

**9** The weight of the nails was fifty shekels of gold. He also covered the upper rooms with gold.

**10** In the most holy place he made two cherubim of wood and covered them with gold.

**11** The wings of the cherubim together were twenty cubits long. One wing of the first, five cubits long, touched the wall of the house, and its other wing, five cubits long, touched the wing of the other cherub.

**12** One wing of the other cherub, five cubits long, touched the wall of the house, and its other wing, also five cubits long, joined the wing of the first cherub.

**13** The wings of these cherubim spread out twenty cubits. They stood on their feet, facing the main hall.

**14** He made the curtain of blue, purple and crimson cloth and fine linen, and worked cherubim into it.

**15** In front of the house he made two pillars, thirty-five cubits high, with a capital on top of each, five cubits high.

**16** He made chains like a necklace and put them on the tops of the pillars, and he made a hundred pomegranates and put them on the chains.

**17** He set up the pillars in front of the temple, one on the south and one on the north. The one on the south he named Jachin, and the one on the north Boaz.

### Chapter 4

**1** He made an altar of bronze, twenty cubits long, twenty cubits wide and ten cubits high.

**2** Then he made the sea of cast metal. It was round, ten cubits from rim to rim, five cubits high, and a line of thirty cubits measured it round.

**3** Under it were figures of oxen, ten to a cubit, going all round the sea. The oxen were in two rows, cast in one piece with it.

**4** It stood on twelve oxen, three facing north, three facing west, three facing south and three facing east. The sea was set on top of them, and all their hindquarters were towards the middle.

**5** It was a handbreadth thick, and its rim was made like the rim of a cup, like the flower of a lily. It held three thousand baths.

**6** He also made ten basins for washing, and put five on the right and five on the left. In them they rinsed what was used for the burnt offering, but the sea was for the priests to wash in.

**7** He made ten gold lampstands as prescribed and put them in the temple, five on the south side and five on the north.

**8** He also made ten tables and put them in the temple, five on the south side and five on the north. And he made a hundred gold bowls.

**9** He made the court of the priests and the great court and doors for the court, and covered their doors with bronze.

**10** He put the sea on the south side, towards the south-east corner.

**11** Huram also made the pots, the shovels and the bowls. So Huram finished the work that he did for King Solomon on the house of God:

**12** the two pillars, the bowls and the two capitals on top of the pillars, and the two networks to cover the two bowls of the capitals on top of the pillars,

**13** and the four hundred pomegranates for the two networks, two rows of pomegranates for each network, to cover the two bowls of the capitals on the pillars.

**14** He made the stands and the basins on the stands,

**15** the one sea and the twelve oxen under it,

**16** and the pots, the shovels, the forks and all the equipment. Huram-abi made all these of polished bronze for King Solomon for the house of the LORD.

**17** The king cast them in the plain of the Jordan, in the clay ground between Succoth and Zeredah.

**18** Solomon made all these things in such great numbers that the weight of the bronze could not be found out.

**19** Solomon also made all the furnishings that were in the house of God: the golden altar and the tables for the bread of the Presence,

**20** the lampstands of pure gold with their lamps, to burn as prescribed in front of the inner room,

**21** the flowers, the lamps and the tongs, of purest gold,

**22** the snuffers, bowls, dishes for incense and fire pans, of pure gold, and the doors of the house, the inner doors to the most holy place and the doors of the main hall, were of gold.

*Review note: 2 Chronicles 1 to 4 was drafted in WABV wording. Chapter 1 is parallel to 1 Kings 3 and 10, chapter 2 to 1 Kings 5, and chapters 3 and 4 to 1 Kings 6 and 7. The wording follows the WABV 1 Kings where the Hebrew is the same. The measurements and the technical terms for the temple are very hard in the Hebrew, and the footnotes record the main differences from Kings. Several figures differ (for example 3:4, 3:15 and 4:3). ## 2 Chronicles 5-7: The Dedication of the Temple

### Chapter 5

**1** So all the work that Solomon did for the house of the LORD was finished. Solomon brought in the things that his father David had dedicated, the silver, the gold and all the furnishings, and put them in the treasuries of the house of God.

**2** Then Solomon assembled in Jerusalem the elders of Israel and all the heads of the tribes, the leaders of the fathers' houses of the Israelites, to bring up the ark of the covenant \[H-BERIT\] of the LORD from the city of David, which is Zion.

**3** All the men of Israel assembled before the king at the festival in the seventh month.

**4** When all the elders of Israel had come, the Levites lifted the ark.

**5** They brought up the ark and the tent of meeting and all the holy \[H-QADOSH\] furnishings that were in the tent. The priests and the Levites brought them up.

**6** King Solomon and the whole community of Israel, who had gathered round him before the ark, sacrificed so many sheep and oxen that they could not be counted or numbered.

**7** Then the priests brought the ark of the covenant of the LORD to its place, in the inner room of the house, in the most holy place, under the wings of the cherubim.

**8** For the cherubim spread their wings over the place of the ark, so that they covered the ark and its poles.

**9** The poles were so long that the ends of the poles could be seen from the holy place in front of the inner room, but they could not be seen from outside. They are there to this day.

**10** There was nothing in the ark except the two tablets that Moses had put there at Horeb, where the LORD made a covenant with the Israelites when they came out of Egypt.

**11** When the priests came out of the holy place (for all the priests who were present had made themselves holy, whatever their divisions,

**12** and all the Levite singers, Asaph, Heman, Jeduthun, and their sons and relatives, dressed in fine linen, stood east of the altar with cymbals, harps and lyres, and with them a hundred and twenty priests blowing trumpets,

**13** and the trumpeters and the singers were to sound as one, to praise and give thanks to the LORD), when the sound was raised with trumpets, cymbals and other instruments, in praise of the LORD, "For he is good, for his faithful love \[H-HESED\] lasts for ever," the house, the house of the LORD, was filled with a cloud,

**14** so that the priests could not stand to do their service because of the cloud, for the glory of the LORD filled the house of God.

### Chapter 6

**1** Then Solomon said, "The LORD has said that he would live in thick darkness.

**2** I have built you a house to be your home, a place for you to live in for ever."

**3** Then the king turned and blessed the whole assembly of Israel, while the whole assembly of Israel stood.

**4** He said, "Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel, who spoke with his mouth to my father David and has fulfilled it with his hands. He said,

**5** 'From the day I brought my people out of the land of Egypt, I chose no town from all the tribes of Israel in which to build a house for my name to be there, and I chose no man to be leader over my people Israel.

**6** But I have chosen Jerusalem for my name to be there, and I have chosen David to be over my people Israel.'

**7** Now it was in the heart of my father David to build a house for the name of the LORD, the God of Israel.

**8** But the LORD said to my father David, 'You had it in your heart to build a house for my name, and you did well that it was in your heart.

**9** But you will not build the house. Your son who will be born to you will build the house for my name.'

**10** Now the LORD has kept the word that he spoke. I have taken the place of my father David and sit on the throne of Israel, as the LORD promised, and I have built the house for the name of the LORD, the God of Israel.

**11** I have put the ark there, in which is the covenant of the LORD that he made with the Israelites."

**12** Then Solomon stood before the altar of the LORD in front of the whole assembly of Israel and spread out his hands.

**13** Solomon had made a bronze platform, five cubits long, five cubits wide and three cubits high, and had set it in the middle of the court. He stood on it, and then knelt down in front of the whole assembly of Israel and spread out his hands towards heaven,

**14** and said, "LORD, God of Israel, there is no God like you in heaven or on earth, keeping covenant and showing faithful love to your servants who walk before you with all their heart.

**15** You have kept for your servant, my father David, what you promised him. You spoke with your mouth, and with your hand you have fulfilled it today.

**16** Now, LORD, God of Israel, keep for your servant, my father David, what you promised him: 'You will never lack a man to sit before me on the throne of Israel, if only your sons take care how they live, walking in my law as you have walked before me.'

**17** Now, LORD, God of Israel, let your word be confirmed, the word that you spoke to your servant David.

**18** But will God really live with human beings on the earth? Look, heaven, and the highest heaven, cannot contain you. How much less this house that I have built.

**19** Yet turn to the prayer of your servant and to his plea, LORD my God, and listen to the cry and the prayer that your servant is praying before you,

**20** that your eyes may be open day and night towards this house, the place where you said you would put your name, and that you may listen to the prayer that your servant prays towards this place.

**21** Listen to the pleas of your servant and of your people Israel when they pray towards this place. Listen from heaven, your dwelling place, and when you hear, forgive.

**22** If someone wrongs his neighbour and is made to swear an oath, and he comes and swears the oath before your altar in this house,

**23** then hear from heaven and act and judge your servants, bringing down the guilty by putting his conduct on his own head, and clearing the innocent by rewarding him according to his righteousness.

**24** When your people Israel are beaten by an enemy because they have sinned against you, and they turn back \[H-SHUV\] and acknowledge your name and pray and plead before you in this house,

**25** then hear from heaven and forgive the sin of your people Israel, and bring them back to the land that you gave to them and to their ancestors.

**26** When the sky is shut and there is no rain because they have sinned against you, and they pray towards this place and acknowledge your name and turn from their sin when you afflict them,

**27** then hear in heaven and forgive the sin of your servants, your people Israel, and teach them the good way in which they should walk, and send rain on your land, which you gave to your people as an inheritance.

**28** If there is famine in the land, if there is plague, blight, mildew, locusts or grasshoppers, if their enemies besiege them in the land at their gates, whatever disaster or sickness there is,

**29** whatever prayer or plea is made by anyone or by all your people Israel, each knowing his own pain and his own sorrow and stretching out his hands towards this house,

**30** then hear from heaven, your dwelling place, and forgive, and repay each one according to all his ways, since you know his heart (for you alone know the hearts of all human beings),

**31** so that they may fear you and walk in your ways all the days that they live in the land that you gave to our ancestors.

**32** Also when a foreigner, who is not of your people Israel, comes from a distant land for the sake of your great name and your strong hand and your outstretched arm, and comes and prays towards this house,

**33** hear from heaven, your dwelling place, and do all that the foreigner asks of you, so that all the peoples of the earth may know your name and fear you, as your people Israel do, and may know that this house that I have built is called by your name.

**34** When your people go out to battle against their enemies, wherever you send them, and they pray to you towards this town that you have chosen and the house that I have built for your name,

**35** then hear from heaven their prayer and their plea, and uphold their cause.

**36** When they sin against you (for there is no one who does not sin) and you are angry with them and hand them over to an enemy, so that they are taken captive to a land far or near,

**37** and they come to their senses in the land where they have been taken captive, and turn back and plead with you in the land of their captivity, saying, 'We have sinned, we have done wrong, we have acted wickedly,'

**38** and turn back to you with all their heart and all their soul in the land of their captivity where they were taken, and pray towards their land, which you gave to their ancestors, the town that you have chosen and the house that I have built for your name,

**39** then hear from heaven, your dwelling place, their prayer and their pleas, and uphold their cause, and forgive your people who have sinned against you.

**40** Now, my God, let your eyes be open and your ears attentive to the prayer made in this place.

**41** Now rise, LORD God, and come to your resting place, you and the ark of your strength. Let your priests, LORD God, be clothed with salvation, and let your faithful people rejoice in your goodness.

**42** LORD God, do not turn away the face of your anointed one \[H-MASHIACH\]. Remember the faithful love promised to David your servant."

### Chapter 7

**1** When Solomon finished praying, fire came down from heaven and burned up the burnt offering and the sacrifices, and the glory of the LORD filled the temple.

**2** The priests could not enter the house of the LORD, because the glory of the LORD filled the house of the LORD.

**3** When all the Israelites saw the fire come down and the glory of the LORD on the temple, they knelt with their faces to the ground on the pavement and bowed down and gave thanks to the LORD: "For he is good, for his faithful love lasts for ever."

**4** Then the king and all the people offered sacrifices before the LORD.

**5** King Solomon offered a sacrifice of twenty-two thousand oxen and a hundred and twenty thousand sheep. So the king and all the people dedicated the house of God.

**6** The priests stood at their posts, and the Levites also, with the instruments for the music of the LORD that King David had made for giving thanks to the LORD, "for his faithful love lasts for ever," whenever David offered praise through them. Opposite them the priests blew trumpets, and all Israel stood.

**7** Solomon also made holy the middle of the court in front of the house of the LORD, for there he offered the burnt offerings and the fat of the peace offerings, because the bronze altar that Solomon had made could not hold the burnt offering, the grain offering and the fat.

**8** At that time Solomon held the festival for seven days, and all Israel with him, a very great assembly, from the entrance of Hamath to the Wadi of Egypt.

**9** On the eighth day they held a closing assembly, for they had kept the dedication of the altar for seven days and the festival for seven days.

**10** On the twenty-third day of the seventh month he sent the people away to their homes, joyful and glad at heart for the good that the LORD had done for David and Solomon and for his people Israel.

**11** So Solomon finished the house of the LORD and the king's house. All that Solomon had planned to do in the house of the LORD and in his own house he carried out successfully.

**12** Then the LORD appeared to Solomon at night and said to him: "I have heard your prayer and have chosen this place for myself as a house of sacrifice.

**13** When I shut up the sky so that there is no rain, or order locusts to eat up the land, or send a plague among my people,

**14** if my people, who are called by my name, humble themselves and pray and seek my face and turn back \[H-SHUV\] from their evil ways, then I will hear from heaven and forgive their sin and heal their land.

**15** Now my eyes will be open and my ears attentive to the prayer made in this place.

**16** For now I have chosen and made this house holy, so that my name may be there for ever. My eyes and my heart will be there at all times.

**17** As for you, if you walk before me as your father David did, doing all that I have commanded you and keeping my statutes and my rules,

**18** then I will establish your royal throne, as I made a covenant with your father David: 'You will never lack a man to rule over Israel.'

**19** But if you turn away and abandon my statutes and my commands that I have set before you, and go and serve other gods and bow down to them,

**20** then I will pull you up from my land that I have given you, and this house that I have made holy for my name I will throw out of my sight, and I will make it a proverb and a taunt among all peoples.

**21** And this house, which was so high, will be shocking to everyone who passes by, and they will say, 'Why has the LORD done this to this land and to this house?'

**22** Then they will say, 'Because they abandoned the LORD, the God of their ancestors, who brought them out of the land of Egypt, and took hold of other gods and bowed down to them and served them. That is why he has brought all this disaster on them.'"

*Review note: 2 Chronicles 5 to 7 was drafted in WABV wording. The chapters are parallel to 1 Kings 8 and 9, and the wording follows the WABV 1 Kings where the Hebrew is the same. ## 2 Chronicles 8-9: Solomon's Works and Wealth

### Chapter 8

**1** At the end of twenty years, in which Solomon had built the house of the LORD and his own house,

**2** Solomon rebuilt the towns that Huram had given him, and settled Israelites in them.

**3** Solomon went to Hamath-zobah and overpowered it.

**4** He built Tadmor in the wilderness and all the storage towns that he built in Hamath.

**5** He also built Upper Beth-horon and Lower Beth-horon, fortified towns with walls, gates and bars,

**6** and Baalath, and all Solomon's storage towns, and all the towns for his chariots and the towns for his horsemen, and whatever Solomon wanted to build in Jerusalem, in Lebanon and in all the land under his rule.

**7** All the people who were left of the Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites, who were not Israelites,

**8** their descendants who were left after them in the land, whom the Israelites had not destroyed, Solomon raised as forced labour, and so they are to this day.

**9** But Solomon made no slaves of the Israelites for his work. They were his soldiers, his officers, the commanders of his chariots and horsemen.

**10** These were the chief officers of King Solomon, two hundred and fifty, who ruled over the people.

**11** Solomon brought Pharaoh's daughter up from the city of David to the house that he had built for her, for he said, "My wife must not live in the house of David king of Israel, for the places where the ark of the LORD has come are holy \[H-QADOSH\]."

**12** Then Solomon offered burnt offerings to the LORD on the altar of the LORD that he had built in front of the porch,

**13** as each day required, offering according to the command of Moses for the sabbaths, the new moons and the three annual festivals: the Festival of Unleavened Bread, the Festival of Weeks and the Festival of Booths.

**14** In keeping with the ruling of his father David, he appointed the divisions of the priests for their service, and the Levites for their duties of praise and of serving alongside the priests as each day required, and the gatekeepers in their divisions at each gate, for this was what David the man of God had commanded.

**15** They did not turn aside from the king's command to the priests and Levites in any matter, or about the treasuries.

**16** So all the work of Solomon was carried out, from the day the foundation of the house of the LORD was laid until it was finished. So the house of the LORD was completed.

**17** Then Solomon went to Ezion-geber and Eloth on the shore of the sea, in the land of Edom.

**18** Huram sent him ships and servants who knew the sea, by the hands of his own servants. They went to Ophir with Solomon's servants and brought from there four hundred and fifty talents of gold and delivered it to King Solomon.

### Chapter 9

**1** When the queen of Sheba heard of Solomon's fame, she came to Jerusalem to test him with hard questions, with a very large retinue, and with camels carrying spices, a great deal of gold and precious stones. When she came to Solomon, she told him everything that was on her mind.

**2** Solomon answered all her questions. There was nothing hidden from Solomon that he could not explain to her.

**3** When the queen of Sheba had seen Solomon's wisdom and the house he had built,

**4** the food on his table, the seating of his officials, the attendance of his servants and their clothing, his cupbearers and their clothing, and the steps by which he went up to the house of the LORD, she was left breathless.

**5** She said to the king, "The report I heard in my own land about your words and your wisdom was true.

**6** But I did not believe their reports until I came and saw with my own eyes. And look, I was not told half of the greatness of your wisdom. You go beyond the report I heard.

**7** How happy your people are, and how happy these servants of yours, who stand before you continually and hear your wisdom.

**8** Blessed be the LORD your God, who delighted in you and set you on his throne as king for the LORD your God. Because your God loved Israel and would establish them for ever, he made you king over them, to do justice and righteousness \[H-TSEDAQAH\]."

**9** Then she gave the king a hundred and twenty talents of gold, a very large amount of spices and precious stones. There were no spices like those the queen of Sheba gave to King Solomon.

**10** (The servants of Huram and the servants of Solomon, who brought gold from Ophir, also brought algum wood and precious stones.

**11** The king used the algum wood to make steps for the house of the LORD and for the king's house, and lyres and harps for the singers. Nothing like them had been seen before in the land of Judah.)

**12** King Solomon gave the queen of Sheba everything she wanted, whatever she asked, more than she had brought to the king. Then she turned and went back to her own land with her servants.

**13** The weight of gold that came to Solomon in one year was six hundred and sixty-six talents,

**14** besides what the traders and merchants brought. All the kings of Arabia and the governors of the land also brought gold and silver to Solomon.

**15** King Solomon made two hundred large shields of beaten gold, with six hundred shekels of beaten gold in each shield.

**16** He also made three hundred smaller shields of beaten gold, with three hundred shekels of gold in each shield. The king put them in the House of the Forest of Lebanon.

**17** The king also made a great ivory throne and covered it with pure gold.

**18** The throne had six steps and a gold footstool attached to the throne, and arms on each side of the seat, with a lion standing beside each arm,

**19** and twelve lions standing there, one at each end of a step on the six steps. Nothing like it had ever been made in any kingdom.

**20** All King Solomon's drinking cups were of gold, and all the vessels in the House of the Forest of Lebanon were of pure gold. Silver was thought of no value in the days of Solomon.

**21** For the king had ships that went to Tarshish with the servants of Huram. Once every three years the ships of Tarshish came back, bringing gold, silver, ivory, apes and baboons.

**22** So King Solomon was greater than all the other kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom.

**23** All the kings of the earth wanted to see Solomon and hear his wisdom, which God had put in his heart.

**16** He restored the altar of the LORD and offered peace offerings and thank offerings on it, and told Judah to serve the LORD, the God of Israel.

**18** The rest of the acts of Manasseh, and his prayer to his God, and the words of the seers who spoke to him in the name of the LORD, the God of Israel, are written in the records of the kings of Israel.

**19** His prayer, and how God was moved by his plea, and all his sin and unfaithfulness, and the places where he built high places and set up the Asherah poles and the images before he humbled himself, are written in the records of the seers.

**20** Manasseh lay down with his ancestors, and they buried him in his own house, and his son Amon became king in his place.

**21** Amon was twenty-two years old when he became king, and he ruled for two years in Jerusalem.

**22** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, as his father Manasseh had done. Amon sacrificed to all the images that his father Manasseh had made, and served them.

**23** He did not humble himself before the LORD, as his father Manasseh had humbled himself, but Amon became more and more guilty.

**24** His servants plotted against him and killed him in his own house.

**25** But the people of the land struck down all those who had plotted against King Amon, and the people of the land made his son Josiah king in his place.

### Chapter 34

**1** Josiah was eight years old when he became king, and he ruled for thirty-one years in Jerusalem.

**2** He did what was right in the eyes of the LORD and walked in the ways of his ancestor David, and did not turn aside to the right or to the left.

**3** In the eighth year of his reign, while he was still young, he began to seek the God of his ancestor David, and in the twelfth year he began to clear Judah and Jerusalem of the high places, the Asherah poles and the carved and cast images.

**4** In his presence they pulled down the altars of the Baals, and he cut down the incense altars that stood above them. He smashed the Asherah poles and the carved and cast images, ground them to dust and scattered it over the graves of those who had sacrificed to them.

**5** He also burned the bones of the priests on their altars, and so cleansed Judah and Jerusalem.

**6** In the towns of Manasseh, Ephraim and Simeon, and as far as Naphtali, in the ruins round about,

**7** he pulled down the altars and beat the Asherah poles and the images into powder and cut down all the incense altars throughout all the land of Israel. Then he went back to Jerusalem.

**8** In the eighteenth year of his reign, when he had cleansed the land and the house, he sent Shaphan son of Azaliah, Maaseiah the governor of the town and Joah son of Joahaz, the recorder, to repair the house of the LORD his God.

**9** They came to Hilkiah the high priest and gave him the money that had been brought into the house of God, which the Levites who kept the threshold had collected from Manasseh and Ephraim and from all the remnant of Israel and from all Judah and Benjamin and the people of Jerusalem.

**10** They handed it to those in charge of the work who supervised the house of the LORD, and they gave it to the workers who were working in the house of the LORD to repair and restore the house.

**11** They gave it to the carpenters and builders to buy cut stone and timber for the joints and the beams for the buildings that the kings of Judah had let fall into ruin.

**12** The men worked faithfully. Over them were put Jahath and Obadiah, Levites of the descendants of Merari, and Zechariah and Meshullam of the descendants of Kohath, to supervise. The Levites, all who were skilled with musical instruments,

**13** were over the carriers and supervised all who did work of any kind, and some of the Levites were secretaries, officials and gatekeepers.

**14** While they were bringing out the money that had been brought into the house of the LORD, the priest Hilkiah found the Book of the Law of the LORD given through Moses.

**15** Hilkiah said to Shaphan the secretary, "I have found the Book of the Law in the house of the LORD." Hilkiah gave the book to Shaphan.

**16** Shaphan took the book to the king and reported to the king, "Your servants are doing all that was entrusted to them.

**17** They have poured out the money that was found in the house of the LORD and have handed it to the supervisors and the workers."

**18** Then Shaphan the secretary told the king, "The priest Hilkiah has given me a book." And Shaphan read from it in the presence of the king.

**19** When the king heard the words of the law, he tore his clothes.

**20** The king commanded Hilkiah, Ahikam son of Shaphan, Abdon son of Micah, Shaphan the secretary and Asaiah the king's servant:

**21** "Go and ask the LORD for me and for those who are left in Israel and Judah about the words of the book that has been found. For great is the anger of the LORD that has been poured out on us, because our ancestors did not keep the word of the LORD and do all that is written in this book."

**22** So Hilkiah and those whom the king had sent went to Huldah the prophet \[H-NAVI\], the wife of Shallum son of Tokhath, son of Hasrah, keeper of the wardrobe. She lived in Jerusalem, in the Second Quarter, and they spoke to her about this.

**23** She said to them, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Tell the man who sent you to me:

**24** This is what the LORD says: I am going to bring disaster on this place and on those who live here, all the curses written in the book that was read in front of the king of Judah.

**25** Because they have abandoned me and burned incense to other gods, to make me angry with all the work of their hands, my anger will be poured out on this place and will not be put out.

**26** But to the king of Judah, who sent you to ask the LORD, say this: This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: As for the words that you have heard,

**27** because your heart was soft and you humbled yourself before God when you heard his words against this place and those who live here, and you humbled yourself before me and tore your clothes and wept before me, I have heard you, declares the LORD.

**28** I will gather you to your ancestors, and you will be gathered to your grave in peace, and your eyes will not see all the disaster that I am going to bring on this place and those who live here." They took the word back to the king.

**29** Then the king sent and gathered all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem.

**30** The king went up to the house of the LORD with all the men of Judah and the people of Jerusalem, the priests and the Levites, and all the people, small and great. He read in their hearing all the words of the Book of the Covenant \[H-BERIT\] that had been found in the house of the LORD.

**31** The king stood in his place and made a covenant before the LORD to follow the LORD and to keep his commands, his testimonies and his statutes with all his heart and all his soul, and to carry out the words of the covenant written in this book.

**32** Then he made everyone present in Jerusalem and Benjamin join in it. The people of Jerusalem acted according to the covenant of God, the God of their ancestors.

**33** Josiah removed all the detestable things from all the territory that belonged to the Israelites and made everyone in Israel serve the LORD their God. All his days they did not turn away from following the LORD, the God of their ancestors.

### Chapter 35

**1** Josiah kept a Passover to the LORD in Jerusalem, and they killed the Passover lamb on the fourteenth day of the first month.

**2** He appointed the priests to their duties and encouraged them in the service of the house of the LORD.

**3** He said to the Levites who taught all Israel and who were holy \[H-QADOSH\] to the LORD, "Put the holy ark in the house that Solomon son of David king of Israel built. You no longer need to carry it on your shoulders. Now serve the LORD your God and his people Israel.

**4** Prepare yourselves by your fathers' houses, by your divisions, as written by David king of Israel and by his son Solomon.

**5** Stand in the holy place according to the groups of the fathers' houses of your relatives the people, with a part of a Levite father's house for each.

**6** Kill the Passover lambs and make yourselves holy and prepare for your relatives, to do as the LORD commanded through Moses."

**7** Josiah gave to the people, for all who were present, lambs and young goats from the flock, thirty thousand in all, and three thousand bulls, as Passover offerings. These were from the king's own property.

**8** His officials also gave willingly to the people, the priests and the Levites. Hilkiah, Zechariah and Jehiel, the leaders of the house of God, gave the priests two thousand six hundred lambs and young goats and three hundred bulls for the Passover offerings.

**9** Conaniah and his brothers Shemaiah and Nethanel, and Hashabiah, Jeiel and Jozabad, the leaders of the Levites, gave the Levites five thousand lambs and young goats and five hundred bulls for the Passover offerings.

**10** When the service was ready, the priests stood in their places, and the Levites in their divisions as the king had commanded.

**11** They killed the Passover lambs, and the priests splashed the blood that they received from them, while the Levites skinned the animals.

**12** They set aside the burnt offerings to give to the groups of the fathers' houses of the people, to offer to the LORD, as it is written in the Book of Moses. They did the same with the bulls.

**13** They roasted the Passover lambs over the fire as prescribed, and boiled the holy offerings in pots, cauldrons and pans, and quickly took them to all the people.

**14** Afterwards they prepared for themselves and for the priests, because the priests, the descendants of Aaron, were offering the burnt offerings and the fat until night. So the Levites prepared for themselves and for the priests, the descendants of Aaron.

**15** The singers, the descendants of Asaph, were in their places as David, Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun the king's seer had commanded. The gatekeepers were at each gate. They did not need to leave their service, because their relatives the Levites prepared for them.

**16** So all the service of the LORD was prepared that day to keep the Passover and to offer burnt offerings on the altar of the LORD, as King Josiah commanded.

**17** The Israelites who were present kept the Passover at that time, and the Festival of Unleavened Bread for seven days.

**18** No Passover like it had been kept in Israel since the days of the prophet Samuel. None of the kings of Israel had kept a Passover like the one Josiah kept, with the priests and the Levites, and all Judah and Israel who were present, and the people of Jerusalem.

**19** This Passover was kept in the eighteenth year of Josiah's reign.

**20** After all this, when Josiah had set the temple in order, Neco king of Egypt went up to fight at Carchemish on the Euphrates, and Josiah went out to meet him.

**21** But Neco sent messengers to him: "What have I to do with you, king of Judah? I am not coming against you today, but against the house with which I am at war, and God has told me to hurry. Stop opposing God, who is with me, or he will destroy you."

**22** But Josiah would not turn away from him but disguised himself to fight with him. He did not listen to the words of Neco from the mouth of God, but went to fight in the plain of Megiddo.

**23** The archers shot King Josiah. The king said to his servants, "Take me away, for I am badly wounded."

**24** So his servants took him out of the chariot and put him in his second chariot and brought him to Jerusalem. He died and was buried in the tombs of his ancestors. All Judah and Jerusalem mourned for Josiah.

**25** Jeremiah also composed a lament for Josiah, and all the male and female singers have spoken of Josiah in their laments to this day. They made these a custom in Israel, and they are written in the Laments.

**26** The rest of the acts of Josiah and his faithful deeds, as written in the law of the LORD,

**27** and his acts, from first to last, are written in the Book of the Kings of Israel and Judah.

### Chapter 36

**1** The people of the land took Jehoahaz son of Josiah and made him king in his father's place in Jerusalem.

**2** Jehoahaz was twenty-three years old when he became king, and he ruled for three months in Jerusalem.

**3** Then the king of Egypt removed him in Jerusalem and imposed on the land a tribute of a hundred talents of silver and a talent of gold.

**4** The king of Egypt made his brother Eliakim king over Judah and Jerusalem and changed his name to Jehoiakim. But Neco took his brother Jehoahaz and brought him to Egypt.

**5** Jehoiakim was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he ruled for eleven years in Jerusalem. He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD his God.

**6** Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up against him and bound him in bronze chains to take him to Babylon.

**7** Nebuchadnezzar also took some of the vessels of the house of the LORD to Babylon and put them in his palace in Babylon.

**8** The rest of the acts of Jehoiakim, and the detestable things that he did, and what was found against him, are written in the Book of the Kings of Israel and Judah. His son Jehoiachin became king in his place.

**9** Jehoiachin was eight years old when he became king, and he ruled for three months and ten days in Jerusalem. He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.

**10** In the spring King Nebuchadnezzar sent and brought him to Babylon, with the valuable vessels of the house of the LORD, and made his relative Zedekiah king over Judah and Jerusalem.

**11** Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he became king, and he ruled for eleven years in Jerusalem.

**12** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD his God. He did not humble himself before the prophet Jeremiah, who spoke from the mouth of the LORD.

**13** He also rebelled against King Nebuchadnezzar, who had made him swear by God. He stiffened his neck and hardened his heart against turning back \[H-SHUV\] to the LORD, the God of Israel.

**14** All the leaders of the priests and the people were also very unfaithful, following all the detestable practices of the nations. They made the house of the LORD, which he had made holy in Jerusalem, unclean.

**15** The LORD, the God of their ancestors, sent word to them again and again through his messengers, because he had compassion on his people and on his dwelling place.

**16** But they kept mocking the messengers of God, despising his words and scoffing at his prophets, until the anger of the LORD rose against his people, until there was no remedy.

**17** So he brought up against them the king of the Chaldeans, who killed their young men with the sword in the house of their sanctuary and had no pity on young man or young woman, old or frail. God handed all of them over to him.

**18** All the vessels of the house of God, large and small, and the treasures of the house of the LORD, and the treasures of the king and his officials, all these he brought to Babylon.

**19** They burned the house of God and broke down the wall of Jerusalem and burned all its palaces with fire and destroyed all its valuable vessels.

**20** He took into exile in Babylon those who had escaped from the sword, and they became servants to him and his sons until the kingdom of Persia came to power,

**21** to fulfil the word of the LORD through Jeremiah, until the land had enjoyed its sabbaths. All the days that it lay desolate it kept sabbath, until seventy years were completed.

**22** In the first year of Cyrus king of Persia, in order to fulfil the word of the LORD spoken by Jeremiah, the LORD stirred up the spirit \[H-RUACH\] of Cyrus king of Persia, so that he sent a proclamation throughout his whole kingdom and put it in writing:

**23** "This is what Cyrus king of Persia says: The LORD, the God of heaven, has given me all the kingdoms of the earth, and he has appointed me to build him a house at Jerusalem in Judah. Whoever among you belongs to all his people, may the LORD his God be with him. Let him go up."

*End of the book of 2 Chronicles, and of the Hebrew Bible in its Jewish order. Review note: 2 Chronicles 33 to 36 was drafted in WABV wording. The chapters are parallel to 2 Kings 21 to 25, and the wording follows the WABV 2 Kings where the Hebrew is the same. Chronicles adds Manasseh's exile and repentance (33:11-17), Josiah's early reform (34:3-7), and a long account of Josiah's Passover (35:1-19), and gives the cause of Josiah's death as his refusal to listen to Neco's word from God (35:21-22). It leaves out the killing of Gedaliah and the release of Jehoiachin, and ends with the decree of Cyrus (36:22-23), which is also the opening of the Book of Ezra (Ezra 1:1-3), and the wording of the two must match. The final verse breaks off in the middle of a sentence that is completed in Ezra 1:3. The Prayer of Manasseh, mentioned in 33:18-19, is not part of this book. The footnotes record the main differences. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **Title** 2 Chronicles continues 1 Chronicles, and the two were one book in the Hebrew Bible. It tells the history of Judah from Solomon to the decree of Cyrus, following Samuel and Kings and other sources that it names. It tells almost nothing of the northern kingdom except where it meets Judah. Its main themes are the temple, the kings who "seek the LORD", prayer, and unfaithfulness (*ma'al*) as the cause of exile.
- **1:1** Chronicles begins Solomon's reign with the LORD's blessing. It leaves out the struggle for the throne (1 Kings 1 and 2).
- **1:3** "The high place at Gibeon" is where the tent of meeting stood. Chronicles says the tent "which Moses the servant of the LORD had made" was there (see 1 Chronicles 16:39 and 21:29).
- **1:4** "David had brought up the ark ... for he had pitched a tent for it in Jerusalem" explains that the ark and the tent of meeting were in two places (1 Chronicles 15 and 16).
- **1:5** "The bronze altar that Bezalel ... had made" is the altar of Exodus 38:1-2. It is not mentioned in 1 Kings 3.
- **1:7** "That night God appeared to Solomon" does not say "in a dream" as 1 Kings 3:5 does.
- **1:8** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED.
- **1:10** "Wisdom and knowledge" is the request here. 1 Kings 3:9 has "a listening heart". Solomon asks to "go out and come in before this people" (see 1 Kings 3:7).
- **1:14** "Fourteen hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen" repeats 1 Kings 10:26. Chronicles puts it here, before the account of the temple. See also 9:25.
- **1:16** "Kue" is Cilicia. The Hebrew of this verse is difficult, as in 1 Kings 10:28.
- **1:17** "The kings of Aram" is the Hebrew reading here. 1 Kings 10:29 has the same word.
- **2:1** "A house for the name of the LORD, and a royal palace for himself" begins the account of the temple building, which is parallel to 1 Kings 5 to 7.
- **2:2** "Seventy thousand ... eighty thousand ... three thousand six hundred" is the Hebrew number. 1 Kings 5:16 has three thousand three hundred supervisors.
- **2:3** "Do for me as you did for my father David" begins Solomon's message to Hiram. It is longer than 1 Kings 5:3-6.
- **2:4** "To make it holy to him" is Hebrew *haqdish*. See entry H-QADOSH. "The bread of the Presence" and the regular offerings are added here.
- **2:6** "Heaven, even the highest heaven, cannot contain him" is also in Solomon's prayer (6:18; 1 Kings 8:27).
- **2:8** "Algum" is a costly timber, also called almug (1 Kings 10:11). It is not identified.
- **2:10** "Twenty thousand cors of ground wheat, twenty thousand cors of barley, twenty thousand baths of wine and twenty thousand baths of oil" differs from 1 Kings 5:11, which has twenty thousand cors of wheat and twenty cors of pressed oil.
- **2:11** "Because the LORD loves his people, he has made you king over them" is Hiram's reply. 1 Kings 5:7 has a different response.
- **2:13** "Huram-abi" is called Hiram in 1 Kings 7:13-14. "Huram" is the same name in another form.
- **2:14** "The son of a woman from the daughters of Dan" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Kings 7:14 says he was the son of a widow from the tribe of Naphtali. Both say his father was from Tyre.
- **2:16** "Joppa" is the port where the logs were landed, which 1 Kings 5:9 does not name.
- **2:17** "The foreigners" is Hebrew *gerim*, resident aliens. See entry H-GER. The count of a hundred and fifty-three thousand six hundred follows David's census (1 Chronicles 22:2). 1 Kings 5:13-18 also speaks of a levy of thirty thousand men from Israel. In 8:7-10 Chronicles says that Solomon made forced labour only of the non-Israelites.
- **3:1** "Mount Moriah" is the place named in Genesis 22:2 as the place of Abraham's offering. Chronicles is the only book that links the temple with Moriah. "The threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite" refers to 1 Chronicles 21:18-28.
- **3:3** "Cubits of the old standard" is a note on the length of the cubit. It may mean the cubit that was in use before the exile.
- **3:4** "A hundred and twenty" is the height of the porch in the Hebrew, which is far too high compared with the main building (thirty cubits, 1 Kings 6:2). Some Greek and other texts have twenty. The Hebrew is followed here and the difficulty noted.
- **3:6** "Parvaim" is a source of gold, a place that is not identified.
- **3:8** "Six hundred talents of fine gold" is not in Kings. It is about twenty tonnes. See also 1 Kings 6:20-22.
- **3:9** "The nails" were of gold. "Fifty shekels" is the weight.
- **3:10** "Two cherubim of wood" is the Hebrew here. 1 Kings 6:23 says they were of olive wood, and ten cubits high.
- **3:13** "Facing the main hall" is the Hebrew reading. The cherubim face inward, not the other way. 1 Kings 6:27 says their wings touched in the middle.
- **3:14** "The curtain" is Hebrew *parokhet*. It is not mentioned in the description of the temple in Kings. It is the curtain of the tabernacle (Exodus 26:31-33).
- **3:15** "Thirty-five cubits high" is the Hebrew number for the two pillars together, or each of them, which is unclear. 1 Kings 7:15 and 2 Kings 25:17 give eighteen cubits for each pillar.
- **3:16** "A hundred pomegranates" is the number on the chains. 1 Kings 7:20 has two hundred in rows round each capital.
- **3:17** "Jachin" and "Boaz" are the names of the pillars, as in 1 Kings 7:21. Their meaning is not certain.
- **4:1** "An altar of bronze, twenty cubits long" is not in the account in 1 Kings 7. It is the altar of burnt offering of the temple court.
- **4:3** "Figures of oxen, ten to a cubit" is the Hebrew reading here. 1 Kings 7:24 has "gourds", with the same measure. The two words (*bekarim*, *peqa'im*) look alike in Hebrew.
- **4:5** "Three thousand baths" is the Hebrew number here. 1 Kings 7:26 has two thousand.
- **4:6** "The sea was for the priests to wash in" gives the use of the sea, which is not stated in Kings.
- **4:7** "Ten gold lampstands" differ from the single lampstand of the tent (Exodus 25:31-40). The same ten are in 1 Kings 7:49.
- **4:9** "The court of the priests and the great court" is parallel to 1 Kings 6:36 and 7:12.
- **4:11** "Huram" is the craftsman, called Hiram in 1 Kings 7:40. The list of his work follows 1 Kings 7:40-47.
- **4:16** "Huram-abi" is the same man as in 2:13. Here his name is given in full.
- **4:17** "Between Succoth and Zeredah" is the Hebrew reading here. 1 Kings 7:46 has "Zarethan".
- **4:18** "The weight of the bronze could not be found out" repeats 1 Kings 7:47.
- **4:19** "The golden altar and the tables for the bread of the Presence" follow 1 Kings 7:48. Chronicles uses "tables" in the plural here and in 4:8.
- **5:1** "The things that his father David had dedicated" repeats 1 Kings 7:51. It closes the account of the building and begins the dedication.
- **5:2** The bringing of the ark (verses 2 to 14) is parallel to 1 Kings 8:1-11. "Covenant" is Hebrew *berit*. See entry H-BERIT. "Zion" is the city of David.
- **5:3** "The festival in the seventh month" is the festival of booths. See also 7:8-10.
- **5:4** "The Levites lifted the ark" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Kings 8:3 says the priests lifted it. Chronicles keeps to the rule that the Levites carry the ark (1 Chronicles 15:2).
- **5:5** "The priests and the Levites" is the Hebrew "the Levitical priests" (*ha-kohanim ha-leviyyim*), the Levites who are priests. "Holy" is *qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **5:9** "They are there to this day" repeats 1 Kings 8:8. It was written while the temple still stood, before 587 BCE.
- **5:10** "Nothing in the ark except the two tablets" repeats 1 Kings 8:9.
- **5:11** "All the priests who were present had made themselves holy, whatever their divisions" is added here. It means the priests did not serve by turns on this day.
- **5:12** "Asaph, Heman, Jeduthun" and the hundred and twenty priests with trumpets are not in 1 Kings 8. See 1 Chronicles 25:1-7.
- **5:13** "Sound as one" is the Chronicler's picture of unity. "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. The refrain "For he is good, for his faithful love lasts for ever" is also in 1 Chronicles 16:34 and 7:3, 6.
- **6:1** Solomon's lines in verses 1 and 2 are poetry in the Hebrew. "Thick darkness" is Hebrew *arafel*, the dark cloud of Sinai (Exodus 20:21). The Greek has a longer form of the poem in 1 Kings 8:53.
- **6:5** "I chose no man to be leader over my people Israel" is not in 1 Kings 8:16. Verses 5 and 6 add the choice of Jerusalem beside the choice of David.
- **6:6** "I have chosen Jerusalem for my name to be there" is also not in 1 Kings. It matches 1 Chronicles 22:1.
- **6:13** "A bronze platform" is not in Kings. Solomon kneels on it in front of the whole assembly. He first stands (verse 12).
- **6:14** "There is no God like you" begins the prayer, which follows 1 Kings 8:23-53 closely up to verse 39. "Faithful love" is *hesed*.
- **6:16** "Walking in my law" is the Hebrew here. 1 Kings 8:25 has "walking before me".
- **6:18** "Heaven, and the highest heaven, cannot contain you" repeats 2:6 and 1 Kings 8:27.
- **6:24** "They turn back" is Hebrew *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV. The same word marks the pattern of the prayer: sin, turning back, forgiveness.
- **6:28** The list of disasters (famine, plague, blight, mildew, locusts) repeats the curses of Deuteronomy 28 and Leviticus 26.
- **6:30** "For you alone know the hearts of all human beings" repeats 1 Kings 8:39.
- **6:32** "A foreigner" is Hebrew *nokhri*, a person from another land, not *ger*, the resident foreigner. See 1 Kings 8:41.
- **6:36** "There is no one who does not sin" repeats 1 Kings 8:46. Verses 36 to 39 look ahead to exile.
- **6:41** Verses 41 and 42 replace the end of the prayer in 1 Kings 8:50-53. They quote Psalm 132:8-10. "The ark of your strength" is the ark that stands for the LORD's power. "Your faithful people" is Hebrew *chasidim*.
- **6:42** "Your anointed one" is Hebrew *meshichekha*. See entry H-MASHIACH. "The faithful love promised to David" is Hebrew *chasdei David*, which may mean the love the LORD showed to David, or the loyalty David showed. It is also hard in Psalm 132:10 and Isaiah 55:3.
- **7:1** "Fire came down from heaven and burned up the burnt offering" is not in 1 Kings 8. Fire from heaven is also in Leviticus 9:24, 1 Kings 18:38 and 1 Chronicles 21:26.
- **7:2** "The priests could not enter the house of the LORD" repeats 5:14 and Exodus 40:35.
- **7:3** "For he is good, for his faithful love lasts for ever" is the people's response, and the refrain of 5:13.
- **7:5** "Twenty-two thousand oxen and a hundred and twenty thousand sheep" repeats 1 Kings 8:63. It is a very large number.
- **7:6** "The instruments for the music of the LORD that King David had made" repeats 1 Chronicles 23:5.
- **7:7** "Made holy the middle of the court" is parallel to 1 Kings 8:64. The bronze altar was too small for the offerings.
- **7:8** "From the entrance of Hamath to the Wadi of Egypt" is the whole land.
- **7:9** "On the eighth day they held a closing assembly" is Hebrew *atseret*. The dedication of the altar lasted seven days and the festival seven days. 1 Kings 8:65 says "seven days and seven days, fourteen days".
- **7:10** "On the twenty-third day of the seventh month" is the date of the people's departure. It is not in 1 Kings 8:66, which says that he sent them away on the eighth day.
- **7:12** "A house of sacrifice" is the LORD's description of the temple. The verse begins the LORD's second appearance (see 1 Kings 9:2-9).
- **7:13** "When I shut up the sky so that there is no rain" is parallel to the prayer in 6:26-28. Verses 13 to 15 are not in 1 Kings 9.
- **7:14** "If my people, who are called by my name, humble themselves and pray and seek my face and turn back from their evil ways" is one of the best-known verses in the book. It is not in Kings. "Turn back" is *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV. "Heal their land" means to end the disaster, such as drought or plague.
- **7:16** "Made this house holy" is Hebrew *hiqdashti*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **7:18** "As I made a covenant with your father David" repeats the promise of 1 Chronicles 17:11-14 and 1 Kings 9:5.
- **7:20** "I will pull you up from my land" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Kings 9:7 has "I will cut Israel off from the land". The change from "Israel" to "you" is in the Hebrew.
- **7:21** "This house, which was so high" is the Hebrew (*elyon*). 1 Kings 9:8 reads "this house will become a heap of ruins". The Hebrew of both is hard.
- **8:1** "At the end of twenty years" repeats 1 Kings 9:10. The account of Solomon's works (verses 1 to 18) is parallel to 1 Kings 9:10-28.
- **8:2** "The towns that Huram had given him" reverses 1 Kings 9:11-13, where Solomon gave twenty towns in Galilee to Hiram. Chronicles has the towns given by Hiram to Solomon, and Solomon rebuilding them. Both can be true of two different exchanges, and the text does not explain.
- **8:3** "Solomon went to Hamath-zobah and overpowered it" is not in 1 Kings. It is the only war that Chronicles attributes to Solomon.
- **8:4** "Tadmor" is the Hebrew reading here. In 1 Kings 9:18 the reading tradition has "Tamar" and the written text "Tadmor".
- **8:7** "All the people who were left of the Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites" repeats 1 Kings 9:20. See also Joshua 9:21-27.
- **8:8** "Whom the Israelites had not destroyed" is the Hebrew reading. The word used for the ban elsewhere is not used here. The forced labour is "to this day".
- **8:9** "Solomon made no slaves of the Israelites" repeats 1 Kings 9:22. In 1 Kings 5:13 the labour force was raised "from all Israel". See also 2:17.
- **8:10** "Two hundred and fifty" is the Hebrew number. 1 Kings 9:23 has five hundred and fifty, and 2:2 has three thousand six hundred supervisors.
- **8:11** "The places where the ark of the LORD has come are holy" is not in 1 Kings 9:24. "Holy" is Hebrew *qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH. The verse explains why Pharaoh's daughter must not live in the house of David. See also 1 Kings 3:1 and 11:1.
- **8:12** "The altar of the LORD that he had built in front of the porch" is the bronze altar (4:1). The account of the offerings in verses 12 and 13 is parallel to 1 Kings 9:25 but is longer.
- **8:13** "The three annual festivals" are the pilgrim festivals of Exodus 23:14-17 and Deuteronomy 16:16.
- **8:14** "The ruling of his father David" refers to the organisation of the priests, Levites and gatekeepers in 1 Chronicles 23 to 26. "David the man of God" is a title also used of Moses.
- **8:17** "Ezion-geber and Eloth" are ports at the head of the Gulf of Aqaba. See 1 Kings 9:26.
- **8:18** "Huram sent him ships and servants who knew the sea" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Kings 9:27 says that Hiram sent sailors with Solomon's fleet. "Four hundred and fifty talents of gold" is the number here. 1 Kings 9:28 has four hundred and twenty.
- **9:1** The account of the queen of Sheba (verses 1 to 12) is parallel to 1 Kings 10:1-13. Chronicles leaves out "his connection with the name of the LORD" (1 Kings 10:1).
- **9:4** "The steps by which he went up to the house of the LORD" is Hebrew *aliyyato*. 1 Kings 10:5 has *olato*, "his burnt offering". The two words differ by one letter. The WABV text of 1 Kings has "the burnt offerings that he offered".
- **9:6** "I was not told half of the greatness of your wisdom" follows 1 Kings 10:7.
- **9:8** "Set you on his throne as king for the LORD your God" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Kings 10:9 has "set you on the throne of Israel". Chronicles says that the king sits on the LORD's own throne (see 1 Chronicles 28:5 and 29:23). "Justice and righteousness" is Hebrew *mishpat u-tsedaqah*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH.
- **9:10** "The servants of Huram and the servants of Solomon" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Kings 10:11 has "the fleet of Hiram".
- **9:11** "Steps" (Hebrew *mesillot*) is the word here. 1 Kings 10:12 has "supports" (*misad*). The meaning of both is uncertain.
- **9:13** "Six hundred and sixty-six talents" repeats 1 Kings 10:14. It is about twenty-two tonnes of gold.
- **9:16** "Three hundred shekels of gold in each" smaller shield is the Hebrew reading. 1 Kings 10:17 has three minas.
- **9:18** "A gold footstool attached to the throne" is not in 1 Kings 10:19. 1 Kings has "a round top at the back", which is hard in the Hebrew.
- **9:21** "The king had ships that went to Tarshish with the servants of Huram" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Kings 10:22 has "a fleet of Tarshish ... with the fleet of Hiram". "Apes and baboons" translates two Hebrew words, *qofim* and *tukkiyyim*, of which the second is uncertain.
- **9:24** "Weapons" is Hebrew *neshek*, arms or armour.
- **9:25** "Four thousand stalls" is the Hebrew number here. 1 Kings 4:26 has forty thousand. The two accounts are kept as they are.
- **9:28** "Horses were imported for Solomon from Egypt and from all lands" is shorter than 1 Kings 10:28-29, and leaves out the prices.
- **9:29** "The records of Nathan the prophet, ... the prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite, and ... the visions of Iddo the seer" are sources that are now lost. Ahijah is the prophet who spoke to Jeroboam (1 Kings 11:29-39), and Chronicles does not tell that story.
- **9:30** "Forty years" repeats 1 Kings 11:42.
- **9:31** "Solomon lay down with his ancestors" ends the account of Solomon. Chronicles leaves out his foreign wives, his turning to other gods, his adversaries and the judgement on his house (1 Kings 11:1-40).
- **10:1** The division of the kingdom (verses 1 to 19) is parallel to 1 Kings 12:1-19. "Shechem" is the old place of assembly of the tribes.
- **10:2** "Came back from Egypt" is the Hebrew reading here. In 1 Kings 12:2 the written text says "stayed in Egypt" and the reading tradition says "came back".
- **10:4** "Yoke" is a figure for the forced labour and taxes of Solomon's reign.
- **10:7** "If you are kind to this people" is the elders' advice. 1 Kings 12:7 has "if you will be a servant to this people".
- **10:10** "My little finger is thicker than my father's waist" is the young men's boast, as in 1 Kings 12:10. The Hebrew words are crude and uncertain.
- **10:15** "The turn of events was from God" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Kings 12:15 has "from the LORD". "Ahijah the Shilonite" is named, though Chronicles does not tell the story of his word to Jeroboam (1 Kings 11:29-39).
- **10:16** "What share do we have in David?" repeats the cry of 2 Samuel 20:1 and 1 Kings 12:16.
- **10:18** "Hadoram" is Adoram in 1 Kings 12:18 (see also 4:6). The two are forms of one name.
- **10:19** "To this day" is the writer's note that the rebellion continues.
- **11:1** Verses 1 to 4 are parallel to 1 Kings 12:21-24. "A hundred and eighty thousand" is the same number there.
- **11:2** "Shemaiah the man of God" is also named in 12:5, 7 and 15.
- **11:4** "For this is from me" says that the division is the LORD's act. "Turned back from going against Jeroboam" is not in 1 Kings.
- **11:5** "He built towns for defence in Judah" begins a list of fifteen fortified towns (verses 6 to 10) that is not in Kings. They form a ring round Jerusalem to the south and west.
- **11:13** "The priests and the Levites throughout Israel came to him" begins a story that is not in Kings. It says that the faithful of the north came to Judah.
- **11:14** "The Levites left their pasturelands" refers to the towns given to them (1 Chronicles 6:54-81). "Dismissed them from serving as priests" refers to Jeroboam's priests who were not Levites (1 Kings 12:31).
- **11:15** "The goat demons" is Hebrew *se'irim*, the word of Leviticus 17:7. It is not in 1 Kings 12.
- **11:16** "Set their hearts to seek the LORD" is the Chronicler's key phrase for a faithful person (see 12:14; 15:12; 19:3; 30:19).
- **11:17** "For three years" says that Rehoboam was faithful for only three years.
- **11:18** "Mahalath" and "Abihail" are not otherwise known. Her father Jerimoth is a son of David not named in the lists of 1 Chronicles 3.
- **11:20** "Maacah daughter of Absalom" is the Hebrew reading. In 1 Kings 15:2 she is "daughter of Abishalom", and in 13:2 she is "Micaiah daughter of Uriel". The word *bat* ("daughter") can mean granddaughter.
- **11:21** "Eighteen wives and sixty concubines" is in contrast to Deuteronomy 17:17. The text records the numbers without comment.
- **11:22** "Abijah" is the Chronicler's name for Abijam of 1 Kings 14:31.
- **12:1** "He abandoned the law of the LORD" is a Chronicler's summary. It says that strength led to unfaithfulness.
- **12:2** "Unfaithful" is Hebrew *ma'al*, a key word of Chronicles (see 1 Chronicles 5:25; 9:1; 10:13). The account of Shishak's invasion (verses 2 to 12) is parallel to 1 Kings 14:25-28, with an added prophetic word.
- **12:3** "Twelve hundred chariots and sixty thousand horsemen" is not in Kings. "Sukkites" are a people of the Egyptian army. "Shishak" is Sheshonq I, who also records the campaign in an inscription at Karnak.
- **12:5** "You have abandoned me, so I have abandoned you" is the word of Shemaiah.
- **12:6** "Humbled themselves" shows the repentance of the king and the leaders. "The LORD is righteous" is Hebrew *tsaddiq YHWH*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH.
- **12:7** "I will give them some deliverance" is the LORD's response to humbling. See 7:14.
- **12:8** "The difference between serving me and serving the kingdoms of other lands" says that the tribute to Shishak will teach Judah the cost of serving a foreign king.
- **12:9** "He took away the treasures" repeats 1 Kings 14:26. Chronicles leaves out nothing there.
- **12:12** "There was also some good in Judah" is the Chronicler's note. 1 Kings 14:22 says Judah did evil.
- **12:13** "Forty-one years old ... seventeen years" repeats 1 Kings 14:21.
- **12:14** "He did not set his heart to seek the LORD" is the Chronicler's verdict on Rehoboam (see 11:16).
- **12:15** "The records of Shemaiah the prophet and of Iddo the seer, in the family records" are sources that are now lost. The Hebrew of "in the family records" (*lehityached*) is uncertain.
- **12:16** "Abijah" is Abijam in 1 Kings 14:31.
- **13:1** The account of Abijah (verses 1 to 22) is parallel to 1 Kings 15:1-8 but is quite different. Kings calls him Abijam and tells only of his sins. Chronicles tells of a speech and a victory.
- **13:2** "Micaiah daughter of Uriel of Gibeah" is the Hebrew reading. In 11:20 and 1 Kings 15:2 his mother is "Maacah daughter of Absalom". The names may be two forms of a name, or the word "daughter" may mean a granddaughter.
- **13:3** "Four hundred thousand ... eight hundred thousand" are very large numbers. In verse 17 five hundred thousand of Israel fall.
- **13:4** "Mount Zemaraim" is in the hill country of Ephraim. It is not otherwise known (see Joshua 18:22).
- **13:5** "A covenant of salt" is Hebrew *berit melach*, a lasting covenant (Numbers 18:19). See entry H-BERIT.
- **13:7** "Young and timid" is Hebrew *na'ar ve-rakh levav*, "young and soft of heart". Rehoboam was forty-one when he became king (12:13), so the words mean inexperienced, not a child.
- **13:8** "The kingdom of the LORD in the hands of David's descendants" repeats the idea of 1 Chronicles 28:5 and 29:23.
- **13:9** "Priests ... like the peoples of other lands" refers to Jeroboam's priests (11:14-15; 1 Kings 12:31). The rule that the priests are descendants of Aaron is in Exodus 29.
- **13:11** "The gold lampstand" is one lampstand here, as in the tent. In 4:7 there are ten in the temple. The two are kept as they are.
- **13:12** "God is with us at our head" and the priests with trumpets repeat Numbers 10:8-9.
- **13:14** "They cried out to the LORD, and the priests blew the trumpets" follows Numbers 10:9. The same pattern is in 14:11 and 20:5-12.
- **13:17** "Five hundred thousand" fall. It is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **13:20** "The LORD struck him down, and he died" is not in 1 Kings 14:20, which says Jeroboam ruled twenty-two years and lay down with his ancestors. The death of Jeroboam in Chronicles may refer to a later time.
- **13:21** "Fourteen wives and twenty-two sons and sixteen daughters" shows that Abijah "grew strong". See 11:21.
- **13:22** "The commentary of the prophet Iddo" is Hebrew *midrash*, an early use of the word. It is a lost source (see 9:29 and 12:15).
- **14:1** In the Hebrew Bible this verse is 13:23, and 14:2-15 are 14:1-14. "Peace for ten years" is also in verse 6.
- **14:3** "He removed the foreign altars and the high places" is the Chronicler's report of Asa's reform (1 Kings 15:11-15). In 15:17, as in 1 Kings 15:14, "the high places were not removed". The two statements are kept as they are.
- **14:4** "To seek the LORD" is the Chronicler's key phrase (see 11:16; 12:14; 15:12).
- **14:7** "We have sought the LORD our God. We have sought him, and he has given us rest" gives the reason for the peace.
- **14:8** "Three hundred thousand ... two hundred and eighty thousand" total five hundred and eighty thousand fighting men.
- **14:9** "Zerah the Cushite" is not identified in any other source. "A million men and three hundred chariots" is rendered as written. "Mareshah" is in the lowland of Judah.
- **14:11** "There is no one but you to help, whether the mighty or the weak" is Asa's prayer. Chronicles often records short prayers before battle (13:14; 18:31; 20:6-12).
- **14:13** "Gerar" is in the south, near the border of Egypt.
- **15:1** "The spirit of God" is Hebrew *ruach Elohim*. See entry H-RUACH. "Azariah son of Oded" is the prophet.
- **15:2** "The LORD is with you when you are with him" is the principle of the Chronicler's history. "If you seek him, he will be found by you" repeats 1 Chronicles 28:9.
- **15:3** "For a long time Israel was without the true God, without a priest to teach, and without law" is hard to place. It may refer to the time of the judges, or to a time of disorder in the north.
- **15:4** "They turned back" is Hebrew *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **15:5** "No peace for anyone going out or coming in" is a figure for disorder. "Great confusion" is Hebrew *mehumot*.
- **15:8** "The prophecy of Azariah son of Oded" follows the Greek, the Syriac and the Latin. The Hebrew has "the prophecy of Oded the prophet", without the name Azariah. The WABV text departs from the Hebrew and is flagged for the scholar.
- **15:9** "Large numbers had come over to him from Israel" says that the faithful of the north came to Asa, as in 11:16.
- **15:11** "Seven hundred oxen and seven thousand sheep" came from the plunder of the war with the Cushites.
- **15:12** "A covenant to seek the LORD" is Hebrew *berit*. See entry H-BERIT. The renewal of the covenant is a main event of Asa's reign.
- **15:13** "Anyone who would not seek the LORD ... would be put to death" follows the law about those who lead Israel to other gods (Deuteronomy 13:6-10; 17:2-7). It is rendered as written.
- **15:16** "His grandmother Maacah" is Hebrew "his mother" (*imo*), which can mean grandmother. See 1 Kings 15:13.
- **15:17** "The high places were not removed from Israel" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Kings 15:14 has "were not removed" without "from Israel". See also 14:3.
- **15:19** "No more war until the thirty-fifth year" and 16:1 ("the thirty-sixth year") cannot be fitted to 1 Kings 16:6-8, which says that Baasha died in the twenty-sixth year of Asa. The dates are kept as in the Hebrew.
- **16:1** The account of Asa and Baasha (verses 1 to 6) is parallel to 1 Kings 15:16-22. "Ramah" is a town north of Jerusalem.
- **16:3** "A treaty between me and you" is the Hebrew reading. See 1 Kings 15:19. Asa pays Ben-hadad from the temple treasury.
- **16:4** "Abel-maim" is Abel-beth-maacah in 1 Kings 15:20. "All the storage towns of Naphtali" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Kings 15:20 has "all Chinneroth".
- **16:7** "Hanani the seer" is not in Kings. His rebuke is the Chronicler's. The father of the prophet Jehu (19:2).
- **16:9** "The eyes of the LORD move to and fro throughout the whole earth" is also in Zechariah 4:10. "Those whose heart is wholly his" is the Hebrew *levavam shalem*.
- **16:10** "Put him in the stocks in prison" is Asa's reaction. The only time in Chronicles that a king punishes a prophet is also Joash in 24:21 and Amaziah in 25:16.
- **16:12** "Diseased in his feet" repeats 1 Kings 15:23. Chronicles adds that he did not seek the LORD but "only the physicians" (Hebrew *rofe'im*).
- **16:14** "A very great fire in his honour" refers to the burning of spices at a royal funeral (see 21:19 and Jeremiah 34:5).
- **17:1** "Strengthened himself against Israel" begins the Chronicler's long account of Jehoshaphat's reign (chapters 17 to 20), which is much fuller than 1 Kings 22.
- **17:2** "The towns of Ephraim that his father Asa had captured" refers to 15:8.
- **17:3** "The earlier ways of his ancestor David" is the Hebrew reading. Some manuscripts and the Greek do not have "earlier". "He did not seek the Baals" contrasts him with Ahab (1 Kings 16:31-32).
- **17:6** "He also removed the high places and the Asherah poles from Judah" is in tension with 20:33 and 1 Kings 22:43, where the high places "were not removed".
- **17:7** "He sent his officials ... to teach in the towns of Judah" is the first teaching mission in the Bible. Five officials, nine Levites and two priests are named.
- **17:9** "The Book of the Law of the LORD" is the written law that the teachers carried with them.
- **17:10** "The fear of the LORD came on all the kingdoms" repeats 14:14.
- **17:11** "Some of the Philistines brought Jehoshaphat gifts" shows tribute from the west and south. "Seven thousand seven hundred rams" is a large amount.
- **17:14** The muster in verses 14 to 18 adds up to one million one hundred and sixty thousand men. It is rendered as written.
- **18:1** "A marriage alliance with Ahab" refers to the marriage of Jehoshaphat's son Jehoram to Athaliah (21:6; 2 Kings 8:18, 26). The text criticises it (19:2; 20:35-37).
- **18:2** "Ahab killed a great number of sheep and oxen" is a feast to persuade Jehoshaphat. It is not in 1 Kings 22:2.
- **18:3** The account of Ahab, Jehoshaphat and Micaiah (verses 3 to 34) is parallel to 1 Kings 22:4-35. The wording follows the WABV 1 Kings where the Hebrew is the same.
- **18:5** "God will hand it over to the king" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Kings 22:6 has "the Lord" (*Adonai*).
- **18:7** "Imla" is the Hebrew form here, and "Imlah" in 1 Kings 22:8.
- **18:13** "I will speak what my God says" is the Hebrew reading. 1 Kings 22:14 has "what the LORD says to me".
- **18:14** "Go up and win. They will be handed over to you" is Micaiah's ironic reply. In 1 Kings 22:15 the words are "the LORD will hand it over to the king".
- **18:19** "Persuade" is Hebrew *patah*, a verb that can mean "entice" or "deceive". "A spirit" (verse 20) is Hebrew *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH.
- **18:21** "A lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets" is rendered as written, with no explanation. See 1 Kings 22:22.
- **18:27** "Listen, all you peoples" is in the Hebrew here and in 1 Kings 22:28, but not in the main Greek manuscripts of Kings. It is also the opening of Micah 1:2.
- **18:31** "The LORD helped him. God drew them away from him" is not in 1 Kings 22:32. Chronicles adds that Jehoshaphat was rescued.
- **18:34** "At sunset he died" ends the account. Chronicles leaves out the burial of Ahab and the dogs licking his blood (1 Kings 22:37-38).
- **19:1** The account of Jehoshaphat's reform (verses 1 to 11) is not in Kings.
- **19:2** "Jehu son of Hanani the seer" also spoke against Baasha (1 Kings 16:1-4, 7). His father Hanani rebuked Asa (16:7-10).
- **19:3** "Yet some good is found in you" is a gentle balance to the rebuke. "Set your heart to seek God" is the Chronicler's key phrase (see 12:14; 15:12).
- **19:4** "Brought them back" is Hebrew *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV. "From Beersheba to the hill country of Ephraim" is the whole land of the two kingdoms.
- **19:5** "Judges in the land, in all the fortified towns" follows the rule of Deuteronomy 16:18-20.
- **19:7** "There is no injustice with the LORD our God, no partiality and no taking of bribes" echoes Deuteronomy 10:17.
- **19:8** "Some of the Levites and priests and heads of the families of Israel" form a court in Jerusalem. See Deuteronomy 17:8-13.
- **19:11** "Amariah the chief priest ... in all matters of the LORD, and Zebadiah ... in all matters of the king" divides the work between the religious and royal courts. "Officers" is Hebrew *shoterim*.
- **20:1** "The Moabites and Ammonites, and with them some of the Meunites" is the Hebrew reading. The Greek has "Meunites" in place of "Ammonites" in some manuscripts. The account of the war (verses 1 to 30) is not in Kings.
- **20:2** "From Edom" follows many manuscripts, the Syriac and the Latin. The Hebrew text has "from Aram" (the words differ by one letter). Edom fits the place names, Mount Seir and En-gedi. The WABV text departs from the Hebrew here and is flagged for the scholar. "Hazazon-tamar" is En-gedi (Genesis 14:7).
- **20:3** "Set his face to seek the LORD" and the fast show the king's response. See also Ezra 8:21.
- **20:5** "The new court" is a court of the temple that is not otherwise known.
- **20:6** "Are you not God in heaven?" begins Jehoshaphat's prayer (verses 6 to 12). It is built from Solomon's prayer (6:28-30) and Deuteronomy.
- **20:7** "Abraham your friend" echoes Isaiah 41:8 and James 2:23.
- **20:9** "We will stand before this house and before you" quotes the prayer of 6:28-30.
- **20:10** "Whom you did not allow Israel to invade" refers to Deuteronomy 2:4-9, 19, where Edom, Moab and Ammon were left alone.
- **20:12** "Our eyes are on you" ends the prayer.
- **20:14** "The spirit of the LORD" is Hebrew *ruach YHWH*. See entry H-RUACH. "Jahaziel" is a Levite of the sons of Asaph, the singers (1 Chronicles 25:1-2).
- **20:15** "The battle is not yours but God's" echoes 1 Samuel 17:47.
- **20:16** "The Ascent of Ziz" and "the wilderness of Jeruel" are places near En-gedi.
- **20:17** "Stand still and see the salvation of the LORD" echoes Exodus 14:13.
- **20:20** "Trust in the LORD your God and you will stand firm" plays on the Hebrew *ha'aminu* and *te'amenu*, "trust" and "be established" (see Isaiah 7:9).
- **20:21** "In holy splendour" is Hebrew *hadrat qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH. "Faithful love" is *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. The singers go in front of the army.
- **20:22** "The LORD set ambushes" says the LORD caused the confusion among the invaders.
- **20:23** "Devoting them to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. The people of Ammon and Moab destroy the people of Mount Seir and then each other. It is rendered as written.
- **20:24** "The watchtower in the wilderness" is a high place from which Judah looked down.
- **20:26** "The Valley of Beracah" means the valley of blessing. The name remains "to this day".
- **20:29** "The fear of God came on all the kingdoms" repeats 17:10 and 14:14.
- **20:31** "Thirty-five years old ... twenty-five years" repeats 1 Kings 22:42.
- **20:33** "The high places were not removed" repeats 1 Kings 22:43, and is in tension with 17:6. "The people had not yet set their hearts on the God of their ancestors" is the Chronicler's addition.
- **20:34** "The records of Jehu son of Hanani, which are included in the Book of the Kings of Israel" are lost sources.
- **20:35** "Jehoshaphat joined with Ahaziah king of Israel" differs from 1 Kings 22:48-49, where Ahaziah offers to join the ships and Jehoshaphat refuses.
- **20:37** "Eliezer son of Dodavahu of Mareshah" is a prophet not named elsewhere. "The ships were wrecked" repeats 1 Kings 22:48.
- **21:1** The account of Jehoram (verses 1 to 20) is parallel to 2 Kings 8:16-24, with much added.
- **21:2** "Jehoshaphat king of Judah" follows the Greek and other versions. The Hebrew text has "king of Israel", which is probably a slip for Judah. The WABV text departs from the Hebrew word here and is flagged for the scholar.
- **21:4** "He killed all his brothers with the sword, and also some of the leaders of Israel" is not in Kings. It is rendered as written.
- **21:6** "He had married a daughter of Ahab" refers to Athaliah (22:2; 2 Kings 8:18, 26).
- **21:7** "Covenant" is Hebrew *berit*. See entry H-BERIT. "A lamp" is a figure for the continuing line of David (1 Kings 11:36).
- **21:8** The revolt of Edom and Libnah (verses 8 to 10) is parallel to 2 Kings 8:20-22. Chronicles leaves out the place name Zair and adds the reason for Libnah's revolt.
- **21:11** "He also made high places in the hill country of Judah" is not in Kings. "Prostitution" is the standard figure for worship of other gods.
- **21:12** "A letter came to him from the prophet Elijah" is the only writing of Elijah in the Bible. Elijah was taken up earlier (2 Kings 2), during the reign of Jehoram of Israel, so the letter may have been written before. The Hebrew does not say that he sent it.
- **21:13** "You have also killed your brothers, your own father's house, who were better than you" refers to verse 4.
- **21:15** "A disease of your bowels, until your bowels come out" is rendered as written. The result is told in verses 18 and 19.
- **21:16** "The spirit of the Philistines and of the Arabs" is Hebrew *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH. The LORD stirs up their will against Jehoram.
- **21:17** "Jehoahaz, his youngest" is called Ahaziah in 22:1 and Azariah in 22:6.
- **21:19** "His people made no fire in his honour" refers to the burning of spices at a royal funeral (see 16:14 and Jeremiah 34:5).
- **21:20** "He died with no one's regret" is the Hebrew idiom for no one missing him. He is buried in the city of David, but not in the tombs of the kings.
- **22:1** "The raiding band that had come with the Arabs" refers to 21:16-17. All the older sons were killed.
- **22:2** "Twenty-two years old" follows 2 Kings 8:26, the Greek and the Syriac. The Hebrew text of Chronicles has "forty-two", which cannot be right, because his father died at forty. The WABV text departs from the Hebrew number and is flagged for the scholar. "A granddaughter of Omri" is Hebrew "daughter of Omri" (*bat Omri*), as in 2 Kings 8:26.
- **22:3** "His mother advised him to act wickedly" is added here. See also 22:4.
- **22:6** "Azariah son of Jehoram" is the Hebrew reading. It is a form of the name Ahaziah. 2 Kings 8:29 has Ahaziah.
- **22:7** "It was from God that Ahaziah's downfall came about" is the Chronicler's comment. "Jehu son of Nimshi, whom the LORD had anointed" refers to 2 Kings 9.
- **22:8** "The sons of Ahaziah's relatives" is the group killed by Jehu in 2 Kings 10:12-14, where forty-two relatives of Ahaziah are killed at Beth-eked. Chronicles tells it briefly.
- **22:9** "Hiding in Samaria ... brought to Jehu and put to death" differs from 2 Kings 9:27, where Ahaziah is shot near Ibleam and dies at Megiddo. The reason for his burial, "He was the grandson of Jehoshaphat", is not in Kings.
- **22:11** "Jehoshabeath" is called Jehosheba in 2 Kings 11:2. That she was "the wife of the priest Jehoiada" is only said here. "A sister of Ahaziah" is the Hebrew reading and means a half-sister.
- **22:12** "In the house of God" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Kings 11:3 has "in the house of the LORD".
- **23:1** The crowning of Joash (verses 1 to 21) is parallel to 2 Kings 11:4-20. "A covenant" is *berit*. See entry H-BERIT. The five commanders are named only here.
- **23:2** "They gathered the Levites from all the towns of Judah" is not in Kings. 2 Kings 11:4 has the captains of the Carites and the guards.
- **23:3** "As the LORD promised about David's descendants" refers to 2 Samuel 7:12-16 and 1 Chronicles 17.
- **23:4** "Priests and Levites ... gatekeepers" is the Chronicler's version. In 2 Kings 11:5-7 the guards do the duty.
- **23:6** "They may enter, for they are holy" is Hebrew *qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH. Only the priests and serving Levites may enter the house. This restriction is not in Kings.
- **23:8** "Jehoiada the priest had not released the divisions" says that both shifts of the courses stayed on duty.
- **23:11** "The testimony" is Hebrew *ha-edut*, which may be a copy of the law or the armlets of the king (see 2 Kings 11:12). "Jehoiada and his sons anointed him" adds that the priests did the anointing.
- **23:13** "The singers with musical instruments leading the praise" is added here.
- **23:14** "Do not put her to death in the house of the LORD" repeats 2 Kings 11:15.
- **23:16** "A covenant between himself and all the people and the king" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Kings 11:17 says it was "between the LORD and the king and the people".
- **23:18** "As written in the law of Moses ... as David had ordered" refers to the offerings and to David's organisation of the Levites (1 Chronicles 23 to 26).
- **23:19** "So that no one who was unclean in any way could enter" is not in Kings. It guards the purity of the house.
- **23:20** "The Upper Gate" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Kings 11:19 has "the gate of the guards".
- **24:1** The reign of Joash (verses 1 to 14) is parallel to 2 Kings 12:1-16.
- **24:2** "All the days of the priest Jehoiada" limits his good record to the time of Jehoiada. 2 Kings 12:2 says "because Jehoiada instructed him".
- **24:3** "Jehoiada took two wives for him" is only in Chronicles.
- **24:5** "Collect money from all Israel" is parallel to 2 Kings 12:4-5. "The Levites did not hurry" is the Chronicler's version of the priests' delay in 2 Kings 12:6.
- **24:6** "The tax that Moses the servant of the LORD laid on the assembly of Israel for the tent of the testimony" refers to the half shekel of Exodus 30:11-16 and 38:25-26. "Jehoiada the chief" is Hebrew *ha-rosh*, the head.
- **24:7** "The sons of that wicked Athaliah had broken into the house of God" says that the temple was plundered, which 2 Kings 12 does not mention. "Holy things" is *qodashim*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **24:8** "A chest" is set outside the gate here. In 2 Kings 12:9 it is beside the altar.
- **24:14** "With it were made vessels" differs from 2 Kings 12:13-14, where the money was not used for new vessels.
- **24:15** "A hundred and thirty years old" is the age of Jehoiada at his death. It is the longest life given since the exile of Israel.
- **24:16** "Among the kings, because he had done good" is an honour given to the priest. He is buried in the royal tombs.
- **24:17** "The leaders of Judah came and bowed down to the king, and the king listened to them" begins the account of Joash's turning away, which is not in Kings.
- **24:19** "To bring them back" is Hebrew *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV. "He sent prophets among them" repeats the pattern of 36:15-16.
- **24:20** "The spirit of God clothed Zechariah" is the Hebrew idiom for being taken over by the spirit (see Judges 6:34 and 1 Chronicles 12:18). "Spirit" is *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH.
- **24:21** "They stoned him to death in the court of the house of the LORD" is rendered as written. Jesus refers to the death of Zechariah in Matthew 23:35 and Luke 11:51.
- **24:22** "The faithful love that Jehoiada ... had shown him" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. "May the LORD see and take revenge" is Zechariah's dying word.
- **24:23** "At the turn of the year the army of Aram came up" is parallel to 2 Kings 12:17-18, where Hazael took Gath and was bought off with the temple gold. Chronicles tells that they destroyed the leaders and sent plunder to Damascus.
- **24:24** "The LORD handed over to them a very large army" gives the reason for the defeat. "They carried out judgement on Joash" says that the invasion was the LORD's judgement.
- **24:25** "Because of the blood of the son of the priest Jehoiada" gives the reason for the plot against Joash. It is not given in 2 Kings 12:20-21. "Not in the tombs of the kings" differs from 2 Kings 12:21, where he is buried with his ancestors.
- **24:26** "Zabad son of Shimeath the Ammonite woman, and Jehozabad son of Shimrith the Moabite woman" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Kings 12:21 has "Jozacar son of Shimeath and Jehozabad son of Shomer".
- **24:27** "The commentary on the Book of the Kings" is Hebrew *midrash sefer ha-melakhim*, a lost source.
- **25:1** The account of Amaziah (verses 1 to 28) is parallel to 2 Kings 14:1-20. "Jehoaddan" is Jehoaddin in 2 Kings 14:2.
- **25:2** "But not with a whole heart" is the Chronicler's addition to the verdict in 2 Kings 14:3.
- **25:4** "As it is written in the law, in the Book of Moses" quotes Deuteronomy 24:16, as in 2 Kings 14:6.
- **25:5** "Three hundred thousand" is the number of Judah's fighting men. Verses 5 to 10, on hiring soldiers from Israel, are not in Kings.
- **25:7** "The LORD is not with Israel, with all these Ephraimites" is the man of God's reason for sending the Israelite soldiers home.
- **25:9** "The LORD can give you much more than this" is the answer about the lost silver.
- **25:11** "The Valley of Salt" is also in 2 Kings 14:7 and 2 Samuel 8:13. "Seir" is Edom.
- **25:12** "The men of Judah also captured ten thousand alive and brought them to the top of a cliff and threw them down" is rendered as written. The word for "cliff" is Hebrew *sela*, which is also the name Sela (2 Kings 14:7). It is not in Kings.
- **25:13** "The soldiers whom Amaziah had sent back" take revenge in raids on Judah. "Three thousand" are killed.
- **25:14** "He brought the gods of the people of Seir and set them up as his own gods" is the king's offence. It is not in Kings.
- **25:16** "Have we made you the king's adviser?" is the king's answer to the prophet. The prophet's reply is the sentence of destruction.
- **25:18** "A thistle in Lebanon sent a message to a cedar" is a fable, as in 2 Kings 14:9.
- **25:20** "For it was from God" is added here. It gives the reason that Amaziah did not listen.
- **25:23** "Four hundred cubits" of the wall are broken down, as in 2 Kings 14:13.
- **25:24** "In the care of Obed-edom" is added. Obed-edom is the Levite gatekeeper of 1 Chronicles 26:15.
- **25:27** "From the time that Amaziah turned away from the LORD" gives a reason for the plot. 2 Kings 14:19 does not give one.
- **25:28** "The city of Judah" is the Hebrew reading. Some manuscripts and 2 Kings 14:20 have "the city of David".
- **26:1** The account of Uzziah (verses 1 to 23) is parallel to 2 Kings 14:21-22 and 15:1-7. "Uzziah" is called Azariah in Kings.
- **26:5** "Who taught him the fear of God" follows many Hebrew manuscripts, the Greek and the Syriac. The Hebrew text most often read has "who understood visions of God". The two words differ by one letter. The WABV text departs from that reading and is flagged for the scholar. "Zechariah" is not otherwise known.
- **26:6** "Gath ... Jabneh ... Ashdod" are Philistine cities.
- **26:7** "Gur-baal" is a place that is not otherwise known. "The Meunites" are an Arabian people.
- **26:9** "The Corner Gate ... the Valley Gate ... the Angle" are parts of the wall of Jerusalem.
- **26:10** "For he loved the soil" says that Uzziah was a king who cared for farming.
- **26:13** "Three hundred and seven thousand five hundred" is the size of the army. "Two thousand six hundred" are the heads of houses (verse 12).
- **26:15** "Machines" translates Hebrew *chishshevonot*, devices designed by skilled men to shoot arrows and stones from the towers. They are the first catapults in the Bible.
- **26:16** "His heart became proud, to his ruin" begins the account of the king's offence. "Unfaithful" is Hebrew *ma'al*, the Chronicler's key word. The king's act is the cause of his skin disease, which 2 Kings 15:5 attributes only to the LORD's striking.
- **26:18** "Set apart" is Hebrew *muqdashim*, "made holy". See entry H-QADOSH. Only the priests may burn incense (Numbers 16:40; 18:7). "The sanctuary" is Hebrew *miqdash*.
- **26:19** "A skin disease" is Hebrew *tsara'at*. See entry H-TSARAAT. It broke out on his forehead as he stood with the censer. See also Numbers 12:10 and 2 Kings 5:27.
- **26:21** "A separate house" repeats 2 Kings 15:5 and Leviticus 13:46.
- **26:22** "The prophet Isaiah son of Amoz wrote" refers to a lost account. Isaiah 6:1 is dated to the year that King Uzziah died.
- **26:23** "In the burial field that belonged to the kings" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Kings 15:7 says he was buried "in the city of David". He was buried in a field because of his disease.
- **27:1** The account of Jotham (verses 1 to 9) is parallel to 2 Kings 15:32-38. "Jerushah daughter of Zadok" is Jerusha in Kings.
- **27:2** "Except that he did not enter the temple of the LORD" is the Chronicler's note, in contrast to Uzziah's act in 26:16-21.
- **27:3** "The Upper Gate of the house of the LORD" repeats 2 Kings 15:35. "The wall of Ophel" is not in Kings.
- **27:5** "A hundred talents of silver, ten thousand cors of wheat and ten thousand of barley" is the tribute of Ammon for three years. It is not in Kings.
- **27:6** "Because he ordered his ways before the LORD his God" is the Chronicler's reason for Jotham's success.
- **27:8** "He was twenty-five years old ... sixteen years" repeats verse 1. It is also in 2 Kings 15:33.
- **28:1** The account of Ahaz (verses 1 to 27) is parallel to 2 Kings 16:1-20, with much added.
- **28:3** "He burned his sons in the fire" is the Hebrew reading here, in the plural. 2 Kings 16:3 has "he made his son pass through the fire". It is rendered as written. "The Valley of Ben-hinnom" is the place of child sacrifice (see 2 Kings 23:10 and Jeremiah 7:31).
- **28:5** "The king of Aram ... the king of Israel" refers to Rezin and Pekah (2 Kings 16:5-6; Isaiah 7:1). Chronicles tells of the results of the war, which Kings does not.
- **28:6** "A hundred and twenty thousand in Judah in one day" is the number killed by Pekah. It is rendered as written.
- **28:7** "Maaseiah the king's son" is a son of Ahaz.
- **28:8** "Two hundred thousand of their relatives" are captives. Israel and Judah are "relatives".
- **28:9** "Oded" is a prophet in Samaria, a rare prophet of the north in Chronicles. "A rage that has reached heaven" is a figure for the extent of the killing.
- **28:10** "To make the people of Judah and Jerusalem ... your slaves" would break the law of Leviticus 25:39-46 against enslaving relatives.
- **28:12** "Some of the leaders of the Ephraimites" are named. They obey the prophet.
- **28:15** "They clothed them and gave them sandals, and gave them food and drink and anointed them" is a picture of care. It is echoed in Jesus' story of the good Samaritan (Luke 10:30-37). "The town of palm trees" is Jericho.
- **28:16** "The kings of Assyria" is the Hebrew plural, as a title for one king (see 2 Kings 16:7).
- **28:17** "The Edomites ... the Philistines" (verses 17 and 18) are further enemies, which are not in Kings.
- **28:19** "Because of Ahaz king of Israel" is the Hebrew reading. The word "Israel" is used here for the king of Judah.
- **28:20** "Tiglath-pileser ... oppressed him instead of helping him" says that the alliance with Assyria did not help (see 2 Kings 16:7-9 and Isaiah 7).
- **28:23** "He sacrificed to the gods of Damascus" differs from 2 Kings 16:10-16, where he builds a copy of the Damascus altar for the LORD's temple.
- **28:24** "Cut them in pieces ... shut the doors of the house of the LORD" is not in Kings. See 29:3 and 29:7, where Hezekiah reopens them.
- **28:27** "They did not bring him into the tombs of the kings of Israel" differs from 2 Kings 16:20, which says he was buried with his ancestors.
- **29:1** Hezekiah's reign is told here at much greater length than in Kings (chapters 29 to 32). "Abijah daughter of Zechariah" is called Abi in 2 Kings 18:2.
- **29:3** "He opened the doors of the house of the LORD and repaired them" reverses Ahaz's closing of the doors (28:24). It is dated to the first month of the first year, which is not in Kings.
- **29:5** "Make yourselves holy" is Hebrew *hitqaddeshu*, and "holy place" is *qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH. The Levites are told to make themselves ready and to clear the filth from the temple.
- **29:6** "Our ancestors were unfaithful" is the Chronicler's word *ma'al*. The confession in verses 6 to 9 blames the fathers for the exile of the north.
- **29:8** "An object of horror, of shock and of mockery" echoes Jeremiah 25:18 and 29:18.
- **29:10** "A covenant" is Hebrew *berit*. See entry H-BERIT. Hezekiah intends to make a covenant with the LORD, so that his anger turns away.
- **29:12** The fourteen Levites in verses 12 to 14 are from the families of Kohath, Merari, Gershon, Elizaphan, Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun.
- **29:16** "The Wadi Kidron" is where the unclean things were carried (see also 30:14).
- **29:17** "On the sixteenth day of the first month they finished" means that the cleansing took sixteen days, so that the Passover of the fourteenth day was missed (see 30:2-3).
- **29:21** "Seven bulls, seven rams, seven lambs and seven male goats" is the number of the sin offering for the kingdom, the sanctuary and Judah.
- **29:24** "To make atonement for all Israel" is Hebrew *lekapper*. See entry H-KAPPER. The sin offering is made "for all Israel", which includes the north.
- **29:25** "As David and Gad the king's seer and Nathan the prophet had commanded" refers to the arrangement of the Levite musicians (1 Chronicles 23:5; 25:1-7).
- **29:30** "The words of David and of Asaph the seer" are psalms. Twelve psalms are attributed to Asaph.
- **29:34** "The Levites were more upright in heart than the priests" is the Chronicler's comment on the priests who had not made themselves ready.
- **30:1** "All Israel" includes the people of the north, after the fall of Samaria in 722 BCE (2 Kings 17:6). The Passover is open to all of them.
- **30:2** "To keep the Passover in the second month" follows the rule for those who were unclean or away (Numbers 9:6-13). This Passover is not in Kings.
- **30:6** "Turn back" is Hebrew *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV. "The kings of Assyria" is a plural title for the king of Assyria. The letters call the north to return to the LORD, so that he may return to them.
- **30:9** "Gracious and compassionate" echoes the LORD's name in Exodus 34:6. The promise is that the captives will find mercy and return.
- **30:10** "People laughed at them and mocked them" is the response of most of the north. Only a few came (verse 11).
- **30:12** "The hand of God was also on Judah, to give them one heart" says that the LORD made Judah united.
- **30:15** "The fourteenth day of the second month" is the date for those who missed the first month (Numbers 9:11). "The priests and the Levites were ashamed" because they had not made themselves ready.
- **30:17** "The Levites had to kill the Passover lambs for everyone who was not clean" differs from the usual rule that the families kill their own lambs.
- **30:18** "They ate the Passover not as it was written" is rendered as written. Hezekiah's prayer asks pardon for those who "set their heart to seek God" though they were not pure.
- **30:20** "The LORD heard Hezekiah and healed the people" says that the LORD accepted the prayer. "Healed" means that the threat of the penalty was removed.
- **30:23** "They kept the festival for another seven days" is a second week of the festival, by agreement.
- **30:25** "The foreigners" is Hebrew *gerim*, resident aliens. See entry H-GER. They are both from the north and from Judah.
- **30:26** "Since the time of Solomon ... nothing like it" is a comparison with Solomon's festival (7:8-10). The Passover of Josiah is said to be greater (35:18).
- **30:27** "Their prayer came to his holy dwelling in heaven" repeats the language of Solomon's prayer (6:21, 30, 33, 39).
- **31:1** "Smashed the sacred pillars and cut down the Asherah poles" repeats 2 Kings 18:4. The reform includes the northern tribes, as in 30:1.
- **31:3** "A portion of his own property" is the king's gift for the regular offerings.
- **31:4** "The portion due to the priests and the Levites" is the tithes and first fruits of Numbers 18. The people give "so that they could devote themselves to the law of the LORD".
- **31:7** "In the third month ... in the seventh month" gives the time for the gifts, from the harvest of grain to the harvest of fruit.
- **31:10** "Azariah the chief priest, of the house of Zadok" is not otherwise known. His words, "we have had enough to eat and plenty left over", report the blessing.
- **31:12** "The holy things" is *qodashim*. See entry H-QADOSH. Conaniah the Levite had charge of the storerooms.
- **31:14** "Kore son of Imnah, the keeper of the east gate" was in charge of the freewill offerings.
- **31:16** "Males three years old and over" are the children of the priests, who were provided with food. The Hebrew of verses 16 to 19 is hard.
- **31:20** "Good and right and faithful" is the Chronicler's summary of Hezekiah's reform.
- **31:21** "With all his heart" repeats the praise in 2 Kings 18:5.
- **32:1** The invasion of Sennacherib (verses 1 to 23) is parallel to 2 Kings 18:13 to 19:37 and Isaiah 36 and 37, but much shorter. "After these acts of faithfulness" ties the invasion to Hezekiah's reform.
- **32:3** "Blocking the water from the springs outside the town" is told in 2 Kings 20:20 only in outline. The Gihon tunnel is in verse 30.
- **32:5** "Strengthened the Millo" is also said of David (1 Chronicles 11:8). "Another wall outside" is a second wall, perhaps the broad wall found in the dig in Jerusalem.
- **32:8** "An arm of flesh" is a figure for human strength (see Isaiah 31:3). Hezekiah's speech is not in Kings.
- **32:9** "He sent his servants to Jerusalem" does not give the titles used in 2 Kings 18:17.
- **32:12** "Is it not the same Hezekiah who removed his high places and altars" repeats the Assyrian argument of 2 Kings 18:22.
- **32:14** "Devoted to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. The Assyrian king says that his ancestors had done this to the nations.
- **32:17** "He also wrote letters to mock the LORD" is the letter in 2 Kings 19:9-14. Chronicles does not tell that Hezekiah spread it out in the temple.
- **32:18** "In the language of Judah" is Hebrew, as in 2 Kings 18:26-28.
- **32:20** "King Hezekiah and the prophet Isaiah son of Amoz prayed" is the whole of the account of the prayers in 2 Kings 19:1-7, 14-34.
- **32:21** "An angel, who wiped out all the brave warriors and commanders and officers" does not give the number of 185,000 in 2 Kings 19:35. "His own sons struck him down" tells of the death of Sennacherib in 681 BCE (2 Kings 19:37).
- **32:23** "Many brought gifts to the LORD in Jerusalem" says that Hezekiah gained honour among the nations.
- **32:24** "Hezekiah became ill and was close to death" is told in two verses. 2 Kings 20:1-11 and Isaiah 38 tell it in detail.
- **32:25** "His heart was proud" is the Chronicler's addition. It is not in Kings. See also verse 26, where he humbles himself.
- **32:30** "Blocked the upper outlet of the waters of Gihon and channelled them down to the west side of the city of David" refers to Hezekiah's water tunnel (2 Kings 20:20).
- **32:31** "The envoys of the officials of Babylon" are told in 2 Kings 20:12-19, with Isaiah's word. Here the visit is a test. "God left him to himself" says that the LORD withdrew to test him.
- **32:32** "The vision of the prophet Isaiah" is a source, which may be the book of Isaiah.
- **32:33** "The upper slope of the tombs" is the Hebrew reading (*ma'aleh*), which may mean the upper part of the royal tombs. Hezekiah is honoured at his death.
- **33:1** The account of Manasseh (verses 1 to 20) is parallel to 2 Kings 21:1-18, with his exile and repentance added.
- **33:6** "He made his sons pass through the fire" is the Hebrew idiom for child sacrifice. See 28:3 and 2 Kings 21:6.
- **33:7** "The carved image of the idol that he had made" is placed in the house of God, against the LORD's word to David and Solomon (see 6:5-6 and 7:16).
- **33:11** "The commanders of the army of the king of Assyria, who captured Manasseh with hooks" tells of a capture that is not in Kings. "Hooks" is a figure of the way that Assyrians led captives. "Babylon" was under the Assyrian king in this time. Assyrian records name Manasseh as a vassal.
- **33:12** "He sought the favour of the LORD ... and humbled himself greatly" is the Chronicler's example of repentance (see 7:14).
- **33:13** "God was moved by his plea" uses a verb (*atar*) for being moved by prayer. "Then Manasseh knew that the LORD was God" says he repented.
- **33:14** "An outer wall for the city of David" and the army commanders are the later works of Manasseh, which are not in Kings. "The Fish Gate" and "the Ophel" are parts of the town.
- **33:17** "The people still sacrificed at the high places, but only to the LORD their God" is the Chronicler's note that the worship was not at the place chosen.
- **33:18** "His prayer to his God" refers to a prayer of Manasseh that the book does not give. A later work called the Prayer of Manasseh is not part of this book.
- **33:19** "The records of the seers" is the Greek reading. The Hebrew text has "the records of Hozai", which may be a name. The WABV text follows the Greek.
- **33:20** "They buried him in his own house" differs from 2 Kings 21:18, which says "in the garden of his house, the garden of Uzza".
- **33:21** The account of Amon (verses 21 to 25) is parallel to 2 Kings 21:19-26.
- **33:23** "He did not humble himself before the LORD, as his father Manasseh had humbled himself" is the Chronicler's contrast.
- **34:1** The account of Josiah (chapters 34 and 35) is parallel to 2 Kings 22:1 to 23:30. "Eight years old" repeats 2 Kings 22:1.
- **34:3** "In the eighth year of his reign, while he was still young, he began to seek the God of his ancestor David" and the clearing in the twelfth year are not in Kings. Kings has the whole reform after the finding of the book (2 Kings 23).
- **34:4** "Scattered it over the graves of those who had sacrificed to them" repeats the manner of the reform in 2 Kings 23:6, 14.
- **34:5** "He also burned the bones of the priests on their altars" follows 1 Kings 13:2 and 2 Kings 23:16-20.
- **34:6** "In the ruins round about" is Hebrew *becharvoteihem*, a hard word that may mean "in their ruins" or "with their swords". The sense is uncertain.
- **34:8** "In the eighteenth year" is the year of the finding of the book, as in 2 Kings 22:3.
- **34:12** "The Levites, all who were skilled with musical instruments" supervised the work. This is not in Kings.
- **34:14** "The Book of the Law of the LORD given through Moses" adds "through Moses" to the account in 2 Kings 22:8.
- **34:20** "Abdon son of Micah" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Kings 22:12 has "Achbor son of Micaiah".
- **34:22** "Huldah the prophet" is Hebrew *neviah*. See entry H-NAVI. "Tokhath" and "Hasrah" are Tikvah and Harhas in 2 Kings 22:14.
- **34:24** "All the curses written in the book" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Kings 22:16 has "all the words of the book".
- **34:28** "You will be gathered to your grave in peace" is also in 2 Kings 22:20, and it is in tension with his death in battle (35:23-24).
- **34:30** "The Book of the Covenant" is Hebrew *sefer ha-berit*. See entry H-BERIT.
- **34:33** "He removed all the detestable things" is the Chronicler's summary of the reform.
- **35:1** The Passover of Josiah (verses 1 to 19) is parallel to 2 Kings 23:21-23, but is much longer.
- **35:3** "You no longer need to carry it on your shoulders" says that the ark is in its resting place. "Holy" is *qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH. The Levites "taught all Israel".
- **35:4** "As written by David king of Israel and by his son Solomon" refers to the arrangements in 1 Chronicles 23 to 26.
- **35:7** "Thirty thousand lambs and young goats ... and three thousand bulls" are the king's gift. The gifts of the officials follow in verses 8 and 9.
- **35:13** "They roasted the Passover lambs over the fire as prescribed, and boiled the holy offerings in pots" joins the rule of Exodus 12:9 (roast, not boiled) with Deuteronomy 16:7, where the Hebrew word for "cook" is the same as "boil". The Hebrew of this verse plays on that word.
- **35:15** "The singers ... as David, Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun the king's seer had commanded" refers to 1 Chronicles 25.
- **35:18** "No Passover like it had been kept in Israel since the days of the prophet Samuel" differs from 2 Kings 23:22, which says "since the days of the judges". It also stands against 30:26.
- **35:20** "Neco king of Egypt went up to fight at Carchemish" is a campaign in support of Assyria (see 2 Kings 23:29).
- **35:21** "God has told me to hurry" and "stop opposing God" are Neco's words, which the Chronicler gives as a word from God. 2 Kings 23:29 says only that Neco killed Josiah at Megiddo.
- **35:22** "Josiah ... disguised himself" repeats the act of Ahab (18:29). "From the mouth of God" says that the warning was from God.
- **35:24** "He died and was buried in the tombs of his ancestors" says he died in Jerusalem. 2 Kings 23:30 says that his servants carried his body from Megiddo.
- **35:25** "Jeremiah also composed a lament for Josiah" is not the book of Lamentations. It is a lost lament. "The Laments" is a collection that is not known.
- **36:1** "The people of the land took Jehoahaz" repeats 2 Kings 23:30. The account of the last four kings (verses 1 to 21) is much shorter than 2 Kings 23:31 to 25:21.
- **36:3** "The king of Egypt removed him" and the tribute repeat 2 Kings 23:33.
- **36:6** "Nebuchadnezzar ... bound him in bronze chains to take him to Babylon" differs from 2 Kings 24:6, which says that Jehoiakim lay down with his ancestors. The text does not say that he arrived.
- **36:8** "What was found against him" is a hard Hebrew phrase. It refers to the record of his deeds.
- **36:9** "Eight years old" is the Hebrew reading here. 2 Kings 24:8 has eighteen. "Three months and ten days" is a more exact period than 2 Kings.
- **36:10** "In the spring" is the time of the first exile, in 597 BCE. "His relative Zedekiah" is Hebrew "his brother", a word that can mean a relative. 2 Kings 24:17 says he was Jehoiachin's uncle.
- **36:12** "He did not humble himself before the prophet Jeremiah" is the Chronicler's note. Jeremiah is not named in 2 Kings.
- **36:13** "Who had made him swear by God" refers to the oath of loyalty to Babylon (Ezekiel 17:13-19). "Turning back" is Hebrew *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **36:14** "They made the house of the LORD ... unclean" is the Chronicler's summary. "Very unfaithful" is the word *ma'al*.
- **36:15** "He sent word to them again and again through his messengers, because he had compassion" gives the LORD's patience. See 24:19.
- **36:16** "Until there was no remedy" is the end of the pattern of prophetic warning. It is the Chronicler's reason for the exile.
- **36:17** "Killed their young men with the sword in the house of their sanctuary" is not in 2 Kings 25. It is rendered as written.
- **36:19** "They burned the house of God" is told in one clause, as in 2 Kings 25:9.
- **36:20** "Until the kingdom of Persia came to power" gives the time of the exile.
- **36:21** "To fulfil the word of the LORD through Jeremiah, until the land had enjoyed its sabbaths" joins Jeremiah 25:11-12 and 29:10 with the law of Leviticus 26:34-35, 43. "Seventy years" is the length of the land's rest.
- **36:22** "The LORD stirred up the spirit of Cyrus" uses *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH. The same words are in Ezra 1:1. The decree of verses 22 and 23 is also the opening of Ezra 1:1-3, and the wording of the two must match.
- **36:23** "Let him go up" ends the book in the Hebrew. In Ezra 1:3 the sentence goes on, "to Jerusalem". The Hebrew Bible ends with this verse.
