# WABV: Ecclesiastes

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-RUACH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Ecclesiastes is set in prose, with poetry in 1:2-11, 3:1-8, 7:1-14 (in part), 11:7 to 12:8 and in many single sayings, and the chapters are numbered in the English way. In the Hebrew, chapter 4 has 17 verses and chapter 5 has 19, so that English 5:1-20 is Hebrew 4:17 to 5:19, and the footnote on 5:1 gives the matter. The book falls into parts: the title and the poem on the breath of everything (1:1-11), the Teacher's search (1:12 to 6:12), the Teacher's advice on how to live (7:1 to 11:6), and the poem on youth and age with the closing words (11:7 to 12:14). The key word is *hevel*, "breath", which is given as "breath" or "a breath" throughout, as in Psalm 144:4, and the footnote on 1:2 records the other readings ("vanity", "vapour", "meaningless"). The name *Qohelet* is given as "the Teacher".*

## Part One: The Teacher's Search

### Chapter 1

**1** The words of the Teacher, son of David, king in Jerusalem.

**2** "A breath of breaths," says the Teacher, "a breath of breaths. Everything is a breath."

**3** What does a person gain from all his toil at which he toils under the sun?

**4** A generation goes, and a generation comes, but the earth remains for ever.

**5** The sun rises, and the sun sets, and hurries back to the place where it rises.

**6** The wind blows to the south and goes round to the north. Round and round goes the wind, and on its rounds the wind returns.

**7** All streams run to the sea, but the sea is not full. To the place where the streams flow, there they flow again.

**8** All things are wearisome. No one can put it into words. The eye is not satisfied with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing.

**9** What has been is what will be, and what has been done is what will be done, and there is nothing new under the sun.

**10** Is there a thing of which it is said, "See, this is new"? It has been already in the ages before us.

**11** There is no remembrance of former things, nor will there be any remembrance of later things yet to be among those who come after.

**12** I, the Teacher, have been king over Israel in Jerusalem.

**13** And I applied my heart to seek and to search out by wisdom all that is done under heaven. It is an unhappy business that God has given to humankind to be busy with.

**14** I have seen everything that is done under the sun, and see, all is a breath and a chasing after wind.

**15** What is crooked cannot be made straight, and what is lacking cannot be counted.

**16** I said in my heart, "I have acquired great wisdom, surpassing all who were over Jerusalem before me, and my heart has had great experience of wisdom and knowledge."

**17** And I applied my heart to know wisdom and to know madness and folly. I perceived that this also is a chasing after wind.

**18** For in much wisdom is much vexation, and those who increase knowledge increase sorrow.

### Chapter 2

**1** I said in my heart, "Come now, I will test you with pleasure. Enjoy yourself." But see, this also was a breath.

**2** I said of laughter, "It is madness," and of pleasure, "What use is it?"

**3** I searched with my heart how to cheer my body with wine, my heart still guiding me with wisdom, and how to lay hold on folly, until I might see what was good for humankind to do under heaven during the few days of their life.

**4** I made great works. I built houses and planted vineyards for myself.

**5** I made myself gardens and parks and planted in them all kinds of fruit trees.

**6** I made myself pools of water from which to water the forest of growing trees.

**7** I bought male and female slaves, and had slaves who were born in my house. I had also great possessions of herds and flocks, more than any who had been before me in Jerusalem.

**8** I also gathered for myself silver and gold and the treasure of kings and provinces. I got singers, both men and women, and many concubines, the delight of humankind.

**9** So I became great and surpassed all who were before me in Jerusalem. My wisdom also remained with me.

**10** And whatever my eyes desired I did not keep from them. I kept my heart from no pleasure, for my heart found pleasure in all my toil, and this was my reward for all my toil.

**11** Then I considered all that my hands had done and the toil I had spent in doing it, and see, all was a breath and a chasing after wind, and there was nothing to be gained under the sun.

**12** So I turned to consider wisdom and madness and folly. For what can the one do who comes after the king? Only what has already been done.

**13** Then I saw that there is more gain in wisdom than in folly, as there is more gain in light than in darkness.

**14** The wise person has his eyes in his head, but the fool walks in darkness. And yet I perceived that the same event happens to all of them.

**15** Then I said in my heart, "What happens to the fool will happen to me also. Why then have I been so very wise?" And I said in my heart that this also is a breath.

**16** For there is no enduring remembrance of the wise any more than of the fool, seeing that in the days to come all will have been long forgotten. How the wise dies just like the fool.

**17** So I hated life, because what is done under the sun was grievous to me, for all is a breath and a chasing after wind.

**18** I hated all my toil in which I toil under the sun, seeing that I must leave it to the one who will come after me,

**19** and who knows whether he will be wise or a fool? Yet he will be master of all for which I toiled and used my wisdom under the sun. This also is a breath.

**20** So I turned about and gave my heart up to despair over all the toil of my labours under the sun,

**21** because sometimes a person who has toiled with wisdom and knowledge and skill must leave all to be enjoyed by someone who did not toil for it. This also is a breath and a great evil.

**22** What does a person get from all the toil and striving of heart with which he toils under the sun?

**23** For all his days are full of sorrow, and his work is a vexation. Even in the night his heart does not rest. This also is a breath.

**24** There is nothing better for a person than that he should eat and drink and find enjoyment in his toil. This also, I saw, is from the hand of God,

**25** for apart from him who can eat or who can have enjoyment?

**26** For to the one who pleases him God gives wisdom and knowledge and joy, but to the sinner he gives the business of gathering and collecting, only to give to the one who pleases God. This also is a breath and a chasing after wind.

### Chapter 3

**1** Everything has its season, and there is a time for every matter under heaven:

**2** a time to be born, and a time to die. A time to plant, and a time to pluck up what is planted.

**3** A time to kill, and a time to heal. A time to break down, and a time to build up.

**4** A time to weep, and a time to laugh. A time to mourn, and a time to dance.

**5** A time to cast away stones, and a time to gather stones together. A time to embrace, and a time to refrain from embracing.

**6** A time to seek, and a time to lose. A time to keep, and a time to cast away.

**7** A time to tear, and a time to sew. A time to keep silence, and a time to speak.

**8** A time to love, and a time to hate. A time for war, and a time for peace \[H-SHALOM\].

**9** What gain has the worker from his toil?

**10** I have seen the business that God has given to humankind to be busy with.

**11** He has made everything beautiful in its time. He has also put eternity into the human heart, yet so that no one can find out what God has done from the beginning to the end.

**12** I perceived that there is nothing better for them than to be joyful and to do good as long as they live,

**13** and also that everyone should eat and drink and see good in all his toil. This is God's gift to humankind.

**14** I perceived that whatever God does endures for ever. Nothing can be added to it, nor anything taken from it. God has done it, so that people fear before him.

**15** That which is, already has been. That which is to be, already has been. And God seeks what has been driven away.

**16** Moreover, I saw under the sun that in the place of justice, even there was wickedness, and in the place of righteousness \[H-TSEDAQAH\], even there was wickedness.

**17** I said in my heart, "God will judge the righteous and the wicked, for there is a time for every matter and for every work."

**18** I said in my heart with regard to humankind that God is testing them that they may see that they themselves are but beasts.

**19** For what happens to humankind and what happens to the beasts is the same. As one dies, so dies the other. They all have the same breath \[H-RUACH\], and humankind has no advantage over the beasts, for all is a breath.

**20** All go to one place. All are from the dust, and to dust all return.

**21** Who knows whether the spirit \[H-RUACH\] of humankind goes upward and the spirit of the beast goes down into the earth?

**22** So I saw that there is nothing better than that a person should rejoice in his work, for that is his lot. Who can bring him to see what will be after him?

*Review note: Ecclesiastes 1 to 3 were drafted in WABV wording. They open the book with the title (1:1), the poem on the breath of everything and the cycles of nature (1:2-11), the Teacher's account of his search as king by wisdom, pleasure and work (1:12 to 2:26), and the poem on the times (3:1-8) with the Teacher's comments on it (3:9-22). The key word *hevel* is given as "breath" throughout (1:2, 1:14, 2:1, 2:11, 2:15, 2:17, 2:19, 2:21, 2:23, 2:26, 3:19), as in Psalm 144:4, and the footnote on 1:2 records the other readings ("vanity", "vapour", "meaningless"), so that the meaning is open. The name *Qohelet* is given as "the Teacher". In 1:14 "a chasing after wind" translates *re'ut ruach*, and the footnote records that the word may also mean "feeding on wind" or "a striving after wind". In 1:2-11 and 3:19-21 the words for wind, breath and spirit are all the Hebrew *ruach*, and the tag is given where the sense is the breath of life or the spirit (3:19, 3:21). In 3:11 "eternity" translates *olam*, and the footnote records that the word may also mean "the world", "a long time" or, in some readings, "darkness". In 3:15 "God seeks what has been driven away" is rendered as written, and the footnote records the other readings. In 3:18-21 the text on the beasts and the spirit is rendered as written, and the Hebrew in 3:21 is a question that may be read in more than one way. In 2:8 the word for concubines is a rare and uncertain one, and the footnote records the matter. The Hebrew is very hard in some places, including 1:6, 1:13, 2:3, 2:8, 2:12, 2:25, 3:11, 3:15 and 3:17. *At 2:25 the WABV text gives "apart from him", following the Greek and Syriac, where the Hebrew has "apart from me", and it departs from the Hebrew. It is the one departure in the section, and it is flagged for the scholar.* The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

### Chapter 4

**1** Again I saw all the oppressions that are done under the sun. And see, the tears of the oppressed, and they had no one to comfort them. On the side of their oppressors there was power, and there was no one to comfort them.

**2** And I thought the dead who are already dead more fortunate than the living who are still alive.

**3** But better than both is the one who has not yet been, who has not seen the evil deeds that are done under the sun.

**4** Then I saw that all toil and all skill in work come from a person's envy of his neighbour. This also is a breath and a chasing after wind.

**5** The fool folds his hands and eats his own flesh.

**6** Better is a handful of quietness than two hands full of toil and a chasing after wind.

**7** Again I saw a breath under the sun:

**8** one person alone, without a second, having neither son nor brother, yet there is no end to all his toil, and his eyes are never satisfied with riches, so that he never asks, "For whom am I toiling and depriving myself of pleasure?" This also is a breath and an unhappy business.

**9** Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their toil.

**10** For if they fall, one will lift up his fellow. But woe to the one who is alone when he falls and has no one to lift him up.

**11** Again, if two lie together, they keep warm, but how can one keep warm alone?

**12** And though a person might prevail against one who is alone, two will withstand him. A threefold cord is not quickly broken.

**13** Better was a poor and wise youth than an old and foolish king who no longer knew how to take advice,

**14** for he went from prison to the throne, though in his own kingdom he had been born poor.

**15** I saw all the living who move about under the sun, along with that youth who was to stand in the king's place.

**16** There was no end to all the people, to all whom he led. Yet those who come later will not rejoice in him. Surely this also is a breath and a chasing after wind.

### Chapter 5

**1** Watch your feet when you go to the house of God. To draw near to listen is better than to offer the sacrifice of fools, for they do not know that they are doing evil.

**2** Do not be rash with your mouth, nor let your heart be hasty to utter a word before God, for God is in heaven and you are on earth. Therefore let your words be few.

**3** For a dream comes with much business, and a fool's voice with many words.

**4** When you make a vow to God, do not delay paying it, for he has no pleasure in fools. Pay what you vow.

**5** It is better that you should not vow than that you should vow and not pay.

**6** Do not let your mouth lead you into sin, and do not say before the messenger that it was a mistake. Why should God be angry at your voice and destroy the work of your hands?

**7** For when dreams increase and words grow many, there is breath. But fear God.

**8** If you see in a province the oppression of the poor and the violation of justice and righteousness \[H-TSEDAQAH\], do not be amazed at the matter, for the high official is watched by a higher, and there are yet higher ones over them.

**9** But the profit of the land is for all. The king himself is served by the field.

**10** The one who loves money will not be satisfied with money, nor the one who loves wealth with his income. This also is a breath.

**11** When goods increase, those who eat them increase, and what advantage has their owner but to see them with his eyes?

**12** Sweet is the sleep of the labourer, whether he eats little or much, but the full stomach of the rich will not let him sleep.

**13** There is a grievous evil that I have seen under the sun: riches were kept by their owner to his hurt,

**14** and those riches were lost in a bad venture. And he is father of a son, but he has nothing in his hand.

**15** As he came from his mother's womb he will go again, naked as he came, and will take nothing for his toil that he may carry away in his hand.

**16** This also is a grievous evil: just as he came, so will he go, and what gain is there to the one who toils for the wind?

**17** Moreover, all his days he eats in darkness in much vexation and sickness and anger.

**18** See, what I have seen to be good and fitting is to eat and drink and find enjoyment in all the toil with which one toils under the sun the few days of his life that God has given him, for this is his lot.

**19** Everyone also to whom God has given wealth and possessions and power to enjoy them, and to accept his lot and rejoice in his toil, this is the gift of God.

**20** For he will not much remember the days of his life, because God keeps him occupied with joy in his heart.

### Chapter 6

**1** There is an evil that I have seen under the sun, and it lies heavy on humankind:

**2** a person to whom God gives wealth, possessions and honour, so that he lacks nothing of all that he desires, yet God does not give him power to enjoy them, but a stranger enjoys them. This is a breath. It is a grievous evil.

**3** If a person fathers a hundred children and lives many years, so that the days of his years are many, but his soul \[H-NEPHESH\] is not satisfied with life's good things, and he has no burial, I say that a stillborn child is better off than he.

**4** For it comes in breath and goes in darkness, and in darkness its name is covered.

**5** Moreover, it has not seen the sun or known anything, yet it finds rest rather than he.

**6** Even if he should live a thousand years twice over, yet enjoy no good, do not all go to the one place?

**7** All the toil of a person is for his mouth, yet his appetite \[H-NEPHESH\] is not satisfied.

**8** For what advantage has the wise person over the fool? And what does the poor person have who knows how to conduct himself before the living?

**9** Better is the sight of the eyes than the wandering of desire \[H-NEPHESH\]. This also is a breath and a chasing after wind.

**10** Whatever has come to be has already been named, and it is known what a person is, and that he is not able to dispute with one stronger than he.

**11** The more words, the more breath, and what is the advantage to a person?

**12** For who knows what is good for a person while he lives the few days of his breath-like life, which he passes like a shadow? For who can tell a person what will be after him under the sun?

## Part Two: The Teacher's Advice

### Chapter 7

**1** A good name is better than precious ointment, and the day of death than the day of birth.

**2** It is better to go to the house of mourning than to go to the house of feasting, for this is the end of all humankind, and the living will lay it to heart.

**3** Sorrow is better than laughter, for by sadness of face the heart is made glad.

**4** The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning, but the heart of fools is in the house of pleasure.

**5** It is better for a person to hear the rebuke of the wise than to hear the song of fools.

**6** For as the crackling of thorns under a pot, so is the laughter of fools. This also is a breath.

**7** Surely oppression drives the wise into madness, and a bribe corrupts the heart.

**8** Better is the end of a thing than its beginning. Better is the patient in spirit than the proud in spirit.

**9** Do not be quick in your spirit \[H-RUACH\] to become angry, for anger lodges in the heart of fools.

**10** Do not say, "Why were the former days better than these?" For it is not from wisdom that you ask this.

**11** Wisdom is good with an inheritance, an advantage to those who see the sun.

**12** For the protection of wisdom is like the protection of money, and the advantage of knowledge is that wisdom preserves the life of the one who has it.

**13** Consider the work of God. Who can make straight what he has made crooked?

**14** In the day of prosperity be joyful, and in the day of adversity consider: God has made the one as well as the other, so that no one can find out anything that will be after him.

**15** In my breath-like days I have seen everything. There is a righteous person who perishes in his righteousness \[H-TSEDAQAH\], and there is a wicked person who prolongs his life in his evildoing.

**16** Do not be overly righteous, and do not make yourself too wise. Why should you destroy yourself?

**17** Do not be overly wicked, neither be a fool. Why should you die before your time?

**18** It is good that you should take hold of the one, and from the other do not withhold your hand, for the one who fears God will come out with both.

**19** Wisdom gives strength to the wise more than ten rulers who are in a city.

**20** Surely there is no righteous person on earth who does good and never sins.

**21** Do not take to heart all the things that people say, lest you hear your servant cursing you.

**22** Your heart knows that many times you yourself have cursed others.

**23** All this I have tested by wisdom. I said, "I will be wise," but it was far from me.

**24** That which has been is far off and deep, very deep. Who can find it out?

**25** I turned my heart to know and to search out and to seek wisdom and the scheme of things, and to know the wickedness of folly and the foolishness that is madness.

**26** And I find something more bitter than death: the woman whose heart is snares and nets, and whose hands are fetters. The one who pleases God escapes her, but the sinner is taken by her.

**27** See, this is what I found, says the Teacher, adding one thing to another to find the scheme of things,

**28** which my soul \[H-NEPHESH\] has sought repeatedly, but I have not found. One man among a thousand I found, but a woman among all these I have not found.

**29** See, this alone I have found, that God made humankind upright, but they have sought out many schemes.

### Chapter 8

**1** Who is like the wise person? And who knows the interpretation of a thing? A person's wisdom makes his face shine, and the hardness of his face is changed.

**2** Keep the king's command, because of God's oath to him.

**3** Do not be hasty to go from his presence. Do not take your stand in an evil cause, for he does whatever he pleases.

**4** For the word of the king is supreme, and who may say to him, "What are you doing?"

**5** Whoever keeps a command will know no evil thing, and the wise heart will know the proper time and the just way.

**6** For there is a time and a way for everything, although a person's trouble lies heavy on him.

**7** For he does not know what is to be, for who can tell him how it will be?

**8** No one has power over the spirit \[H-RUACH\] to retain the spirit, or power over the day of death. There is no discharge from war, nor will wickedness deliver those who are given to it.

**9** All this I have seen, applying my heart to all that is done under the sun, when one person has power over another to his hurt.

**10** Then I saw the wicked buried. They used to go in and out of the holy place \[H-QADOSH\], and they were forgotten in the city where they had done so. This also is a breath.

**11** Because the sentence against an evil deed is not executed speedily, the heart of humankind is fully set to do evil.

**12** Though a sinner does evil a hundred times and prolongs his life, yet I know that it will be well with those who fear God, because they fear before him.

**13** But it will not be well with the wicked, neither will he prolong his days like a shadow, because he does not fear before God.

**14** There is a breath that takes place on earth, that there are righteous people to whom it happens according to the deeds of the wicked, and there are wicked people to whom it happens according to the deeds of the righteous. I said that this also is a breath.

**15** And I commend joy, for there is nothing better for a person under the sun but to eat and drink and be joyful, for this will go with him in his toil through the days of his life that God has given him under the sun.

**16** When I applied my heart to know wisdom, and to see the business that is done on earth, how neither day nor night do one's eyes see sleep,

**17** then I saw all the work of God, that a person cannot find out the work that is done under the sun. However much a person may toil in seeking, he will not find it out. Even though a wise person claims to know, he cannot find it out.

### Chapter 9:1-12

**1** But all this I laid to heart, examining it all, that the righteous and the wise and their deeds are in the hand of God. Whether it is love or hate, a person does not know. Everything before them is a breath,

**2** since the same event happens to all, to the righteous and the wicked, to the good and the evil, to the clean and the unclean, to those who sacrifice and those who do not sacrifice. As the good one is, so is the sinner, and the one who swears is as the one who shuns an oath.

**3** This is an evil in all that happens under the sun, that the same event happens to all. Also, the hearts of humankind are full of evil, and madness is in their hearts while they live, and after that they go to the dead.

**4** But the one who is joined with all the living has hope, for a living dog is better than a dead lion.

**5** For the living know that they will die, but the dead know nothing, and they have no more reward, for the memory of them is forgotten.

**6** Their love and their hate and their envy have already perished, and for ever they have no more share in all that is done under the sun.

**7** Go, eat your bread with joy, and drink your wine with a merry heart, for God has already approved what you do.

**8** Let your garments be always white. Do not let oil be lacking on your head.

**9** Enjoy life with the wife whom you love, all the days of your breath-like life that he has given you under the sun, because that is your portion in life and in your toil at which you toil under the sun.

**10** Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might, for there is no work or thought or knowledge or wisdom in Sheol \[H-SHEOL\], to which you are going.

**11** Again I saw that under the sun the race is not to the swift, nor the battle to the strong, nor bread to the wise, nor riches to the intelligent, nor favour to those with knowledge, but time and chance happen to them all.

**12** For a person does not know his time. Like fish that are taken in an evil net, and like birds that are caught in a snare, so the children of humankind are snared at an evil time, when it suddenly falls on them.

### Chapter 9:13-18

**13** I have also seen this example of wisdom under the sun, and it seemed great to me:

**14** there was a little city with few people in it, and a great king came against it and besieged it, building great siegeworks against it.

**15** But there was found in it a poor wise person, and he by his wisdom delivered the city. Yet no one remembered that poor person.

**16** But I say that wisdom is better than might, though the poor person's wisdom is despised and his words are not heard.

**17** The words of the wise heard in quiet are better than the shouting of a ruler among fools.

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

- **1:1** "The words of the Teacher" translates *divrei Qohelet*. The word *Qohelet* is a feminine form from the verb *qahal*, "assemble", and may mean "one who assembles people", "one who gathers sayings", "teacher" or "preacher". The book does not name him, and the tradition that he is Solomon is based on "son of David, king in Jerusalem". The WABV text gives "the Teacher".
- **1:2** "A breath of breaths" translates *havel havalim*. The word *hevel* means "breath", "vapour" or "puff of air", and it is a figure for what is brief, empty or without substance. It is often given as "vanity", "meaningless" or "futile", and the WABV text gives "breath" throughout, as in Psalm 144:4. The form "a breath of breaths" is a superlative, like "a song of songs". "Everything" translates *ha-kol*. The words are repeated in 12:8.
- **1:3** "Gain" translates *yitron*, which means "surplus" or "profit", and is a key word in the book. "Toil" translates *amal*. "Under the sun" translates *tachat ha-shamesh*, a phrase that is used about thirty times in the book.
- **1:6** The order of the lines (the wind goes south, then north) is as in the Hebrew, and the verse is hard. The word for wind is *ruach*.
- **1:8** "All things are wearisome" translates *kol-ha-devarim yegei'im*, in which *devarim* may mean "words" or "things". The line may be read "all words are weary".

- **9:10** "With your might" translates *be-kochakha*. "Sheol" is the place of the dead. See entry H-SHEOL. The saying is rendered as written, with no softening.
- **9:11** "Time and chance" translates *et va-pega*.
- **9:12** "An evil net" translates *metsodah ra'ah*, and "an evil time" translates *et ra'ah*.
- **9:13** "An example of wisdom" translates *zo gam-zo ra'iti chokhmah*, "this too I have seen as wisdom".
- **9:14** "A little city" translates *ir qetannah*, and "siegeworks" translates *metsodim gedolim*.
- **9:15** "A poor wise person" translates *ish misken chakham*. "Delivered the city" translates *u-millat hu-hu et-ha-ir*, which may be read "he could have delivered the city".
- **9:16** "Might" translates *gevurah*.
- **9:17** "Heard in quiet" translates *be-nachat nishma'im*, and "the shouting" translates *za'aqat*.
- **9:18** "Weapons of war" translates *keli qrav*, and "much good" translates *tovah harbeh*.
- **10:1** "Dead flies make the perfumer's ointment give off a stench" translates *zevuvei mavet yav'ish yabbia shemen roqeach*, which is hard in the Hebrew. "A little folly outweighs wisdom and honour" translates *yaqar me-chokhmah me-kavod sikhlut me'at*, which is also hard.
- **10:2** "Toward the right" translates *li-yemino*, and "toward the left" translates *li-smolo*.
- **10:3** "Lacks sense" translates *libbo chaser*, "his heart is lacking".
- **10:4** "The anger of the ruler" translates *ruach ha-moshel*, in which *ruach* means "spirit" or "temper". "Calmness" translates *marpe*, "healing".
- **10:5** "An error" translates *shegagah*.
- **10:6** "In many high places" translates *ba-merommim rabbim*.
- **10:8** "A pit" translates *gummats*, and "a wall" translates *gader*.
- **10:9** "Quarries" translates *massi'a*, and "is endangered" translates *yissakhen*. The saying is hard.
- **10:10** "If the iron is blunt, and one does not sharpen the edge, he must use more strength, but wisdom helps one to succeed" is among the hardest verses in the book (*im-keha ha-barzel ve-hu lo-fanim qilqal ve-chayalim yegabber ve-yitron hakhsher chokhmah*).
- **10:11** "The charmer" translates *ba'al ha-lashon*, "the master of the tongue".
- **10:12** "Win him favour" translates *chen*, and "consume him" translates *tevalle'ennu*.
- **10:13** "Evil madness" translates *holelut ra'ah*.
- **10:14** "No one knows what is to be" translates *lo-yeda ha-adam mah-shehayah*, as in 6:12.
- **10:15** "He does not know the way to the city" is a proverb of one who cannot find the plain way.
- **10:16** "A child" translates *na'ar*, and "feast in the morning" translates *ba-boqer yokhelu*.
- **10:17** "The son of the nobles" translates *ben-chorim*.
- **10:18** "Sloth" translates *atsaltayim*, "the roof sinks in" translates *yimmakh ha-meqareh*, and "leaks" translates *yidlof*.
- **10:19** "Money answers everything" translates *ve-ha-kesef ya'aneh et-ha-kol*. It is rendered as written, with no softening.
- **10:20** "A bird of the heavens" translates *of ha-shamayim*, and "some winged creature" translates *ba'al kenafayim*, "a master of wings".
- **11:1** "Send out your bread on the waters" translates *shallach lachmekha al-penei ha-mayim*. It may refer to trade by sea, to charity, or to a risk taken in faith. The footnote does not decide the matter.
- **11:2** "Give a portion to seven, or even to eight" translates *ten-chelleq la-shiv'ah ve-gam li-shemonah*.
- **11:5** "The way the breath comes to the bones" translates *derekh ha-ruach ka-atsamim*, in which *ruach* may mean "breath", "wind" or "spirit". See entry H-RUACH. "In the womb of a pregnant woman" translates *be-veten ha-meleah*. The saying is like Psalm 139:15 and Job 10:8-11.
- **11:7** "Light is sweet" translates *u-matoq ha-or*.
- **11:8** "The days of darkness" translates *yemei ha-choshekh*.
- **11:9** "Rejoice, young person, in your youth" translates *simach bachur be-yaldutekha*. "Walk in the ways of your heart and the sight of your eyes" translates *halekh be-darkhei libbekha u-ve-mar'ei einekha*, which may be read in tension with Numbers 15:39. "Judgement" translates *mishpat*.
- **11:10** "Vexation" translates *ka'as*, and "pain" translates *ra'ah*. "Youth and the dawn of life" translates *ha-yaldut ve-ha-shacharut*, in which *shacharut* is rare and may mean "dawn" or "black hair".
- **12:1** "Your Creator" translates *bor'ekha*, which is a plural form, and may be a plural of majesty. Some read the word as "your well" or "your grave", and the footnote does not decide the matter.
- **12:3** The verses 3 to 5 are a series of images of old age, and the readings are open. "The keepers of the house" may be the arms, "the strong men" the legs, "the grinders" the teeth, and "those who look through the windows" the eyes.
- **12:4** "The doors on the street are shut" may refer to the ears or the lips. "One rises up at the sound of a bird" means that sleep is light. "The daughters of song" may be the voice, or the ears that hear it.
- **12:5** "They are afraid also of what is high" translates *gam mi-gavoah yira'u*. "The almond tree blossoms" may be the white hair, and "the grasshopper drags itself along" translates *yistabbel ha-chagav*. "Desire fails" translates *tafer ha-aviyyonah*, in which *aviyyonah* is the caper berry, which was thought to rouse appetite. "His eternal home" translates *beit olamo*. "The mourners" translates *ha-sofedim*.
- **12:6** "The silver cord is snapped" and "the golden bowl is broken" are images of death. "The pitcher" translates *kad*, and "the wheel" translates *galgal*.
- **12:7** "The dust returns to the earth as it was" is as in Genesis 3:19. "The spirit returns to God who gave it" translates *ve-ha-ruach tashuv el-ha-Elohim asher netanah*. See entry H-RUACH. It is as in Genesis 2:7 and Psalm 104:29, and the question of 3:21 is not answered here.
- **12:8** The verse is the same as 1:2, and is the frame of the book.
- **12:9** "Taught the people knowledge" translates *limmad da'at et-ha-am*. "Weighing" translates *izzen*, a rare verb, "studying" translates *chiqqer*, and "arranging" translates *tiqqen*.
- **12:10** "Words of delight" translates *divrei-chefets*, and "words of truth" translates *divrei emet*. The verse is hard.
- **12:11** "Goads" translates *dorvonot*, and "nails" translates *masmerot*. "The collected sayings" translates *ba'alei asuppot*, "masters of collections", which is hard. "One Shepherd" translates *ro'eh echad*, and may refer to God or to a leader of the sages.
- **12:12** "My son" translates *beni*, as in Proverbs. "Beware" translates *hizzahar*. "Study" translates *lahag*, and "weariness of the flesh" translates *yegi'at basar*.
- **12:13** "The end of the matter" translates *sof davar*. "Fear God" translates *et-ha-Elohim yera*, in which the Hebrew has *Elohim* and is not the phrase "the fear of the LORD" of Proverbs. "This is the whole of a person" translates *ki-zeh kol-ha-adam*, which is hard and may be read "this is the whole duty of a person", "this is for every person" or "this is the whole of humankind".
- **12:14** "Every secret thing" translates *kol ne'lam*.
