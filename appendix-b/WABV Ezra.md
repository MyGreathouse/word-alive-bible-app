# WABV: Ezra

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-QADOSH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Parts of this book (4:8 to 6:18 and 7:12-26) are in Aramaic in the original, and this is noted in the footnotes.*

## Ezra 1-2: The Return from Exile

### Chapter 1

**1** In the first year of Cyrus king of Persia, to fulfil the word of the LORD spoken by Jeremiah, the LORD stirred up the spirit \[H-RUACH\] of Cyrus king of Persia, so that he sent a proclamation throughout his whole kingdom and put it in writing:

**2** "This is what Cyrus king of Persia says: The LORD, the God of heaven, has given me all the kingdoms of the earth, and he has appointed me to build him a house at Jerusalem in Judah.

**3** Whoever among you belongs to all his people, may his God be with him, and let him go up to Jerusalem in Judah and build the house of the LORD, the God of Israel. He is the God who is in Jerusalem.

**4** And let everyone who survives, wherever he lives as a foreigner, be helped by the people of his place with silver and gold, with goods and with animals, besides the freewill offerings for the house of God in Jerusalem."

**5** Then the heads of the fathers' houses of Judah and Benjamin, and the priests and the Levites, everyone whose spirit God had stirred, got ready to go up and build the house of the LORD in Jerusalem.

**6** All their neighbours supported them with silver vessels, gold, goods, animals and valuable things, besides all that was willingly given.

**7** King Cyrus also brought out the vessels of the house of the LORD that Nebuchadnezzar had carried off from Jerusalem and put in the house of his gods.

**8** Cyrus king of Persia had them brought out by Mithredath the treasurer, who counted them out to Sheshbazzar the leader of Judah.

**9** This was the count: thirty gold basins, a thousand silver basins, twenty-nine knives,

**10** thirty gold bowls, four hundred and ten silver bowls of a second kind, and a thousand other vessels.

**11** All the gold and silver vessels numbered five thousand four hundred. Sheshbazzar brought all these up when the exiles were brought up from Babylon to Jerusalem.

## Ezra 3-4: The Altar, the Foundation and the Opposition

### Chapter 3

**1** When the seventh month came and the Israelites were in their towns, the people gathered as one in Jerusalem.

**2** Then Jeshua son of Jozadak, with his fellow priests, and Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel with his relatives, got up and built the altar of the God of Israel, to offer burnt offerings on it, as it is written in the law of Moses the man of God.

**3** They set the altar on its base, for they were afraid of the peoples of the lands, and they offered burnt offerings on it to the LORD, burnt offerings morning and evening.

**4** They kept the Festival of Booths, as it is written, and offered the burnt offerings by number each day, as the rule required,

**5** and after that the regular burnt offerings, the offerings at the new moons and at all the appointed festivals of the LORD that are made holy \[H-QADOSH\], and the offerings of everyone who gave a freewill offering to the LORD.

**6** From the first day of the seventh month they began to offer burnt offerings to the LORD, but the foundation of the temple of the LORD had not yet been laid.

**7** They gave money to the stonecutters and the carpenters, and food, drink and oil to the people of Sidon and Tyre to bring cedar logs from Lebanon by sea to Joppa, as Cyrus king of Persia had authorised.

**8** In the second year after they came to the house of God in Jerusalem, in the second month, Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel and Jeshua son of Jozadak made a start, with the rest of their relatives, the priests and the Levites, and all who had come to Jerusalem from the captivity. They appointed Levites twenty years old and over to supervise the work on the house of the LORD.

**9** Jeshua with his sons and his relatives, and Kadmiel and his sons, the descendants of Judah, together took charge of the workers on the house of God, with the descendants of Henadad, and their sons and relatives the Levites.

**10** When the builders laid the foundation of the temple of the LORD, the priests in their robes stood with trumpets, and the Levites, the descendants of Asaph, with cymbals, to praise the LORD, as David king of Israel had ordered.

**11** They sang to each other in praise and thanks to the LORD: "For he is good, for his faithful love \[H-HESED\] to Israel lasts for ever." All the people gave a great shout in praise of the LORD, because the foundation of the house of the LORD had been laid.

**12** But many of the priests and Levites and heads of fathers' houses, old men who had seen the first house, wept loudly when they saw the foundation of this house laid, while many others shouted aloud for joy,

**13** so that the people could not tell the sound of the joyful shout from the sound of the weeping of the people. For the people shouted with a great shout, and the sound was heard far away.

### Chapter 4

**1** When the enemies of Judah and Benjamin heard that the returned exiles were building a temple for the LORD, the God of Israel,

**2** they came to Zerubbabel and to the heads of the fathers' houses and said to them, "Let us build with you, for like you we seek your God, and we have been sacrificing to him since the days of Esar-haddon king of Assyria, who brought us here."

**3** But Zerubbabel, Jeshua and the rest of the heads of the fathers' houses of Israel said to them, "You have no part with us in building a house for our God. We alone will build for the LORD, the God of Israel, as King Cyrus, king of Persia, has commanded us."

**4** Then the people of the land discouraged the people of Judah and frightened them from building,

**5** and hired advisers against them to frustrate their plan, all the days of Cyrus king of Persia and until the reign of Darius king of Persia.

**6** In the reign of Ahasuerus, at the beginning of his reign, they wrote an accusation against the people of Judah and Jerusalem.

**7** In the days of Artaxerxes, Bishlam, Mithredath, Tabeel and the rest of their associates wrote to Artaxerxes king of Persia. The letter was written in Aramaic script and put into Aramaic.

*(From here to 6:18 the original is in Aramaic.)*

**8** Rehum the commander and Shimshai the secretary wrote a letter against Jerusalem to King Artaxerxes as follows.

**9** Then Rehum the commander, Shimshai the secretary and the rest of their associates, the judges, the governors, the officials, the Persians, the men of Erech, the Babylonians, the men of Susa, that is the Elamites,

**10** and the rest of the nations whom the great and noble Osnappar deported and settled in the city of Samaria and the rest of the province Beyond the River, and so on.

**11** This is a copy of the letter that they sent: "To King Artaxerxes, from your servants, the men of the province Beyond the River, and so on.

**12** Let the king know that the Jews who came up from you to us have come to Jerusalem. They are rebuilding the rebellious and evil city. They are finishing the walls and repairing the foundations.

**13** Now let the king know that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are finished, they will not pay tribute, tax or toll, and in the end it will damage the royal revenue.

**14** Now since we are bound by the salt of the palace, and it is not right for us to see the king dishonoured, we are sending word to the king

**15** so that a search may be made in the record book of your ancestors. You will find in the record book and learn that this city is a rebellious city, harmful to kings and provinces, and that they have made revolt in it from long ago. That is why this city was destroyed.

**16** We make known to the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are finished, you will then have no share in the province Beyond the River."

**17** The king sent this reply: "To Rehum the commander and Shimshai the secretary and the rest of their associates who live in Samaria and in the rest of the province Beyond the River, greetings, and so on.

**18** The letter that you sent to us has been read out clearly before me.

**19** I gave an order, and a search was made, and it was found that this city has from long ago risen up against kings, and that rebellion and revolt have been made in it.

**20** Powerful kings have ruled in Jerusalem, who ruled over the whole province Beyond the River, and tribute, tax and toll were paid to them.

**21** Now give an order to make these men stop, and this city is not to be rebuilt until an order is given by me.

**22** Take care not to be careless in this matter. Why should the damage grow to the harm of the kings?"

**23** As soon as the copy of the letter of King Artaxerxes was read in front of Rehum and Shimshai the secretary and their associates, they went in haste to Jerusalem, to the Jews, and by force and power made them stop.

**24** Then the work on the house of God in Jerusalem stopped, and it stayed stopped until the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia.

*Review note: Ezra 3 and 4 was drafted in WABV wording. Chapter 3 is in Hebrew. Chapter 4:1-7 is in Hebrew, and from 4:8 to 6:18 the original is in Aramaic, which the footnotes mark. The order of chapter 4 is not the order of time: verses 6 to 23 tell of opposition under later kings (Ahasuerus and Artaxerxes), and verse 24 returns to the time of Darius, in the same way as 5:1. The account of the altar and the festival (3:1-6) and the foundation (3:7-13) is rendered as written. The wording of the Aramaic letters is to be checked by a scholar with particular care. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The section needs a check against the Hebrew and Aramaic by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Ezra 5-6: The Temple Finished

### Chapter 5

**1** Now the prophets \[H-NAVI\], Haggai the prophet and Zechariah son of Iddo, prophesied to the Jews in Judah and Jerusalem in the name of the God of Israel, who was over them.

**2** Then Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel and Jeshua son of Jozadak got up and began to rebuild the house of God in Jerusalem, and the prophets of God were with them, helping them.

**3** At that time Tattenai, governor of the province Beyond the River, and Shethar-bozenai and their associates came to them and said, "Who gave you an order to build this house and to finish this structure?"

**4** They also asked them, "What are the names of the men who are building this building?"

**5** But the eye of their God was on the elders of the Jews, and they were not stopped until a report could go to Darius and a written answer come back about it.

**6** This is a copy of the letter that Tattenai, governor of the province Beyond the River, and Shethar-bozenai and his associates, the officials of the province Beyond the River, sent to King Darius.

**7** They sent him a report, and in it was written: "To King Darius, all peace.

**8** Let the king know that we went to the province of Judah, to the house of the great God. It is being built with large stones, and timber is being laid in the walls. This work is being done with care and is succeeding in their hands.

**9** We asked those elders and said to them, 'Who gave you an order to build this house and to finish this structure?'

**10** We also asked them their names, to let you know, so that we could write down the names of the men at their head.

**11** This is the answer they gave us: 'We are the servants of the God of heaven and earth, and we are rebuilding the house that was built many years ago, which a great king of Israel built and finished.

**12** But because our ancestors made the God of heaven angry, he handed them over to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, the Chaldean, who destroyed this house and took the people away to Babylon.

**13** But in the first year of Cyrus king of Babylon, King Cyrus gave an order that this house of God should be rebuilt.

**14** And King Cyrus took out of the temple of Babylon the gold and silver vessels of the house of God that Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the temple in Jerusalem and brought to the temple in Babylon, and they were handed to a man named Sheshbazzar, whom he had appointed governor.

**15** He said to him: "Take these vessels, go and put them in the temple in Jerusalem, and let the house of God be rebuilt on its site."

**16** Then this Sheshbazzar came and laid the foundations of the house of God in Jerusalem, and from then until now it has been in the process of building, and it is not yet finished.'

**17** Now if it seems good to the king, let a search be made in the royal archives there in Babylon to see whether an order was given by King Cyrus to rebuild this house of God in Jerusalem. And let the king send us his decision about this matter."

### Chapter 6

**1** Then King Darius gave an order, and a search was made in the archives, where the documents were stored in Babylon.

**2** In Ecbatana, the fortress in the province of Media, a scroll was found, and this was written in it: "Record.

**3** In the first year of King Cyrus, King Cyrus gave an order: Concerning the house of God at Jerusalem, let the house be rebuilt as a place where sacrifices are offered, with its foundations kept. Its height is to be sixty cubits and its width sixty cubits,

**4** with three layers of large stones and one layer of timber. The cost is to be paid from the royal treasury.

**5** Also, the gold and silver vessels of the house of God, which Nebuchadnezzar took from the temple in Jerusalem and brought to Babylon, are to be returned and brought back to the temple in Jerusalem, each to its place. You are to put them in the house of God."

**6** "Now, Tattenai, governor of the province Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai and your associates, the officials of the province Beyond the River, keep away from there.

**7** Leave the work on this house of God alone. Let the governor of the Jews and the elders of the Jews rebuild this house of God on its site.

**8** I also give an order about what you are to do for these elders of the Jews for the rebuilding of this house of God. The full cost is to be paid to these men from the royal revenue, from the tribute of the province Beyond the River, without delay.

**9** Whatever is needed, young bulls, rams or lambs for burnt offerings to the God of heaven, wheat, salt, wine or oil, as the priests in Jerusalem say, is to be given to them day by day without fail,

**10** so that they may offer pleasing sacrifices to the God of heaven and pray for the life of the king and his sons.

**11** I also give an order that if anyone changes this decree, a beam is to be pulled out of his house and he is to be impaled on it, and his house is to be made a rubbish heap for this.

**12** May the God who has made his name live there overthrow any king or people who reach out a hand to change this or to destroy this house of God in Jerusalem. I, Darius, have given this order. Let it be carried out with care."

**13** Then Tattenai, governor of the province Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai and their associates carried it out with care, exactly as King Darius had ordered.

**14** The elders of the Jews built and prospered through the prophesying of the prophet Haggai and of Zechariah son of Iddo. They finished building by the order of the God of Israel and by the order of Cyrus and Darius and Artaxerxes king of Persia.

**15** This house was completed on the third day of the month of Adar, in the sixth year of the reign of King Darius.

**16** The Israelites, the priests and the Levites and the rest of the returned exiles, celebrated the dedication of this house of God with joy.

**17** For the dedication of this house of God they offered a hundred bulls, two hundred rams and four hundred lambs, and as a sin offering for all Israel twelve male goats, according to the number of the tribes of Israel.

**18** They set the priests in their divisions and the Levites in their groups for the service of God in Jerusalem, as it is written in the Book of Moses.

*(From here the original is in Hebrew again.)*

**19** The returned exiles kept the Passover on the fourteenth day of the first month.

**20** For the priests and the Levites had purified themselves together; all of them were clean. They killed the Passover lamb for all the returned exiles, for their fellow priests and for themselves.

**21** It was eaten by the Israelites who had returned from exile and by everyone who had separated himself from the uncleanness of the nations of the land and joined them to seek the LORD, the God of Israel.

**22** They kept the Festival of Unleavened Bread for seven days with joy, for the LORD had made them glad and had turned the heart of the king of Assyria towards them, to strengthen their hands in the work on the house of God, the God of Israel.

*Review note: Ezra 5 and 6 was drafted in WABV wording. Chapters 5:1 to 6:18 are in Aramaic in the original, and from 6:19 the Hebrew begins again, which the text and the footnotes mark. The chapters contain two official letters (Tattenai's report in 5:7-17 and Darius's decree in 6:3-12), and their wording is to be checked by a scholar with particular care. The curse on anyone who changes the decree (6:11) is rendered as written. The Persian king is called "the king of Assyria" in 6:22, and the footnote records the matter. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The section needs a check against the Aramaic and Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Ezra 7-8: Ezra's Mission

### Chapter 7

**1** After this, in the reign of Artaxerxes king of Persia, Ezra son of Seraiah, son of Azariah, son of Hilkiah,

**2** son of Shallum, son of Zadok, son of Ahitub,

**3** son of Amariah, son of Azariah, son of Meraioth,

**4** son of Zerahiah, son of Uzzi, son of Bukki,

**5** son of Abishua, son of Phinehas, son of Eleazar, son of Aaron the chief priest,

**6** this Ezra went up from Babylon. He was a scribe skilled in the law \[H-TORAH\] of Moses that the LORD, the God of Israel, had given. The king gave him everything he asked, for the hand of the LORD his God was on him.

**7** Some of the Israelites, and some of the priests, the Levites, the singers, the gatekeepers and the temple servants, also went up to Jerusalem in the seventh year of King Artaxerxes.

**8** Ezra came to Jerusalem in the fifth month, in the seventh year of the king.

**9** On the first day of the first month he had begun the journey up from Babylon, and on the first day of the fifth month he came to Jerusalem, for the good hand of his God was on him.

**10** For Ezra had set his heart to study the law of the LORD, and to do it, and to teach its statutes and rules in Israel.

**11** This is a copy of the letter that King Artaxerxes gave to Ezra the priest, the scribe, a scribe of the words of the commands of the LORD and his statutes for Israel:

*(From here to verse 26 the original is in Aramaic.)*

**12** "Artaxerxes, king of kings, to Ezra the priest, the scribe of the law of the God of heaven. Greetings, and so on.

**13** I have given an order that anyone in my kingdom from the people of Israel and their priests and Levites who is willing to go to Jerusalem may go with you.

**14** For you are sent by the king and his seven advisers to inquire about Judah and Jerusalem according to the law of your God, which is in your hand,

**15** and to bring the silver and gold that the king and his advisers have freely given to the God of Israel, whose dwelling is in Jerusalem,

**16** with all the silver and gold that you find in the whole province of Babylon, along with the freewill offerings of the people and the priests, given willingly for the house of their God in Jerusalem.

**17** With this money you are to be careful to buy bulls, rams and lambs, with their grain offerings and drink offerings, and offer them on the altar of the house of your God in Jerusalem.

**18** What seems good to you and your relatives to do with the rest of the silver and gold, you may do, according to the will of your God.

**19** The vessels that are given to you for the service of the house of your God, deliver in full before the God of Jerusalem.

**20** And whatever else is needed for the house of your God that you are called on to provide, you may provide from the royal treasury.

**21** I, King Artaxerxes, give an order to all the treasurers in the province Beyond the River: Whatever Ezra the priest, the scribe of the law of the God of heaven, asks of you is to be done with care,

**22** up to a hundred talents of silver, a hundred cors of wheat, a hundred baths of wine, a hundred baths of oil, and salt without limit.

**23** Whatever the God of heaven commands is to be done fully for the house of the God of heaven. Why should there be anger against the kingdom of the king and his sons?

**24** We also make known to you that it is not permitted to impose tribute, tax or toll on any of the priests, Levites, singers, gatekeepers, temple servants or other servants of this house of God.

**25** And you, Ezra, in keeping with the wisdom of your God that is in your hand, appoint magistrates and judges to judge all the people in the province Beyond the River who know the laws of your God, and you are to teach those who do not know them.

**26** Anyone who does not obey the law of your God and the law of the king is to have judgement carried out on him without delay, whether death or banishment or confiscation of property or imprisonment."

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **Title** Ezra and Nehemiah were one book in the Hebrew Bible. Ezra tells of the return from exile under Cyrus, the rebuilding of the temple, and Ezra's mission. It uses documents, lists and a first-person account, and two long parts (4:8 to 6:18 and 7:12-26) are in Aramaic in the original. The events are from about 538 to 458 BCE, but the book is not in strict order of time. The date of Ezra's own mission (the seventh year of Artaxerxes) is disputed: it may be 458 BCE or 398 BCE.
- **1:1** "The first year of Cyrus king of Persia" is 538 BCE, the year after his capture of Babylon. "The word of the LORD spoken by Jeremiah" refers to Jeremiah 25:11-12 and 29:10. "Stirred up the spirit" is Hebrew *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH. The verses 1 to 3 are also the end of 2 Chronicles 36:22-23.
- **1:2** "The God of heaven" is the usual title of the LORD in the books of the Persian period. Cyrus speaks of the LORD as the God who gave him his empire.
- **1:3** "He is the God who is in Jerusalem" is a note within the decree. "May his God be with him" is a blessing on those who go up.
- **1:4** "Everyone who survives" is Hebrew *kol-ha-nishar*, those who are left. "Wherever he lives as a foreigner" is Hebrew *ger*, and it means wherever he lives in exile. The help from neighbours is like the gifts of the Egyptians at the exodus (Exodus 3:21-22; 12:35-36).
- **1:5** "Everyone whose spirit God had stirred" says that the LORD moved the people as he had moved Cyrus (verse 1).
- **1:7** "The vessels of the house of the LORD that Nebuchadnezzar had carried off" refers to 2 Kings 24:13 and 25:13-15. See also Daniel 1:2 and 5:2.
- **1:8** "Mithredath" is a Persian name. "Sheshbazzar the leader of Judah" is called governor in 5:14. The Hebrew word for "leader" is *nasi*. Some think Sheshbazzar is Shenazzar, the son of Jehoiachin (1 Chronicles 3:18). His relation to Zerubbabel is not clear.
- **1:9** "Basins" and "knives" translate two Hebrew words (*agartelim* and *machalafim*) that are rare and uncertain. The first may mean "basins" or "baskets", and the second may mean "knives" or "duplicates".
- **1:10** "Silver bowls of a second kind" is Hebrew *kefore chesef mishneh*. The sense of "second kind" is uncertain.
- **1:11** "Five thousand four hundred" is the total. The items listed in verses 9 and 10 add up to two thousand four hundred and ninety-nine, so the figures do not match. In 1 Esdras 2:13-14 the total is five thousand four hundred and sixty-nine.
- **2:1** The list of those who returned (verses 1 to 70) is parallel to Nehemiah 7:6-73 and 1 Esdras 5:7-43. The names and numbers differ in many places. "The province" is Judah, a province of the Persian empire.
- **2:2** "Zerubbabel, Jeshua, Nehemiah, Seraiah, Reelaiah, Mordecai, Bilshan, Mispar, Bigvai, Rehum and Baanah" are eleven leaders. Nehemiah 7:7 has twelve, with Nahamani added, and some names differ (Azariah for Seraiah, Raamiah for Reelaiah, Mispereth for Mispar, Nehum for Rehum). This Nehemiah is not the governor Nehemiah who comes later.
- **2:3** The families and towns of verses 3 to 35 have several different numbers in Nehemiah 7. Examples are Arah (775 here, 652 in Nehemiah), Azgad (1,222 here, 2,322 there), Adonikam (666 and 667), Bigvai (2,056 and 2,067), Adin (454 and 655), and Hashum (223 and 328).
- **2:21** "Bethlehem" is the first of the towns named by their men. Verses 21 to 35 name towns of Judah and Benjamin near Jerusalem.
- **2:36** "The priests" are four families, with a total of four thousand two hundred and eighty-nine.
- **2:40** "The Levites ... seventy-four" is a small number. Few Levites returned, which is also noted in 8:15.
- **2:41** "The singers: the descendants of Asaph, a hundred and twenty-eight" is the Hebrew number. Nehemiah 7:44 has a hundred and forty-eight.
- **2:42** "The gatekeepers ... a hundred and thirty-nine" is the Hebrew number. Nehemiah 7:45 has a hundred and thirty-eight.
- **2:43** "The temple servants" is Hebrew *netinim*, "those given", who helped the Levites. Their names are mostly foreign.
- **2:55** "Solomon's servants" are a group of descendants of royal servants. Their names are listed in verses 55 to 57.
- **2:59** "Could not prove that their fathers' houses and their descent were of Israel" refers to those who came from places in Babylonia without family records.
- **2:62** "They were put out of the priesthood as unclean" uses the Hebrew verb *ga'al*, "to be defiled". They were excluded because they could not prove descent.
- **2:63** "The governor" is Hebrew *tirshata*, a Persian title. "The most holy food" is *qodesh haqqodashim*. See entry H-QADOSH. "Urim and Thummim" were the priestly lots (Exodus 28:30). No priest had them in this time.
- **2:64** "Forty-two thousand three hundred and sixty" is the total here and in Nehemiah 7:66. The numbers listed in verses 3 to 60 add up to about thirty thousand, so the totals do not match.
- **2:65** "Two hundred male and female singers" is the Hebrew number here. Nehemiah 7:67 has two hundred and forty-five.
- **2:66** "Seven hundred and thirty-six horses" and the other animals in verses 66 and 67 are not in some manuscripts of Nehemiah 7.
- **2:69** "Darics" is Hebrew *darkemonim*, a Persian gold coin. "Sixty-one thousand darics of gold, five thousand minas of silver and a hundred priests' robes" is the total of the gifts. Nehemiah 7:70-72 gives different amounts.
- **2:70** "All Israel in their towns" closes the list. It prepares for the gathering in the seventh month (3:1).
- **3:1** "The seventh month" is Tishri, the month of the festivals of the trumpets, the Day of Atonement and Booths (Leviticus 23:23-43). "As one" says that the people gathered in unity.
- **3:2** "Jeshua son of Jozadak" is the high priest (Haggai 1:1, where his father is Jehozadak; 1 Chronicles 6:15). "Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel" is the governor of Judah. In 1 Chronicles 3:19 he is the son of Pedaiah. They build the altar "as it is written in the law of Moses the man of God".
- **3:3** "On its base" translates a Hebrew word (*mekhonotav*) that may mean "its bases" or "its old foundations". "They were afraid of the peoples of the lands" gives the reason for building the altar first.
- **3:4** "The Festival of Booths" follows Leviticus 23:34-43 and Numbers 29:12-38. "By number" refers to the sacrifices for each day of the festival.
- **3:5** "The appointed festivals of the LORD that are made holy" is Hebrew *mo'adei YHWH ha-mequddashim*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **3:7** "Cedar logs from Lebanon by sea to Joppa" repeats the way Solomon brought timber (2 Chronicles 2:16). "As Cyrus king of Persia had authorised" refers to a grant that is not quoted.
- **3:8** "In the second year ... in the second month" is the year 536 BCE. The month matches the month in which Solomon began the first temple (1 Kings 6:1). "Levites twenty years old and over" follows 1 Chronicles 23:24 and Numbers 8:24.
- **3:9** "The descendants of Judah" is the Hebrew reading. Some think it is a form of Hodaviah (2:40), which the Greek has. The names "Kadmiel" and "Henadad" are Levite families.
- **3:10** "As David king of Israel had ordered" refers to the arrangement of the Levites' music (1 Chronicles 25).
- **3:11** "They sang to each other" may mean that they answered one another in song. "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. The refrain is the same as in 1 Chronicles 16:34 and 2 Chronicles 5:13.
- **3:12** "Old men who had seen the first house" refers to the temple destroyed in 587 BCE, about fifty years before. Their weeping is echoed in Haggai 2:3. The text does not say whether they wept for loss or from joy.
- **3:13** "The people could not tell the sound of the joyful shout from the sound of the weeping" ends the chapter. It is rendered as written.
- **4:1** "The enemies of Judah and Benjamin" are the people of the land around, who came to help. The verse begins a story of opposition that runs to 6:15.
- **4:2** "Like you we seek your God" is the Hebrew. The written text has "we do not" and the reading tradition has "to him", "we sacrifice to him". The WABV text follows the reading tradition and the footnote gives the other. "Esar-haddon king of Assyria" ruled from 681 to 669 BCE. The people were settled by the Assyrians (2 Kings 17:24-41).
- **4:3** "You have no part with us" is the answer of the leaders. It refuses help from those who were settled in the land after the fall of the north.
- **4:4** "The people of the land" is Hebrew *am ha-arets*. Here it means the non-Jewish or mixed population around.
- **4:5** "All the days of Cyrus ... and until the reign of Darius" covers the time from 538 to 520 BCE.
- **4:6** "Ahasuerus" is Xerxes I, who ruled from 486 to 465 BCE.
- **4:7** "Artaxerxes" is Artaxerxes I, who ruled from 465 to 424 BCE. "Written in Aramaic script and put into Aramaic" is hard. The Hebrew words *ketav* and *targum* may mean that the letter was written in Aramaic and then explained or translated.
- **4:8** The Aramaic begins here and goes to 6:18. "Rehum the commander" is Aramaic *be'el te'em*, the chief of decrees. "Shimshai the secretary" is *safra*, the scribe.
- **4:9** "The Persians, the men of Erech, the Babylonians, the men of Susa, that is the Elamites" lists peoples settled in Samaria. The Aramaic names are uncertain.
- **4:10** "Osnappar" is probably Ashurbanipal. "The province Beyond the River" is the Persian province west of the Euphrates. "And so on" is the Aramaic abbreviation for the rest of the greeting.
- **4:12** "The Jews who came up from you to us" refers to those who returned. "They are rebuilding the rebellious and evil city ... finishing the walls" is about the walls of Jerusalem, not the temple. The events of verses 6 to 23 belong to a time after the temple was built, probably in the time of Artaxerxes (see Nehemiah 1 to 6).
- **4:13** "Tribute, tax or toll" translates three Aramaic words (*mindah*, *belo*, *halakh*) that are taxes of the Persian empire.
- **4:14** "We are bound by the salt of the palace" is an idiom for being in the king's pay and under oath. "Dishonoured" is Aramaic *arvah*, a hard word.
- **4:15** "The record book of your ancestors" is the royal archive. The claim that Jerusalem was always "a rebellious city" is made by the enemies.
- **4:18** "Read out clearly" is Aramaic *mefarash*, which can also mean "translated". The same word is used in Nehemiah 8:8.
- **4:20** "Powerful kings have ruled in Jerusalem" refers to David and Solomon (2 Samuel 8; 1 Kings 4:21).
- **4:21** "This city is not to be rebuilt until an order is given by me" keeps the order open to change. See 6:1-12.
- **4:23** "By force and power" says that the Jews were stopped by armed men. The text does not say who they were.
- **4:24** "The work on the house of God in Jerusalem stopped" returns to the story of the temple, which was stopped until the second year of Darius (520 BCE). See Haggai 1:1 and Ezra 5:1.
- **5:1** "Haggai the prophet and Zechariah son of Iddo" prophesied in the second year of Darius, 520 BCE (Haggai 1:1; Zechariah 1:1). "Prophet" is Hebrew *navi*, here in its Aramaic form *nevi'a*. See entry H-NAVI. This chapter is in Aramaic.
- **5:2** "Got up and began to rebuild" is the resumption of the work after the stop described in 4:24. See Haggai 1:12-15.
- **5:3** "Tattenai, governor of the province Beyond the River" is known from a Babylonian text of 502 BCE. "Shethar-bozenai" is a Persian name.
- **5:5** "The eye of their God was on the elders of the Jews" is a figure of the LORD's care (see Psalm 33:18). The work is not stopped while the report goes to Darius.
- **5:6** The letter in verses 6 to 17 is the official report of Tattenai to Darius. It is in Aramaic, and the wording follows the style of Persian letters.
- **5:8** "The house of the great God" is Tattenai's way of speaking of the temple. "Large stones" is Aramaic *even gelal*, "stones of rolling", which may mean stones that were rolled into place.
- **5:11** "A great king of Israel" refers to Solomon, who built the first temple.
- **5:12** "Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, the Chaldean" repeats the account of the destruction (2 Kings 25). The answer of the elders ascribes the exile to the people's anger of the God of heaven.
- **5:13** "Cyrus king of Babylon" is a title Cyrus took after he conquered Babylon in 539 BCE.
- **5:14** "Governor" is Aramaic *pechah*. It is the title of Sheshbazzar. See 1:8.
- **5:16** "This Sheshbazzar came and laid the foundations" differs from 3:8-13, where Zerubbabel and Jeshua lay the foundation. Haggai 2:18 dates the foundation to the second year of Darius. The accounts are kept as they are.
- **5:17** "The royal archives there in Babylon" is where Tattenai asks Darius to search.
- **6:1** "Darius gave an order, and a search was made" begins the answer. The Aramaic gives the decree of Cyrus as a record found in the archives.
- **6:2** "Ecbatana" is the Aramaic *Achmetha*, the summer capital of the Persian kings in Media.
- **6:3** "Record" is Aramaic *dikhronah*, a memorandum. The decree of Cyrus here is an official note in Aramaic and differs in form from the proclamation in 1:1-4. "Sixty cubits ... sixty cubits" are the height and width, which differ from the measures of Solomon's temple (1 Kings 6:2).
- **6:4** "Three layers of large stones and one layer of timber" is a form of building like the one in 1 Kings 6:36 and 7:12. "The cost is to be paid from the royal treasury" is a grant that the Hebrew proclamation does not mention.
- **6:5** "The gold and silver vessels ... are to be returned" repeats 1:7-11 and 5:14-15.
- **6:6** "Keep away from there" is an order to Tattenai and his officials.
- **6:7** "The governor of the Jews" is Zerubbabel (see Haggai 1:1).
- **6:8** "From the tribute of the province Beyond the River" says that the cost would be met by local taxes.
- **6:10** "Pleasing sacrifices" is Aramaic *nichochin*. "To pray for the life of the king and his sons" is a request for prayer for the ruler, as in Jeremiah 29:7.
- **6:11** "He is to be impaled on it" translates the Aramaic *yezeqef*, "be lifted up", which may mean to be impaled or hanged. "A rubbish heap" is Aramaic *nevali*. The curse is rendered as written.
- **6:12** "The God who has made his name live there" echoes Deuteronomy 12:5, 11. The curse on any king or people who would destroy the house is a Persian way of ending a decree.
- **6:14** "By the order of the God of Israel and by the order of Cyrus and Darius and Artaxerxes" lists the three kings. Artaxerxes is added, though the temple was finished in the time of Darius. It refers to his later help (7:11-26).
- **6:15** "The third day of the month of Adar, in the sixth year of the reign of King Darius" is about 12 March 515 BCE. It is the only date in the Bible for the completion of the second temple.
- **6:16** "The dedication" is Hebrew *chanukkat*, "dedication".
- **6:17** "A hundred bulls, two hundred rams and four hundred lambs" is a small number compared with the sacrifices at the dedication of Solomon's temple (2 Chronicles 7:5). "Twelve male goats" represent the twelve tribes.
- **6:18** "The priests in their divisions and the Levites in their groups ... as it is written in the Book of Moses" refers to Numbers 3 and 8 and to the arrangements of David (1 Chronicles 23 and 24). The Aramaic ends here.
- **6:19** The Hebrew begins again. "The Passover on the fourteenth day of the first month" is the festival of spring 515 BCE.
- **6:20** "The priests and the Levites had purified themselves together" is in contrast to the lack in the time of Hezekiah (2 Chronicles 30:15).
- **6:21** "Everyone who had separated himself from the uncleanness of the nations of the land and joined them to seek the LORD" describes people of the land who joined the returned exiles. It is rendered as written, with no comment on who they were.
- **6:22** "The king of Assyria" is the Hebrew reading. It refers to the Persian king, probably as ruler of the old Assyrian lands. "Turned the heart" says that the LORD moved the king (see Proverbs 21:1).
- **7:1** "After this" leaves a gap of nearly sixty years after the dedication of 515 BCE (6:15). "Artaxerxes" is probably Artaxerxes I (the seventh year is 458 BCE). Some scholars think it is Artaxerxes II (the seventh year is 398 BCE), and the date is disputed. "Ezra son of Seraiah" means a descendant of Seraiah the chief priest killed in 587 BCE (2 Kings 25:18-21).
- **7:2** The line from Ezra to Aaron (verses 1 to 5) leaves out several generations that 1 Chronicles 6:3-15 includes (for example between Meraioth and Azariah). The genealogy is shortened.
- **7:6** "The law of Moses" is Hebrew *torat Mosheh*. See entry H-TORAH. "A scribe skilled in" means one who knows and teaches the law. "The hand of the LORD his God was on him" is the repeated phrase of Ezra's account (7:9, 28; 8:18, 22, 31).
- **7:8** "The fifth month" is Ab, in July and August. The journey took about four months (verse 9).
- **7:9** "On the first day of the first month he had begun the journey up" is the departure from Babylon. In 8:31 they leave the river Ahava on the twelfth day of the first month, after a halt of three days (8:15).
- **7:10** "Set his heart to study the law of the LORD, and to do it, and to teach" is the three tasks of the scribe. The Hebrew words are *lidrosh*, *la'asot* and *lelammed*.
- **7:11** "A scribe of the words of the commands of the LORD and his statutes for Israel" is the king's title for Ezra.
- **7:12** The Aramaic letter runs from verse 12 to 26. "King of kings" is the Persian royal title. "The law of the God of heaven" translates Aramaic *dat*, a Persian loan-word for law. It is not certain whether it means the Torah as a whole or a part of it. "And so on" is the Aramaic abbreviation of the greeting.
- **7:14** "The king and his seven advisers" refers to the seven highest Persian counsellors (Esther 1:14). "The law of your God, which is in your hand" means that Ezra carries the law with him.
- **7:15** "Whose dwelling is in Jerusalem" is the Persian way of describing the LORD's temple.
- **7:22** "Salt without limit" is Aramaic *melach di lo ketav*, "salt without writing", that is without a set amount.
- **7:23** "Why should there be anger against the kingdom of the king and his sons?" is a Persian concern that the gods should not be angry.
- **7:24** "Tribute, tax or toll" repeats the three taxes of 4:13. The exemption of the temple staff is a privilege.
- **7:25** "Appoint magistrates and judges" gives Ezra authority to set up courts for the Jews of the province. "Who know the laws of your God" means the Jewish community. The authority may be limited to the Jews.
- **7:26** "Death or banishment" translates Aramaic *lemot* and *leshorashu*, where the second word, "uprooting", is hard and may mean banishment. The penalties are rendered as written.
- **7:27** The Hebrew begins again, and Ezra speaks in the first person, as far as 9:15, apart from 10:1 onwards where he is spoken of in the third person. "To glorify the house of the LORD" is the purpose of the king's gift.
- **7:28** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. "I gathered leaders from Israel to go up with me" introduces the list in chapter 8.
- **8:1** The list of those who went up with Ezra (verses 1 to 14) names fifteen families, most of them the same as in chapter 2. It has about 1,500 men.
- **8:2** "Of the descendants of Phinehas, Gershom. Of the descendants of Ithamar, Daniel" are the two priestly families. "Of the descendants of David, Hattush" is the line of David (1 Chronicles 3:22). The Hebrew of verses 2 and 3 is hard, and 1 Esdras 8:29-30 arranges the names differently.
- **8:5** "Of the descendants of Zattu" supplies the family name, which is missing from the Hebrew and is taken from the Greek (1 Esdras 8:32) and from 2:8. The WABV text departs from the Hebrew text here and is flagged for the scholar.
- **8:10** "Of the descendants of Bani" is also supplied from the Greek (1 Esdras 8:36) and 2:10, where the Hebrew text has only "of the descendants of Shelomith son of Josiphiah". It is flagged for the scholar.
- **8:13** "The last ones" is Hebrew *acharonim*. It may mean the later members of the family, who came after the first return.
- **8:14** "Zabbud" follows the reading tradition. The written text has "Zaccur".
- **8:15** "The river that flows towards Ahava" is a river or canal in Babylonia, not otherwise known. "I found no descendants of Levi there" says that no Levites had joined the group. Few Levites had returned earlier (2:40).
- **8:17** "Casiphia" is a place that is not otherwise known. "Temple servants" is Hebrew *netinim*. See 2:43. The appeal is for "servants for the house of our God", Hebrew *meshartim*.
- **8:18** "Sherebiah" is a Levite of the family of Mahli. He is also named in Nehemiah 8:7 and 9:4.
- **8:20** "Two hundred and twenty of the temple servants, whom David and the officials had given to serve the Levites" is an addition to the party. All of them were listed by name.
- **8:21** "Proclaimed a fast" so that they "might humble ourselves" and ask for "a safe journey" (literally "a straight way").
- **8:22** "I was ashamed to ask the king for soldiers and horsemen" is Ezra's reason for trusting the LORD's protection. "The hand of our God is for good on all who seek him" repeats the theme of the book.
- **8:26** "Six hundred and fifty talents of silver" is about twenty-two tonnes. "A hundred talents of gold" is about three and a half tonnes. These are very large amounts.
- **8:27** "Darics" is Hebrew *adarkonim*, the Persian gold coin (see 2:69). "Fine polished bronze" translates a rare phrase of uncertain meaning.
- **8:28** "You are holy to the LORD" is Hebrew *qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH. The priests and the vessels are set apart.
- **8:31** "The twelfth day of the first month" is the day they leave the river Ahava. "He rescued us from the hand of enemies and from ambushes" is the answer to the fast (verse 23).
- **8:33** "Meremoth son of Uriah the priest" is also named in Nehemiah 3:4, 21.
- **8:35** "Twelve bulls for all Israel, ninety-six rams, seventy-seven lambs, and as a sin offering twelve male goats" are the numbers of the offering. The numbers twelve and ninety-six (eight times twelve) represent the tribes.
- **8:36** "The king's governors" is Hebrew *ahashdarpenim*, "satraps", a Persian title.
- **9:1** "When these things were finished" follows the arrival and the offerings of 8:35-36. "Have not kept themselves separate" is Hebrew *lo nivdelu*, the verb of separation. The list "the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Jebusites, the Ammonites, the Moabites, the Egyptians and the Amorites" follows the lists in Deuteronomy 7:1 and 23:3-8, joining the old peoples of Canaan with the neighbours (Ammon, Moab, Egypt).
- **9:2** "The holy seed" is Hebrew *zera ha-qodesh*. See entry H-QADOSH. The phrase is used of the people as set apart (see Isaiah 6:13 and Deuteronomy 7:6). "Unfaithfulness" is Hebrew *ma'al*, the same word that Chronicles uses for the cause of the exile (2 Chronicles 36:14). "The leaders and officials have been the first" says that those in authority had led the way.
- **9:3** "I tore my clothes and my cloak and pulled out some of the hair of my head and beard" are signs of deep grief. "Stunned" translates Hebrew *meshomem*, "appalled" or "desolate".
- **9:4** "Everyone who trembled at the words of the God of Israel" is a group who took the law seriously. They are mentioned again in 10:3. "The evening offering" is the afternoon grain offering of the temple.
- **9:5** The prayer of Ezra (verses 6 to 15) is a confession made in the first person plural. Ezra himself is not said to have married a foreign woman. "Spread out my hands" is the posture of prayer.
- **9:6** "Our wrongs have risen higher than our heads, and our guilt has reached to the heavens" is a figure of the size of the guilt (see Psalm 38:4).
- **9:8** "A remnant" is Hebrew *peletah*. "A firm hold" is Hebrew *yated*, a peg or tent peg. It is a figure of a place to stand.
- **9:9** "Faithful love" is Hebrew *hesed*. See entry H-HESED. "A wall in Judah and Jerusalem" is Hebrew *gader*, which can mean a wall or a fence. The city wall was not yet built (see Nehemiah 1 to 6), so the word may be a figure of protection.
- **9:11** "Which you gave through your servants the prophets" introduces a command that is not quoted exactly in any one book. It joins Deuteronomy 7:1-6, Leviticus 18:24-30 and other passages. "A land made unclean" is the language of Leviticus 18:24-28.
- **9:12** "Do not give your daughters to their sons or take their daughters for your sons" follows Deuteronomy 7:3. "Never seek their peace or their prosperity" follows Deuteronomy 23:6, which is about Ammon and Moab.
- **9:13** "You, our God, have held back from punishing us as our wrongs deserve" says that the exile was less than the guilt. See Psalm 103:10.
- **9:15** "You are righteous" is Hebrew *tsaddiq attah*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. "No one can stand before you because of this" ends the prayer without a request.
- **10:1** "A very large crowd ... men, women and children, gathered round him" shows that the people shared in the weeping. It is told in the third person from here, and the first-person account of Ezra ends at 9:15.
- **10:2** "Shecaniah son of Jehiel" is a member of the family of Elam. "We have been unfaithful to our God and have married foreign women" uses *ma'al* and *nashim nokhriyyot*. "Foreign" here means not of Israel. "There is hope for Israel" says that repentance is possible.
- **10:3** "A covenant" is Hebrew *berit*. See entry H-BERIT. "To send away all these women and their children" is Hebrew *lehotsi kol-nashim ve-hayyoladot mehem*, "to bring out all the wives and those born of them". The text does not say where they were sent. "My lord" may be Ezra, or it may be the LORD (the Hebrew *adonai*). "Let it be done according to the law" appeals to the law, which does not command the sending away of wives who were already married. See Deuteronomy 7:1-6 and 21:10-14.
- **10:5** "Made ... swear" repeats the oath of the leaders.
- **10:6** "Jehohanan son of Eliashib" is a priest, the son of the high priest Eliashib (Nehemiah 3:1; 12:10-11, 22-23). Ezra fasts there.
- **10:8** "Lose all his property" is Hebrew *yacharam kol-rekhusho*, "all his property would be devoted to destruction", from the root of *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. "Excluded from the community of the exiles" means he is separated from the assembly.
- **10:9** "The ninth month, on the twentieth day" is Kislev, in December. "The heavy rain" is the winter rain. It is the setting of the assembly.
- **10:10** "You have been unfaithful and have married foreign women, adding to the guilt of Israel" is Ezra's charge. "Foreign women" is the same as in 10:2.
- **10:11** "Separate yourselves from the peoples of the land and from the foreign wives" is Ezra's command. The assembly agrees (verse 12).
- **10:14** "Let our officials act for the whole assembly" proposes a committee instead of a mass hearing. "Until the fierce anger of our God ... is turned away" gives the purpose.
- **10:15** "Only Jonathan ... and Jahzeiah ... opposed this" translates a Hebrew phrase (*amdu al zot*) that can mean "opposed this" or "were appointed over this". The Greek (1 Esdras 9:14) takes it as the second. The WABV text follows "opposed" and flags it for the scholar. Only four named persons are said to have spoken against or for it.
- **10:16** "Ezra the priest chose men" begins the commission that examined each case. It took from the tenth month to the first month of the next year (verse 17), about three months.
- **10:18** The list of those found with foreign wives (verses 18 to 43) has about a hundred and eleven names, by families: priests (verses 18 to 22), Levites (verse 23), singers and gatekeepers (verse 24), and the families of Israel (verses 25 to 43). The first four are from the family of Jeshua the high priest.
- **10:19** "They gave their hands" is a pledge. "A guilt offering" is Hebrew *asham*. See entry H-ASHAM. The priests who had married offered a ram.
- **10:23** "Kelaiah (that is Kelita)" gives two names for one man.
- **10:25** "Malchijah" occurs twice in the Hebrew list of the family of Parosh. The Greek has "Hashabiah" in the second place. The WABV text follows the Hebrew.
- **10:44** "All these had married foreign women, and some of them had wives by whom they had children" is hard in the Hebrew (*ve-yesh mehem nashim vayyasimu banim*). The Greek (1 Esdras 9:36) has "they sent them away with their children". Many scholars think the Hebrew text has been damaged. The WABV text follows the Hebrew as it stands and flags it for the scholar. The book ends without telling what happened to the women and children. Nehemiah 13:23-31 tells of a later dispute about the same matter.
