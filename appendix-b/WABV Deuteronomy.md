## Deuteronomy 1-4: Moses Looks Back and Calls Israel to Obey

### Chapter 1

**1** These are the words that Moses spoke to all Israel on the far side of the Jordan, in the wilderness, in the Arabah opposite Suph, between Paran and Tophel, Laban, Hazeroth and Dizahab.

**2** It is an eleven-day journey from Horeb to Kadesh-barnea by way of the hill country of Seir.

**3** In the fortieth year, on the first day of the eleventh month, Moses told the Israelites everything the LORD had commanded him to tell them.

**4** This was after he had defeated Sihon king of the Amorites, who ruled in Heshbon, and Og king of Bashan, who ruled in Ashtaroth and Edrei.

**5** On the far side of the Jordan, in the land of Moab, Moses began to set out this teaching \[H-TORAH\]. He said:

**6** "At Horeb the LORD our God said to us, 'You have stayed long enough at this mountain.

**7** Break camp and go to the hill country of the Amorites and to all the places near it: the Arabah, the hills, the lowland, the Negev and the coast, the land of the Canaanites and Lebanon, as far as the great river, the Euphrates.

**8** Look, I have placed the land in front of you. Go in and take possession of the land that the LORD promised by oath to your ancestors, to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, to give to them and to their descendants after them.'

**9** At that time I told you, 'I cannot carry you by myself.

**10** The LORD your God has made you many, and today you are as numerous as the stars of the sky.

**11** May the LORD, the God of your ancestors, make you a thousand times as many as you are, and bless you as he promised.

**12** But how can I alone bear your burden, your load and your quarrels?

**13** Choose for each of your tribes men who are wise, discerning and respected, and I will make them your heads.'

**14** You answered me, 'What you propose is good.'

**15** So I took the heads of your tribes, wise and respected men, and appointed them as your leaders: commanders of thousands, of hundreds, of fifties and of tens, and officers for your tribes.

**16** At that time I instructed your judges: 'Listen to the disputes between your kinsmen, and judge fairly between one man and another, whether he is a fellow Israelite or a foreigner living with him.

**17** Do not show favouritism in judging. Hear the small and the great alike. Do not be afraid of anyone, because judgement belongs to God. Bring me any case too hard for you, and I will hear it.'

**18** At that time I also told you everything you were to do.

**19** We set out from Horeb and crossed all that vast and fearsome wilderness that you saw, on the road to the hill country of the Amorites, as the LORD our God had commanded us, and we reached Kadesh-barnea.

**20** I said to you, 'You have come to the hill country of the Amorites, which the LORD our God is giving us.

**21** Look, the LORD your God has placed the land in front of you. Go up and take possession of it, as the LORD, the God of your ancestors, told you. Do not be afraid or discouraged.'

**22** Then all of you came to me and said, 'Let us send men ahead of us to search the land for us and bring back word about the road we should take and the cities we will come to.'

**23** The idea seemed good to me, so I chose twelve of you, one man for each tribe.

**24** They set out and went up into the hills and came to the Valley of Eshcol and scouted it.

**25** They took some of the fruit of the land in their hands and brought it down to us. They reported, 'It is a good land that the LORD our God is giving us.'

**26** But you refused to go up. You rebelled against the command of the LORD your God.

**27** You murmured in your tents and said, 'The LORD hates us. He brought us out of the land of Egypt to hand us over to the Amorites and destroy us.

**28** Where can we go? Our brothers have made our hearts melt. They say, "The people are bigger and taller than we are. The cities are huge, with walls up to the sky. We even saw the sons of the Anakites there."'

**29** I said to you, 'Do not be terrified and do not be afraid of them.

**30** The LORD your God, who goes ahead of you, will fight for you himself, as he did for you in Egypt before your eyes,

**31** and in the wilderness, where you saw how the LORD your God carried you, as a father carries his son, all the way you travelled until you reached this place.'

**32** Yet in spite of this word, you did not trust the LORD your God,

**33** who went ahead of you on the road to find you a place to camp, in fire by night and in a cloud by day, to show you the way you were to go.

**34** The LORD heard what you said and was angry, and he swore an oath:

**35** 'Not one of these men, this wicked generation, will see the good land that I promised by oath to give to your ancestors,

**36** except Caleb son of Jephunneh. He will see it, and I will give him and his descendants the land he has walked on, because he has followed the LORD fully.'

**37** With me too the LORD was angry because of you. He said, 'You also will not go in there.

**38** Joshua son of Nun, who stands in your service, will go in. Encourage him, for he will lead Israel to inherit it.

**39** As for your little ones, whom you said would be taken as spoil, and your children, who do not yet know good from evil, they will go in. I will give the land to them, and they will possess it.

**40** But you, turn round and set out for the wilderness, on the road to the Red Sea.'

**41** Then you answered me, 'We have sinned against the LORD. We will go up and fight, as the LORD our God commanded us.' Each of you strapped on his weapons of war, thinking it would be easy to go up into the hills.

**42** The LORD said to me, 'Tell them: Do not go up and do not fight, for I am not in your midst, and you will be struck down by your enemies.'

**43** I told you, but you would not listen. You rebelled against the LORD's command and, in your arrogance, you went up into the hills.

**44** The Amorites who lived in those hills came out against you and chased you like a swarm of bees, and they beat you down in Seir as far as Hormah.

**45** You came back and wept before the LORD, but the LORD would not hear your voice or turn his ear to you.

**46** So you stayed in Kadesh for many days, as long as you stayed."

### Chapter 2

**1** "We turned and set out for the wilderness on the road to the Red Sea, as the LORD had told me, and for many days we went round the hill country of Seir.

**2** Then the LORD said to me:

**3** 'You have gone round this hill country long enough. Turn north.

**4** Give the people this command: You are about to pass through the territory of your kinsmen, the descendants of Esau, who live in Seir. They will be afraid of you, so take great care.

**5** Do not provoke them, for I will not give you any of their land, not even a foot's width, because I have given the hill country of Seir to Esau as his possession.

**6** You may buy food from them with silver, so that you can eat, and you may buy water from them with silver, so that you can drink.

**7** For the LORD your God has blessed you in everything you have done. He has watched over your journey through this great wilderness. For these forty years the LORD your God has been with you, and you have lacked nothing.'

**8** So we passed by our kinsmen, the descendants of Esau, who live in Seir, away from the road of the Arabah, away from Elath and Ezion-geber. We turned and went by the road of the wilderness of Moab.

**9** The LORD said to me, 'Do not harass Moab or provoke them to war, for I will not give you any of their land as a possession, because I have given Ar to the descendants of Lot as their possession.'

**10** (The Emites had lived there before, a people great and many and as tall as the Anakites.

**11** They too are counted as Rephaim, like the Anakites, though the Moabites call them Emites.

**12** The Horites also lived in Seir before, but the descendants of Esau drove them out and destroyed them and settled in their place, just as Israel did to the land of its possession that the LORD gave them.)

**13** 'Now get up and cross the valley of the Zered.' So we crossed the valley of the Zered.

**14** The time from our leaving Kadesh-barnea until we crossed the valley of the Zered was thirty-eight years, until the whole generation of fighting men had died out from the camp, as the LORD had sworn to them.

**15** The hand of the LORD was against them to remove them from the camp, until they had all died.

**16** When all the fighting men had died and were gone from among the people,

**17** the LORD said to me:

**18** 'Today you are to cross the border of Moab at Ar.

**19** When you come near the Ammonites, do not harass them or provoke them, for I will not give you any of the land of the Ammonites as a possession, because I have given it to the descendants of Lot as their possession.'

**20** (That also is counted as a land of the Rephaim. The Rephaim lived there before, though the Ammonites call them Zamzummites,

**21** a people great and many and as tall as the Anakites. But the LORD destroyed them before the Ammonites, who drove them out and settled in their place,

**22** just as he did for the descendants of Esau who live in Seir, when he destroyed the Horites before them, and they drove them out and settled in their place, as they are to this day.

**23** As for the Avvites, who lived in villages as far as Gaza, the Caphtorites, who came out of Caphtor, destroyed them and settled in their place.)

**24** 'Get up, set out and cross the valley of the Arnon. Look, I have given into your hand Sihon the Amorite, king of Heshbon, and his land. Begin to take possession of it, and engage him in battle.

**25** This day I will begin to put the dread and fear of you on the peoples under all the heavens. When they hear the report of you, they will tremble and be in anguish because of you.'

**26** From the wilderness of Kedemoth I sent messengers to Sihon king of Heshbon with words of peace:

**27** 'Let me pass through your land. I will keep to the road, and I will not turn aside to the right or to the left.

**28** You will sell me food for silver, so that I may eat, and you will give me water for silver, so that I may drink. Only let me pass through on foot,

**29** as the descendants of Esau who live in Seir and the Moabites who live in Ar did for me, until I cross the Jordan into the land that the LORD our God is giving us.'

**30** But Sihon king of Heshbon would not let us pass through, for the LORD your God made his spirit stubborn and his heart obstinate, in order to hand him over to you, as he is today.

**31** The LORD said to me, 'Look, I have begun to give Sihon and his land over to you. Begin to take possession of it, so that you may occupy his land.'

**32** Sihon came out against us, he and all his people, to battle at Jahaz.

**33** The LORD our God gave him over to us, and we struck him down, with his sons and all his people.

**34** At that time we captured all his towns, and we devoted to destruction \[H-CHEREM\] every town, men, women and little ones. We left no survivor.

**35** Only the livestock we took as plunder for ourselves, with the spoil of the towns we had captured.

**36** From Aroer, on the edge of the valley of the Arnon, and from the town in the valley, as far as Gilead, there was not a town too high for us. The LORD our God gave everything over to us.

**37** Only to the land of the Ammonites you did not go near, to all the banks of the Jabbok and the towns of the hill country, or to anything the LORD our God had forbidden."

### Chapter 3

**1** "Then we turned and went up the road to Bashan, and Og king of Bashan came out against us, he and all his people, for battle at Edrei.

**2** The LORD said to me, 'Do not be afraid of him, for I have handed him over to you, with all his people and his land. Do to him as you did to Sihon king of the Amorites, who ruled in Heshbon.'

**3** So the LORD our God also handed over to us Og king of Bashan and all his people. We struck him down until no survivor was left to him.

**4** At that time we captured all his towns. There was not one of the towns we did not take from them: sixty towns, the whole region of Argob, the kingdom of Og in Bashan.

**5** All these were towns fortified with high walls, gates and bars, besides a great many unwalled villages.

**6** We devoted them to destruction, as we had done to Sihon king of Heshbon, devoting every town, men, women and little ones.

**7** But all the livestock and the plunder from the towns we took as spoil for ourselves.

**8** So at that time we took from the two kings of the Amorites the land east of the Jordan, from the valley of the Arnon to Mount Hermon

**9** (the Sidonians call Hermon Sirion, and the Amorites call it Senir),

**10** all the towns of the plateau, all Gilead, and all Bashan as far as Salecah and Edrei, towns of the kingdom of Og in Bashan.

**11** (Only Og king of Bashan was left of the remnant of the Rephaim. His bed was an iron bed. Is it not in Rabbah of the Ammonites? It is nine cubits long and four cubits wide, by the standard cubit.)

**12** When we took possession of this land at that time, I gave the Reubenites and the Gadites the land from Aroer, on the edge of the valley of the Arnon, and half of the hill country of Gilead with its towns.

**13** The rest of Gilead, and all of Bashan, the kingdom of Og, I gave to the half-tribe of Manasseh. (The whole region of Argob, all that Bashan, used to be called a land of the Rephaim.

**14** Jair, a descendant of Manasseh, took the whole region of Argob as far as the border of the Geshurites and the Maacathites, and called the villages after his own name, Havvoth-jair, as they are still called today.)

**15** To Machir I gave Gilead.

**16** To the Reubenites and the Gadites I gave the land from Gilead down to the valley of the Arnon, with the middle of the valley as the border, as far as the Jabbok, the border of the Ammonites,

**17** and the Arabah, with the Jordan as its border, from Chinnereth down to the Sea of the Arabah, the Salt Sea, below the slopes of Pisgah on the east.

**18** At that time I commanded you: 'The LORD your God has given you this land to possess. All your fighting men are to cross over armed ahead of your kinsmen the Israelites.

**19** Only your wives, your little ones and your livestock (I know you have a great deal of livestock) are to stay in the towns I have given you,

**20** until the LORD gives rest to your kinsmen as he has to you, and they too possess the land that the LORD your God is giving them across the Jordan. Then each of you may return to the possession I have given you.'

**21** I also commanded Joshua at that time: 'Your own eyes have seen all that the LORD your God has done to these two kings. The LORD will do the same to all the kingdoms you are about to cross into.

**22** Do not be afraid of them, for the LORD your God is the one who fights for you.'

**23** At that time I pleaded with the LORD:

**24** 'Lord GOD, you have only begun to show your servant your greatness and your strong hand. What god in heaven or on earth can do deeds and mighty acts like yours?

**25** Please let me cross over and see the good land across the Jordan, that good hill country and Lebanon.'

**26** But the LORD was angry with me because of you and would not listen to me. The LORD said to me, 'Enough! Do not speak to me again about this matter.

**27** Go up to the top of Pisgah and lift up your eyes to the west, the north, the south and the east, and look at it with your own eyes, for you will not cross this Jordan.

**28** But commission Joshua, and encourage him and make him strong, for he will cross over at the head of this people and will bring them into possession of the land you will see.'

**29** So we stayed in the valley opposite Beth-peor.

### Chapter 4

**1** "Now, Israel, listen to the statutes and the rulings that I am teaching you, and do them, so that you may live and go in and take possession of the land that the LORD, the God of your ancestors, is giving you.

**2** You are not to add anything to the word that I am commanding you, and you are not to take anything away from it, so that you may keep the commands of the LORD your God that I am giving you.

**3** Your own eyes have seen what the LORD did at Baal-peor, for the LORD your God destroyed from among you every man who followed the Baal of Peor.

**4** But you who held fast to the LORD your God are all alive today.

**5** See, I have taught you statutes and rulings, as the LORD my God commanded me, for you to do in the land that you are entering to take possession of it.

**6** Keep them and do them, for this is your wisdom and your understanding in the eyes of the peoples. When they hear all these statutes, they will say, 'This great nation is surely a wise and understanding people.'

**7** For what great nation has a god so near to it as the LORD our God is to us whenever we call on him?

**8** And what great nation has statutes and rulings as just as all this teaching that I am setting before you today?

**9** Only be careful, and guard yourselves closely, so that you do not forget the things your eyes have seen, and so that they do not slip from your heart all the days of your life. Make them known to your children and to your children's children,

**10** how on the day you stood before the LORD your God at Horeb, the LORD said to me, 'Gather the people to me, so that I may let them hear my words, so that they may learn to fear \[H-YIRAH\] me all the days they live on the earth, and may teach their children.'

**11** You came near and stood at the foot of the mountain, while the mountain burned with fire up to the heart of the sky, with darkness, cloud and thick gloom.

**12** The LORD spoke to you out of the middle of the fire. You heard the sound of words, but you saw no form. There was only a voice.

**13** He declared to you his covenant \[H-BERIT\], which he commanded you to keep, the Ten Words, and he wrote them on two tablets of stone.

**14** And at that time the LORD commanded me to teach you statutes and rulings, for you to do in the land that you are crossing over to possess.

**15** So be very careful, for you saw no form on the day the LORD spoke to you at Horeb out of the middle of the fire.

**16** Take care that you do not act corruptly by making for yourselves a carved image in the shape of any figure: the likeness of a male or a female,

**17** the likeness of any animal on the earth, the likeness of any winged bird that flies in the sky,

**18** the likeness of anything that crawls on the ground, the likeness of any fish in the waters below the earth.

**19** And take care that you do not lift up your eyes to the sky and see the sun and the moon and the stars, all the host of heaven, and are drawn away to bow down to them and serve them, things that the LORD your God has given to all the peoples under all the heavens.

**20** But the LORD has taken you and brought you out of the iron furnace, out of Egypt, to be a people for his own possession, as you are today.

**21** The LORD was angry with me because of you, and he swore that I would not cross the Jordan and not enter the good land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance.

**22** For I am going to die in this land. I will not cross the Jordan. But you are going to cross over and take possession of that good land.

**23** Take care that you do not forget the covenant of the LORD your God that he made with you, and make for yourselves a carved image in the shape of anything the LORD your God has forbidden you.

**24** For the LORD your God is a consuming fire, a jealous \[H-QANNA\] God.

**25** When you have children and grandchildren and have lived long in the land, if you act corruptly and make a carved image in the shape of anything, and do what is evil in the eyes of the LORD your God, to make him angry,

**26** I call heaven and earth as witnesses against you today that you will soon perish from the land that you are crossing the Jordan to possess. You will not live long in it, but will be completely destroyed.

**27** The LORD will scatter you among the peoples, and only a few of you will be left among the nations where the LORD will drive you.

**28** There you will serve gods made by human hands, of wood and stone, which cannot see or hear or eat or smell.

**29** But from there you will seek the LORD your God, and you will find him, if you search for him with all your heart and with all your soul.

**30** In your distress, when all these things have come upon you, in the later days, you will return to the LORD your God and listen to his voice.

**31** For the LORD your God is a compassionate God. He will not abandon you or destroy you or forget the covenant with your ancestors that he swore to them.

**32** Ask now about the days of long ago, before your time, from the day God created humankind on the earth, and ask from one end of the sky to the other: has anything as great as this ever happened, or has anything like it ever been heard of?

**33** Has a people ever heard the voice of a god speaking out of the middle of the fire, as you have, and lived?

**34** Or has a god ever tried to come and take a nation for himself from the midst of another nation, by trials, by signs and wonders, by war, by a strong hand and an outstretched arm, and by great and terrifying acts, as the LORD your God did for you in Egypt before your eyes?

**35** You were shown these things so that you would know that the LORD is God. There is no other besides him.

**36** From heaven he let you hear his voice to instruct you. On earth he showed you his great fire, and you heard his words out of the middle of the fire.

**37** And because he loved your ancestors and chose their descendants after them, he brought you out of Egypt himself, with his own presence, by his great power,

**38** driving out before you nations greater and stronger than you, to bring you in and give you their land as an inheritance, as it is today.

**39** So know today, and take it to heart: the LORD is God in the heavens above and on the earth below. There is no other.

**40** Keep his statutes and his commands, which I am giving you today, so that it may go well with you and with your children after you, and so that you may live long in the land that the LORD your God is giving you for all time."

**41** Then Moses set apart three towns east of the Jordan,

**42** so that someone who killed another person without intending it, and had not been his enemy in the past, could flee there. Fleeing to one of these towns, he could save his life:

**43** Bezer in the wilderness on the plateau for the Reubenites, Ramoth in Gilead for the Gadites, and Golan in Bashan for the Manassites.

**44** This is the teaching that Moses set before the Israelites.

**45** These are the decrees, the statutes and the rulings that Moses spoke to the Israelites when they came out of Egypt,

**46** across the Jordan in the valley opposite Beth-peor, in the land of Sihon king of the Amorites, who ruled in Heshbon, whom Moses and the Israelites defeated when they came out of Egypt.

**47** They took possession of his land and the land of Og king of Bashan, the two kings of the Amorites who were across the Jordan to the east,

**48** from Aroer, on the edge of the valley of the Arnon, as far as Mount Sirion (that is, Hermon),

**49** together with all the Arabah on the east side of the Jordan down to the Sea of the Arabah, below the slopes of Pisgah.

## Deuteronomy 5-11: The Ten Words and the Great Command

### Chapter 5

**1** Moses summoned all Israel and said to them: "Hear, Israel, the statutes and rulings that I am speaking in your hearing today. Learn them, and take care to do them.

**2** The LORD our God made a covenant with us at Horeb.

**3** The LORD did not make this covenant with our ancestors, but with us, with all of us who are alive here today.

**4** The LORD spoke with you face to face at the mountain, out of the middle of the fire.

**5** At that time I stood between the LORD and you, to tell you the word of the LORD, because you were afraid of the fire and did not go up the mountain. He said:

**6** 'I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.

**7** You will have no other gods in my presence.

**8** You will not make for yourself a carved image, or any likeness of what is in the heavens above, or on the earth beneath, or in the waters under the earth.

**9** You will not bow down to them or serve them, for I, the LORD your God, am a jealous God, bringing the guilt of parents on their children, to the third and fourth generation of those who hate me,

**10** and showing faithful love \[H-HESED\] to the thousandth generation of those who love me and keep my commands.

**11** You will not use the name of the LORD your God for a worthless purpose, for the LORD will not leave unpunished anyone who uses his name in that way.

**12** Keep the Sabbath day \[H-SHABBAT\] and make it holy, as the LORD your God commanded you.

**13** For six days you are to labour and do all your work,

**14** but the seventh day is a Sabbath for the LORD your God. On it you will do no work, neither you, nor your son or your daughter, nor your male or female servant, nor your ox or your donkey or any of your livestock, nor the foreigner who lives within your gates, so that your male and female servants may rest as you do.

**15** Remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and that the LORD your God brought you out from there with a strong hand and an outstretched arm. That is why the LORD your God commanded you to keep the Sabbath day.

**16** Honour your father and your mother, as the LORD your God commanded you, so that your days may be long and it may go well with you in the land that the LORD your God is giving you.

**17** You will not murder.

**18** You will not commit adultery.

**19** You will not steal.

**20** You will not give false evidence against your neighbour.

**21** You will not covet your neighbour's wife. You will not desire your neighbour's house, his field, his male or female servant, his ox or donkey, or anything that belongs to your neighbour.'

**22** These words the LORD spoke to your whole assembly at the mountain, out of the middle of the fire, the cloud and the thick darkness, in a great voice, and he added nothing more. He wrote them on two tablets of stone and gave them to me.

**23** When you heard the voice out of the darkness, while the mountain was burning with fire, you came to me, all the heads of your tribes and your elders,

**24** and said, 'Look, the LORD our God has shown us his glory and his greatness, and we have heard his voice out of the middle of the fire. Today we have seen that God can speak with a human being, and the human being still lives.

**25** But now, why should we die? This great fire will consume us. If we hear the voice of the LORD our God any more, we will die.

**26** For what mortal has ever heard the voice of the living God speaking out of the middle of the fire, as we have, and lived?

**27** You go near, and hear all that the LORD our God says. Then tell us everything that the LORD our God tells you, and we will listen and do it.'

**28** The LORD heard what you said when you spoke to me, and the LORD said to me, 'I have heard what this people said to you. Everything they said was right.

**29** If only their hearts were always like this, so that they would fear me and keep all my commands, so that it would go well with them and with their children for ever!

**30** Go and tell them, "Return to your tents."

**31** But you, stay here beside me, and I will tell you the whole command, the statutes and the rulings that you are to teach them, so that they may do them in the land that I am giving them to possess.'

**32** So take care to do as the LORD your God has commanded you. You are not to turn aside to the right or to the left.

**33** You are to walk in the whole way that the LORD your God has commanded you, so that you may live, and that it may go well with you, and that you may live long in the land that you will possess."

### Chapter 6

**1** "This is the command, the statutes and the rulings that the LORD your God commanded me to teach you, so that you may do them in the land you are crossing into to possess,

**2** so that you may fear the LORD your God, you and your son and your grandson, all the days of your life, by keeping all his statutes and commands that I am giving you, and so that you may live long.

**3** Listen, Israel, and take care to do it, so that it may go well with you, and so that you may become very many, as the LORD, the God of your ancestors, has promised you, in a land flowing with milk and honey.

**4** Hear, Israel \[H-SHEMA\]: the LORD is our God, the LORD alone.

**5** You are to love \[H-AHAV\] the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your strength.

**6** These words that I am commanding you today are to be on your heart.

**7** You are to repeat them to your children, and talk about them when you sit in your house and when you walk on the road, when you lie down and when you get up.

**8** You are to tie them as a sign on your hand, and they are to be as bands between your eyes.

**9** You are to write them on the doorposts of your house and on your gates.

**10** When the LORD your God brings you into the land that he swore to your ancestors, to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob, to give you, a land with great and good cities that you did not build,

**11** with houses full of every good thing that you did not fill, with cisterns cut out of the rock that you did not cut, with vineyards and olive trees that you did not plant, and you eat and are full,

**12** take care that you do not forget the LORD, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.

**13** You are to fear the LORD your God. You are to serve him, and you are to swear by his name.

**14** You are not to follow other gods, any of the gods of the peoples around you,

**13** the red kite, the falcon, the kite of any kind,

**14** every kind of raven,

**15** the ostrich, the nighthawk, the seagull, every kind of hawk,

**16** the little owl, the long-eared owl, the white owl,

**17** the desert owl, the osprey, the cormorant,

**18** the stork, every kind of heron, the hoopoe and the bat.

**19** All winged insects are unclean for you. They are not to be eaten.

**20** You may eat any clean winged creature.

**21** You are not to eat anything that has died naturally. You may give it to the foreigner living in your towns, and he may eat it, or you may sell it to a foreigner. For you are a people holy to the LORD your God. You are not to boil a young goat in its mother's milk.

**22** Every year you are to set aside a tenth of all the produce of your seed that comes from the field.

**23** In the presence of the LORD your God, in the place he will choose to make his name live there, you are to eat the tithe of your grain, your new wine and your oil, and the firstborn of your herds and flocks, so that you may learn always to fear the LORD your God.

**24** If the journey is too long for you, and you cannot carry it, because the place the LORD your God will choose to put his name is too far from you, when the LORD your God has blessed you,

**25** then you may exchange it for silver, bind up the silver in your hand and go to the place the LORD your God will choose.

**26** There you may spend the silver on anything you want: oxen, sheep, wine or strong drink, anything you crave. You are to eat there in the presence of the LORD your God and rejoice, you and your household.

**27** Do not neglect the Levite who lives in your towns, for he has no portion or inheritance with you.

**28** At the end of every three years you are to bring out the whole tithe of your produce for that year and store it in your towns.

**29** Then the Levite, because he has no portion or inheritance with you, and the foreigner, the orphan and the widow who live in your towns, may come and eat and be satisfied, so that the LORD your God may bless you in all the work of your hands.

### Chapter 15

**1** "At the end of every seven years you are to grant a release.

**2** This is how the release is to be done: every creditor is to release what he has lent to his neighbour. He is not to press his neighbour, his fellow Israelite, for repayment, because a release for the LORD has been proclaimed.

**3** You may press a foreigner for it, but whatever your fellow Israelite owes you, your hand is to release.

**4** But there will be no poor among you, for the LORD will certainly bless you in the land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance to possess,

**5** if only you listen carefully to the voice of the LORD your God and take care to keep all this command that I am giving you today.

**6** For the LORD your God will bless you as he promised. You will lend to many nations but will not borrow, and you will rule over many nations but they will not rule over you.

**7** If there is a poor person among you, one of your fellow Israelites, in any of the towns of the land that the LORD your God is giving you, do not harden your heart or close your hand against your poor kinsman.

**8** Instead, open your hand to him and lend him enough to meet his need, whatever it may be.

**9** Take care that no worthless thought enters your mind, saying, 'The seventh year, the year of release, is near,' so that you look with ill will on your poor kinsman and give him nothing, and he cries out to the LORD against you, and it becomes sin for you.

**10** Give to him freely, and do not let your heart be grudging when you give, because for this the LORD your God will bless you in all your work and in everything you put your hand to.

**11** For there will always be poor people in the land. That is why I am commanding you: open your hand wide to your kinsman, to the needy and to the poor in your land.

**12** If your fellow Hebrew, a man or a woman, is sold to you, he is to serve you for six years, and in the seventh year you are to let him go free.

**13** When you let him go free, do not send him away empty-handed.

**14** Provide for him generously from your flock, your threshing floor and your winepress. Give to him as the LORD your God has blessed you.

**15** Remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and the LORD your God redeemed you. That is why I am commanding you this today.

**16** But if he says to you, 'I do not want to leave you,' because he loves you and your household, since he is well off with you,

**17** then take an awl and push it through his ear into the door, and he will be your servant for life. Do the same with your female servant.

**18** Do not think it hard to let him go free, because in six years he has given you the service of a hired worker at half the cost. And the LORD your God will bless you in everything you do.

**19** Every firstborn male born in your herd and flock you are to set apart for the LORD your God. You are not to work the firstborn of your herd or shear the firstborn of your flock.

**20** You and your household are to eat it every year before the LORD your God, in the place the LORD will choose.

**21** But if it has a defect, if it is lame or blind or has any serious defect, you are not to sacrifice it to the LORD your God.

**22** You may eat it within your towns. The unclean and the clean alike may eat it, as with the gazelle and the deer.

**23** Only you are not to eat its blood. Pour it out on the ground like water.

### Chapter 16

**1** "Keep the month of Abib and hold the Passover for the LORD your God, because in the month of Abib the LORD your God brought you out of Egypt by night.

**2** You are to sacrifice the Passover to the LORD your God, from the flock or the herd, in the place the LORD will choose to make his name live there.

**3** Do not eat anything leavened with it. For seven days you are to eat unleavened bread with it, the bread of affliction, because you came out of the land of Egypt in a hurry, so that all the days of your life you may remember the day you came out of Egypt.

**4** For seven days no yeast is to be seen with you anywhere in your territory, and none of the meat that you sacrifice on the evening of the first day is to be left until morning.

**5** You are not to sacrifice the Passover in any of the towns that the LORD your God is giving you,

**6** but only in the place the LORD your God will choose to make his name live. There you are to sacrifice the Passover in the evening, at sunset, at the time of day you came out of Egypt.

**7** You are to cook it and eat it in the place the LORD your God will choose. In the morning you may turn back and go to your tents.

**8** For six days you are to eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there is to be a closing assembly for the LORD your God. You are not to do any work.

**9** You are to count seven weeks. Begin to count the seven weeks from the time you first put the sickle to the standing grain.

**10** Then you are to keep the Festival of Weeks for the LORD your God, with a freewill offering from your hand, which you are to give according to the way the LORD your God has blessed you.

**11** You are to rejoice before the LORD your God, you and your son and daughter, your male and female servants, the Levite who lives in your towns, and the foreigner, the orphan and the widow among you, in the place the LORD your God will choose to make his name live.

**12** Remember that you were a slave in Egypt, and take care to keep these statutes.

**13** You are to keep the Festival of Booths for seven days, after you have gathered in the produce from your threshing floor and your winepress.

**14** You are to rejoice in your festival, you and your son and daughter, your male and female servants, the Levite, and the foreigner, the orphan and the widow who live in your towns.

**15** For seven days you are to keep the festival for the LORD your God in the place the LORD will choose, because the LORD your God will bless you in all your produce and in all the work of your hands, and you will be completely joyful.

**16** Three times a year all your men are to appear before the LORD your God in the place he will choose: at the Festival of Unleavened Bread, the Festival of Weeks and the Festival of Booths. They are not to appear before the LORD empty-handed.

**17** Each is to give as he is able, according to the blessing the LORD your God has given you.

**18** You are to appoint judges and officers for yourselves in all your towns that the LORD your God is giving you, tribe by tribe, and they are to judge the people with right judgement.

**19** You are not to twist justice. You are not to show favouritism, and you are not to take a bribe, for a bribe blinds the eyes of the wise and distorts the words of the righteous.

**20** Justice, only justice, you are to pursue, so that you may live and possess the land that the LORD your God is giving you.

**21** You are not to plant any wooden pole of Asherah beside the altar that you build for the LORD your God.

**22** And you are not to set up a sacred pillar, which the LORD your God hates."

## Deuteronomy 17-20: Courts, King, Priests, Prophets and War

### Chapter 17

**1** "You are not to sacrifice to the LORD your God an ox or a sheep that has a defect, any serious flaw, for that is abhorrent to the LORD your God.

**2** If a man or a woman is found among you, in any of the towns that the LORD your God is giving you, who does what is evil in the eyes of the LORD your God and breaks his covenant,

**3** and goes and serves other gods and bows down to them, to the sun or the moon or any of the host of heaven, which I have forbidden,

**4** and it is reported to you and you hear of it, you are to make a thorough enquiry. If it is true and certain that this abhorrent thing has been done in Israel,

**5** you are to bring out to your gates that man or that woman who has done this evil thing, and stone that man or woman to death.

**6** A person is to be put to death on the evidence of two witnesses or three. He is not to be put to death on the evidence of one witness alone.

**7** The hands of the witnesses are to be the first against him to put him to death, and afterwards the hands of all the people. So you are to clear the evil out from among you.

**8** If a case is too hard for you to decide, whether between one kind of bloodshed and another, one kind of legal claim and another, or one kind of assault and another, any matter of dispute in your towns, you are to get up and go to the place the LORD your God will choose.

**9** Go to the Levitical priests and to the judge who is in office at that time. Ask them, and they will tell you the verdict.

**10** You are to do what they tell you from that place that the LORD will choose. Take care to do everything they instruct you.

**11** You are to act according to the instruction they give you and the verdict they pronounce for you. Do not turn aside from what they tell you, to the right or to the left.

**12** The man who acts with arrogance and refuses to listen to the priest who stands there to serve the LORD your God, or to the judge, that man is to die. So you are to clear the evil out of Israel.

**13** All the people will hear and be afraid, and will not act with arrogance again.

**14** When you come into the land that the LORD your God is giving you, and take possession of it and live in it, and then say, 'I want to set a king over me, like all the nations around me,'

**15** you may set over you a king whom the LORD your God chooses. You are to set over you a king from among your own kinsmen. You are not to put a foreigner over you, one who is not your kinsman.

**16** Only he is not to build up many horses for himself or send the people back to Egypt to build up many horses, since the LORD has told you, 'You are never to go back that way again.'

**17** He is not to build up many wives for himself, or his heart will turn away. He is not to build up large amounts of silver and gold for himself.

**18** When he sits on the throne of his kingdom, he is to write for himself on a scroll a copy of this teaching, taken from the one that is in the care of the Levitical priests.

**19** It is to stay with him, and he is to read it all the days of his life, so that he may learn to fear the LORD his God, and take care to keep all the words of this teaching and these statutes, and do them,

**20** so that his heart is not raised up above his kinsmen, and so that he does not turn aside from the command to the right or to the left, and so that he and his sons may reign long over his kingdom in Israel.

### Chapter 18

**1** "The Levitical priests, the whole tribe of Levi, are to have no portion or inheritance with Israel. They are to eat the LORD's food offerings as their inheritance.

**2** They are to have no inheritance among their kinsmen. The LORD is their inheritance, as he promised them.

**3** This is to be the priests' due from the people, from those who offer a sacrifice, whether an ox or a sheep: they are to give the priest the shoulder, the two cheeks and the stomach.

**4** You are to give him the first of your grain, your new wine and your oil, and the first fleece of your sheep.

**5** For the LORD your God has chosen him and his sons out of all your tribes to stand and serve in the name of the LORD for ever.

**6** If a Levite comes from any of your towns, from any place in Israel where he is living, and comes with all the longing of his heart to the place the LORD will choose,

**7** he may serve in the name of the LORD his God, like all his fellow Levites who stand there before the LORD.

**8** They are to have equal shares to eat, apart from what he receives from the sale of his family property.

**9** When you come into the land that the LORD your God is giving you, you are not to learn to do the abhorrent practices of those nations.

**10** No one is to be found among you who makes his son or daughter pass through the fire, or who practises divination, or tells fortunes, or reads omens, or is a sorcerer,

**11** or casts spells, or consults a medium or a spirit-consulter, or enquires of the dead.

**12** Anyone who does these things is abhorrent to the LORD, and because of these abhorrent practices the LORD your God is driving them out before you.

**13** You are to be wholly devoted to the LORD your God.

**14** For these nations that you are about to dispossess listen to fortune-tellers and diviners. But as for you, the LORD your God has not allowed you to do this.

**15** The LORD your God will raise up for you a prophet \[H-NAVI\] like me from among you, from your own kinsmen. You are to listen to him,

**16** just as you asked of the LORD your God at Horeb on the day of the assembly, when you said, 'Do not let me hear the voice of the LORD my God again or see this great fire any more, or I will die.'

**17** The LORD said to me, 'They are right in what they have said.

**18** I will raise up for them a prophet like you from among their kinsmen. I will put my words in his mouth, and he will tell them everything I command him.

**19** If anyone does not listen to my words that he speaks in my name, I myself will call that person to account.

**20** But the prophet who presumes to speak in my name a word that I have not commanded him to speak, or who speaks in the name of other gods, that prophet is to die.'

**21** You may say in your heart, 'How can we know a word that the LORD has not spoken?'

**22** When a prophet speaks in the name of the LORD and the word does not happen or come true, that is a word the LORD has not spoken. The prophet has spoken it with arrogance. Do not be afraid of him.

### Chapter 19

**1** "When the LORD your God cuts off the nations whose land the LORD your God is giving you, and you dispossess them and live in their cities and houses,

**2** you are to set apart three towns for yourselves in the land that the LORD your God is giving you to possess.

**3** You are to prepare the roads and divide into three parts the territory of your land that the LORD your God is giving you as a possession, so that anyone who has killed a person may flee there.

**4** This is the rule for the one who kills and may flee there and live: he is someone who kills his neighbour unintentionally, without having hated him in the past.

**5** For example, a man goes into the forest with his neighbour to cut wood, and his hand swings the axe to cut down a tree, and the iron head slips from the handle and strikes his neighbour so that he dies. That man may flee to one of these towns and live,

**6** so that the avenger of blood, in the heat of his anger, does not chase the killer and overtake him, because the way is long, and strike him dead, though he does not deserve to die, since he had not hated him in the past.

**7** That is why I am commanding you: set apart three towns for yourselves.

**8** If the LORD your God enlarges your territory, as he swore to your ancestors, and gives you all the land he promised to give to your ancestors,

**9** if you take care to keep all this command that I am giving you today, by loving the LORD your God and walking in his ways always, then you are to add three more towns to these three,

**10** so that innocent blood is not shed in your land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance, and so that the guilt of bloodshed does not rest on you.

**11** But if someone hates his neighbour and lies in wait for him, attacks him and strikes him dead, and then flees to one of these towns,

**12** the elders of his town are to send and take him from there and hand him over to the avenger of blood, so that he may die.

**13** Do not let your eye pity him. You are to clear the guilt of innocent blood out of Israel, so that it may go well with you.

**14** You are not to move your neighbour's boundary stone, which former generations have set, in the inheritance you will receive in the land that the LORD your God is giving you to possess.

**15** A single witness is not enough to convict anyone of any crime or any wrong in respect of any offence. A matter is to be established on the evidence of two witnesses or three.

**16** If a malicious witness stands up against someone to accuse him of wrongdoing,

**17** both parties to the dispute are to stand before the LORD, before the priests and the judges who are in office at that time.

**18** The judges are to investigate thoroughly, and if the witness is a false witness who has made a false accusation against his kinsman,

**19** you are to do to him what he meant to do to his kinsman. So you are to clear the evil out from among you.

**20** The rest will hear and be afraid, and never again do such an evil thing among you.

**21** Do not let your eye pity. It is to be life for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot.

### Chapter 20

**1** "When you go out to war against your enemies and see horses and chariots and an army larger than yours, do not be afraid of them, for the LORD your God, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt, is with you.

**2** When you are about to go into battle, the priest is to come forward and speak to the people.

**3** He is to say to them: 'Hear, Israel. Today you are going into battle against your enemies. Do not let your heart grow faint. Do not be afraid or panic or tremble before them,

**4** for the LORD your God is the one who goes with you, to fight for you against your enemies and to save you.'

**5** Then the officers are to say to the people: 'Is there anyone who has built a new house and has not dedicated it? Let him go home, or he may die in the battle and someone else dedicate it.

**6** Is there anyone who has planted a vineyard and has not yet enjoyed it? Let him go home, or he may die in the battle and someone else enjoy it.

**7** Is there anyone who is engaged to a woman and has not yet married her? Let him go home, or he may die in the battle and someone else marry her.'

**8** The officers are to go on and say to the people: 'Is there anyone who is afraid and fainthearted? Let him go home, so that he does not make the hearts of his fellows melt as his own has.'

**9** When the officers have finished speaking to the people, they are to appoint commanders at the head of the people.

**10** When you approach a town to attack it, make an offer of peace to it.

**11** If it accepts your offer of peace and opens its gates to you, all the people in it are to serve you in forced labour.

**12** But if it will not make peace with you and makes war against you, you are to besiege it.

**13** When the LORD your God hands it over to you, you are to put all its males to the sword.

**14** But the women, the little ones, the livestock and everything else in the town, all its plunder, you may take as spoil for yourselves. You may enjoy the plunder from your enemies that the LORD your God has given you.

**15** This is how you are to treat all the towns that are very far from you, those that are not towns of the nations nearby.

**16** But in the towns of these peoples that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance, you are not to leave alive anything that breathes.

**17** You are to devote them to destruction \[H-CHEREM\]: the Hittites, the Amorites, the Canaanites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites, as the LORD your God has commanded you,

**18** so that they do not teach you to do all the abhorrent practices that they do for their gods, and you sin against the LORD your God.

**19** When you besiege a town for a long time, fighting against it to capture it, you are not to destroy its trees by swinging an axe against them. You may eat from them, but you are not to cut them down. Are the trees of the field human beings, that you should besiege them?

**20** Only the trees that you know are not fruit trees may you destroy and cut down, to build siege works against the town that is making war with you, until it falls."

## Deuteronomy 21-26: Family, Community and Thanksgiving

### Chapter 21

**1** "If someone is found killed in the land that the LORD your God is giving you to possess, lying in the open country, and it is not known who struck him down,

**2** your elders and your judges are to go out and measure the distances to the towns around the body.

**3** The town nearest the body is to take a heifer that has never been worked and has never pulled in a yoke.

**4** The elders of that town are to bring the heifer down to a valley with a flowing stream, one that has not been ploughed or sown, and break the heifer's neck there in the valley.

**5** Then the priests, the sons of Levi, are to come forward, for the LORD your God has chosen them to serve him and to bless in the name of the LORD, and every dispute and every case of assault is to be settled by their word.

**6** All the elders of the town nearest the body are to wash their hands over the heifer whose neck was broken in the valley,

**7** and declare: 'Our hands did not shed this blood, and our eyes did not see it done.

**8** LORD, forgive your people Israel, whom you redeemed. Do not let the guilt of innocent blood remain among your people Israel.' Then the bloodshed will be forgiven them.

**9** So you are to clear the guilt of innocent blood from among you, when you do what is right in the eyes of the LORD.

**10** When you go out to war against your enemies, and the LORD your God hands them over to you and you take captives,

**11** and you see among the captives a beautiful woman, and you desire her and want to take her as your wife,

**12** you are to bring her into your house. She is to shave her head and trim her nails,

**13** and take off the clothes she was wearing when she was captured. She is to stay in your house and mourn for her father and mother for a full month. After that you may go to her and be her husband, and she will be your wife.

**14** But if you are not pleased with her, you are to let her go wherever she wishes. You are not to sell her for money or treat her as a slave, since you have humiliated her.

**15** If a man has two wives, one loved and the other unloved, and both the loved and the unloved bear him sons, and the firstborn son belongs to the unloved wife,

**16** then on the day he divides his property among his sons, he is not allowed to treat the son of the loved wife as the firstborn in place of the son of the unloved wife, who is the firstborn.

**17** He is to acknowledge the firstborn, the son of the unloved wife, by giving him a double share of all he has, for he is the first sign of his strength. The right of the firstborn is his.

**18** If a man has a stubborn and rebellious son, who will not listen to the voice of his father or the voice of his mother, and does not listen to them even when they discipline him,

**19** his father and mother are to take hold of him and bring him out to the elders of his town, to the gate of the place where he lives,

**20** and say to the elders of his town, 'This son of ours is stubborn and rebellious. He will not listen to us. He is a glutton and a drunkard.'

**21** Then all the men of his town are to stone him to death. So you are to clear the evil out from among you, and all Israel will hear and be afraid.

**22** If a man has committed a sin that carries the death penalty and is put to death, and you hang him on a tree,

**23** his body is not to stay on the tree overnight. You must bury him that same day, for anyone hung on a tree is under God's curse. You are not to defile the land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance.

### Chapter 22

**1** "You are not to see your kinsman's ox or sheep straying and ignore it. You must take it back to your kinsman.

**2** If your kinsman does not live near you, or you do not know who he is, take it to your own house and keep it until he comes looking for it. Then give it back to him.

**3** Do the same with his donkey, his cloak, or anything else your kinsman has lost and you find. You are not to ignore it.

**4** You are not to see your kinsman's donkey or ox fallen on the road and ignore it. You must help him lift it up again.

**5** A woman is not to wear a man's clothing, and a man is not to put on a woman's garment, for anyone who does this is abhorrent to the LORD your God.

**6** If you come across a bird's nest beside the road, in any tree or on the ground, with young birds or eggs, and the mother sitting on the young or on the eggs, you are not to take the mother together with the young.

**7** You may take the young, but you must let the mother go, so that it may go well with you and you may live long.

**8** When you build a new house, make a parapet around your roof, so that you do not bring the guilt of bloodshed on your house if someone falls from it.

**9** You are not to plant your vineyard with two kinds of seed, or the whole yield will be forfeit, the seed you sowed and the produce of the vineyard.

**10** You are not to plough with an ox and a donkey yoked together.

**11** You are not to wear cloth made of wool and linen woven together.

**12** You are to make tassels on the four corners of the cloak you wear.

**13** If a man marries a woman, and after sleeping with her turns against her,

**14** and charges her with shameful conduct and gives her a bad name, saying, 'I married this woman, but when I came near her I did not find the proofs of her virginity,'

**15** then the young woman's father and mother are to take the proofs of her virginity and bring them to the elders of the town, at the gate.

**16** The young woman's father is to say to the elders, 'I gave my daughter to this man as his wife, and he has turned against her.

**17** Now he has charged her with shameful conduct, saying, "I did not find the proofs of virginity in your daughter." But these are the proofs of my daughter's virginity.' Then they are to spread the cloth before the elders of the town.

**18** The elders of that town are to take the man and punish him.

**19** They are to fine him a hundred shekels of silver and give them to the young woman's father, because he has given an Israelite virgin a bad name. She is to remain his wife. He is not allowed to divorce her as long as he lives.

**20** But if the charge is true, and the proofs of the young woman's virginity were not found,

**21** then they are to bring the young woman to the door of her father's house, and the men of her town are to stone her to death. She has done a disgraceful thing in Israel, by acting as a prostitute in her father's house. So you are to clear the evil out from among you.

**22** If a man is found lying with a woman who is married to another man, both of them are to die, the man who lay with the woman and the woman. So you are to clear the evil out of Israel.

**23** If there is a young woman who is a virgin, engaged to a man, and another man finds her in the town and lies with her,

**24** you are to bring both of them out to the gate of that town and stone them to death, the young woman because she did not cry out in the town, and the man because he violated his neighbour's wife. So you are to clear the evil out from among you.

**25** But if the man finds the engaged young woman in the open country, and the man seizes her and lies with her, only the man who lay with her is to die.

**26** You are to do nothing to the young woman. She has committed no sin that deserves death. This case is like that of someone who attacks his neighbour and murders him,

**27** for he found her in the open country, and the engaged young woman cried out, but there was no one to save her.

**28** If a man finds a young woman who is a virgin, who is not engaged, and seizes her and lies with her, and they are found,

**29** the man who lay with her is to give fifty shekels of silver to the young woman's father, and she is to become his wife, because he has violated her. He is not allowed to divorce her as long as he lives.

**30** A man is not to take his father's wife, so that he does not uncover his father's skirt.

### Chapter 23

**1** "No man whose testicles are crushed or whose penis is cut off may enter the assembly of the LORD.

**2** No one born of a forbidden union may enter the assembly of the LORD. None of his descendants, even to the tenth generation, may enter the assembly of the LORD.

*Review note: Deuteronomy 21 to 26 was drafted in WABV wording. This section contains many laws about family, marriage and the community, some of which are very difficult and sensitive: the captive woman (21:10-14), the rebellious son (21:18-21), the proofs of virginity and the penalties for sexual offences (22:13-29), divorce (24:1-4), levirate marriage (25:5-10) and the cutting off of a hand (25:11-12). They are rendered as written, and the footnotes explain the language without comment. The firstfruits declaration (26:5-10) and the closing verses of chapter 26 are very familiar. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **1:1** The English name "Deuteronomy" comes from the Greek for "second law". The Hebrew name is *Devarim*, "words", from the opening phrase. "Across the Jordan" means the east side, the plains of Moab. Several of the places named are now unknown.
- **1:3** The date is in the fortieth year, close to the end of the wandering. The book is a series of speeches by Moses, ending with his death in chapter 34.
- **1:5** "Teaching" is Hebrew *torah*. See entry H-TORAH. "Set out" means to make plain or explain.
- **1:7** "The great river" is the Euphrates, the northern limit of the promised land.
- **1:9** The appointment of leaders recalls Exodus 18 and Numbers 11, but here Moses is the one who asks for help.
- **1:16** "Judge fairly" is Hebrew *tsedeq*, to judge rightly. The foreigner is the *ger*, the resident alien, who must be treated equally.
- **1:17** "Judgement belongs to God" explains why favouritism is wrong. A judge acts for God.
- **1:22** Here the people propose sending scouts. In Numbers 13:1 the LORD commands it, and both accounts are kept.
- **1:28** "Walls up to the sky" is a figure for very high walls. "Anakites" is the same as the descendants of Anak in Numbers 13.
- **1:31** The image of a father carrying his son shows the LORD's care in the wilderness. See also Hosea 11:1-4.
- **1:37** Moses says the LORD was angry with him "because of you". In Numbers 20:12 the reason given is his own failure at Meribah. The two statements are held together.
- **1:44** "Like a swarm of bees" is a figure for fast, angry pursuit.
- **2:4** "Your kinsmen, the descendants of Esau" are the Edomites. The Israelites are told not to attack them.
- **2:7** "You have lacked nothing" refers to the whole forty years.
- **2:10** Verses 10 to 12 and 20 to 23 are explanatory notes about earlier peoples of the land, such as the Emites, Horites and Zamzummites. Many scholars think they were added to the speech.
- **2:14** The thirty-eight years run from Kadesh-barnea (Numbers 14) to crossing the Zered.
- **2:23** The Caphtorites came from Caphtor, usually taken to be Crete. They are linked with the Philistines.
- **2:30** "Made his spirit stubborn" means the LORD brought about Sihon's refusal. The same phrase is used of Pharaoh in Exodus.
- **2:34** "Devoted to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM.
- **3:11** Og's bed was of iron, nine cubits (about four metres) long. The word *bed* may also mean a coffin or tomb of basalt, which is a black stone.
- **3:14** Jair is also named in Numbers 32:41.
- **3:17** "The Salt Sea" is the Dead Sea.
- **3:24** Moses' prayer begins with praise before it asks for anything.
- **3:26** "Because of you" again places part of the blame on the people. See the note on 1:37.
- **3:27** Pisgah is a mountain ridge east of the Jordan. Moses sees the land from it, as in Numbers 27:12.
- **4:2** The command not to add to the word or take away from it protects the teaching from change.
- **4:3** Baal-peor is the event in Numbers 25.
- **4:6** The nations will see Israel's wisdom in the way it keeps the teaching.
- **4:10** "Fear" is Hebrew *yirah*, awe and reverence. See entry H-YIRAH.
- **4:12** "You saw no form" is the basis for the ban on images in verses 15 to 19.
- **4:13** "The Ten Words" is Hebrew *aseret ha-devarim*. "Covenant" is *berit*. See entry H-BERIT.
- **4:19** "The host of heaven" means the stars and heavenly bodies, which other peoples worshipped.
- **4:20** "The iron furnace" is a figure for Egypt, where metal is smelted. It stands for hard slavery.
- **4:24** "A consuming fire" shows God's holiness. "Jealous" is Hebrew *qanna*, passionately devoted. See entry H-QANNA.
- **4:26** "Heaven and earth" are called as witnesses, as in a legal case.
- **4:29** To seek the LORD "with all your heart and with all your soul" is a phrase that recurs through the book.
- **4:31** "Compassionate" is Hebrew *rachum*, a word from *rechem*, "womb".
- **4:34** "A strong hand and an outstretched arm" is the standard phrase for the exodus.
- **4:35** "There is no other besides him" is a statement that the LORD alone is God.
- **4:41** The three cities of refuge east of the Jordan are named here ahead of the cities in the west. See Numbers 35:9-15.
- **4:45** "Decrees" is Hebrew *edot*, solemn instructions.
- **5:2** The covenant at Horeb is renewed for the new generation. Moses speaks as if they had all been present.
- **5:3** "With us, with all of us who are alive here today" means every generation stands in the covenant as if it had been made with them.
- **5:5** Moses stands between the LORD and the people because they were afraid of the fire.
- **5:6** The Ten Words repeat Exodus 20:2-17, with some differences. The WABV text keeps the common wording the same as in Exodus, so that the two versions can be compared.
- **5:12** "Keep the Sabbath day" has the verb *shamar*, "keep" or "guard", where Exodus 20:8 has "remember". See entry H-SHABBAT.
- **5:14** The added clause, "so that your male and female servants may rest as you do", gives the social reason for the Sabbath.
- **5:15** Here the Sabbath is based on the memory of slavery in Egypt. In Exodus 20:11 it is based on creation.
- **5:16** The clause "as the LORD your God commanded you" and "it may go well with you" are added in this version.
- **5:21** The wife is named first, and the verb for the house and other property is *hitaveh* ("desire"), not *chamad* ("covet") as in Exodus 20:17.
- **5:22** "He added nothing more" means that only the Ten Words were spoken by the LORD directly to the whole people.
- **5:24** "God can speak with a human being, and the human being still lives" shows the awe at the mountain.
- **5:29** "If only their hearts were always like this" is the LORD's wish that the people's reverence would last.
- **6:1** "The command" is Hebrew *mitsvah*, used here for the whole body of the teaching.
- **6:4** The Hebrew is four words, *YHWH elohenu YHWH echad*. It can be read "the LORD our God, the LORD is one", or "the LORD is our God, the LORD alone", or "the LORD our God is one LORD". The WABV text follows the second. See entry H-SHEMA.
- **6:5** "Love" is Hebrew *ahav*. See entry H-AHAV. "Soul" is *nefesh*, the whole self. "Strength" is *me'od*, literally "very much", so "with all you have".
- **6:8** The sign on the hand and the bands between the eyes were later taken literally as *tefillin*, small boxes with the words inside. The writing on the doorposts (6:9) became the *mezuzah*.
- **6:10** The houses, wells and vineyards that Israel did not build or plant are a gift, not an achievement.
- **6:16** "As you tested him at Massah" refers to Exodus 17:1-7.
- **6:20** The question of the child gives the reason for the whole teaching: it answers a family's question with the story of the exodus.
- **6:25** "Righteousness" is Hebrew *tsedaqah*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. Here it means being in the right with the LORD by keeping his command.
- **7:1** The seven nations are named in several places. The list is probably a standard one.
- **7:2** "Devote to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. The command assumes the nations are strong and present, and yet verse 3 forbids intermarriage with them, which assumes some survive. Some scholars read the language as the stock rhetoric of ancient war.
- **7:5** "Sacred poles" is Hebrew *asherim*, as in Exodus 34:13.
- **7:6** "Holy" is *qadosh*. See entry H-QADOSH. "Chosen" is *bachar*. See entry H-BACHAR. "Treasured people" is *am segullah*, as in Exodus 19:5.
- **7:7** The LORD chose Israel for no reason of size or merit, but out of love and loyalty to his oath.
- **7:9** "Faithful love" is *hesed*. See entry H-HESED.
- **7:10** "To their face" means the LORD acts in person against those who hate him.
- **7:15** "The dreadful diseases of Egypt" recalls the plagues of Exodus.
- **7:20** "The hornet" is also in Exodus 23:28. It may be an insect or a figure for panic.
- **7:22** The nations will go "little by little", so that the land does not become empty of people and full of wild animals.
- **7:25** "Abhorrent" is Hebrew *to'evah*.
- **8:2** "To humble you and to test you" are two purposes of the wilderness years. The test shows what was in the heart.
- **8:3** "A person does not live on bread alone" is quoted in Matthew 4:4 and Luke 4:4.
- **8:5** The picture of a father disciplining his son is one of care. See Hebrews 12:5-11.
- **8:9** The land has iron and copper. Both were found in the Arabah and Transjordan.
- **8:17** The warning is against pride in wealth. See also Deuteronomy 9:4-6.
- **9:1** "Fortified up to the sky" is the same figure as in 1:28.
- **9:4** "Righteousness" is *tsedaqah*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. Israel is told that the gift of the land is no reward for its goodness.
- **9:9** Forty days and forty nights without food repeats Exodus 34:28.
- **9:21** The calf was burned, crushed to powder and thrown into the stream. See Exodus 32:20.
- **9:22** Taberah, Massah and Kibroth-hattaavah are named from Numbers 11:3, Exodus 17:7 and Numbers 11:34.
- **9:26** "Inheritance" is Hebrew *nachalah*, the people as the LORD's own possession.
- **10:1** Here Moses says he made the chest of acacia wood. In Exodus 37:1 Bezalel makes the ark. The accounts may refer to different stages.
- **10:6** The verses 6 to 9 are a note about Aaron's death and the setting apart of the Levites. The stages named do not match the order in Numbers 33:30-38.
- **10:9** "The LORD is his inheritance" repeats the principle of Numbers 18:20.
- **10:12** "What does the LORD your God ask of you?" begins a summary of the whole requirement. See also Micah 6:8.
- **10:16** "Circumcise your hearts" means to remove what keeps the heart closed. See Jeremiah 4:4.
- **10:17** "God of gods and Lord of lords" is a way of saying he is supreme over all. "Takes no bribe" gives the LORD as the model judge.
- **10:18** "Foreigner" is *ger*. See entry H-GER. The command to love the foreigner follows the example of God.
- **10:22** "Seventy persons" is the number of Jacob's family in Genesis 46:27.
- **11:2** "It is not your children ... but you yourselves" means those who saw the exodus are those being addressed, though the generation has changed.
- **11:6** The judgement on Dathan and Abiram is told in Numbers 16.
- **11:10** "Watered it by foot" is a phrase of uncertain meaning. It may refer to opening channels with the foot, or to a foot-driven water wheel.
- **11:14** "The autumn rain and the spring rain" are the early and the later rains. The Hebrew words are *yoreh* and *malqosh*.
- **11:24** The territory described is larger than the land in Numbers 34.
- **11:29** Mount Gerizim and Mount Ebal face each other near Shechem. The ceremony is described in chapter 27 and carried out in Joshua 8:30-35.
- **11:30** "The oaks of Moreh" are near Shechem, where Abraham first built an altar (Genesis 12:6).
- **12:1** The chapter begins the main body of laws (chapters 12 to 26), which are given for life in the land.
- **12:2** "High places" are hilltop shrines. "Under every leafy tree" describes the shrines of other peoples.
- **12:3** "Wipe out their names" means to remove even the memory of the gods' names from the place.
- **12:5** "The place the LORD will choose" refers to a single sanctuary. The text does not name it. "To put his name there" and "to make his name live there" express the LORD's presence in the place without limiting him to it.
- **12:6** "Contribution" is Hebrew *terumah*, a portion set apart.
- **12:8** "Each doing whatever is right in his own eyes" is a phrase that recurs in Judges 17:6 and 21:25 of the later time before the kings.
- **12:12** The Levite is named repeatedly in the book as someone to be included in the rejoicing, since he has no land.
- **12:15** The right to slaughter animals for meat anywhere was a change from Leviticus 17:3-7, where all slaughter had to be at the tent of meeting. It fits a people settled across the land.
- **12:16** Pouring the blood on the ground returns the life to God. See also verses 23 to 25.
- **12:23** "Life" is Hebrew *nefesh*. See entry H-NEPHESH.
- **12:31** "They even burn their sons and daughters" refers to child sacrifice to other gods. See also Leviticus 18:21.
- **12:32** In the Hebrew Bible this verse is 13:1. It repeats the command of 4:2 not to add or take away.
- **13:1** The "prophet or a dreamer of dreams" may give a true sign and still be false. The test is where he leads (verse 2). See also 18:20-22.
- **13:3** "The LORD your God is testing you" means that even a real sign may be a test of loyalty.
- **13:5** "Clear the evil out from among you" is a phrase found in the book, as in 17:7 and 19:19. The Hebrew verb *bi'er* means to remove or burn away.
- **13:6** The list of the nearest family and friends shows that no relationship excuses secret enticement to worship other gods. "The wife you hold in your arms" is literally "the wife of your bosom".
- **13:9** The one who knew of the enticement must throw the first stone.
- **13:12** The case of a whole town led astray is extreme. Scholars debate whether it was ever applied. It is similar in wording to the ban in chapter 7.
- **13:13** "Worthless men" is Hebrew *bene beliyya'al*, "sons of worthlessness".
- **13:15** "Devoting it to destruction" is the ban, *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM.
- **13:16** The town becomes a permanent heap (a *tel*) and is not rebuilt.
- **14:1** "Children of the LORD your God" gives the reason for the ban on mourning customs. See Leviticus 19:27-28.
- **14:3** "Abhorrent" is Hebrew *to'evah*.
- **14:5** The names of the animals in the list of clean animals are uncertain. Several of the animals are named only here and in 1 Kings 4:23.
- **14:12** The birds are listed in the same way as in Leviticus 11:13-19. The names are uncertain.
- **14:21** The ban on boiling a kid in its mother's milk is also in Exodus 23:19 and 34:26. Here it ends a section on food. The foreigner is allowed what the Israelite is not.
- **14:22** The tithe here is eaten by the family at the sanctuary. This is different from the tithe given to the Levites in Numbers 18, and the two are explained in different ways.
- **14:26** Spending the money on "wine or strong drink" shows that the festival meal is meant to be a joyful one.
- **14:28** Every third year the tithe is kept in the towns for the Levite, the foreigner, the orphan and the widow.
- **15:1** "A release" is Hebrew *shemittah*. It is a cancellation or suspension of debts every seven years. This is different from the fallow year of Leviticus 25.
- **15:2** "A release for the LORD" means it is done in his honour and by his command.
- **15:3** The foreigner is not covered, probably because he was a trader and not a poor neighbour.
- **15:4** "There will be no poor among you" is a promise that depends on obedience. Verse 11 says there will always be poor people. The two statements are kept as written.
- **15:6** To lend to many nations without borrowing means to be financially strong, not to be dependent.
- **15:9** "Worthless thought" is Hebrew *devar beliyya'al*.
- **15:12** The law covers a Hebrew man or woman. Exodus 21:7-11 treats a woman sold as a servant differently, so the two laws differ.
- **15:13** The gift to a freed servant is new here. It is not in Exodus 21.
- **15:17** The awl pushed through the ear into the door marks permanent service. See Exodus 21:6.
- **15:18** "At half the cost of a hired worker" is difficult. It may mean that the servant's value was double that of a hired worker, or that the master saved half the wages.
- **15:19** The firstborn animals are given to the LORD. See Exodus 13:11-15.
- **15:22** An animal with a defect is eaten as ordinary meat, not offered.
- **16:1** "Abib" is the spring month of the exodus.
- **16:2** The Passover may be from "the flock or the herd" here. Exodus 12:3-5 says a lamb or kid. This verse may refer to the offerings during the festival.
- **16:3** "The bread of affliction" is the unleavened bread, a reminder of the hurry of the exodus.
- **16:7** "Cook" is Hebrew *bishel*, which can mean boil. Exodus 12:9 forbids boiling. The two are explained in different ways.
- **16:9** The count of seven weeks begins when the sickle is first put to the grain. See Leviticus 23:15-16.
- **16:10** "The Festival of Weeks" is also called Pentecost.
- **16:13** "The Festival of Booths" is also called Ingathering.
- **16:16** "Appear before the LORD" means to go to the sanctuary three times a year.
- **16:18** "Judges and officers" are the leaders of justice in each town.
- **16:19** "A bribe blinds the eyes of the wise" repeats Exodus 23:8.
- **16:20** "Justice, only justice" translates Hebrew *tsedeq, tsedeq*, a doubled word that stresses the whole command.
- **16:21** "Wooden pole of Asherah" is the *asherah*. See the note on Exodus 34:13.
- **16:22** "Sacred pillar" is *matstsevah*, a standing stone.
- **17:1** The rule on offering only animals without defect repeats Leviticus 22:20-25.
- **17:3** "The host of heaven" means the sun, moon and stars as objects of worship (see 4:19).
- **17:6** Two or three witnesses are needed for a death sentence, as also in 19:15 and Numbers 35:30.
- **17:7** The witnesses throw the first stone. This makes them accountable for their testimony.
- **17:8** The three types of case, "bloodshed", "legal claim" and "assault", are broad headings. They cover matters of killing, property and injury. The central court is for the cases that local judges cannot decide.
- **17:9** "The Levitical priests and ... the judge" is a high court at the sanctuary, similar to the court set up in 2 Chronicles 19:8-11.
- **17:12** "Acts with arrogance" is Hebrew *zadon*. A person who defies the high court is put to death.
- **17:14** The law of the king allows a king but does not command one. The words "like all the nations" echo 1 Samuel 8:5.
- **17:15** The king must be from among his kinsmen and one the LORD chooses.
- **17:16** The king is not to build up horses or send the people to Egypt for them. Solomon later broke this (1 Kings 10:26-29).
- **17:17** The king is not to build up many wives or great wealth. See 1 Kings 11:1-8.
- **17:18** "A copy of this teaching" is Hebrew *mishneh ha-torah*. This phrase is the source of the Greek name "Deuteronomy". It means a copy, not a second law.
- **17:20** The king is not to see himself as above his kinsmen. He is under the teaching like everyone else.
- **18:1** The Levites' share is the same as in Numbers 18, the offerings of the LORD.
- **18:3** "The shoulder, the two cheeks and the stomach" are the priests' portions of the sacrifice, which differ from the breast and thigh in Leviticus 7:28-34.
- **18:6** A Levite who moves to the sanctuary is allowed to serve and take an equal share.
- **18:10** The list of banned practices in verses 10 to 11 contains eight terms, whose exact meanings are uncertain. They cover several kinds of divination, sorcery and consulting spirits or the dead.
- **18:13** "Wholly devoted" is Hebrew *tamim*, which can also mean "blameless" or "complete".
- **18:15** "A prophet like me" is Hebrew *navi*. See entry H-NAVI. The phrase can be read as a line of prophets or as one figure. In later times it was understood in both ways. See Acts 3:22.
- **18:16** The people had asked for Moses to speak to them instead of God (5:23-27).
- **18:20** A prophet who speaks what God has not commanded, or who speaks for other gods, is to die.
- **18:22** The test of a prophet is whether his word comes true. Chapter 13:1-5 gives a second test: a prophet whose sign comes true is still false if he leads people to other gods.
- **19:2** Three towns of refuge are set aside west of the Jordan, to match the three in the east (4:41-43).
- **19:5** The case of the axe head slipping from the handle is an example of accidental killing.
- **19:9** Three more towns may be added if the land is enlarged. The text makes this depend on obedience.
- **19:13** "Do not let your eye pity him" means the guilty person is not to be spared.
- **19:14** The boundary stone marked the limits of a family's land. Moving it was a theft of inheritance. See Proverbs 22:28.
- **19:15** One witness is not enough to convict. This applies to any crime.
- **19:16** "A malicious witness" is Hebrew *ed chamas*, one who bears false evidence in order to harm.
- **19:19** A false witness suffers the penalty that he meant for the accused. This is the law of equal retaliation.
- **19:21** "Life for life, eye for eye" repeats Exodus 21:23-25.
- **20:1** The assurance that the LORD who brought Israel out of Egypt is with them is the basis of the whole chapter.
- **20:5** The exemptions from service cover a new house, a new vineyard, a new marriage and fear. See also 24:5.
- **20:10** An offer of peace is to be made before any attack.
- **20:11** "Forced labour" is Hebrew *mas*, a work levy.
- **20:13** All the males of a town that refuses peace are killed. This is rendered as written.
- **20:14** The women, the little ones and the livestock are taken as spoil, in contrast to the stricter rule in verses 16 and 17.
- **20:15** The rules for distant towns differ from the rules for the nearby peoples.
- **20:16** "Anything that breathes" refers to the towns of the nearby peoples. "Devote to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. This command and 7:1-5 are among the hardest passages in the book.
- **20:19** The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. It can be read as a question, "Are the trees of the field human beings, that they should be besieged?", or as "the tree of the field is the life of man". Both readings give the same sense, a limit on destroying food-bearing trees.
- **21:1** The case of an unsolved killing sets out a rite to clear the town of the guilt. The nearest town takes responsibility, as no one can identify the killer.
- **21:4** "A valley with a flowing stream" that has not been worked marks the place as clean. "Break the heifer's neck" is Hebrew *araf*, to break the neck. The animal takes the place of the unknown killer.
- **21:5** The priests take part because every dispute and assault is decided by their word. This links to the central court in 17:8-13.
- **21:8** "Forgive" is Hebrew *kapper*, to cover or atone. See entry H-KAPPER.
- **21:10** The law on the captive woman gives her protections in a war setting. She has a month to mourn, she may not be sold or made a slave, and she is freed if he is not pleased with her. "Humiliated" is Hebrew *innah*, the word often used of sexual violation.
- **21:12** "Trim her nails" is uncertain. It may also mean to let them grow. The shaving and the change of clothes mark a change of status.
- **21:15** "Loved" and "unloved" mean favoured and not favoured, as in Genesis 29:30-31.
- **21:17** "The first sign of his strength" is a figure for the firstborn. A double share is the firstborn son's portion.
- **21:18** The law of the stubborn and rebellious son requires both parents to bring him, and the charge includes being "a glutton and a drunkard". Jewish tradition set conditions so narrow that it was almost never applied. The text is rendered as written.
- **21:22** Hanging a body on a tree was a public display after an execution. The body had to be buried on the same day.
- **21:23** "Under God's curse" is quoted in Galatians 3:13 in connection with the cross.
- **22:1** The commands to return lost animals and property extend Exodus 23:4-5 to cover any lost item.
- **22:5** "A man's clothing" is Hebrew *keli gever*, which can mean clothing, equipment or weapons. The exact scope of the ban is debated, and may have been a response to some cultic practice.
- **22:6** The ban on taking a mother bird with her young shows concern for living creatures. The promised reward of long life is the same as for honouring parents (5:16).
- **22:8** "A parapet" is Hebrew *ma'aqeh*, a low wall round a flat roof.
- **22:9** The ban on mixing kinds is the same in principle as Leviticus 19:19. The vineyard law says the yield would be "forfeit", that is, made holy and forbidden for use.
- **22:12** "Tassels" is Hebrew *gedilim*. See Numbers 15:38.
- **22:13** The case of the accused bride deals with a husband who turns against his wife and slanders her. "The proofs of virginity" is Hebrew *betulim*. The law is hard on both sides: a false accuser is fined and cannot divorce her (verses 18-19), and if the charge is true the penalty is death (verses 20-21).
- **22:19** A hundred shekels is double the bride-price of fifty shekels in verse 29.
- **22:22** Both are to die in the case of adultery, as in Leviticus 20:10.
- **22:24** The engaged woman in a town is held at fault because she "did not cry out", on the view that she could have been heard.
- **22:25** In the open country, no one could hear her, and she is not at fault. The text uses a strong verb, *chazaq*, "overpower", and compares the case to murder.
- **22:28** The verbs here differ from verse 25. "Seizes" is *tafas*, and "violated" is *innah*. The law on the unengaged virgin is read by some as rape and by others as seduction, and the question is debated.
- **22:30** In the Hebrew Bible this verse is 23:1. A man's father's wife is the same as in Leviticus 18:8. "Uncover his father's skirt" is a figure for sexual relations with a father's wife.
- **23:1** This verse is 23:2 in the Hebrew Bible, and the chapter has twenty-five verses in English and twenty-six in Hebrew. The ban on certain men from the assembly is softened in Isaiah 56:3-5 and Acts 8:26-39.
- **23:2** "A forbidden union" is Hebrew *mamzer*, a word of uncertain meaning. It has been read as a child of an incestuous or adulterous union, or as a person of mixed or foreign descent.
- **23:3** The ban on Ammonites and Moabites is based on how they treated Israel. See Nehemiah 13:1-3. Yet Ruth the Moabite is an ancestor of David.
- **23:4** Balaam is the story in Numbers 22 to 24.
- **23:7** The Edomite is a kinsman, and the Egyptian a former host. Both are given a path into the assembly in the third generation.
- **23:10** The unclean state after a nocturnal emission is dealt with by washing and waiting until evening, as in Leviticus 15:16.
- **23:13** "A small tool" is Hebrew *yated*, a peg or small digging implement.
- **23:14** "Anything indecent" is Hebrew *ervat davar*, the same phrase as in 24:1. It stands here for uncleanness in the camp.
- **23:15** An escaped slave who flees to Israel is not to be returned. This is unusual among ancient laws. It probably refers to a slave who has escaped from a foreign master.
- **23:17** "Temple prostitute" is Hebrew *qedeshah* (woman) and *qadesh* (man), those who served in pagan shrines.
- **23:18** "The price of a dog" is Hebrew *mechir keleb*, probably the earnings of a male prostitute. "Dog" is a term of contempt.
- **23:19** "Interest" is Hebrew *neshekh*. It is banned on loans to fellow Israelites, but allowed for foreigners, who were traders.
- **23:21** The law on vows is the same as Ecclesiastes 5:4-5, that a vow is better not made than made and broken.
- **23:24** The right of a passer-by to eat from a neighbour's vineyard or grain is a way of meeting hunger without stealing.
- **24:1** "Something indecent" is Hebrew *ervat davar*, literally "nakedness of a matter". Its meaning is debated and was argued by Jewish teachers. The law does not command divorce. It assumes it and regulates it, with a written certificate. See Matthew 5:31-32 and 19:7-9.
- **24:4** "Made unclean" is Hebrew *huttamma'ah*, a word that is used in this form only here. A divorced woman who has married another man cannot return to her first husband.
- **24:5** A newly married man is free of military service for a year. See also 20:7.
- **24:6** A millstone is a household's means of making bread, so taking one would take a livelihood.
- **24:7** Kidnapping an Israelite and selling him is a capital crime. See also Exodus 21:16.
- **24:9** The memory of what happened to Miriam (Numbers 12) is a warning to follow the priests' rulings on skin disease.
- **24:13** "Righteousness" is Hebrew *tsedaqah*. See entry H-TSEDAQAH. Returning a poor man's cloak counts as righteousness.
- **24:15** Wages are to be paid on the same day. See also Leviticus 19:13.
- **24:16** Each person is responsible for his own sin. See 2 Kings 14:6 and Ezekiel 18.
- **24:19** Leaving a forgotten sheaf for the poor is the basis of the story in Ruth 2.
- **25:3** A limit of forty blows protects the person's dignity. Later Jewish practice used thirty-nine, as in 2 Corinthians 11:24.
- **25:4** "You are not to muzzle an ox" is quoted in 1 Corinthians 9:9 and 1 Timothy 5:18.
- **25:5** The law of the brother-in-law (*yibbum*, levirate marriage) is the basis of the story of Judah and Tamar (Genesis 38) and of Ruth.
- **25:9** Pulling off the sandal (*chalitsah*) and spitting in his face shows public shame for refusing to keep his brother's name alive.
- **25:10** "The House of the Unsandalled" is the nickname of the family.
- **25:11** The case of the woman who seizes a man in a fight is unusual. The penalty of cutting off her hand is severe, and some Jewish tradition read it as a money payment.
- **25:13** The ban on false weights repeats Leviticus 19:35-36. See also Proverbs 11:1.
- **25:17** Amalek's attack is in Exodus 17:8-16. "Wipe out the memory" repeats Exodus 17:14.
- **26:2** The firstfruits are brought to the sanctuary in a basket as an act of thanksgiving.
- **26:5** "A wandering Aramean" is Hebrew *arami oved*. "Wandering" can also mean "perishing". The "father" may be Jacob or Abraham. The declaration is a short creed that sums up Israel's story.
- **26:12** "The year of the tithe" is the third year, when the tithe goes to the poor (14:28-29).
- **26:13** "The holy portion" is the tithe. The declaration says he has given it to those in need.
- **26:14** The promises not to use it while in mourning or give it to the dead refer to practices connected with the dead, which are forbidden.
- **26:17** "You have declared" is Hebrew *he'emarta*, "you have caused to say". The covenant is described as a mutual declaration, in which each side declares the other as theirs.
- **26:18** "Treasured possession" is Hebrew *segullah*, as in Exodus 19:5.
- **27:2** The large plastered stones carry the words of the teaching in writing, like the display of a treaty. This is the first of two sets of stones (see also verse 4).
- **27:4** The Hebrew text reads "Mount Ebal". The Samaritan Pentateuch reads "Mount Gerizim", and the difference is important to the Samaritans. See John 4:20.
- **27:5** An altar of uncut stones, with no iron tool, repeats Exodus 20:25.
- **27:9** "Today you have become the people of the LORD" means the covenant is renewed with each generation.
- **27:12** Six tribes stand on Gerizim for the blessing and six on Ebal for the curse. The two mountains face each other near Shechem. The ceremony is carried out in Joshua 8:30-35.
- **27:15** The twelve curses deal with sins that are done in secret, where no human judge may see. "Amen" means "truly", and the people say it to accept each curse.
- **27:19** The curse on those who twist justice for the foreigner, the orphan and the widow repeats a theme of the book.
- **27:20** The sexual curses (verses 20 to 23) repeat the forbidden relationships of Leviticus 18 and 20.
- **27:26** "Uphold the words of this teaching by doing them" is a general curse that covers the whole law. See Galatians 3:10.
- **28:1** Chapter 28 has fourteen verses of blessing and fifty-three of curse. It has a close parallel in Leviticus 26.
- **28:3** The blessings in verses 3 to 6 are in matching pairs, city and field, womb and ground, basket and kneading bowl, coming in and going out. The curses in verses 16 to 19 match them.
- **28:7** "By one road ... by seven" means a total defeat of the enemy.
- **28:9** "A holy people" repeats 7:6 and 26:19.
- **28:10** "The name of the LORD is called over you" means Israel is marked as his.
- **28:12** "His good storehouse, the heavens" is a figure for rain, which comes from heaven.
- **28:13** "The head and not the tail" is a figure for leading and not following.
- **28:20** "Confusion" is Hebrew *mehumah*, panic or disorder.
- **28:22** The terms for disease and blight are uncertain. Several occur only here and in Leviticus 26.
- **28:23** Bronze sky and iron earth reverse Leviticus 26:19, but the order of the metals is the opposite.
- **28:27** "The boils of Egypt" refers to the plague in Exodus 9:9-11.
- **28:30** The curse that another man will lie with one's engaged woman uses a strong, blunt verb, which the Hebrew reading tradition softened. The text is rendered as written.
- **28:36** The mention of a king here looks ahead to the monarchy of 17:14-20.
- **28:42** "The whirring locust" is Hebrew *tselatsal*, a word that imitates the sound of the insect.
- **28:46** "A sign and a wonder" turns the language of the exodus into a warning.
- **28:48** "An iron yoke" is a figure for harsh slavery. It is the opposite of the broken yoke in Leviticus 26:13.
- **28:49** "A nation from afar ... like an eagle" is later read as Assyria, Babylon or Rome. The text names no nation.
- **28:53** The siege scene in verses 53 to 57, with parents eating their children, is the most severe curse in the book. It is rendered as written. See also 2 Kings 6:28-29 and Lamentations 4:10.
- **28:58** "This glorious and awe-inspiring name" refers to the divine name, the LORD.
- **28:60** "The diseases of Egypt" are those of the plagues and the Egyptian diseases that Israel feared.
- **28:63** "As the LORD took delight ... so he will take delight in destroying" is a stark statement of a reversal.
- **28:64** The scattering of Israel among the nations is the exile.
- **28:68** "Back to Egypt in ships" is a reversal of the exodus. Egypt is not reached by sea, so the verse is a figure.
- **29:1** In the Hebrew Bible this verse is 28:69, and chapter 29 begins at the English 29:2. The covenant in Moab is made in addition to the one at Horeb.
- **29:4** "A mind to understand" is literally "a heart to know". The people have seen the signs, but have not understood them.
- **29:6** "You have not eaten bread or drunk wine" means they lived on manna and water, so that they might know the LORD is their God.
- **29:11** "From the one who cuts your wood to the one who draws your water" shows that even the foreign labourers are included in the covenant (see Joshua 9:21, 23).
- **29:14** "Not with you alone" means future generations are bound by the covenant as if they were present.
- **29:18** "A root that produces poison and bitterness" is a figure for a person or a hidden sin that grows to harm the whole community. See Hebrews 12:15.
- **29:19** "Sweeping away the watered land with the dry" is obscure. The Hebrew is very compressed. It seems to say that the secret rebel brings ruin on everyone, the good and the bad.
- **29:20** "Wipe out his name" means that his memory is lost.
- **29:23** Sodom and Gomorrah are named with Admah and Zeboiim, which are also in Genesis 14:2 and Hosea 11:8.
- **29:29** "The hidden things" is Hebrew *nistarot*. In the Hebrew manuscripts, some of the letters in this verse are marked with dots. The meaning of the verse is that God keeps some things hidden, and has revealed what people need in order to obey.
- **30:1** In this chapter the blessing and curse lead to the promise of return after the curse has come.
- **30:2** "Return" is Hebrew *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV. The word means to turn back, to repent and to come home. It is used here both of the people's turning and of the restoration of their fortunes.
- **30:3** "Turn your captivity around" is Hebrew *shav shevut*, a phrase for restoration.
- **30:6** "The LORD your God will circumcise your heart" says that the LORD himself will do what the people were told to do in 10:16.
- **30:11** "Not too hard for you, and not far off" means the command can be done.
- **30:12** The words "in heaven" and "across the sea" are used by Paul in Romans 10:6-8.
- **30:15** "Life" is Hebrew *chayyim*. See entry H-CHAYYIM.
- **30:19** "Heaven and earth" are called as witnesses, as in 4:26. The command "Choose life" is the heart of the book.
- **30:20** "He is your life" means the LORD himself is the source of life.
- **31:2** "A hundred and twenty years old" is the full span of Moses' life. "Go out and come in" is a phrase for leading, especially in war.
- **31:6** "He will not fail you or abandon you" is quoted in Hebrews 13:5.
- **31:7** Moses commissions Joshua in front of all Israel. The same assurance is given to Joshua in Joshua 1:5-9.
- **31:9** Moses writes down the teaching and gives it to the priests who carry the ark and to the elders, so that its keeping is shared.
- **31:10** The teaching is to be read aloud every seventh year, in the year of release, at the Festival of Booths. This ties the reading to the release of debts in 15:1-11.
- **31:12** The foreigner is included in the audience, with men, women and children.
- **31:16** "Lie down with your ancestors" is a way of speaking of death. "Prostitute themselves" is the standard figure for unfaithful worship.
- **31:17** "Hide my face" means to withdraw God's favour and protection.
- **31:19** The song is given as a witness against the people, so that it is remembered by their descendants.
- **31:24** "The book of the teaching" is a written record that is kept as a witness.
- **31:26** The book is placed "beside" the ark, not inside it, as the tablets were.
- **31:28** "Heaven and earth" are called as witnesses against the people, as in 4:26 and 30:19.
- **32:1** The song opens by calling heaven and earth to listen. Compare Isaiah 1:2.
- **32:2** The images of rain and dew show the teaching as something that gives life.
- **32:4** "The Rock" is Hebrew *tsur*. See entry H-TSUR. The title is used repeatedly in the poem (verses 15, 18, 30 and 31) and means a firm, safe place of protection.
- **32:5** The Hebrew of this verse is very difficult. The first line can be read "they have acted corruptly towards him, not his children, their blemish", or in other ways. The WABV text gives one reading.
- **32:6** "Set you firm" is Hebrew *konenekha*, a word that can mean "established you".
- **32:8** The Hebrew text reads "the number of the sons of Israel". The Dead Sea Scrolls and the Greek translation read "the sons of God", which would mean the nations were assigned to heavenly beings and Israel to the LORD (verse 9). The WABV text follows the Hebrew.
- **32:10** "The pupil of his eye" is Hebrew *ishon*, the sensitive centre of the eye.
- **32:11** The image of the eagle shows protection and teaching its young to fly. See Exodus 19:4.
- **32:13** "Honey from the crag and oil from the flinty rock" is a figure for the richness of the land.
- **32:14** "The blood of the grape" means red wine.
- **32:15** "Jeshurun" is a poetic name for Israel, perhaps meaning "the upright one". It is also used in 33:5 and Isaiah 44:2. The verse plays on it, saying that the upright one grew fat and kicked.
- **32:17** "Demons" is Hebrew *shedim*, a word used only here and in Psalm 106:37. It names spirit beings that are worshipped, and says they are not gods.
- **32:18** The line "the God who gave you birth" gives God a mother's role, with the father's role in verse 6.
- **32:20** "Upside-down ways" is Hebrew *tahpukhot*, a rare word for perversity.
- **32:21** The punishment fits the sin: because Israel made God jealous by what is not a god, God will make them jealous by those who are not a people. See Romans 10:19.
- **32:22** "Sheol" is the realm of the dead. See entry H-SHEOL.
- **32:24** Several of the words for the plagues in this verse are rare and uncertain.
- **32:26** "Cut them to pieces" is a translation of an uncertain verb, *afehem*.
- **32:30** "Sold them" means the LORD handed them over, as a slave is sold.
- **32:32** The vine of Sodom is a figure for corrupt rulers or a corrupt people.
- **32:35** "Vengeance and repayment are mine" is quoted in Romans 12:19 and Hebrews 10:30.
- **32:36** "Bond or free" is a Hebrew idiom, *atsur ve'azuv*, meaning "everyone". Its exact sense is uncertain.
- **32:39** "I, I am he" is a statement of the LORD's uniqueness. "I put to death and I make alive" is also in 1 Samuel 2:6.
- **32:42** "Drunk with blood" is a figure for the arrows' work in battle.
- **32:43** The Hebrew text is short. The Dead Sea Scrolls and the Greek translation have a longer text, which includes a call to the gods to bow before him. See Hebrews 1:6 and Romans 15:10. "Cleanse his land" translates *kipper*, atone for.
- **32:44** In some ancient texts the name of Joshua here is Hoshea, as in Numbers 13:8.
- **32:47** "It is not an empty word for you. It is your life" says that the teaching is the source of life.
- **32:49** Mount Nebo is part of the Abarim range, from which Moses will see the land. See 34:1.
- **32:51** "Meribah-kadesh" is the place of Numbers 20:1-13.
- **33:1** "The blessing" is Hebrew *berakhah*. See entry H-BARAK. "The man of God" is a title for a prophet. This is the only place it is used of Moses in the Torah, though it is also in the title of Psalm 90. The poem stands beside Jacob's blessing of his sons in Genesis 49, with some differences in the order and the tribes.
- **33:2** The coming of the LORD from Sinai, Seir and Paran is a picture of his appearance in glory. "The myriads of holy ones" are the heavenly host. "With fire flashing at his right hand" is uncertain, because the Hebrew word *eshdat* is rare and unclear.
- **33:3** "He loves the peoples" is uncertain. The word can mean "peoples" or "tribes". The verse as a whole is difficult, and its subjects are not clear.
- **33:5** "A king in Jeshurun" most likely refers to the LORD as king, though some have read it as Moses. See also Exodus 15:18.
- **33:6** Reuben is the firstborn but receives the shortest and most uncertain blessing. The Hebrew of the last line is very compressed.
- **33:7** Judah is separate from his people in this verse, and the blessing asks the LORD to bring him back to them.
- **33:8** The order "Thummim and Urim" is the reverse of the usual "Urim and Thummim". They are the objects used by the priests to decide questions (Exodus 28:30).
- **33:9** Levi's loyalty above his own family refers to the golden calf (Exodus 32:25-29). The Hebrew has "they kept" in the plural.
- **33:12** "Between his shoulders" is a figure for the land of Benjamin, which includes the area of the later temple, or for a place of safety.
- **33:13** "The deep that crouches below" means the water under the earth.
- **33:16** "Him who lived in the bush" refers to the LORD in the burning bush (Exodus 3:2-4).
- **33:17** "A firstborn bull" and "a wild ox" are figures for strength. Ephraim and Manasseh, Joseph's two tribes, are named as the "ten thousands" and "thousands".
- **33:19** "They will call peoples to the mountain" is uncertain. It may refer to a place of worship, and to the sea trade of Zebulun.
- **33:20** The blessing of Gad is obscure in several lines. "Enlarges Gad" can also be read as the one who gives him room.
- **33:21** "A commander's portion" refers to the best land east of the Jordan, taken first by Gad (Numbers 32).
- **33:22** Dan's blessing is very short. A lion's cub "from Bashan" places the tribe in the north.
- **33:23** "The lake and the south" is probably the Sea of Galilee. The Hebrew is uncertain.
- **33:24** "Dip his foot in oil" is a figure for rich olive groves.
- **33:26** "The God of Jeshurun" repeats the name for Israel used in 32:15.
- **33:27** "The God of old is a dwelling place" is uncertain. The Hebrew can also be read "a refuge". "The arms that last for ever" is a figure for support underneath.
- **33:28** "The spring of Jacob" is a figure for the people as a source of life.
- **33:29** "Happy" is Hebrew *ashrei*, the word that opens Psalm 1.
- **33:1** The tribe of Simeon is not named in the blessing. Some ancient manuscripts add a line, and some scholars think Simeon is counted with Judah.
- **34:1** The last chapter, which tells of Moses' death, was added to the speeches. "As far as Dan" names a town that is called Dan only after Judges 18, and so reflects a later hand.
- **34:5** The death of Moses is told in the third person. Jewish and Christian tradition has read the passage either as written by Joshua or by a later editor, or as written by Moses in advance.
- **34:6** "He buried him" says the LORD buried Moses. His grave is unknown, so that it would not become a shrine.
- **34:7** "His eye was not dim" means his sight was still clear. His strength had not gone.
- **34:9** "The spirit of wisdom" refers to the gift passed on when Moses laid his hands on Joshua (Numbers 27:18-23).
- **34:10** "No prophet has risen in Israel like Moses" looks back from a later time. "Face to face" repeats Numbers 12:6-8.
- **34:12** The book ends with Moses' deeds before all Israel. The Torah closes with Israel on the edge of the land.
