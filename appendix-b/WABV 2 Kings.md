## 2 Kings 1-3: Elijah Taken Up and Elisha's Beginning

### Chapter 1

**1** After Ahab's death, Moab rebelled against Israel.

**2** Ahaziah fell through the lattice of his upper room in Samaria and was injured. So he sent messengers and told them, "Go and ask Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron, whether I will recover from this injury."

**3** But the angel of the LORD said to Elijah the Tishbite, "Get up and go to meet the messengers of the king of Samaria, and say to them, 'Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are going to ask Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron?'

**4** Therefore this is what the LORD says: You will not come down from the bed that you have gone up to, but you will certainly die." So Elijah went.

**5** The messengers went back to the king, and he said to them, "Why have you come back?"

**6** They said to him, "A man came up to meet us and said to us, 'Go back to the king who sent you and tell him, This is what the LORD says: Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are sending to ask Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron? Therefore you will not come down from the bed that you have gone up to, but you will certainly die.'"

**7** He said to them, "What was the man like who came to meet you and told you these things?"

**8** They answered him, "He was wearing a garment of hair, with a leather belt round his waist." He said, "It is Elijah the Tishbite."

**9** Then the king sent a captain of fifty with his fifty men to him. The captain went up to Elijah, who was sitting on top of a hill, and said to him, "Man of God, the king says, 'Come down.'"

**10** Elijah answered the captain of fifty, "If I am a man of God, let fire come down from heaven and burn you up, you and your fifty." Then fire came down from heaven and burned him up, and his fifty.

**11** The king sent another captain of fifty with his fifty to him. He said to him, "Man of God, this is what the king says: 'Come down at once.'"

**12** Elijah answered them, "If I am a man of God, let fire come down from heaven and burn you up, you and your fifty." Then the fire of God came down from heaven and burned him up, and his fifty.

**13** Again the king sent a third captain of fifty with his fifty. The third captain of fifty went up and came and fell on his knees before Elijah and begged him, "Man of God, please let my life and the lives of these fifty servants of yours be precious in your eyes.

**14** Look, fire came down from heaven and burned up the two earlier captains of fifty with their fifties. But now let my life be precious in your eyes."

**15** The angel of the LORD said to Elijah, "Go down with him. Do not be afraid of him." So he got up and went down with him to the king,

**16** and said to him, "This is what the LORD says: Because you sent messengers to ask Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron, as if there were no God in Israel to ask for his word, you will not come down from the bed that you have gone up to, but you will certainly die."

**17** So he died, as the word of the LORD that Elijah had spoken said. Jehoram became king in his place, in the second year of Jehoram son of Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, because Ahaziah had no son.

**18** The rest of the acts of Ahaziah that he did, are they not written in the Book of the Annals of the Kings of Israel?

### Chapter 2

**1** When the LORD was about to take Elijah up to heaven in a whirlwind, Elijah and Elisha were on their way from Gilgal.

**2** Elijah said to Elisha, "Stay here, for the LORD has sent me as far as Bethel." But Elisha said, "As the LORD lives, and as you live, I will not leave you." So they went down to Bethel.

**3** The company of the prophets who were at Bethel came out to Elisha and said to him, "Do you know that today the LORD will take your master from you?" He said, "Yes, I know. Be quiet."

**4** Elijah said to him, "Elisha, stay here, for the LORD has sent me to Jericho." But he said, "As the LORD lives, and as you live, I will not leave you." So they came to Jericho.

**5** The company of the prophets who were at Jericho came up to Elisha and said to him, "Do you know that today the LORD will take your master from you?" He answered, "Yes, I know. Be quiet."

**6** Elijah said to him, "Stay here, for the LORD has sent me to the Jordan." But he said, "As the LORD lives, and as you live, I will not leave you." So the two of them went on.

**7** Fifty of the company of the prophets also went and stood at a distance opposite them, as the two of them stood by the Jordan.

**8** Then Elijah took his cloak and rolled it up and struck the water, and the water divided to one side and the other, so that the two of them crossed over on dry ground.

**9** When they had crossed, Elijah said to Elisha, "Ask what I may do for you before I am taken from you." Elisha said, "Please let me have a double share of your spirit \[H-RUACH\]."

**10** He said, "You have asked a hard thing. Yet if you see me when I am taken from you, it will be so for you, but if not, it will not."

**11** As they were walking on and talking, a chariot of fire and horses of fire appeared and separated the two of them, and Elijah went up to heaven in a whirlwind.

**12** Elisha saw it and cried out, "My father, my father, the chariots of Israel and its horsemen." And he saw him no more. Then he took hold of his own clothes and tore them in two.

**13** He picked up the cloak of Elijah that had fallen from him and went back and stood on the bank of the Jordan.

**14** He took the cloak that had fallen from Elijah and struck the water and said, "Where is the LORD, the God of Elijah?" When he struck the water, it divided to one side and the other, and Elisha crossed over.

**15** When the company of the prophets who were at Jericho saw him opposite, they said, "The spirit of Elijah rests on Elisha." They came to meet him and bowed to the ground before him.

**16** They said to him, "Look, there are fifty strong men among your servants. Please let them go and look for your master. Perhaps the spirit of the LORD has taken him up and thrown him down on some mountain or in some valley." He said, "Do not send them."

**17** But when they pressed him until he was embarrassed, he said, "Send them." So they sent fifty men, and for three days they searched but did not find him.

**18** They came back to him while he was staying at Jericho, and he said to them, "Did I not tell you not to go?"

**19** The men of the town said to Elisha, "Look, the town is well placed, as my lord can see, but the water is bad and the land is unfruitful."

**20** He said, "Bring me a new bowl and put salt in it." So they brought it to him.

**21** Then he went out to the spring and threw salt into it and said, "This is what the LORD says: I have healed this water. From now on neither death nor miscarriage will come from it."

**22** So the water has been healed to this day, as Elisha's word said.

**23** He went up from there to Bethel. As he was going up the road, some small boys came out of the town and jeered at him, saying, "Go up, baldhead. Go up, baldhead."

**24** He turned round and looked at them and cursed them in the name of the LORD. Then two she-bears came out of the woods and mauled forty-two of the boys.

**25** From there he went on to Mount Carmel, and from there he returned to Samaria.

### Chapter 3

**1** In the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah, Jehoram son of Ahab became king over Israel in Samaria, and he ruled for twelve years.

**2** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, though not like his father and mother, for he removed the sacred pillar of Baal that his father had made.

**3** Even so, he held on to the sin of Jeroboam son of Nebat, which he made Israel commit, and did not turn away from it.

**4** Now Mesha king of Moab was a sheep breeder, and he used to deliver to the king of Israel a hundred thousand lambs and the wool of a hundred thousand rams.

**5** But when Ahab died, the king of Moab rebelled against the king of Israel.

**6** So King Jehoram marched out of Samaria at that time and called out all Israel.

**7** He went and sent word to Jehoshaphat king of Judah: "The king of Moab has rebelled against me. Will you go with me to fight against Moab?" He said, "I will go. I am as you are, my people as your people, my horses as your horses."

**8** Then he said, "By which way shall we go up?" Jehoram answered, "By the way of the wilderness of Edom."

**9** So the king of Israel went with the king of Judah and the king of Edom. When they had gone round by a seven-day route, there was no water for the army or for the animals that followed them.

**10** The king of Israel said, "Oh no. The LORD has called these three kings together to hand them over to Moab."

**11** Jehoshaphat said, "Is there no prophet of the LORD here, through whom we can ask the LORD?" One of the servants of the king of Israel answered, "Elisha son of Shaphat is here, who used to pour water on the hands of Elijah."

**12** Jehoshaphat said, "The word of the LORD is with him." So the king of Israel, Jehoshaphat and the king of Edom went down to him.

**13** Elisha said to the king of Israel, "What have you and I in common? Go to the prophets of your father and the prophets of your mother." But the king of Israel said to him, "No. It is the LORD who has called these three kings together to hand them over to Moab."

**14** Elisha said, "As the LORD of hosts lives, before whom I stand, if I did not have regard for Jehoshaphat king of Judah, I would not look at you or see you.

**15** But now bring me someone to play the harp." While the harpist played, the hand of the LORD came on him.

**16** He said, "This is what the LORD says: Make this valley full of ditches.

**17** For this is what the LORD says: You will not see wind or rain, but that valley will be filled with water, and you will drink, you and your cattle and your animals.

**18** This is a small thing in the eyes of the LORD. He will also hand Moab over to you.

**19** You will strike every fortified town and every important town. You will cut down every good tree, stop up every spring of water and ruin every good field with stones."

**20** The next morning, at the time of the offering, water came from the direction of Edom, and the land was filled with water.

**21** When all the Moabites heard that the kings had come up to fight against them, everyone who could put on armour, young and old, was called out and stood at the border.

**22** When they got up early in the morning and the sun shone on the water, the Moabites saw the water opposite them as red as blood.

**23** They said, "That is blood. The kings must have fought each other and struck each other down. Now to the plunder, Moab."

**24** But when they came to the camp of Israel, the Israelites rose up and struck the Moabites, who ran away from them. The Israelites went forward into the land, striking the Moabites as they went.

**25** They pulled down the towns, and each man threw a stone on every good field until it was covered. They stopped every spring and cut down every good tree, until only the stones of Kir-hareseth were left, and the slingers surrounded it and attacked it.

**26** When the king of Moab saw that the battle was going against him, he took seven hundred men with swords to break through to the king of Edom, but they could not.

**27** Then he took his firstborn son, who was to rule in his place, and offered him as a burnt offering on the wall. There was great anger against Israel. So they left him and went back to their own land.

## 2 Kings 4-8: Elisha

### Chapter 4

**1** The wife of one of the company of the prophets cried out to Elisha, "Your servant my husband is dead, and you know that your servant feared the LORD. But now the creditor has come to take my two sons as his slaves."

**2** Elisha said to her, "What can I do for you? Tell me, what do you have in the house?" She said, "Your servant has nothing in the house except a jar of oil."

**3** He said, "Go and borrow vessels from all your neighbours, empty vessels, as many as you can.

**4** Then go in and shut the door behind you and your sons, and pour oil into all those vessels. As each one is full, put it to one side."

**5** So she left him and shut the door behind her and her sons. They brought the vessels to her, and she poured.

**6** When the vessels were full, she said to her son, "Bring me another vessel." He said to her, "There are no more." Then the oil stopped.

**7** She went and told the man of God, and he said, "Go and sell the oil and pay your debt, and you and your sons can live on what is left."

**8** One day Elisha went on to Shunem, where a wealthy woman lived, who urged him to eat some food. So whenever he passed that way, he would turn in there to eat.

**9** She said to her husband, "Look, I know that this man who often passes by us is a holy \[H-QADOSH\] man of God.

**10** Let us make a small room on the roof, with walls, and put a bed there for him, with a table, a chair and a lamp, so that whenever he comes to us he can stay there."

**11** One day he came there, and he went up to the room and lay down there.

**12** He said to his servant Gehazi, "Call this Shunammite woman." He called her, and she stood before him.

**13** He said to him, "Say to her, 'You have gone to all this trouble for us. What can be done for you? Shall a word be put in for you to the king or to the commander of the army?'" She answered, "I live among my own people."

**14** He said, "What then can be done for her?" Gehazi answered, "Well, she has no son, and her husband is old."

**15** He said, "Call her." He called her, and she stood in the doorway.

**16** He said, "At this time next year you will be holding a son." She said, "No, my lord, man of God. Do not lie to your servant."

**17** But the woman conceived, and she bore a son at that time the next year, as Elisha had told her.

**18** When the child was older, he went out one day to his father among the reapers.

**19** He said to his father, "My head, my head." The father said to a servant, "Carry him to his mother."

**20** He picked him up and brought him to his mother, and the child sat on her lap until noon, and then he died.

**21** She went up and laid him on the bed of the man of God, and shut the door behind him and went out.

**22** She called to her husband and said, "Send me one of the servants and one of the donkeys, so that I can hurry to the man of God and come back."

**23** He said, "Why go to him today? It is not the new moon or the sabbath." She said, "It will be all right."

**24** Then she saddled the donkey and said to her servant, "Drive on and do not slow down for me unless I tell you."

**25** So she set out and came to the man of God at Mount Carmel. When the man of God saw her in the distance, he said to his servant Gehazi, "Look, there is the Shunammite.

**26** Run to meet her and ask her, 'Are you well? Is your husband well? Is the child well?'" She answered, "Well."

**27** When she came to the man of God at the mountain, she caught hold of his feet. Gehazi came near to push her away, but the man of God said, "Leave her alone, for she is in bitter distress, and the LORD has hidden it from me and has not told me."

**28** Then she said, "Did I ask my lord for a son? Did I not say, 'Do not deceive me'?"

**29** He said to Gehazi, "Tie up your clothes, take my staff in your hand and go. If you meet anyone, do not greet him, and if anyone greets you, do not answer. Lay my staff on the face of the child."

**30** The child's mother said, "As the LORD lives, and as you live, I will not leave you." So he got up and followed her.

**31** Gehazi went on ahead of them and laid the staff on the face of the child, but there was no sound and no sign of life. So he went back to meet him and told him, "The child has not woken."

**32** When Elisha came into the house, there was the child, lying dead on his bed.

**33** He went in and shut the door on the two of them and prayed to the LORD.

**34** Then he got on the bed and lay on the child, putting his mouth on his mouth, his eyes on his eyes and his hands on his hands. As he stretched himself over him, the child's flesh became warm.

**35** Then he got up and walked once up and down in the house, and went up and stretched himself over him again. The child sneezed seven times, and the child opened his eyes.

**36** Elisha called Gehazi and said, "Call this Shunammite woman." So he called her. When she came to him, he said, "Pick up your son."

**37** She came in and fell at his feet and bowed to the ground. Then she picked up her son and went out.

**38** Elisha went back to Gilgal when there was a famine in the land. While the company of the prophets were sitting in front of him, he said to his servant, "Put the large pot on and make a stew for the company of the prophets."

**39** One of them went out into the field to gather herbs, and found a wild vine and gathered as many wild gourds from it as his cloak would hold, and came and cut them into the pot of stew, not knowing what they were.

**40** They served it for the men to eat. But as they were eating the stew, they cried out, "Man of God, there is death in the pot." And they could not eat it.

**41** He said, "Bring some flour." He threw it into the pot and said, "Serve it for the people to eat." And there was nothing harmful in the pot.

**42** A man came from Baal-shalishah and brought the man of God bread from the first harvest, twenty barley loaves and fresh grain in his sack. Elisha said, "Give it to the people to eat."

**43** But his servant said, "How can I set this in front of a hundred men?" He said again, "Give it to the people to eat, for this is what the LORD says: They will eat and have some left over."

**44** So he set it before them, and they ate and had some left over, as the word of the LORD said.

### Chapter 5

**1** Naaman, commander of the army of the king of Aram, was a great man in his master's eyes and highly respected, because through him the LORD had given victory to Aram. He was a brave warrior, but he had a skin disease \[H-TSARAAT\].

**2** On one of their raids the Arameans had taken captive a young girl from the land of Israel, and she served Naaman's wife.

**3** She said to her mistress, "If only my master were with the prophet who is in Samaria. He would cure him of his skin disease."

**4** So Naaman went in and told his master, "This is what the girl from the land of Israel said."

**5** The king of Aram said, "Go, and I will send a letter to the king of Israel." So he went, taking with him ten talents of silver, six thousand shekels of gold and ten changes of clothes.

**6** He brought the letter to the king of Israel. It read: "When this letter reaches you, know that I have sent my servant Naaman to you, so that you may cure him of his skin disease."

**7** When the king of Israel read the letter, he tore his clothes and said, "Am I God, to kill and to give life, that this man sends to me to cure a man of his skin disease? Just think, and see how he is looking for a quarrel with me."

**8** When Elisha the man of God heard that the king of Israel had torn his clothes, he sent word to the king: "Why have you torn your clothes? Let him come to me, so that he will know that there is a prophet in Israel."

**9** So Naaman came with his horses and chariots and stopped at the door of Elisha's house.

**10** Elisha sent a messenger to him, saying, "Go and wash in the Jordan seven times, and your flesh will be restored and you will be clean."

**11** But Naaman was angry and went away, saying, "I thought he would surely come out to me and stand and call on the name of the LORD his God, and wave his hand over the spot and cure the disease.

**12** Are not Abana and Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? Could I not wash in them and be clean?" So he turned and went away in a rage.

**13** But his servants came near and said to him, "My father, if the prophet had told you to do some great thing, would you not have done it? How much more when he says to you, 'Wash and be clean.'"

**14** So he went down and dipped himself seven times in the Jordan, as the man of God had said, and his flesh was restored like the flesh of a young boy, and he was clean.

**15** Then he went back to the man of God, he and all his company, and stood before him and said, "Now I know that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel. So please accept a gift from your servant."

**16** But he said, "As the LORD lives, before whom I stand, I will not accept anything." He urged him to take it, but he refused.

**17** Then Naaman said, "If not, please let your servant be given as much earth as a pair of mules can carry, for from now on your servant will not offer burnt offering or sacrifice to any god except the LORD.

**18** But may the LORD forgive your servant in this one thing: when my master goes into the temple of Rimmon to worship there and leans on my arm, and I bow down in the temple of Rimmon, may the LORD forgive your servant in this thing."

**19** He said to him, "Go in peace." But when Naaman had gone a short distance from him,

**20** Gehazi, the servant of Elisha the man of God, said to himself, "My master has spared this Aramean Naaman by not accepting what he brought. As the LORD lives, I will run after him and get something from him."

**21** So Gehazi went after Naaman. When Naaman saw someone running after him, he got down from the chariot to meet him and said, "Is everything all right?"

**22** He said, "Everything is all right. My master has sent me to say, 'Two young men of the company of the prophets have just come to me from the hill country of Ephraim. Please give them a talent of silver and two changes of clothes.'"

**23** Naaman said, "Please take two talents." He urged him and tied up two talents of silver in two bags, with two changes of clothes, and gave them to two of his servants, and they carried them ahead of Gehazi.

**24** When he came to the hill, he took them from them and put them in the house, and sent the men away, and they left.

**25** He went in and stood before his master, and Elisha said to him, "Where have you been, Gehazi?" He said, "Your servant has not gone anywhere."

**26** But he said to him, "Did my heart not go with you when the man turned back from his chariot to meet you? Is this the time to accept money and clothes, olive groves and vineyards, sheep and oxen, male and female servants?

**27** So Naaman's skin disease will cling to you and to your descendants for ever." Gehazi left his presence with a skin disease, as white as snow.

### Chapter 6

**1** The company of the prophets said to Elisha, "Look, the place where we live with you is too small for us.

**2** Let us go to the Jordan, and each of us get a beam from there, and let us make a place there for us to live." He said, "Go."

**3** One of them said, "Please come with your servants." He said, "I will come."

**4** So he went with them. When they came to the Jordan, they cut down trees.

**5** While one was felling a beam, the iron axe head fell into the water, and he cried out, "Oh no, my master. It was borrowed."

**6** The man of God said, "Where did it fall?" When he showed him the place, he cut off a stick and threw it in there, and the iron floated.

**7** He said, "Lift it out." So he reached out his hand and took it.

**8** When the king of Aram was at war with Israel, he took counsel with his officers and said, "My camp will be in such and such a place."

**9** But the man of God sent word to the king of Israel: "Be careful not to pass this place, for the Arameans are going down there."

**10** So the king of Israel sent word to the place that the man of God had told him about. He warned him again and again, so that he was on his guard there, not just once or twice.

**11** The king of Aram was very troubled about this, and he called his officers and said to them, "Will you not tell me which of us is on the side of the king of Israel?"

**12** One of his officers said, "No one, my lord the king. But Elisha, the prophet who is in Israel, tells the king of Israel the words that you speak in your bedroom."

**13** He said, "Go and find out where he is, so that I can send and capture him." It was reported to him, "He is in Dothan."

**14** So he sent horses and chariots and a large force there, and they came by night and surrounded the town.

**15** When the servant of the man of God got up early in the morning and went out, there was an army with horses and chariots all round the town. His servant said to him, "Oh no, my master. What shall we do?"

**16** He said, "Do not be afraid, for there are more with us than with them."

**17** Then Elisha prayed, "LORD, please open his eyes, so that he may see." The LORD opened the servant's eyes, and he saw that the hill was full of horses and chariots of fire all round Elisha.

**18** When the Arameans came down towards him, Elisha prayed to the LORD, "Please strike this people with blindness." So he struck them with blindness, as Elisha had asked.

**19** Elisha said to them, "This is not the road, and this is not the town. Follow me, and I will take you to the man you are looking for." And he led them to Samaria.

**20** When they had entered Samaria, Elisha said, "LORD, open the eyes of these men, so that they can see." The LORD opened their eyes, and they saw that they were inside Samaria.

**21** When the king of Israel saw them, he said to Elisha, "My father, shall I strike them down? Shall I strike them down?"

**22** He answered, "You must not strike them down. Do you strike down those you have captured with your own sword and bow? Set bread and water in front of them, so that they can eat and drink and go back to their master."

**23** So he prepared a great feast for them, and when they had eaten and drunk, he sent them away, and they went back to their master. And the Aramean raiding bands did not come again into the land of Israel.

**24** Some time later Ben-hadad king of Aram gathered his whole army and went up and besieged Samaria.

**25** There was a great famine in Samaria during the siege, until a donkey's head was sold for eighty shekels of silver and a quarter of a cab of dove's dung for five shekels of silver.

**26** As the king of Israel was passing by on the wall, a woman cried out to him, "Help, my lord the king."

**27** He said, "If the LORD does not help you, how can I help you? From the threshing floor, or from the winepress?"

**28** Then the king asked her, "What is the matter?" She answered, "This woman said to me, 'Give up your son so that we can eat him today, and we will eat my son tomorrow.'

**29** So we boiled my son and ate him. The next day I said to her, 'Give up your son so that we can eat him.' But she has hidden her son."

**30** When the king heard the woman's words, he tore his clothes. (He was passing by on the wall, and the people looked, and there was sackcloth underneath, next to his skin.)

**31** He said, "May God do this to me, and more also, if the head of Elisha son of Shaphat stays on his shoulders today."

**32** Elisha was sitting in his house, and the elders were sitting with him. The king had sent a man from his presence, but before the messenger arrived, Elisha said to the elders, "Do you see how this murderer's son has sent to cut off my head? Look, when the messenger comes, shut the door and hold it shut against him. Is the sound of his master's feet not behind him?"

**33** While he was still speaking with them, the messenger came down to him, and the king said, "This disaster is from the LORD. Why should I wait for the LORD any longer?"

### Chapter 7

**1** Elisha said, "Listen to the word of the LORD. This is what the LORD says: By this time tomorrow a measure of fine flour will be sold for a shekel, and two measures of barley for a shekel, at the gate of Samaria."

**2** The officer on whose arm the king leaned said to the man of God, "Even if the LORD made windows in the sky, could this happen?" He said, "You will see it with your own eyes, but you will not eat any of it."

**3** Now there were four men with a skin disease \[H-TSARAAT\] at the entrance of the gate. They said to each other, "Why are we sitting here until we die?

**4** If we say, 'Let us go into the town,' the famine is in the town, and we will die there. And if we stay here, we die too. So now, come, let us go over to the camp of the Arameans. If they let us live, we will live, and if they kill us, we will only die."

**5** So they got up at dusk to go to the camp of the Arameans. When they came to the edge of the camp, there was no one there.

**6** For the Lord had made the Aramean army hear the sound of chariots and horses, the sound of a great army, and they said to each other, "The king of Israel has hired the kings of the Hittites and the kings of Egypt to attack us."

**7** So they got up and ran away at dusk and abandoned their tents, their horses and their donkeys, leaving the camp as it was, and ran for their lives.

**10** "This is what you are to say to Hezekiah king of Judah: 'Do not let your God, on whom you rely, deceive you by saying, "Jerusalem will not be handed over to the king of Assyria."

**11** You have heard what the kings of Assyria have done to all the lands, devoting them to destruction \[H-CHEREM\]. And will you be rescued?

**12** Did the gods of the nations rescue them, the nations that my ancestors destroyed: Gozan, Haran, Rezeph, and the people of Eden who were in Tel-assar?

**13** Where is the king of Hamath, the king of Arpad, the king of the town of Sepharvaim, of Hena or of Ivvah?'"

**14** Hezekiah took the letter from the hand of the messengers and read it. Then he went up to the house of the LORD and spread it out before the LORD.

**15** Hezekiah prayed before the LORD: "LORD, God of Israel, enthroned above the cherubim, you alone are God over all the kingdoms of the earth. You made heaven and earth.

**16** Turn your ear, LORD, and hear. Open your eyes, LORD, and see. Hear the words of Sennacherib, which he has sent to mock the living God.

**17** It is true, LORD, that the kings of Assyria have laid waste the nations and their lands,

**18** and have thrown their gods into the fire, for they were not gods, but the work of human hands, wood and stone. So they destroyed them.

**19** Now, LORD our God, save us from his hand, so that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that you, LORD, are God alone."

**20** Then Isaiah son of Amoz sent word to Hezekiah: "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: I have heard your prayer to me about Sennacherib king of Assyria.

**21** This is the word that the LORD has spoken against him:

The virgin daughter of Zion despises you and mocks you. The daughter of Jerusalem shakes her head behind you.

**22** Whom have you mocked and insulted? Against whom have you raised your voice and lifted your eyes so high? Against the Holy One \[H-QADOSH\] of Israel.

**23** Through your messengers you have mocked the Lord. You have said, 'With my many chariots I have gone up the heights of the mountains, to the far reaches of Lebanon. I have cut down its tallest cedars, its finest cypresses. I have reached its farthest lodging, its thickest forest.

**24** I dug wells and drank foreign water, and with the soles of my feet I dried up all the streams of Egypt.'

**25** Have you not heard? Long ago I did it. In days of old I planned it, and now I have brought it about, that you should turn fortified towns into heaps of ruins.

**26** Their people had little strength. They were dismayed and ashamed. They were like plants in the field, like green grass, like grass on the roofs, scorched before it grows up.

**27** I know where you sit, and when you go out and come in, and how you rage against me.

**28** Because you rage against me, and your arrogance has come to my ears, I will put my hook in your nose and my bit in your mouth, and I will make you go back by the road you came on.

**29** This will be the sign for you: this year you will eat what grows by itself, and in the second year what springs up from that. But in the third year sow and reap, and plant vineyards and eat their fruit.

**30** The surviving remnant of the house of Judah will again take root downwards and bear fruit upwards.

**31** For a remnant will go out from Jerusalem, and survivors from Mount Zion. The zeal \[H-QANNA\] of the LORD will do this.

**32** So this is what the LORD says about the king of Assyria: He will not come into this town or shoot an arrow here. He will not come before it with a shield or build a siege ramp against it.

**33** He will go back by the way he came, and he will not come into this town, declares the LORD.

**34** I will defend this town and save it, for my own sake and for the sake of my servant David."

**35** That night the angel of the LORD went out and struck down a hundred and eighty-five thousand in the Assyrian camp. When the people got up early in the morning, there were all the dead bodies.

**36** So Sennacherib king of Assyria left and went back and stayed at Nineveh.

**37** One day, as he was worshipping in the temple of his god Nisroch, his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer struck him down with the sword. They escaped to the land of Ararat. And his son Esarhaddon became king in his place.

### Chapter 20

**1** In those days Hezekiah became ill and was close to death. The prophet Isaiah son of Amoz came to him and said, "This is what the LORD says: Put your house in order, for you will die. You will not recover."

**2** Then he turned his face to the wall and prayed to the LORD:

**3** "Please, LORD, remember how I have walked before you in faithfulness and with a whole heart, and have done what is good in your eyes." And Hezekiah wept bitterly.

**4** Before Isaiah had left the middle court, the word of the LORD came to him:

**5** "Go back and say to Hezekiah, the leader of my people: This is what the LORD, the God of your ancestor David, says: I have heard your prayer. I have seen your tears. Look, I will heal you. On the third day you will go up to the house of the LORD.

**6** I will add fifteen years to your life. I will rescue you and this town from the hand of the king of Assyria, and I will defend this town for my own sake and for the sake of my servant David."

**7** Isaiah said, "Bring a cake of figs." They brought it and put it on the boil, and he recovered.

**8** Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "What will be the sign that the LORD will heal me, and that I will go up to the house of the LORD on the third day?"

**9** Isaiah said, "This will be the sign for you from the LORD that the LORD will do what he has said: Shall the shadow go forward ten steps, or go back ten steps?"

**10** Hezekiah said, "It is easy for the shadow to lengthen by ten steps. No, let the shadow go back ten steps."

**11** The prophet Isaiah called on the LORD, and he made the shadow go back the ten steps that it had gone down on the steps of Ahaz.

**12** At that time Merodach-baladan son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a gift to Hezekiah, for he had heard that Hezekiah had been ill.

**13** Hezekiah welcomed them, and showed them all his treasury, the silver, the gold, the spices, the fine oil, his whole armoury and everything found in his storehouses. There was nothing in his palace or in all his kingdom that Hezekiah did not show them.

**14** Then the prophet Isaiah came to King Hezekiah and said to him, "What did these men say, and where did they come from?" Hezekiah said, "They came from a far country, from Babylon."

**15** He said, "What did they see in your palace?" Hezekiah answered, "They saw everything in my palace. There is nothing in my storehouses that I did not show them."

**16** Then Isaiah said to Hezekiah, "Hear the word of the LORD:

**17** The days are coming when everything in your palace, and all that your ancestors have stored up until today, will be carried off to Babylon. Nothing will be left, says the LORD.

**18** Some of your own descendants, who come from you, whom you father, will be taken away, and they will be officials in the palace of the king of Babylon."

**19** Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "The word of the LORD that you have spoken is good." For he thought, "Why not, if there will be peace and security in my days?"

**20** The rest of the acts of Hezekiah and all his power, and how he made the pool and the channel and brought water into the town, are they not written in the Book of the Annals of the Kings of Judah?

**21** Hezekiah lay down with his ancestors, and his son Manasseh became king in his place.

## 2 Kings 21-25: The End of Judah

### Chapter 21

**1** Manasseh was twelve years old when he became king, and he ruled for fifty-five years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Hephzibah.

**2** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, following the detestable practices of the nations whom the LORD had driven out before the Israelites.

**3** He rebuilt the high places that his father Hezekiah had destroyed, and set up altars for Baal and made an Asherah pole, as Ahab king of Israel had done, and he bowed down to all the host of heaven and served them.

**4** He built altars in the house of the LORD, of which the LORD had said, "In Jerusalem I will put my name."

**5** He built altars for all the host of heaven in the two courts of the house of the LORD.

**6** He made his son pass through the fire and practised soothsaying and divination and dealt with mediums and spirit-consulters. He did great evil in the eyes of the LORD, making him angry.

**7** He put the carved image of Asherah that he had made in the house of which the LORD had said to David and to his son Solomon, "In this house and in Jerusalem, which I have chosen from all the tribes of Israel, I will put my name for ever.

**8** I will not again make the feet of the Israelites wander from the land that I gave to their ancestors, if only they take care to do all that I have commanded them, and all the law that my servant Moses commanded them."

**9** But they did not listen, and Manasseh led them astray to do more evil than the nations that the LORD had destroyed before the Israelites.

**10** The LORD spoke through his servants the prophets:

**11** "Because Manasseh king of Judah has done these detestable things, and has done more evil than all that the Amorites did before him, and has made Judah sin with his idols,

**12** this is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: I am bringing such disaster on Jerusalem and Judah that the ears of everyone who hears of it will tingle.

**13** I will stretch over Jerusalem the measuring line of Samaria and the plumb line of the house of Ahab, and I will wipe Jerusalem as one wipes a dish, wiping it and turning it upside down.

**14** I will abandon the remnant of my inheritance and hand them over to their enemies, and they will become plunder and spoil for all their enemies,

**15** because they have done what is evil in my eyes and have made me angry from the day their ancestors came out of Egypt until today."

**16** Manasseh also shed a great deal of innocent blood, until he had filled Jerusalem from one end to the other, besides the sin that he made Judah commit, so that they did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.

**17** The rest of the acts of Manasseh and all that he did, and the sin that he committed, are they not written in the Book of the Annals of the Kings of Judah?

**18** Manasseh lay down with his ancestors and was buried in the garden of his house, the garden of Uzza, and his son Amon became king in his place.

**19** Amon was twenty-two years old when he became king, and he ruled for two years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Meshullemeth daughter of Haruz of Jotbah.

**20** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, as his father Manasseh had done.

**21** He walked in all the way that his father had walked, and served the idols that his father had served and bowed down to them.

**22** He abandoned the LORD, the God of his ancestors, and did not walk in the way of the LORD.

**23** The servants of Amon plotted against him and killed the king in his palace.

**24** But the people of the land struck down all those who had plotted against King Amon, and the people of the land made his son Josiah king in his place.

**25** The rest of the acts of Amon that he did, are they not written in the Book of the Annals of the Kings of Judah?

**26** He was buried in his tomb in the garden of Uzza, and his son Josiah became king in his place.

### Chapter 22

**1** Josiah was eight years old when he became king, and he ruled for thirty-one years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Jedidah daughter of Adaiah of Bozkath.

**2** He did what was right in the eyes of the LORD and walked in all the way of his ancestor David, and did not turn aside to the right or to the left.

**3** In the eighteenth year of King Josiah, the king sent Shaphan son of Azaliah, son of Meshullam, the secretary, to the house of the LORD with this order:

**4** "Go up to Hilkiah the high priest and have him count the money that has been brought into the house of the LORD, which the keepers of the threshold have collected from the people.

**5** Let it be handed to those in charge of the work, who supervise the house of the LORD, and let them pay it to the workers who are at the house of the LORD, repairing the damage to the house,

**6** that is, to the carpenters, the builders and the masons, and for buying timber and cut stone to repair the house.

**7** But no account is to be asked of them for the money that is handed to them, for they act honestly."

**8** Hilkiah the high priest said to Shaphan the secretary, "I have found the Book of the Law in the house of the LORD." Hilkiah gave the book to Shaphan, and he read it.

**9** Shaphan the secretary came to the king and reported to the king, "Your servants have poured out the money that was found in the house and have handed it to those in charge of the work, who supervise the house of the LORD."

**10** Then Shaphan the secretary told the king, "Hilkiah the priest has given me a book." And Shaphan read it in the presence of the king.

**11** When the king heard the words of the Book of the Law, he tore his clothes.

**12** The king commanded Hilkiah the priest, Ahikam son of Shaphan, Achbor son of Micaiah, Shaphan the secretary and Asaiah the king's servant:

**13** "Go and ask the LORD for me, and for the people and for all Judah, about the words of this book that has been found. For great is the anger of the LORD that has been kindled against us, because our ancestors did not listen to the words of this book and do all that is written about us."

**14** So Hilkiah the priest, Ahikam, Achbor, Shaphan and Asaiah went to Huldah the prophet \[H-NAVI\], the wife of Shallum son of Tikvah, son of Harhas, keeper of the wardrobe. She lived in Jerusalem, in the Second Quarter, and they spoke with her.

**15** She said to them, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Tell the man who sent you to me:

**16** This is what the LORD says: I am going to bring disaster on this place and on those who live here, all the words of the book that the king of Judah has read.

**17** Because they have abandoned me and burned incense to other gods, to make me angry with all the work of their hands, my anger will be kindled against this place, and it will not be put out.

**18** But to the king of Judah, who sent you to ask the LORD, say this: This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: As for the words that you have heard,

**19** because your heart was soft and you humbled yourself before the LORD when you heard what I spoke against this place and those who live here, that they would become a desolation and a curse, and because you tore your clothes and wept before me, I have heard you, declares the LORD.

**20** So I will gather you to your ancestors, and you will be gathered to your grave in peace, and your eyes will not see all the disaster that I am going to bring on this place." They took the word back to the king.

### Chapter 23

**1** Then the king sent word, and all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem were gathered to him.

**2** The king went up to the house of the LORD, and with him all the men of Judah and all who lived in Jerusalem, the priests and the prophets, and all the people, small and great. He read in their hearing all the words of the Book of the Covenant \[H-BERIT\] that had been found in the house of the LORD.

**3** The king stood by the pillar and made a covenant before the LORD, to follow the LORD and to keep his commands, his testimonies and his statutes with all his heart and all his soul, and to carry out the words of this covenant that were written in this book. All the people joined in the covenant.

**4** The king commanded Hilkiah the high priest and the priests of the second rank and the keepers of the threshold to bring out of the temple of the LORD all the objects that had been made for Baal, for Asherah and for all the host of heaven. He burned them outside Jerusalem in the fields of the Kidron and carried their ashes to Bethel.

**5** He removed the priests of the idols whom the kings of Judah had appointed to burn incense on the high places in the towns of Judah and round Jerusalem, and those who burned incense to Baal, to the sun and the moon and the constellations and all the host of heaven.

**6** He brought the Asherah pole out of the house of the LORD to the Wadi Kidron, outside Jerusalem, and burned it at the Wadi Kidron and ground it to dust and threw the dust on the graves of the common people.

**7** He pulled down the houses of the male shrine prostitutes that were in the house of the LORD, where the women wove hangings for the Asherah.

**8** He brought all the priests from the towns of Judah and made unclean the high places where the priests had burned incense, from Geba to Beersheba. He pulled down the high places of the gates that were at the entrance of the Gate of Joshua the governor of the town, which was on the left of the town gate.

**9** The priests of the high places, however, did not come up to the altar of the LORD in Jerusalem, but ate unleavened bread among their fellow priests.

**10** He made Topheth unclean, which is in the Valley of Ben-hinnom, so that no one could make his son or daughter pass through the fire to Molech.

**11** He removed the horses that the kings of Judah had dedicated to the sun, at the entrance to the house of the LORD, by the room of the official Nathan-melech, which was in the precincts. And he burned the chariots of the sun with fire.

**12** The altars on the roof of the upper room of Ahaz, which the kings of Judah had made, and the altars that Manasseh had made in the two courts of the house of the LORD, the king pulled down and smashed to pieces there, and threw the dust into the Wadi Kidron.

**13** The king made unclean the high places that were east of Jerusalem, to the south of the Mount of Destruction, which Solomon king of Israel had built for Ashtoreth the detestable goddess of the Sidonians, for Chemosh the detestable god of Moab and for Milcom the detestable god of the Ammonites.

**14** He smashed the sacred pillars and cut down the Asherah poles and filled their places with human bones.

**15** He also pulled down the altar at Bethel, the high place made by Jeroboam son of Nebat, who made Israel sin. He burned the high place and ground it to dust, and burned the Asherah pole.

**16** As Josiah turned, he saw the tombs that were there on the hill. He sent and took the bones out of the tombs and burned them on the altar and made it unclean, as the word of the LORD said, which the man of God had proclaimed, who had foretold these things.

**17** Then he said, "What is that monument that I see?" The men of the town told him, "It is the tomb of the man of God who came from Judah and foretold these things that you have done to the altar at Bethel."

**18** He said, "Leave him alone. Let no one move his bones." So they left his bones alone, with the bones of the prophet who came from Samaria.

**19** Josiah also removed all the shrines of the high places in the towns of Samaria that the kings of Israel had made, making the LORD angry. He did to them just as he had done at Bethel.

**20** He killed on the altars all the priests of the high places who were there, and burned human bones on them. Then he went back to Jerusalem.

**21** The king commanded all the people, "Keep the Passover to the LORD your God, as it is written in this Book of the Covenant."

**22** No Passover like it had been kept since the days of the judges who judged Israel, or in all the days of the kings of Israel and the kings of Judah.

**23** But in the eighteenth year of King Josiah, this Passover was kept to the LORD in Jerusalem.

**24** Josiah also removed the mediums, the spirit-consulters, the household gods, the idols, and all the detestable things that were seen in the land of Judah and in Jerusalem, so as to carry out the words of the law that were written in the book that the priest Hilkiah found in the house of the LORD.

**25** Before him there was no king like him, who turned back \[H-SHUV\] to the LORD with all his heart and all his soul and all his strength, in keeping with all the law of Moses. And after him no one like him arose.

**26** Even so, the LORD did not turn from the heat of his great anger, which had been kindled against Judah because of all that Manasseh had done to make him angry.

**27** The LORD said, "I will remove Judah also from my presence, as I removed Israel. I will reject this town that I chose, Jerusalem, and the house of which I said, 'My name will be there.'"

**28** The rest of the acts of Josiah and all that he did, are they not written in the Book of the Annals of the Kings of Judah?

**29** In his days Pharaoh Neco king of Egypt went up to the king of Assyria at the River Euphrates. King Josiah went to meet him, and Pharaoh Neco killed him at Megiddo when he saw him.

**30** His servants carried his body in a chariot from Megiddo and brought him to Jerusalem and buried him in his own tomb. The people of the land took Jehoahaz son of Josiah and anointed him and made him king in his father's place.

**31** Jehoahaz was twenty-three years old when he became king, and he ruled for three months in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Hamutal daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah.

**32** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, as his ancestors had done.

**33** Pharaoh Neco put him in chains at Riblah in the land of Hamath, so that he could not rule in Jerusalem, and imposed on the land a tribute of a hundred talents of silver and a talent of gold.

**34** Pharaoh Neco made Eliakim son of Josiah king in place of his father Josiah and changed his name to Jehoiakim. But he took Jehoahaz away, and he went to Egypt and died there.

**35** Jehoiakim gave the silver and gold to Pharaoh, but he taxed the land to pay the money at Pharaoh's command. He collected the silver and gold from the people of the land, from each according to his assessment, to give to Pharaoh Neco.

**36** Jehoiakim was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he ruled for eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Zebidah daughter of Pedaiah of Rumah.

**37** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, as his ancestors had done.

### Chapter 24

**5** The rest of the acts of Jehoiakim and all that he did, are they not written in the Book of the Annals of the Kings of Judah?

**6** Jehoiakim lay down with his ancestors, and his son Jehoiachin became king in his place.

**10** At that time the servants of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up to Jerusalem, and the town was besieged.

**15** He took Jehoiachin into exile to Babylon. He took the king's mother, the king's wives, his officials and the leading men of the land into exile from Jerusalem to Babylon.

**19** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, as Jehoiakim had done.

**20** It was because of the anger of the LORD that this happened to Jerusalem and Judah, until he threw them out from his presence. And Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.

### Chapter 25

**1** In the ninth year of his reign, on the tenth day of the tenth month, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came with all his army against Jerusalem and camped against it and built siege works all round it.

**2** The town was under siege until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah.

**3** On the ninth day of the fourth month the famine in the town was so severe that there was no food for the people of the land.

**4** Then the town wall was broken through, and all the soldiers fled by night by way of the gate between the two walls, by the king's garden, though the Chaldeans were round the town. They went in the direction of the Arabah.

**5** But the Chaldean army chased the king and overtook him in the plains of Jericho, and all his army was scattered from him.

**6** They captured the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon at Riblah, and they passed judgement on him.

**7** They killed the sons of Zedekiah in front of his eyes. Then they put out the eyes of Zedekiah and bound him in bronze chains and took him to Babylon.

**8** On the seventh day of the fifth month, which was the nineteenth year of King Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, a servant of the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem.

**10** The whole Chaldean army that was with the captain of the guard broke down the walls round Jerusalem.

**13** The Chaldeans broke up the bronze pillars that were in the house of the LORD, and the stands and the bronze sea that were in the house of the LORD, and carried the bronze to Babylon.

**14** They also took the pots, the shovels, the snuffers, the dishes for incense and all the bronze vessels used in the service of the temple.

**15** The captain of the guard took away the fire pans and the bowls, whatever was of gold or of silver.

**16** As for the two pillars, the one sea and the stands that Solomon had made for the house of the LORD, the bronze of all these vessels was too much to weigh.

**17** One pillar was eighteen cubits high, and it had a bronze capital on top. The capital was three cubits high, with a network and pomegranates all round the capital, all of bronze. The second pillar was like it, with a network.

**18** The captain of the guard took Seraiah the chief priest and Zephaniah the second priest and the three keepers of the threshold.

**21** The king of Babylon struck them down and put them to death at Riblah in the land of Hamath. So Judah went into exile from its land.

**22** Over the people who were left in the land of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had left, he appointed Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan, as governor.

**23** When all the army commanders and their men heard that the king of Babylon had appointed Gedaliah as governor, they came to Gedaliah at Mizpah, Ishmael son of Nethaniah, Johanan son of Kareah, Seraiah son of Tanhumeth the Netophathite, and Jaazaniah son of the Maacathite, with their men.

**24** Gedaliah swore an oath to them and their men: "Do not be afraid of the Chaldean officials. Live in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and it will go well with you."

**25** But in the seventh month Ishmael son of Nethaniah, son of Elishama, of the royal family, came with ten men and struck down Gedaliah and killed him, with the Judeans and Chaldeans who were with him at Mizpah.

**26** Then all the people, small and great, and the army commanders, got up and went to Egypt, for they were afraid of the Chaldeans.

**27** In the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah, on the twenty-seventh day of the twelfth month, Evil-merodach king of Babylon, in the year he became king, released Jehoiachin king of Judah from prison.

**28** He spoke kindly to him and gave him a seat above the seats of the other kings who were with him in Babylon.

**29** So Jehoiachin took off his prison clothes and ate at the king's table every day of his life.

**30** For his allowance, a regular allowance was given to him by the king, a portion every day, as long as he lived.

*End of the book of 2 Kings, and of the books from Joshua to 2 Kings. Review note: 2 Kings 21 to 25 was drafted in WABV wording. These chapters tell of Manasseh's sins (including making his son pass through the fire and shedding innocent blood), Josiah's finding of the book of the law and his reform (including the killing of the priests of the high places in Samaria, 23:20), the death of Josiah at Megiddo, the fall of Jerusalem, the blinding of Zedekiah after the killing of his sons (25:7), the burning of the temple, the exile, the murder of Gedaliah, and the release of Jehoiachin. These are rendered as written. The Hebrew of chapter 25 is closely parallel to Jeremiah 39 and 52, and the wording of the parallels in the WABV Jeremiah will follow the wording used here. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Translation Footnotes

*Footnotes sit here, not in the reading text. They are listed by chapter and verse.*

- **Title** 2 Kings continues 1 Kings, and the two were one book in the Hebrew Bible. It follows the kings of Israel and Judah to the fall of Samaria and then of Jerusalem. It names its sources, such as "the Book of the Annals of the Kings of Israel" and "of Judah". The Hebrew text differs in places from the Greek translation, and the footnotes record the main differences.
- **1:1** "Moab rebelled" is also mentioned in 3:5. The inscription of King Mesha of Moab, found in 1868, tells the story from Moab's side.
- **1:2** "Baal-zebub" means "lord of flies" and is probably a mocking form of "Baal-zebul" ("lord of the princely house"). Ekron was a Philistine city.
- **1:3** "Is it because there is no God in Israel?" is a rhetorical question. It is repeated in verses 6 and 16.
- **1:8** "A garment of hair" is the Hebrew phrase *ba'al se'ar*, which can also mean "a hairy man". The WABV text takes it as a garment, as in Zechariah 13:4.
- **1:10** The fire from heaven that burns the first captain and his fifty is rendered as written. "If I am a man of God" is a test, and the fire answers it. The same happens to the second captain (verse 12). The third captain begs for his life and is spared (verses 13 to 15).
- **1:17** "The second year of Jehoram son of Jehoshaphat" conflicts with 3:1, which says the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat. The two synchronisms of the kings do not fit, and some Greek manuscripts have the second in 1:18. The Hebrew is followed in both places.
- **2:1** "A whirlwind" is Hebrew *se'arah*, a storm wind. "Gilgal" is probably not the Gilgal near Jericho but a place near Bethel.
- **2:3** "The company of the prophets" is Hebrew "sons of the prophets", bands of prophets at Bethel, Jericho and Gilgal. They know that Elijah will be taken.
- **2:8** Elijah's cloak, rolled up, divides the Jordan, as the water was divided in Exodus 14 and Joshua 3.
- **2:9** "A double share" is Hebrew *pi shenayim*, the share of the firstborn son (Deuteronomy 21:17). "Spirit" is *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH. Elisha asks to be Elijah's heir.
- **2:11** "A chariot of fire and horses of fire" separate the two men. Elijah goes up "in a whirlwind", not in the chariot.
- **2:12** "My father, my father, the chariots of Israel and its horsemen" is Elisha's cry. The same words are said of Elisha himself in 13:14. They honour the prophet as the defender of Israel.
- **2:14** "Where is the LORD, the God of Elijah?" is followed in the Hebrew by words (*aph hu*) that are hard to read and may mean "even he" or "surely he". The WABV text keeps the main question.
- **2:16** "The spirit of the LORD" is Hebrew *ruach YHWH*. See entry H-RUACH. The men think the spirit may have thrown Elijah down on a mountain.
- **2:19** "The water is bad" is the town's complaint, and the cure follows in verses 20 to 22.
- **2:21** "Neither death nor miscarriage" translates a Hebrew word (*meshakkelet*) that means causing childlessness or loss of young.
- **2:23** "Small boys" is Hebrew *ne'arim qetannim*, a phrase that can mean boys or young men. "Baldhead" is Hebrew *qereach*. The jeer "Go up" may mock Elijah's going up to heaven.
- **2:24** "He cursed them in the name of the LORD" and the two bears that maul forty-two of them is rendered as written. The Hebrew gives no further comment. The number forty-two recurs in 10:14.
- **3:1** "The eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat" differs from 1:17. Jehoram of Israel is the son of Ahab.
- **3:2** "The sacred pillar of Baal" is the one Ahab set up (1 Kings 16:32). Jehoram removes it, but not the calves.
- **3:4** "Sheep breeder" is Hebrew *noqed*, a word used also for the prophet Amos (Amos 1:1).
- **3:8** The route through Edom is possible because Edom is under Judah (1 Kings 22:47).
- **3:11** "Who used to pour water on the hands of Elijah" says that Elisha was Elijah's servant (1 Kings 19:21).
- **3:13** "The prophets of your father and the prophets of your mother" refers to the prophets of Baal and Asherah, the court prophets of Ahab and Jezebel.
- **3:15** "Someone to play the harp" is a musician. The music prepares for the prophetic word, as in 1 Samuel 10:5.
- **3:19** "You will cut down every good tree" and "stop up every spring" is the command of a scorched-earth campaign. It is against Deuteronomy 20:19-20, which forbids cutting down fruit trees in a siege. The text does not comment.
- **3:22** "As red as blood" is the sun on the water, and perhaps the red soil of Edom.
- **3:25** "Kir-hareseth" is the fortress of Moab, the modern Kerak.
- **3:27** "He offered him as a burnt offering on the wall" is rendered as written. "Great anger against Israel" is Hebrew *qetsef gadol*. The text does not say whose anger it is. It may be the anger of Moab's god, or the horror of the Israelites, or the LORD's, and the Israelites withdraw.
- **4:1** "To take my two sons as his slaves" refers to the custom of debt slavery (see Leviticus 25:39-41 and Nehemiah 5:1-5). The woman is the widow of one of the company of the prophets.
- **4:8** "A wealthy woman" is Hebrew *ishah gedolah*, "a great woman". Shunem is in the plain of Jezreel.
- **4:9** "A holy man of God" uses *qadosh*. See entry H-QADOSH. She sees that the visiting prophet is set apart for the LORD.
- **4:13** "Shall a word be put in for you to the king?" offers the woman a patron at court. She says she needs none, since she lives among her own people.
- **4:16** "Do not lie to your servant" is the woman's disbelief, since her husband is old (verse 14).
- **4:19** "My head, my head" may be the sign of a sunstroke or a fever. The child dies at noon (verse 20).
- **4:23** "It is neither new moon nor sabbath" shows that people visited a prophet on those days (see Amos 8:5). "All is well" is Hebrew *shalom*. See entry H-SHALOM.
- **4:27** "The LORD has hidden it from me" says that a prophet does not know everything.
- **4:29** Gehazi is sent with Elisha's staff, but it does not work (verse 31). The raising of the child comes only when Elisha himself comes.
- **4:34** "Lay on the child" is the same act as Elijah's in 1 Kings 17:21. The child "sneezed seven times" is the sign of life.
- **4:39** "Wild gourds" are probably the bitter colocynth, which is poisonous.
- **4:42** "Baal-shalishah" is a town in Ephraim. The bread of the first harvest was offered to the LORD (Leviticus 23:17), and here it is given to the prophet and his company.
- **4:43** "They will eat and have some left over" is the same as in the feeding of the crowd in Matthew 14:13-21 and John 6:9, where the loaves are also barley.
- **5:1** "A skin disease" is Hebrew *tsara'at*. See entry H-TSARAAT. The word covers a range of skin conditions and is not the same as the modern disease called leprosy. "Through him the LORD had given victory to Aram" says that the LORD was also at work for a foreign general.
- **5:2** "A young girl" was taken captive in a raid. She is not named, and she tells of the prophet in Samaria.
- **5:5** "Ten talents of silver, six thousand shekels of gold and ten changes of clothes" is a very rich gift.
- **5:7** "Am I God, to kill and to give life?" echoes Deuteronomy 32:39 and 1 Samuel 2:6.
- **5:10** Elisha does not come out but sends a messenger. This is what offends Naaman.
- **5:12** "Abana and Pharpar" are the rivers of Damascus.
- **5:14** "Like the flesh of a young boy" says his skin was healed completely.
- **5:17** "As much earth as a pair of mules can carry" is Israelite soil on which to worship the LORD in Aram.
- **5:18** "The temple of Rimmon" is the temple of the Aramean god Hadad-Rimmon. Naaman asks to be pardoned when he must bow down at his master's side. Elisha says, "Go in peace", and does not answer further.
- **5:20** Gehazi's wish to get something from Naaman is greed. He uses the name of the LORD in an oath.
- **5:26** "Did my heart not go with you?" says that Elisha knew by the spirit what Gehazi did. The list of things Gehazi could now buy is a list of what he wanted.
- **5:27** "As white as snow" is the sign of the skin disease also in Exodus 4:6 and Numbers 12:10. The skin disease passes from Naaman to Gehazi and his descendants.
- **6:1** "The place where we live with you" is the house of the company of the prophets, which has become too small.
- **6:5** "It was borrowed" says that the axe head was not his own. Elisha makes the iron float, as a sign of care for a small matter.
- **6:8** The Hebrew of the king's words, "My camp will be in such and such a place", is hard to read. It may mean "my camp in a hidden place" or "my camp at such and such a place".
- **6:13** "Dothan" is a town north of Samaria.
- **6:17** "Horses and chariots of fire all round Elisha" is the sign of the LORD's armies, as in 2:11.
- **6:18** "Blindness" is Hebrew *sanverim*, a rare word for a confusing or dazzling blindness, as in Genesis 19:11.
- **6:19** Elisha tells the soldiers, "This is not the road", and leads them into Samaria. He deceives them, and then the LORD opens their eyes.
- **6:22** "Those you have captured with your own sword and bow" is the Hebrew reading. The question is whether the king kills prisoners of war. Elisha tells him to feed them and send them home.
- **6:25** "Dove's dung" is Hebrew *charey yonim*. It may be a plant that was eaten in famine, or literal dung. The prices show how severe the famine was.
- **6:28** The two women who agreed to eat their sons are told as written. The story is told in the siege-curses of Leviticus 26:29 and Deuteronomy 28:53-57, and in Lamentations 4:10.
- **6:30** "Sackcloth underneath" shows that the king was already in mourning.
- **6:31** "May God do this to me, and more also" is the oath formula. The king blames Elisha for the disaster.
- **6:32** "This murderer's son" is Elisha's term for the king, the son of Ahab, who had Naboth killed (1 Kings 21).
- **6:33** The Hebrew does not say who speaks. The speaker is the messenger in the Hebrew text, and the king in some readings. The WABV text takes it as the king, who has come down. "This disaster is from the LORD" is the king's complaint.
- **7:1** "A measure" is Hebrew *seah*, about seven litres. The price is of normal times.
- **7:2** "Windows in the sky" is a figure from the flood (Genesis 7:11) and for blessing (Malachi 3:10). The officer's doubt is punished in verses 17 to 20.
- **7:3** "Four men with a skin disease" sat outside the gate, as the law required (Leviticus 13:46). They find the Aramean camp empty.
- **7:6** "The Lord" is Hebrew *Adonai*. "The kings of the Hittites" are the small Syrian states. The Arameans hear a sound of a great army and flee.
- **7:12** The king thinks the empty camp is a trap, as in Joshua 8.
- **7:13** The officer's advice about the horses is hard to read in the Hebrew. The meaning is that those left are already doomed, so little is lost by sending them to look.
- **8:1** "Live as a foreigner" is Hebrew *gur*, to live as a resident alien. The seven-year famine is the LORD's.
- **8:4** Gehazi is talking with the king here, although in 5:27 he was struck with a skin disease. The stories may not be in order of time.
- **8:6** "An official" is Hebrew *saris*, a court officer. The king returns the woman's land and its income.
- **8:9** "Forty camel loads" is a very large gift.
- **8:10** "You will certainly recover" follows the reading tradition (*lo*, "to him"). The written text has *lo*, "not", which would read "You will certainly not recover". The WABV text follows the reading tradition, which fits Hazael's report in verse 14.
- **8:11** "He fixed his gaze and stared" does not say whose gaze it is. It may be Elisha's, or Hazael's. Elisha then weeps.
- **8:12** "The harm you will do" is a prediction of the atrocities of Hazael's wars (see 10:32-33; 13:3, 22; Amos 1:3-5). "Smash their little children and rip open their pregnant women" is rendered as written.
- **8:13** "A mere dog" is a figure of insignificance.
- **8:15** "He took the blanket" does not name the subject. It is probably Hazael, who kills Ben-hadad and becomes king.
- **8:16** "Jehoshaphat being king of Judah" is in the Hebrew but not in some manuscripts. The dates of Jehoram of Judah and of Israel are hard to fit together.
- **8:18** "He had married a daughter of Ahab" is Athaliah. She is said in verse 26 to be a "granddaughter of Omri". The Hebrew word for "daughter" (*bat*) can also mean a granddaughter.
- **8:19** "A lamp" is a figure for the continuing line of David (see 1 Kings 11:36).
- **8:21** "Zair" is a place that is not known. It may be Zoar.
- **8:22** "To this day" is the writer's note that Edom did not return to Judah's rule. Libnah was a town in Judah's lowland.
- **8:26** "Twenty-two years old" is the Hebrew reading here. 2 Chronicles 22:2 has forty-two in the Hebrew, which cannot be right, since his father died at forty.
- **8:28** "Ramoth-gilead" is the town that Ahab and Jehoshaphat fought for (1 Kings 22). "Ramah" in verse 29 is a short form of the same name.
- **9:1** Elisha sends one of the company of the prophets to anoint Jehu. This carries out the command given to Elijah in 1 Kings 19:16. The oil is poured in an inner room, and the young man runs away as he was told.
- **9:7** "The blood of my servants the prophets" refers to Jezebel's killing of the LORD's prophets (1 Kings 18:4, 13). The word is that the house of Ahab will be struck down.
- **9:8** "Every male, slave and free" repeats the idioms of 1 Kings 14:10 and 21:21.
- **9:11** "This madman" is how the officers describe the young prophet. The Hebrew word (*meshugga'*) is used in a scornful way of prophets.
- **9:13** The officers spread their cloaks on the bare steps and blow the trumpet. This is the acclamation of a king.
- **9:17** "Is it peace?" is Hebrew *shalom*. See entry H-SHALOM. Jehu's reply, "What have you to do with peace?", is repeated in verses 18 to 22.
- **9:20** "Like a madman" is Hebrew *bashiggaon*, with the same root as the word in verse 11.
- **9:22** "Prostitution and witchcraft" are the standard terms for unfaithfulness to the LORD and for worshipping other gods (see Nahum 3:4).
- **9:24** Jehu shoots Joram himself with a bow. The arrow goes through his heart.
- **9:25** "Bidkar" is Jehu's officer. His words recall the word of the LORD to Ahab in 1 Kings 21:19.
- **9:26** "The blood of Naboth and the blood of his sons" is not in the account of 1 Kings 21, where only Naboth's death is told. The sons may have been killed too.
- **9:27** The death of Ahaziah is told in several ways. Here he is shot at the ascent of Gur and dies at Megiddo. In 2 Chronicles 22:9 he is found hiding in Samaria and put to death by Jehu's order.
- **9:29** "The eleventh year of Joram" differs from 8:25, which has the twelfth. Some Greek manuscripts have the eleventh in both.
- **9:30** "She painted her eyes" means she used eye paint. Jezebel makes herself ready to meet Jehu as a queen.
- **9:31** "You Zimri" refers to the man who killed his master Elah and reigned seven days (1 Kings 16:8-20).
- **9:33** "Officials" are Hebrew *sarisim*, court officers. They throw Jezebel from the window at Jehu's order, and he tramples her.
- **9:34** "A king's daughter" says that Jezebel was a princess of Tyre. Jehu orders her burial and then learns that she cannot be buried.
- **9:36** "In the plot of Jezreel" is the Hebrew reading (*cheleq*), and 1 Kings 21:23 has "by the wall" (*chel*) in the Hebrew. The fulfilment of Elijah's word is marked here.
- **10:1** "Seventy sons" are the descendants of Ahab, in the care of the leading men of Samaria. The text says "sons", and the word can include grandsons.
- **10:4** "Two kings could not stand against him" shows the fear of the rulers of Samaria. They submit to Jehu.
- **10:7** The killing of the seventy and the sending of their heads in baskets is rendered as written. The heads are laid in two heaps at the gate (verse 8).
- **10:9** "You are innocent" is Jehu's claim to the people. He says that he alone killed Joram, and that the rest was done by the rulers.
- **10:14** "Forty-two men" are the relatives of Ahaziah. The same number is mauled in 2:24. "The pit of Beth-eked" is a cistern.
- **10:15** "Jehonadab son of Rechab" is the founder of the Rechabites, who lived in tents and did not drink wine (Jeremiah 35).
- **10:16** "My zeal for the LORD" is Hebrew *qin'ah*. See entry H-QANNA.
- **10:19** "Jehu was acting with cunning" is the narrator's note. The trap of the worshippers of Baal depends on a lie.
- **10:23** "Make sure that there is no servant of the LORD here" is part of the trap, as Jehu wants to be sure that only worshippers of Baal are killed.
- **10:27** "A latrine" is the reading tradition (*machara'ot*). The written text has *mots'ot*. The place remained a latrine "to this day".
- **10:29** "The golden calves" at Bethel and Dan are the sin of Jeroboam (1 Kings 12:28-30), which Jehu keeps.
- **10:30** "Your descendants to the fourth generation" is fulfilled in Jehoahaz, Joash, Jeroboam II and Zechariah (15:12). The prophet Hosea says that the LORD will punish the house of Jehu for the blood of Jezreel (Hosea 1:4).
- **10:32** "Hazael struck them" begins the loss of land east of the Jordan to Aram, which Elisha had foretold (8:12).
- **11:1** Athaliah's destruction of the royal family is also told in 2 Chronicles 22:10. It is the only break in the line of David's house on the throne.
- **11:2** "Jehosheba, the daughter of King Joram" is Ahaziah's sister. 2 Chronicles 22:11 says she was the wife of the priest Jehoiada.
- **11:4** "The Carites" are a force of foreign guards, perhaps Carians. "A covenant" is Hebrew *berit*. See entry H-BERIT.
- **11:5** The arrangement of the three groups of guards in verses 5 to 8 is hard to follow in the Hebrew.
- **11:12** "The testimony" is Hebrew *ha-edut*, which may be a copy of the covenant or law (see Deuteronomy 17:18-20), or armlets that were a sign of the king (as in 2 Samuel 1:10). It is uncertain.
- **11:14** "By the pillar, as was the custom" is the king's place in the temple court.
- **11:15** "She must not be put to death in the house of the LORD" is the reason Athaliah is taken out of the temple.
- **11:17** "A covenant between the LORD and the king and the people" has two parts: the people with the LORD, and the king with the people.
- **11:18** "Mattan the priest of Baal" is killed in front of the altars. The temple of Baal in Jerusalem was probably built by Athaliah.
- **11:21** In the Hebrew Bible this verse is 12:1, so the English chapter 12 has twenty-one verses and the Hebrew has twenty-two.
- **12:1** "Jehoash" is also called Joash. "Zibiah of Beersheba" is his mother.
- **12:2** "Because Jehoiada the priest instructed him" says that the king's good record depended on the priest. After Jehoiada's death the king turned away (2 Chronicles 24:15-22).
- **12:4** "The money from the holy gifts ... the money from those who are counted" lists three sources of money for the temple. The Hebrew phrase "the money of the assessment of persons" (*kesef erkei nefashot*) refers to the money set for vows (Leviticus 27:1-8), and may include the half shekel of Exodus 30:11-16. "Holy" is *qodashim*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **12:6** "The twenty-third year" shows that the priests had put off the repairs for many years.
- **12:9** "A chest" with a hole in the lid is the first collection box in the Bible.
- **12:13** The money for repairs is not used to make new vessels.
- **12:15** "They acted honestly" is the note that the workers were trusted and no account was needed.
- **12:16** "The money from the guilt offerings and the money from the sin offerings" belonged to the priests by law (Leviticus 5:14 to 6:7; 7:7). "Guilt offering" is Hebrew *asham*. See entry H-ASHAM.
- **12:17** "Hazael ... captured Gath" shows how far the Aramean army went. Joash saves Jerusalem by paying tribute from the temple.
- **12:20** "The house of the Millo" and "Silla" are in Jerusalem. The place Silla is not otherwise known.
- **12:21** "Jozacar son of Shimeath" is called Zabad in 2 Chronicles 24:26. 2 Chronicles says that the king was murdered for killing the priest Zechariah son of Jehoiada.
- **13:1** "Jehoahaz son of Jehu" is the second king of Jehu's line. The names of the kings of Israel and Judah are often alike. "Joash" and "Jehoash" are two forms of the same name, and two kings (of Judah and of Israel) bear it in these chapters.
- **13:3** "Hazael" and his son "Ben-hadad" are the Aramean kings who oppress Israel (see 8:7-15 and 10:32-33).
- **13:5** "A deliverer" is not named. It may refer to the Assyrian attack on Damascus about 805 BCE, or to Joash or Jeroboam, who recover the towns (verse 25; 14:25-27).
- **13:6** "The Asherah pole also remained" says that the symbol of Asherah survived Jehu's reform.
- **13:7** "Fifty horsemen, ten chariots and ten thousand foot soldiers" is all that is left of the army of Jehoahaz.
- **13:14** "My father, my father, the chariots of Israel and its horsemen" repeats Elisha's cry at Elijah's departure (2:12). Now it is said to Elisha.
- **13:17** "The LORD's arrow of victory" is a symbolic act, as the king shoots to the east, towards Aram. The king strikes the ground only three times and so wins only three victories (verse 25).
- **13:21** The dead man who revives on touching Elisha's bones is rendered as written. It is the last of Elisha's miracles.
- **13:23** "Covenant" is Hebrew *berit*. See entry H-BERIT. The LORD acts for Israel because of the covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob.
- **13:25** "Three times Joash defeated him" fulfils Elisha's word in verse 19.
- **14:5** "The servants who had killed the king his father" are the murderers of Joash (12:20-21).
- **14:6** "The Book of the Law of Moses" is quoted from Deuteronomy 24:16. Amaziah does not kill the children of the murderers.
- **14:7** "Sela" may be Petra, or another rock fortress in Edom. "Joktheel" is the new name Amaziah gave it. The "Valley of Salt" is also in 8:13 and 2 Samuel 8:13.
- **14:9** "A thistle in Lebanon sent a message to a cedar" is a fable, like Jotham's (Judges 9:8-15). The thistle is Amaziah, the cedar Jehoash, and the wild animal the Israelite army.
- **14:13** "Four hundred cubits" is about 180 metres of wall broken down. Jehoash also takes hostages and the temple treasure.
- **14:21** "Azariah" is also called Uzziah (15:13, 30, 32, 34).
- **14:22** "Elath" is the port on the Gulf of Aqaba. Azariah rebuilds it after his father's death.
- **14:25** "Jonah son of Amittai, the prophet from Gath-hepher" is the prophet of the book of Jonah (Jonah 1:1). He prophesies the recovery of Israel's borders under Jeroboam II.
- **14:26** "Slave or free" translates a Hebrew idiom that means everyone (see also 1 Kings 14:10).
- **14:28** The Hebrew of "how he recovered Damascus and Hamath for Israel, which had belonged to Judah" is difficult. It may mean that Jeroboam recovered for Israel places that David had held.
- **15:1** "Azariah" is called Uzziah in verses 13, 30, 32 and 34.
- **15:5** "A skin disease" is Hebrew *tsara'at*. See entry H-TSARAAT. The king lived in a separate house, as the law required (Leviticus 13:46). 2 Chronicles 26:16-21 says it came because he tried to burn incense in the temple.
- **15:10** "In front of the people" is the Hebrew reading (*qabal-am*), which is uncertain. The Greek has "at Ibleam".
- **15:12** The word to Jehu about four generations is in 10:30. Zechariah is the fourth.
- **15:16** "Tiphsah" is the Hebrew reading. Some Greek manuscripts have "Tappuah", which is nearer. Menahem's ripping open of the pregnant women is rendered as written. The same cruelty is foretold in 8:12.
- **15:19** "Pul" is another name of Tiglath-pileser III of Assyria (1 Chronicles 5:26). The tribute is a thousand talents of silver.
- **15:20** "Fifty shekels of silver from each" of the wealthy men is a tax raised to pay the tribute.
- **15:25** Pekah, an army officer, kills Pekahiah in the palace.
- **15:29** "Tiglath-pileser ... carried the people off to Assyria" is the first deportation from Israel, about 733 BCE. The places named are in the north.
- **15:30** "Hoshea son of Elah" kills Pekah and becomes the last king of Israel.
- **15:37** "Rezin king of Aram and Pekah" attack Judah. The events are told in Isaiah 7.
- **16:3** "He even made his son pass through the fire" is the Hebrew idiom for child sacrifice (see Leviticus 18:21; Deuteronomy 18:10; 2 Chronicles 28:3). It is rendered as written.
- **16:5** "They besieged Ahaz but could not overcome him" is the background to Isaiah 7:1-9.
- **16:6** "The king of Edom recovered Elath for Edom" follows the reading tradition. The written text has "Rezin king of Aram recovered Elath for Aram". The two words are alike in Hebrew.
- **16:7** "I am your servant and your son" is the language of a vassal. Isaiah 7:10-17 shows the prophet's warning against this.
- **16:9** "Captured Damascus" and "killed Rezin" happened in 732 BCE.
- **16:10** "Uriah the priest" is the priest who builds the new altar. A man of the same name is a witness in Isaiah 8:2.
- **16:14** "The bronze altar" is Solomon's (1 Kings 8:64). Ahaz moves it to the north side of the new altar.
- **16:15** "For me to consult" is a hard phrase (*baqqer*). It may mean the bronze altar is for the king's own use in seeking guidance.
- **16:17** Ahaz takes the sea off the bronze oxen and cuts up the stands. The bronze may have been paid to Assyria.
- **17:1** "Hoshea" is the last king of Israel. The twelfth year of Ahaz is about 732 BCE.
- **17:3** "Shalmaneser" is Shalmaneser V of Assyria.
- **17:4** "So king of Egypt" is a ruler of Egypt who is not identified with certainty.
- **17:6** "Captured Samaria" is about 722 or 721 BCE. The Assyrian king Sargon II claims the capture in his own inscriptions. "Halah, the Habor and Gozan" are in northern Mesopotamia.
- **17:7** "Feared other gods" is Hebrew *yare*. See entry H-YIRAH. The long explanation in verses 7 to 23 is the narrator's reason for the fall of Israel, in the language of Deuteronomy.
- **17:13** "Turn back" is Hebrew *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV. "Every prophet and every seer" says that the LORD warned Israel and Judah again and again.
- **17:15** "Covenant" is *berit*. See entry H-BERIT. "They followed worthless things and became worthless" is a play on the word *hebel*, "breath" or "vanity".
- **17:16** "Two cast calves" are those of Jeroboam (1 Kings 12:28). "All the host of heaven" are the stars worshipped as gods.
- **17:17** "Made their sons and daughters pass through the fire" is child sacrifice, as in 16:3. It is rendered as written.
- **17:19** The verse on Judah is a later comment. It prepares for the fall of Judah.
- **17:24** "Babylon, Cuthah, Avva, Hamath and Sepharvaim" are places from which the Assyrians brought people to Samaria. The settlers later come to be called Samaritans.
- **17:25** "The LORD sent lions among them" is the settlers' misfortune.
- **17:27** "The rules of the god of the land" is the settlers' view that each land has its own god.
- **17:30** The gods "Succoth-benoth", "Nergal" and "Ashima" were worshipped in Babylon, Cuth and Hamath.
- **17:31** "Burned their children in the fire to Adrammelech and Anammelech" is rendered as written.
- **17:34** "To this day they follow their former practices" is the writer's comment on the mixed worship of the settlers.
- **17:35** "The LORD had made a covenant with them" begins a short restatement of the first commandments (verses 35 to 39), which the writer says the people did not keep.
- **17:41** "These nations feared the LORD and also served their carved images" ends the chapter with the mixed worship still going on.
- **18:1** The dates in this chapter are hard to fit. Verses 1 and 9 to 10 put the start of Hezekiah's reign about 729 BCE, and verse 13 puts Sennacherib's invasion in his fourteenth year (701 BCE), which gives about 715 BCE. The Hebrew numbers are kept as they are.
- **18:4** "The bronze snake that Moses had made" refers to Numbers 21:4-9. It had become an object of worship and was called "Nehushtan", a name that plays on the word for snake (*nachash*) and bronze (*nechoshet*). "The Asherah pole" is the wooden symbol of the goddess.
- **18:5** "There was no one like him" is repeated of Josiah in 23:25.
- **18:7** "He rebelled against the king of Assyria" is the cause of the invasion in verse 13.
- **18:9** Verses 9 to 12 repeat the fall of Samaria in 17:5-6 from Judah's point of view, and dated by Hezekiah's reign.
- **18:13** "Sennacherib" is the Assyrian king, who ruled 705 to 681 BCE. His own annals describe this campaign, claiming that he took forty-six towns and shut up Hezekiah "like a bird in a cage". The account of verses 13 to 16 is also in Isaiah 36:1.
- **18:14** "Lachish" was a strong town of Judah. A series of Assyrian wall-reliefs at Nineveh shows its capture. "Three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold" is Kings' figure. The Assyrian annals give eight hundred talents of silver.
- **18:16** "He stripped off the gold" from the temple doors to pay tribute is told without comment.
- **18:17** "The Tartan, the Rabsaris and the Rabshakeh" are Assyrian titles. The *turtanu* was the commander of the army, the *rab sha-reshi* a chief official, and the *rab-shaqeh* a chief cupbearer and spokesman. "The channel of the upper pool" is the place where Isaiah met Ahaz (Isaiah 7:3).
- **18:21** "That broken reed of a staff" is a figure for the unreliable help of Egypt, as in Isaiah 30:1-7 and 36:6.
- **18:22** "He whose high places and altars Hezekiah has removed" is the Rabshakeh's misreading of the reform. He takes it as an insult to the LORD.
- **18:25** "The LORD said to me, 'Go up against this land'" is the Rabshakeh's claim to speak for the LORD. The narrator does not comment.
- **18:26** "In Aramaic" is the language of diplomacy. "The language of Judah" is Hebrew.
- **18:27** "Eat their own dung and drink their own urine" is a threat of the siege. It is rendered as written.
- **18:31** "Make peace with me" is literally "make a blessing with me" (Hebrew *asu iti berakhah*). It means to make terms. "Each of you will eat from his own vine and fig tree" imitates the language of peace in 1 Kings 4:25.
- **18:32** "A land like your own" is the Assyrian promise of deportation presented as a blessing.
- **18:34** "Hamath and Arpad ... Sepharvaim" are northern towns that Assyria had destroyed. The Rabshakeh argues that their gods were no help.
- **19:2** "The senior priests" are the elders of the priests. Isaiah is named for the first time in Kings.
- **19:3** "Children have come to the point of birth, and there is no strength to bring them out" is a figure for a crisis that cannot be passed.
- **19:7** "A spirit" is Hebrew *ruach*. See entry H-RUACH. The LORD will put a spirit in Sennacherib so that he hears a report and goes home.
- **19:9** "Tirhakah king of Cush" is Taharqa. He did not become king until about 690 BCE, though in 701 he was a young army commander. The text calls him king as a title.
- **19:11** "Devoting them to destruction" is Hebrew *cherem*. See entry H-CHEREM. The Assyrian king uses the word for what he did to the nations.
- **19:15** "Enthroned above the cherubim" is the title of the LORD at the ark (see 1 Samuel 4:4).
- **19:18** "They were not gods, but the work of human hands" is the language of Deuteronomy 4:28 and Isaiah 44:9-20.
- **19:21** Isaiah's word against Sennacherib is poetry (verses 21 to 28). "The virgin daughter of Zion" is a figure for Jerusalem, who mocks the king.
- **19:22** "The Holy One of Israel" is Hebrew *qadosh Yisra'el*. See entry H-QADOSH. It is Isaiah's favourite name for God.
- **19:23** The king's boast of cutting down the cedars of Lebanon is the boast of an empire.
- **19:24** "Foreign water" is in the Hebrew of this verse but not in the parallel in Isaiah 37:25.
- **19:25** "Long ago I did it" says that the LORD planned the Assyrian conquests. The king was an instrument.
- **19:28** "My hook in your nose and my bit in your mouth" is a figure for leading a captive animal. Assyrian reliefs show captives led with hooks.
- **19:29** "This will be the sign for you" is a promise that after two years of eating what grows without sowing, the farming will return in the third year.
- **19:31** "Zeal" is Hebrew *qin'ah*. See entry H-QANNA. "A remnant" is Hebrew *she'erit*, a key word in Isaiah.
- **19:34** "For my own sake and for the sake of my servant David" gives the two reasons for the defence of the town.
- **19:35** "A hundred and eighty-five thousand" died in the Assyrian camp, struck by the angel of the LORD. It is rendered as written. The Assyrian records do not mention it. The Greek historian Herodotus tells of mice at the Egyptian border. Some readers have thought of a plague.
- **19:37** "Nisroch" is a god of Assyria. Sennacherib was killed by his sons in 681 BCE. "Ararat" is Urartu in the north.
- **20:1** "In those days" does not fix the time. The promise to rescue the town from Assyria (verse 6) shows that the illness came before the end of the invasion.
- **20:3** "Remember how I have walked before you in faithfulness" is Hezekiah's plea. He weeps bitterly.
- **20:5** "The leader of my people" is Hebrew *nagid*, the old title for the king (see 1 Samuel 9:16).
- **20:7** "A cake of figs" on the boil is a poultice, a known remedy.
- **20:9** "The steps" are Hebrew *ma'alot*, steps or degrees of the steps of Ahaz. The shadow was cast on them. It was a sort of sundial.
- **20:12** "Merodach-baladan" is Marduk-apla-iddina II, king of Babylon, who led a revolt against Assyria. His envoys come to seek an alliance.
- **20:13** "All his treasury" shows the king's display to the envoys. The text does not say whether this was an error, but Isaiah's response in verses 17 to 18 treats it as one.
- **20:17** "Carried off to Babylon" is the first mention of Babylon as the destroyer of Judah.
- **20:18** "Officials" is Hebrew *sarisim*, a word that can mean court officers or eunuchs.
- **20:19** "The word of the LORD that you have spoken is good" is Hezekiah's reply. The text records his thought, "Why not, if there will be peace and security in my days?" and gives no judgement.
- **20:20** "The pool and the channel" refers to Hezekiah's water tunnel, which brought water from the Gihon spring into the town. An inscription found in the tunnel describes how it was cut.
- **21:1** "Fifty-five years" is the longest reign of any king of Judah. Manasseh's long reign (about 697 to 642 BCE) was a time of Assyrian rule. The account in Kings gives only his sins. 2 Chronicles 33:10-20 adds his repentance.
- **21:3** "He rebuilt the high places that his father Hezekiah had destroyed" undoes Hezekiah's reform (18:4). "All the host of heaven" are the stars.
- **21:6** "He made his son pass through the fire" is child sacrifice, as in 16:3. It is rendered as written. "Soothsaying and divination" and dealing with mediums break the law of Deuteronomy 18:10-11.
- **21:7** "The carved image of Asherah" in the temple is a sign of the worst abuse of the house of the LORD.
- **21:8** The promise is repeated that Israel will stay in the land if they keep the law. It is conditional, as in 1 Kings 9:6-9.
- **21:12** "The ears of everyone who hears of it will tingle" repeats 1 Samuel 3:11.
- **21:13** "The measuring line of Samaria and the plumb line of the house of Ahab" means that Jerusalem will be judged as Samaria was. "Wipe Jerusalem as one wipes a dish" is a figure for a complete clearing out.
- **21:16** "Innocent blood" is not otherwise described. It may refer to the killing of prophets or of those who opposed him (see also 24:4).
- **21:18** "The garden of Uzza" is where Manasseh and Amon are buried, rather than in the tombs of the kings.
- **21:24** "The people of the land" put down the plot against Amon and make Josiah king. They are the landowners of Judah.
- **22:1** "Eight years old" shows that others ruled for Josiah at first.
- **22:3** "The eighteenth year" is about 622 BCE. The repair of the temple follows the arrangement in 12:4-16.
- **22:8** "The Book of the Law" is usually thought to be Deuteronomy or part of it. The text does not say who wrote it, or how it came to be in the temple. The story is told also in 2 Chronicles 34:8-33.
- **22:11** "He tore his clothes" at the words of the book, which include the curses of Deuteronomy 28.
- **22:14** "Huldah the prophet" is the only woman prophet named in Kings. "Prophet" is Hebrew *neviah*. See entry H-NAVI. "The Second Quarter" is Hebrew *mishneh*, a district of Jerusalem.
- **22:20** "You will be gathered to your grave in peace" is a promise that does not match Josiah's death in battle at Megiddo (23:29). It may mean he is spared seeing the fall of the town.
- **23:2** "The Book of the Covenant" is Hebrew *sefer ha-berit*. See entry H-BERIT. The king reads it aloud to the people, as Joshua did (Joshua 8:34-35).
- **23:3** "By the pillar" is the king's place in the temple court (see 11:14). The covenant is made before the LORD, and all the people join.
- **23:4** "They burned them ... and carried their ashes to Bethel" starts the cleansing of the temple. "The priests of the second rank" are the deputy priests.
- **23:5** "The priests of the idols" translates Hebrew *kemarim*, a word for priests of other gods. It is also used in Hosea 10:5 and Zephaniah 1:4.
- **23:7** "The houses of the male shrine prostitutes" were in the temple precinct. "Where the women wove hangings for the Asherah" is the detail of the cult practices.
- **23:8** "From Geba to Beersheba" is the whole land of Judah, from north to south.
- **23:9** The priests of the high places could not serve at the altar in Jerusalem, but they could eat with the others. See Deuteronomy 18:6-8.
- **23:10** "Topheth" is a place of child sacrifice in the Valley of Ben-hinnom, south of Jerusalem (Jeremiah 7:31; 19:5-6). "Molech" is the god to whom they were offered.
- **23:11** "The horses that the kings of Judah had dedicated to the sun" were part of a sun cult.
- **23:13** "The Mount of Destruction" is the name given here to the Mount of Olives. The Hebrew name *mashchit* ("destruction") plays on the name *mishchah* ("anointing"). The high places for Ashtoreth, Chemosh and Milcom are those that Solomon built (1 Kings 11:5-7).
- **23:14** "Filled their places with human bones" is a way of making them unclean.
- **23:15** "The altar at Bethel" was made by Jeroboam (1 Kings 12:32-33).
- **23:16** Josiah burns the bones on the altar, as the man of God had foretold (1 Kings 13:2). The prediction named Josiah.
- **23:18** "The prophet who came from Samaria" is the old prophet of Bethel (1 Kings 13:11, 31). Josiah leaves both tombs.
- **23:20** "He killed on the altars all the priests of the high places who were there" is rendered as written. It is the only killing of priests in Josiah's reform. It follows the idea of Deuteronomy 13 and 17.
- **23:22** "No Passover like it" is also said in 2 Chronicles 35:18, where it is compared to the time of Samuel.
- **23:24** "The household gods" is Hebrew *teraphim*. See also Judges 17:5 and 1 Samuel 19:13.
- **23:25** "Turned back" is Hebrew *shuv*. See entry H-SHUV. The words "with all his heart and all his soul and all his strength" repeat the Shema (Deuteronomy 6:5). The verse says that there was no king like Josiah, just as 18:5 says that there was none like Hezekiah.
- **23:26** "Even so, the LORD did not turn from the heat of his great anger" says that the judgement on Judah stands because of Manasseh's sins, though Josiah repented.
- **23:29** "Pharaoh Neco killed him at Megiddo" is told very briefly. 2 Chronicles 35:20-24 says that Josiah was wounded by archers and died in Jerusalem. Neco was marching to help Assyria against Babylon, in 609 BCE.
- **23:30** "The people of the land" make Jehoahaz king. He is called Shallum in Jeremiah 22:11.
- **23:33** "Riblah" is a town in Syria, the Egyptian camp. Jehoahaz is put in chains.
- **23:34** "Eliakim" is renamed "Jehoiakim" by Neco. This shows that he is Neco's vassal. Jehoahaz dies in Egypt, as Jeremiah said (Jeremiah 22:10-12).
- **24:1** "Nebuchadnezzar" is the Babylonian king (the Hebrew often has Nebuchadrezzar). Jehoiakim served him for three years, then rebelled, about 605 to 601 BCE.
- **24:2** "Raiding bands" from the neighbouring peoples attack Judah.
- **24:3** "At the command of the LORD" says that the Babylonian attacks were from the LORD. The cause given is Manasseh's sins (verse 4).
- **24:7** "The king of Egypt did not come out of his land again" says that Babylon took over Egypt's holdings in the region.
- **24:8** "Eighteen years old" is the Hebrew reading. 2 Chronicles 36:9 has eight in the Hebrew, which is probably wrong.
- **24:12** "Gave himself up to the king of Babylon" was in 597 BCE. The Babylonian Chronicle records the capture of Jerusalem on the second of Adar in that year.
- **24:13** "Cut up all the gold objects" refers to the vessels made for the temple by Solomon.
- **24:14** "Ten thousand captives" does not match the "seven thousand warriors and a thousand craftsmen" of verse 16, which total eight thousand. Jeremiah 52:28 gives three thousand and twenty-three. The numbers are kept as in the Hebrew.
- **24:17** "Mattaniah" is renamed "Zedekiah" by the king of Babylon, a sign of his rule.
- **24:20** "It was because of the anger of the LORD that this happened" gives the reason for the final rebellion and fall.
- **25:1** "The tenth day of the tenth month" is the start of the siege, in January 588 BCE. The date is still remembered as a fast (Zechariah 8:19).
- **25:4** "The town wall was broken through" in July 586 BCE (the ninth day of the fourth month). The king and the army flee by night towards the Arabah.
- **25:6** "Riblah" is where Nebuchadnezzar judged Zedekiah, as Neco had put Jehoahaz in chains there.
- **25:7** "They killed the sons of Zedekiah in front of his eyes. Then they put out the eyes of Zedekiah" is rendered as written. The last sight he saw was the death of his sons. See also Jeremiah 39:6-7 and Ezekiel 12:13.
- **25:8** "The seventh day of the fifth month" is the date given here for the arrival of Nebuzaradan. Jeremiah 52:12 has the tenth.
- **25:9** "He burned the house of the LORD" is the destruction of the temple, in 586 BCE. It is told in one short line.
- **25:13** "The bronze pillars ... the stands and the bronze sea" are those made by Hiram for Solomon (1 Kings 7:15-26, 27-39).
- **25:17** "Three cubits" is the height of the capital here. In 1 Kings 7:16 it is five cubits, and Jeremiah 52:22 also has five.
- **25:18** "Seraiah the chief priest" is the ancestor of Ezra (Ezra 7:1).
- **25:21** "The king of Babylon struck them down and put them to death at Riblah" is rendered as written. "So Judah went into exile from its land" is the summary.
- **25:22** "Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan" is a member of the family of the officials who had supported Josiah (22:12). He is governor at Mizpah.
- **25:25** "Ishmael son of Nethaniah" is of the royal family and kills Gedaliah. The story is told at greater length in Jeremiah 40 and 41.
- **25:26** "They went to Egypt" because they feared Babylon. Jeremiah warned against this (Jeremiah 42 and 43).
- **25:27** "The thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin" is 561 BCE. "Evil-merodach" is Amel-Marduk, king of Babylon. The release of Jehoiachin is also told in Jeremiah 52:31-34.
- **25:29** "Ate at the king's table every day of his life" is the closing picture of the book. Babylonian ration tablets found at Babylon name "Yaukin, king of Judah". The text does not say what this means for the line of David.
