# WABV: Jonah

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[H-RUACH\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Jonah is the fifth of the twelve books of the Minor Prophets in the Protestant order that the WABV text follows. It is in Hebrew and is a story in prose, with one poem, the psalm of Jonah in 2:2-9. The book names Jonah son of Amittai, a prophet of the northern kingdom who is also named in 2 Kings 14:25, and it has no date of its own. The WABV text does not decide on questions of whether the book is history, parable or both, or when it was written, and the footnotes record only the language. The chapter and verse numbering is the English numbering. English 1:17 is Hebrew 2:1, so English 2:1-10 is Hebrew 2:2-11. Chapters 3 and 4 are numbered alike in English and Hebrew. The book falls into two parts in the WABV text: the flight, the storm and the great fish, and the psalm (chapters 1 and 2), and the turning of Nineveh and the anger of Jonah (3 and 4). The headings of the parts are not in the Hebrew. The words of 3:9 and 4:2 are the same in the Hebrew as Joel 2:13-14 and, in part, Exodus 34:6, and the wording of the lines that are the same is the same in the WABV text, as the footnotes record. The names of God are given as in the earlier books: "the LORD" for YHWH, "God" for Elohim, and "the LORD God" for YHWH Elohim, and the footnotes mark the places where the book uses one name and not the other. The Hebrew of Jonah is clear in most verses, and the footnotes record the few uncertain terms.*

## Part One: Chapters 1 and 2, the flight, the fish and the psalm

### Chapter 1

**1** The word of the LORD came to Jonah son of Amittai:

**2** "Get up, go to Nineveh, that great city, and cry out against it, for their evil has come up before me."

**3** But Jonah got up to flee to Tarshish, away from the presence of the LORD. He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish. So he paid the fare and went down into it, to go with them to Tarshish, away from the presence of the LORD.

**4** But the LORD hurled a great wind \[H-RUACH\] onto the sea, and there was a mighty storm on the sea, so that the ship threatened to break up.

**5** Then the sailors were afraid \[H-YIRAH\], and each cried out to his own god. And they threw the cargo that was in the ship into the sea to lighten it for them. But Jonah had gone down into the inner part of the ship and lay down and was fast asleep.

**6** So the captain came and said to him, "What do you mean, sleeper? Get up, call out to your god. Perhaps the god will take thought for us, so that we do not perish."

**7** And they said to one another, "Come, let us cast lots, that we may know on whose account this evil has come on us." So they cast lots, and the lot fell on Jonah.

**8** Then they said to him, "Tell us on whose account this evil has come on us. What is your work? Where do you come from? What is your country? And what people are you from?"

**9** And he said to them, "I am a Hebrew, and I fear \[H-YIRAH\] the LORD, the God of heaven, who made the sea and the dry land."

**10** Then the men were exceedingly afraid \[H-YIRAH\] and said to him, "What is this that you have done?" For the men knew that he was fleeing from the presence of the LORD, because he had told them.

**11** Then they said to him, "What should we do to you, that the sea may quiet down for us?" For the sea was growing more and more stormy.

**12** He said to them, "Pick me up and hurl me into the sea. Then the sea will quiet down for you, for I know that it is because of me that this great storm has come on you."

**13** Nevertheless, the men rowed hard to get back \[H-SHUV\] to dry land, but they could not, for the sea was growing more and more stormy against them.

**14** Therefore they called out to the LORD, "Please, LORD, do not let us perish for this man's life \[H-NEPHESH\], and do not lay on us innocent blood, for you, LORD, have done as you pleased."

**15** So they picked up Jonah and hurled him into the sea, and the sea stopped its raging.

**16** Then the men feared \[H-YIRAH\] the LORD exceedingly, and they offered a sacrifice to the LORD and made vows.

**17** And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.

### Chapter 2

**1** Then Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish,

**2** saying, "I called out to the LORD out of my distress, and he answered me. From the belly of Sheol \[H-SHEOL\] I cried for help, and you heard my voice.

**3** You threw me into the deep, into the heart of the seas, and the flood surrounded me. All your waves and your billows passed over me.

**4** Then I said, 'I am driven away from your sight. Yet I will again look on your holy \[H-QADOSH\] temple.'

**5** The waters closed in over me, to the point of death \[H-NEPHESH\]. The deep surrounded me. Weeds were wrapped around my head

**6** at the roots of the mountains. I went down to the land whose bars closed on me for ever, yet you brought up my life \[H-CHAYYIM\] from the pit, LORD my God.

**7** When my life \[H-NEPHESH\] was fainting away, I remembered the LORD, and my prayer came to you, into your holy \[H-QADOSH\] temple.

**8** Those who pay regard to worthless idols forsake their own faithful love \[H-HESED\].

**9** But I, with a voice of thanksgiving, will sacrifice to you. What I have vowed I will pay. Salvation belongs to the LORD."

**10** And the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out onto the dry land.

*Review note: Jonah 1 and 2 were drafted in WABV wording. They are the call to Nineveh and the flight to Tarshish (1:1-3), the storm, the lot and the sailors (1:4-16), the great fish (1:17), Jonah's psalm (2:1-9) and the vomiting onto dry land (2:10). The numbering is the English numbering: English 1:17 is Hebrew 2:1, and English 2:1-10 is Hebrew 2:2-11. Chapter 1 and 2:1 and 2:10 are prose, and the psalm in 2:2-9 is in poetry and is set in lines. In 1:4 "a great wind" translates ruach gedolah and is tagged H-RUACH. In 1:5, 1:9, 1:10 and 1:16 forms of yare ("afraid", "I fear", "feared") are tagged H-YIRAH. In 1:13 "to get back" translates lehashiv and is tagged H-SHUV. In 1:14, 2:5 and 2:7 "life", "to the point of death" and "my life" translate nefesh and nafshi and are tagged H-NEPHESH. In 2:2 "Sheol" is tagged H-SHEOL. In 2:4 and 2:7 "your holy temple" translates heikhal qodshekha and is tagged H-QADOSH. In 2:6 "my life" translates chayyai and is tagged H-CHAYYIM. In 2:8 "faithful love" translates chasdam and is tagged H-HESED. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapters 1 and 2. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 1:4 (*chishsheva*, "threatened"), 1:5 (*yarketei ha-sefinah*), 2:5 (the words "to the point of death"), 2:6 (*le-qitsvei harim*) and 2:8. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words of 1:17 and 2:10 (the great fish) are rendered as written, with no comment on how the story is read. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Two: Chapters 3 and 4, Nineveh and Jonah's anger

### Chapter 3

**1** The word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time:

**2** "Get up, go to Nineveh, that great city, and proclaim to it the message that I will tell you."

**3** So Jonah got up and went to Nineveh, as the LORD had said. Now Nineveh was an exceedingly great city, a three days' walk across.

**4** Jonah began to go into the city, a day's walk, and he cried out, "Forty more days, and Nineveh will be overturned."

**5** And the people of Nineveh believed God. They called a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least.

**6** When the word reached the king of Nineveh, he rose from his throne, took off his robe, covered himself with sackcloth and sat in the ashes.

**7** And he had a proclamation made in Nineveh: "By the decree of the king and his nobles: no human being or animal, herd or flock, is to taste anything. They are not to eat or drink water,

**8** but human beings and animals are to be covered with sackcloth, and they are to cry out to God with all their strength. Everyone is to turn \[H-SHUV\] from his evil way and from the violence in his hands.

**9** Who knows whether God will turn \[H-SHUV\] and relent \[H-NACHAM\], and turn \[H-SHUV\] from his burning anger, so that we do not perish?"

**10** When God saw what they did, that they turned \[H-SHUV\] from their evil way, God relented \[H-NACHAM\] concerning the calamity that he had said he would do to them, and he did not do it.

### Chapter 4

**1** But it displeased Jonah exceedingly, and he was angry.

**2** He prayed to the LORD and said, "Please, LORD, was this not what I said when I was still in my own country? That is why I fled to Tarshish at the start, for I knew that you are a gracious God and compassionate, slow to anger, rich in faithful love \[H-HESED\], and relenting \[H-NACHAM\] from sending calamity.

**3** So now, LORD, take my life \[H-NEPHESH\] from me, for it is better for me to die than to live."

**4** And the LORD said, "Is it right for you to be angry?"

**5** Then Jonah went out of the city and sat east of it, and made himself a booth there. He sat under it in the shade until he should see what would happen in the city.

**6** And the LORD God appointed a plant and made it grow up over Jonah, to be a shade over his head, to deliver him from his discomfort. And Jonah was exceedingly glad about the plant.

**7** But when dawn came the next day, God appointed a worm, and it attacked the plant, so that it withered.

**8** When the sun rose, God appointed a scorching east wind \[H-RUACH\], and the sun beat down on Jonah's head so that he was faint. He asked that he might die, and said, "It is better for me to die than to live."

**9** But God said to Jonah, "Is it right for you to be angry about the plant?" And he said, "It is right for me to be angry, angry enough to die."

**10** And the LORD said, "You pity the plant, for which you did not work and which you did not make grow, which came up in a night and perished in a night.

**11** And should I not pity Nineveh, that great city, in which there are more than a hundred and twenty thousand people who do not know their right hand from their left, and also many animals?"

*Review note: Jonah 3 and 4 were drafted in WABV wording. They are the second call, the cry in Nineveh and the turning of the people and the king (3:1-10), and the anger of Jonah, the plant and the LORD's last question (4:1-11). The whole of the part is prose. The numbering is the same as the Hebrew. In 3:8, 3:9 (twice) and 3:10 forms of shuv ("turn") are tagged H-SHUV. In 3:9, 3:10 and 4:2 "relent" and "relenting" translate nicham and are tagged H-NACHAM. In 4:2 "faithful love" is tagged H-HESED. In 4:3 "my life" translates nafshi and is tagged H-NEPHESH. In 4:8 "a scorching east wind" translates ruach qadim charishit and is tagged H-RUACH. The words of 3:9 ("who knows whether God will turn and relent") are the same in the Hebrew as Joel 2:14 ("who knows whether he will turn and relent"), and the words of 4:2 ("a gracious God and compassionate, slow to anger, rich in faithful love, and relenting from sending calamity") are the same in the Hebrew as Joel 2:13, and they are given the same wording. They are also close to Exodus 34:6 and the other places, and the footnotes record the difference in the order of "gracious" and "compassionate". The word *ra'ah* is rendered "evil" for the people (3:8, 3:10) and "calamity" for the act of God (3:10, 4:2), and it is the same open decision as Isaiah 45:7. There are no departures from the Hebrew in chapters 3 and 4, and none in the whole book. The Hebrew is hard in some places, including 3:3 ("a three days' walk across"), 3:7 (*ta'am*) and 4:6 (*qiqayon*, the plant). The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words of the decree that the animals are covered with sackcloth (3:7-8) and the last question (4:11) are rendered as written, with no comment. The section needs a check against the Hebrew by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Appendix A: Footnotes

- **1:1** "Jonah" translates *Yonah*, "dove", and "Amittai" translates *Amittai*, "my truth". See 2 Kings 14:25, where Jonah son of Amittai is a prophet of the northern kingdom.
- **1:2** "Nineveh" translates *Nineveh*, the great city of Assyria. "Cry out" translates *qera*, and "their evil has come up" translates *alta ra'atam le-fanai*. "Evil" (*ra'ah*) is the same word as "disaster" and "calamity" in 3:10 and 4:2, and the same open decision as in Isaiah 45:7.
- **1:3** "Tarshish" is a far place in the west. "To flee" translates *livroach*, "Joppa" translates *Yafo*, and "the fare" translates *sekhar*. "Away from the presence of the LORD" translates *milifnei YHWH*, and it is said twice in the verse.
- **1:4** "Hurled" translates *hetil*, and "a great wind" translates *ruach gedolah*. See entry H-RUACH. "Threatened to break up" translates *chishsheva le-hishaver*, literally "thought she would break up".
- **1:5** "Were afraid" translates *va-yire'u*. See entry H-YIRAH. "The cargo" translates *ha-kelim*, "the inner part" translates *yarketei*, "the ship" translates *ha-sefinah*, and "was fast asleep" translates *va-yeradam*.
- **1:6** "The captain" translates *rav ha-chovel*, literally "the chief of the sailors". "Take thought for us" translates *yit'asheth lanu*, which is rare.
- **1:7** "Cast lots" translates *nappilah goralot*.
- **1:9** "A Hebrew" translates *Ivri*, and "I fear" translates *yare*. See entry H-YIRAH. "The God of heaven" translates *Elohei ha-shamayim*.
- **1:10** "Exceedingly afraid" translates *va-yire'u ha-anashim yir'ah gedolah*. See entry H-YIRAH.
- **1:13** "Rowed hard" translates *va-yachteru*, and "to get back" translates *lehashiv*. See entry H-SHUV.
- **1:14** "This man's life" translates *be-nefesh ha-ish ha-zeh*. See entry H-NEPHESH. "Innocent blood" translates *dam naqi*.
- **1:16** "Feared the LORD exceedingly" translates *va-yir'u ... yir'ah gedolah et-YHWH*. See entry H-YIRAH. "Vows" translates *nedarim*.
- **1:17** The verse is Hebrew 2:1. "Appointed" translates *va-yman*, and "a great fish" translates *dag gadol*. See also Matthew 12:40.
- **2:1** The verses 1 to 10 are Hebrew 2:2-11. "Prayed" translates *va-yitpallel*.
- **2:2** "Sheol" translates *She'ol*. See entry H-SHEOL. "Distress" translates *tsarah*. The verses 2 to 9 are Jonah's psalm.
- **2:3** "Billows" translates *mishbarekha*, and "waves" translates *galleikha*.
- **2:4** "I am driven away" translates *nigrashti*, and "I will again look" translates *ach osif lehabbit*. "Your holy temple" translates *heikhal qodshekha*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **2:5** "To the point of death" translates *ad-nafesh*, literally "up to the throat" or "to the life", which is hard. See entry H-NEPHESH. "Weeds" translates *suf*.
- **2:6** "The roots of the mountains" translates *le-qitsvei harim*, and "bars" translates *berichehah*. "The pit" translates *shachat*, and "my life" translates *chayyai*. See entry H-CHAYYIM.
- **2:7** "My life was fainting away" translates *be-hit'attef alai nafshi*. See entry H-NEPHESH. "Your holy temple" translates *heikhal qodshekha*. See entry H-QADOSH.
- **2:8** "Worthless idols" translates *havlei shav*, in which *hevel* is the word that is an open decision in the Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes and the prophets. "Their own faithful love" translates *chasdam*. See entry H-HESED.
- **2:9** "Thanksgiving" translates *todah*, and "salvation" translates *yeshu'ah*.
- **2:10** "Vomited" translates *va-yaqe*.
- **3:1** "A second time" translates *sheynit*.
- **3:3** "A three days' walk across" translates *mahalakh sheloshet yamim*, which may mean that the city took three days to cross or to visit.
- **3:4** "Will be overturned" translates *nehpakhet*, which may also mean "will be overthrown" or "will be turned around" (from *hafakh*, not from *shuv*).
- **3:5** "Believed God" translates *va-ya'aminu be-Elohim*, and "called a fast" translates *va-yiqre'u tsom*.
- **3:6** "Sat in the ashes" translates *va-yeshev al-ha-efer*.
- **3:7** "The decree" translates *ta'am*, a word that is also used in Aramaic, and "his nobles" translates *gedolav*.
- **3:8** "Is to turn" translates *ve-yashuvu*, and "his evil way" translates *darko ha-ra'ah*. See entry H-SHUV. "The violence" translates *ha-chamas*.
- **3:9** "Who knows whether God will turn and relent" translates *mi yode'a yashuv ve-nicham ha-Elohim*, and the words are the same as Joel 2:14. "Turn from his burning anger" translates *ve-shav me-charon appo*. See entries H-SHUV and H-NACHAM.
- **3:10** "Turned" translates *shavu*. See entry H-SHUV. "Relented concerning the calamity" translates *va-yinnachem ha-Elohim al-ha-ra'ah*. See entry H-NACHAM. "Evil" for the people (verses 8 and 10) and "calamity" for the act of God are both *ra'ah*, the same open decision as in Isaiah 45:7.
- **4:2** "A gracious God and compassionate, slow to anger, rich in faithful love, and relenting from sending calamity" translates *El-channun ve-rachum erekh appayim ve-rav-chesed ve-nicham al-ha-ra'ah*, and the words are the same as Joel 2:13. In Exodus 34:6 the order is "compassionate and gracious" (*rachum ve-channun*), and *emet*, "truth", is added. See entries H-HESED and H-NACHAM. "At the start" translates *qiddamti*, literally "I went before".
- **4:3** "Take my life from me" translates *qach-na et-nafshi mimmeni*. See entry H-NEPHESH.
- **4:4** "Is it right for you to be angry" translates *ha-heitev charah lakh*.
- **4:5** "A booth" translates *sukkah*.
- **4:6** "The LORD God" translates *YHWH Elohim*, which is used in this book only here. "A plant" translates *qiqayon*, a rare word that may be a castor oil plant or a gourd, and "discomfort" translates *ra'atho*.
- **4:7** "A worm" translates *tola'at*, and "attacked" translates *va-tach*.
- **4:8** "A scorching east wind" translates *ruach qadim charishit*, in which *charishit* is rare. See entry H-RUACH.
- **4:10** "You pity" translates *chasta*.
- **4:11** "I pity" translates *achus*. "Who do not know their right hand from their left" translates *asher lo-yada bein-yemino li-smolo*, and "animals" translates *behemah*. The last question is rendered as written, with no comment.
