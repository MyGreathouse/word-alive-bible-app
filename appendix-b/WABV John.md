# WABV: John

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[G-LOGOS\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. John is the fourth book of the New Testament in the order that the WABV text follows. It is written in Greek, and the WABV text is drafted from a critical Greek text, with the Byzantine tradition noted in the footnotes where it differs, as the Charter requires. The book is a gospel, and it is very different in form from the other three. Its sentences are short, and it keeps coming back to a small number of weighty words: *logos* (word), *zoe* (life), *phos* (light), *aletheia* (truth), *doxa* (glory), *semeion* (sign), *pisteuo* (believe), *agapao* (love), *menein* (stay) and *kosmos* (world). It has no parables of the kind that the other Gospels have, and no list of the twelve, and no account of the supper in the form of the other Gospels; it has seven signs, and long discourses, and sayings that begin "I am". The book is anonymous in the Greek, though it speaks of a disciple "whom Jesus loved" (13:23; 19:26; 20:2; 21:7, 21:20) and ends with a note that this disciple wrote these things (21:24), and the WABV text does not decide on the questions of authorship, date or sources, and the footnotes record only the language. The chapter and verse numbering is the usual numbering. Some verses are not in the critical Greek text, and the passage about the woman taken in adultery (7:53 to 8:11) is a special case, and is given in a separate section with a footnote. The WABV text of John makes a number of fixed choices that follow from its Greek, and they are written down here so that they are used throughout. "The Word" translates *logos* in 1:1-14 and is tagged G-LOGOS. *Ioudaioi* is rendered "the Judeans", because the word is a name of a people and a place (*Ioudaia*, Judea), and not only of a religion, and the team should decide the rendering (see the Charter's open decisions). *Menein* is rendered "stay" in every place, so that the repetition is kept (1:32-39; 6:56; 15:4-10). *Semeion* is rendered "sign". *Monogenes* is rendered "one-of-a-kind" (1:14, 1:18, 3:16, 3:18). *Eskenosen* (1:14) is rendered "pitched his tent". *Paraklētos* is rendered "Advocate" and is tagged G-PARAKLETOS, with other renderings in the footnote. *Pisteuo* is rendered "believe" and is tagged G-PISTIS, because it is the verb of *pistis*, "faith". The words "Amen, amen, I tell you" translate *amen amen lego soi (humin)*, which John always doubles. The words "I am" (*ego eimi*) are rendered "I am", and the footnote records the places where the Greek has no predicate. The words "Son of Man", "brothers" and "Christ" are open decisions, rendered literally in the draft and flagged in the footnotes for the team. The book falls into seven parts in the WABV text: the Word, the witness and the first signs (chapters 1 to 3), the Samaritan woman, the official's son and the bread of life (4 to 6), the festival of Tents, the light and the man born blind (7 to 9), the shepherd, Lazarus and the entry (10 to 12), the feet washed, the farewell words and the vine (13 to 15), the Advocate, the prayer and the arrest (16 to 18), and the cross, the tomb and the appearances (19 to 21). The headings of the parts are not in the Greek. The markers beginning with G- (for Greek words) are defined in the Biblical Concepts and Language System, and the new ones are listed in the review note of the part where each first appears. The names of God and of Jesus are given as the Greek has them: "God" for *theos*, "the Lord" for *kyrios*, "Jesus" and "Christ". The wording of the WABV text is drafted from the Greek and is not taken from any other English translation.*

## Part One: Chapters 1 to 3, the Word, the witness and the first signs

### Chapter 1

**1** At the beginning there was the Word \[G-LOGOS\], and the Word was in God's company, and the Word was God.

**2** He was in God's company at the beginning.

**3** All things came into being through him, and apart from him not one thing came into being that has come into being.

**4** In him was life \[G-ZOE\], and the life was the light of people.

**5** The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overpowered it.

**6** There came a man, sent from God, whose name was John.

**7** He came as a witness, to bear witness about the light, so that everyone might believe \[G-PISTIS\] through him.

**8** He was not the light. He came to bear witness about the light.

**9** The true light, which shines on everyone, was coming into the world.

**10** He was in the world, and the world came into being through him, yet the world did not know him.

**11** He came to his own place, and his own people did not take him in.

**12** But to as many as did take him in, to those who believe \[G-PISTIS\] in his name, he gave the right to become children of God,

**13** who were born not from blood, nor from the desire of the flesh, nor from the desire of a man, but from God.

**14** And the Word \[G-LOGOS\] became flesh and pitched his tent among us, and we saw his glory \[G-DOXA\], the glory of the one-of-a-kind Son from the Father, full of grace \[G-CHARIS\] and truth \[G-ALETHEIA\].

**15** John bears witness about him and has cried out, "This was the one of whom I said: The one coming after me has taken a place ahead of me, because he was before me."

**16** For from his fullness we have all received, grace on top of grace.

**17** For the Law \[G-NOMOS\] was given through Moses. Grace and truth came into being through Jesus Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\].

**18** No one has ever seen God. The one-of-a-kind God, who is at the Father's breast, has told us about him.

**19** This is John's witness, when the Judeans sent priests and Levites from Jerusalem to ask him, "Who are you?"

**20** He confessed, and did not deny, but confessed, "I am not the Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\]."

**21** They asked him, "What then? Are you Elijah?" He says, "I am not." "Are you the Prophet?" He answered, "No."

**22** So they said to him, "Who are you? We have to give an answer to those who sent us. What do you say about yourself?"

**23** He said, "I am a voice shouting in the wilderness, 'Make the Lord's road straight,' as Isaiah the prophet said."

**24** Those who had been sent were from the Pharisees.

**25** They asked him, "Then why do you baptise, if you are not the Christ, or Elijah, or the Prophet?"

**26** John answered them, "I baptise in water. In the middle of you stands one whom you do not know,

**27** the one coming after me, the strap of whose sandal I am not worthy to untie."

**28** These things happened in Bethany across the Jordan, where John was baptising.

**29** The next day he sees Jesus coming toward him and says, "Look, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world.

**30** This is the one of whom I said, 'After me comes a man who has taken a place ahead of me, because he was before me.'

**31** I did not know him, but I came baptising in water so that he might be shown to Israel."

**32** And John bore witness: "I watched the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] come down like a dove out of heaven, and it stayed on him.

**33** I did not know him, but the one who sent me to baptise in water said to me, 'The one on whom you see the Spirit come down and stay, this is the one who baptises in the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\].'

**34** I have seen it, and I have borne witness that this is the Son of God."

**35** The next day John was again standing with two of his disciples,

**36** and as he looked at Jesus walking by he says, "Look, the Lamb of God."

**37** The two disciples heard him say it and followed Jesus.

**38** Jesus turned and saw them following and says to them, "What are you looking for?" They said to him, "Rabbi" (which means Teacher), "where are you staying?"

**39** He says to them, "Come, and you will see." So they went and saw where he was staying, and they stayed with him that day. It was about the tenth hour.

**40** Andrew, the brother of Simon Peter, was one of the two who had heard John and followed him.

**41** He first finds his own brother Simon and says to him, "We have found the Messiah" (which means Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\]).

**42** He led him to Jesus. Jesus looked at him and said, "You are Simon, the son of John. You will be called Cephas" (which means Peter).

**43** The next day he decided to leave for Galilee. He finds Philip, and Jesus says to him, "Follow me."

**44** Philip was from Bethsaida, the town of Andrew and Peter.

**45** Philip finds Nathanael and says to him, "We have found the one about whom Moses wrote in the Law, and the Prophets as well: Jesus, son of Joseph, from Nazareth."

**46** Nathanael said to him, "Can anything good come out of Nazareth?" Philip says to him, "Come and see."

**47** Jesus saw Nathanael coming toward him and says of him, "Look, a true Israelite, in whom there is no deceit."

**48** Nathanael says to him, "How do you know me?" Jesus answered him, "Before Philip called you, when you were under the fig tree, I saw you."

**49** Nathanael answered him, "Rabbi, you are the Son of God. You are the King of Israel."

**50** Jesus answered him, "Do you believe \[G-PISTIS\] because I told you I saw you under the fig tree? You will see greater things than these."

**51** And he says to him, "Amen, amen, I tell you, you will see heaven opened and the angels of God going up and coming down on the Son of Man \[G-HUIOS-ANTHROPOU\]."

### Chapter 2

**1** On the third day there was a wedding at Cana in Galilee, and the mother of Jesus was there.

**2** Jesus also was invited to the wedding, with his disciples.

**3** When the wine ran out, the mother of Jesus says to him, "They have no wine."

**4** Jesus says to her, "Woman, what is that to you and to me? My hour has not yet come."

**5** His mother says to the servants, "Do whatever he tells you."

**6** Now there were six stone water jars standing there, for the Judeans' rites of purification, each holding two or three measures.

**7** Jesus says to them, "Fill the jars with water." And they filled them to the brim.

**8** He says to them, "Now draw some out and take it to the master of the feast." And they took it.

**9** When the master of the feast tasted the water that had become wine, and did not know where it came from (though the servants who had drawn the water knew), the master of the feast calls the bridegroom

**10** and says to him, "Everyone serves the good wine first, and when people have drunk freely, the worse. You have kept the good wine until now."

**11** This, the first of his signs, Jesus did in Cana of Galilee, and he showed his glory \[G-DOXA\], and his disciples believed \[G-PISTIS\] in him.

**12** After this he went down to Capernaum, with his mother and his brothers and his disciples, and they stayed there for a few days.

**13** The Passover of the Judeans was near, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem.

**14** In the temple he found those who were selling oxen and sheep and doves, and the money changers sitting there.

**15** Making a whip out of cords, he drove them all out of the temple, with the sheep and the oxen. He poured out the coins of the money changers and overturned their tables.

**16** And he said to those who were selling the doves, "Take these things out of here. Do not make my Father's house a house of trade."

**17** His disciples remembered that it is written, "Zeal for your house will consume me."

**18** So the Judeans answered him, "What sign do you show us, for doing these things?"

**19** Jesus answered them, "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up."

**20** The Judeans then said, "This temple was built over forty-six years, and will you raise it up in three days?"

**21** But he was speaking about the temple of his body.

**22** When he was raised from the dead, his disciples remembered that he had said this, and they believed \[G-PISTIS\] the scripture and the word that Jesus had spoken.

**23** While he was in Jerusalem at the Passover, during the festival, many believed \[G-PISTIS\] in his name when they saw the signs that he was doing.

**24** But Jesus did not entrust himself to them, because he knew everyone,

**25** and had no need for anyone to bear witness about a person, for he himself knew what was in a person.

### Chapter 3

**1** Now there was a man of the Pharisees, named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Judeans.

**2** This man came to him by night and said to him, "Rabbi, we know that you have come from God as a teacher, for no one can do these signs that you do unless God is with him."

**3** Jesus answered him, "Amen, amen, I tell you, unless someone is born from above, he cannot see the kingdom \[G-BASILEIA\] of God."

**4** Nicodemus says to him, "How can a person be born when he is old? Can he enter a second time into his mother's womb and be born?"

**5** Jesus answered, "Amen, amen, I tell you, unless someone is born of water and Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\], he cannot enter the kingdom of God.

**6** What is born of the flesh is flesh, and what is born of the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] is spirit.

**7** Do not be amazed that I said to you, 'You must be born from above.'

**8** The wind blows where it wishes, and you hear its sound, but you do not know where it comes from or where it goes. So it is with everyone who is born of the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\]."

**9** Nicodemus answered him, "How can these things be?"

**10** Jesus answered him, "You are the teacher of Israel, and you do not understand these things?

**11** Amen, amen, I tell you, we speak of what we know and bear witness to what we have seen, and you do not accept our witness.

**12** If I have told you earthly things and you do not believe \[G-PISTIS\], how will you believe if I tell you heavenly things?

**13** No one has gone up into heaven except the one who came down from heaven, the Son of Man \[G-HUIOS-ANTHROPOU\].

**14** And as Moses lifted up the snake in the wilderness, so the Son of Man must be lifted up,

**15** so that everyone who believes \[G-PISTIS\] in him may have eternal life \[G-ZOE\].

**16** For God loved \[G-AGAPE\] the world in this way: he gave his one-of-a-kind Son, so that everyone who believes \[G-PISTIS\] in him may not perish but have eternal life \[G-ZOE\].

**17** For God did not send the Son into the world to condemn the world, but so that the world might be saved \[G-SOZO\] through him.

**18** The one who believes in him is not condemned. The one who does not believe is condemned already, because he has not believed in the name of the one-of-a-kind Son of God.

**19** This is the judgement: the light has come into the world, and people loved \[G-AGAPE\] the darkness more than the light, because their deeds were evil.

**20** For everyone who does wicked things hates the light and does not come to the light, so that his deeds may not be exposed.

**21** But the one who does the truth \[G-ALETHEIA\] comes to the light, so that it may be shown that his deeds have been done in God."

**22** After this Jesus and his disciples went into the Judean countryside, and he stayed there with them and was baptising.

**23** John also was baptising at Aenon near Salim, because there was much water there, and people kept coming and being baptised

**24** (for John had not yet been thrown into prison).

**25** Now a dispute arose between some of John's disciples and a Judean over purification.

**26** And they came to John and said to him, "Rabbi, the one who was with you across the Jordan, to whom you bore witness, look, he is baptising, and everyone is going to him."

**27** John answered, "A person cannot receive anything unless it has been given him from heaven.

**28** You yourselves bear me witness that I said, 'I am not the Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\],' but, 'I have been sent ahead of him.'

**29** The one who has the bride is the bridegroom. The friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices greatly at the bridegroom's voice. So this joy of mine is now complete.

**30** He must increase, but I must decrease.

**31** The one who comes from above is above all. The one who is from the earth belongs to the earth and speaks from the earth. The one who comes from heaven is above all.

**32** He bears witness to what he has seen and heard, yet no one accepts his witness.

**33** Whoever has accepted his witness has set his seal that God is true.

**34** For the one whom God has sent speaks the words of God, for he gives the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] without measure.

**35** The Father loves \[G-AGAPE\] the Son and has given all things into his hand.

**36** Whoever believes \[G-PISTIS\] in the Son has eternal life \[G-ZOE\]. Whoever disobeys the Son will not see life, but the wrath of God stays on him."

*Review note: John 1 to 3 were drafted in WABV wording, from the Greek, using the fixed choices set out in the introduction to this book. They are the prologue (1:1-18), the witness of John to the Judeans and to Jesus (1:19-34), the first disciples (1:35-51), the wedding at Cana, the first sign (2:1-12), the cleansing of the temple (2:13-25), Nicodemus and the new birth (3:1-21), and the last witness of John (3:22-36). The numbering is the usual numbering. The G- markers used here for the first time are G-LOGOS (1:1, 1:14), G-DOXA (1:14, 2:11) and G-ALETHEIA (1:14, 3:21), and the others are as in the earlier books, with G-ZOE (1:4, 3:15-16, 3:36), G-PISTIS (1:7, 1:12, 2:11, 2:22-23, 3:12-18, 3:36), G-CHARIS (1:14), G-NOMOS (1:17), G-CHRISTOS (1:17, 1:20, 1:41, 3:28), G-PNEUMA (1:32-33, 3:5-8, 3:34), G-HUIOS-ANTHROPOU (1:51, 3:13-14), G-BASILEIA (3:3, 3:5), G-AGAPE (3:16, 3:19, 3:35) and G-SOZO (3:17). In 1:3-4 the sentence is divided as in the critical text, with "what has come into being" at the end of verse 3, and the footnote records that other divisions are found. In 1:5 "has not overpowered" translates *katelaben*, which may also be read "has not grasped" or "has not understood", and the footnote records the matter. In 1:14 "pitched his tent" translates *eskenosen*, and the footnote records the link with the tent of meeting. In 1:18 "the one-of-a-kind God" is the reading of the critical text (*monogenes theos*), and the Byzantine tradition has "the one-of-a-kind Son", and the footnote records the matter. In 1:28 "Bethany across the Jordan" is the reading of the critical text, and some manuscripts have "Bethabara". In 1:34 some manuscripts have "the chosen one of God" in place of "the Son of God". In 3:3 and 3:7 "from above" translates *anothen*, which may also be read "again" (as Nicodemus takes it in 3:4), and the footnote records the matter. In 3:5 "born of water and Spirit" is rendered as written, with no comment on how it is read. In 3:13 some manuscripts add "who is in heaven" after "the Son of Man", and the footnote records the matter. In 3:16-21 the words may be the words of Jesus or of the writer, and the WABV text sets them as speech, and the footnote records the matter. In 3:31-36 the words are given as the words of John, and the footnote records that some read them as the words of the writer. In 3:25 "a Judean" is the reading of the critical text, and some manuscripts have "Judeans". In 2:12 "his brothers" is literal. In 2:4 "Woman, what is that to you and to me?" is rendered as written. In 2:20 "forty-six years" is the Greek of the text. The word *Ioudaioi* is rendered "the Judeans" in 1:19, 2:6, 2:13, 2:18-20, 3:1 and 3:25, and the team should decide the rendering. There are no departures from the critical Greek text in chapters 1 to 3. The Greek is hard in some places, including 1:3-5, 1:9, 1:18, 2:4, 3:3, 3:8 (*pneuma*, "wind" and "spirit"), 3:13, 3:16-21 and 3:31-36. The footnotes record the main differences and the uncertain terms. The words on the Word and on God (1:1-18), the Lamb of God (1:29, 1:36), "destroy this temple" (2:19) and the new birth (3:3-8) are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. The section needs a check against the Greek by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked.*

## Part Two: Chapters 4 to 6, the woman at the well, the pool and the bread

### Chapter 4

**1** Now when the Lord learned that the Pharisees had heard that Jesus was making and baptising more disciples than John

**2** (though Jesus himself did not baptise, but his disciples did),

**3** he left Judea and went away again to Galilee.

**4** He had to go through Samaria.

**5** So he comes to a town in Samaria called Sychar, near the plot of ground that Jacob gave to his son Joseph.

**6** Jacob's well was there. So Jesus, tired from the journey, was sitting by the well just as he was. It was about the sixth hour.

**7** A woman of Samaria comes to draw water. Jesus says to her, "Give me a drink."

**8** (For his disciples had gone off into the town to buy food.)

**9** The Samaritan woman says to him, "How is it that you, a Judean, ask for a drink from me, a Samaritan woman?" (For Judeans do not use vessels together with Samaritans.)

**10** Jesus answered her, "If you knew the gift of God, and who it is that says to you, 'Give me a drink,' you would have asked him, and he would have given you living water."

**11** She says to him, "Sir, you have no bucket, and the well is deep. Where then do you get this living water?

**12** Are you greater than our father Jacob, who gave us the well and drank from it himself, along with his sons and his flocks?"

**13** Jesus answered her, "Everyone who drinks from this water will be thirsty again,

**14** but whoever drinks from the water that I will give him will never be thirsty. The water that I will give him will become in him a spring of water bubbling up into eternal life \[G-ZOE\]."

**15** The woman says to him, "Sir, give me this water, so that I will not be thirsty or have to come here to draw."

**16** He says to her, "Go, call your husband and come back."

**17** The woman answered him, "I have no husband." Jesus says to her, "You are right to say, 'I have no husband,'

**18** for you have had five husbands, and the one you have now is not your husband. What you have said is true."

**19** The woman says to him, "Sir, I see that you are a prophet.

**20** Our fathers worshipped on this mountain, and you people say that the place where one must worship is in Jerusalem."

**21** Jesus says to her, "Woman, believe \[G-PISTIS\] me, an hour is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem.

**22** You worship what you do not know. We worship what we know, for salvation \[G-SOZO\] is from the Judeans.

**23** But an hour is coming, and is now here, when the true worshippers will worship the Father in spirit \[G-PNEUMA\] and truth \[G-ALETHEIA\], for the Father looks for such people to worship him.

**24** God is spirit \[G-PNEUMA\], and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth \[G-ALETHEIA\]."

**25** The woman says to him, "I know that Messiah is coming (the one called Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\]). When he comes, he will tell us everything."

**26** Jesus says to her, "I am he, the one speaking to you."

**27** At that moment his disciples came. They were amazed that he was talking with a woman, but no one said, "What are you looking for?" or "Why are you talking with her?"

**28** So the woman left her water jar and went away into the town and says to the people,

**29** "Come, see a man who told me everything I have done. Could this be the Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\]?"

**30** They went out of the town and were coming to him.

**31** Meanwhile the disciples were urging him, saying, "Rabbi, eat."

**32** But he said to them, "I have food to eat that you do not know about."

**33** So the disciples said to one another, "Has anyone brought him something to eat?"

**34** Jesus says to them, "My food is to do the will of the one who sent me and to finish his work.

**35** Do you not say, 'Four months more, and then comes the harvest'? Look, I tell you, lift up your eyes and see the fields, that they are white for harvest.

**36** The one who reaps is already receiving wages and gathering fruit for eternal life \[G-ZOE\], so that sower and reaper may rejoice together.

**37** For in this the saying holds true: 'One sows and another reaps.'

**38** I sent you to reap what you did not labour for. Others have laboured, and you have entered into their labour."

**39** Many of the Samaritans from that town believed \[G-PISTIS\] in him because of the woman's witness: "He told me everything I have done."

**40** So when the Samaritans came to him, they asked him to stay with them, and he stayed there two days.

**41** And many more believed \[G-PISTIS\] because of his word.

**42** They said to the woman, "It is no longer because of what you said that we believe, for we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this is truly the Saviour of the world."

**43** After the two days he went from there to Galilee.

**44** (For Jesus himself testified that a prophet has no honour in his own country.)

**45** So when he came to Galilee, the Galileans welcomed him, since they had seen everything he did in Jerusalem at the festival, for they too had gone to the festival.

**46** So he came again to Cana in Galilee, where he had made the water wine. And there was a royal official whose son was ill in Capernaum.

**47** When he heard that Jesus had come from Judea to Galilee, he went to him and asked him to come down and heal his son, for he was about to die.

**48** So Jesus said to him, "Unless you people see signs and wonders, you will never believe \[G-PISTIS\]."

**49** The official says to him, "Sir, come down before my child dies."

**50** Jesus says to him, "Go. Your son lives." The man believed \[G-PISTIS\] the word that Jesus spoke to him and went.

**51** As he was going down, his slaves met him and told him that his boy was living.

**52** So he asked them the hour when he got better, and they said to him, "Yesterday at the seventh hour the fever left him."

**53** The father knew that it was the hour when Jesus had said to him, "Your son lives." And he himself believed \[G-PISTIS\], and his whole household.

**54** This was again the second sign that Jesus did when he had come from Judea to Galilee.

### Chapter 5

**1** After this there was a festival of the Judeans, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem.

**2** Now in Jerusalem, by the Sheep Gate, there is a pool, called in Aramaic Bethzatha, which has five porticoes.

**3** In these lay a large number of the sick, the blind, the lame and the paralysed.

**4** \[This verse is not in the critical Greek text. See the footnote.\]

**5** One man was there who had been ill for thirty-eight years.

**6** When Jesus saw him lying there and knew that he had already been there a long time, he says to him, "Do you want to get well?"

**7** The sick man answered him, "Sir, I have no one to put me into the pool when the water is stirred up, and while I am going, another steps down ahead of me."

**8** Jesus says to him, "Get up, pick up your mat and walk."

**9** At once the man was made well, and he picked up his mat and walked. Now it was the Sabbath \[G-SABBATON\] on that day.

**10** So the Judeans said to the man who had been cured, "It is the Sabbath, and it is not permitted for you to carry your mat."

**11** But he answered them, "The one who made me well said to me, 'Pick up your mat and walk.'"

**12** They asked him, "Who is the man who said to you, 'Pick it up and walk'?"

**13** But the man who was healed did not know who it was, for Jesus had slipped away, since there was a crowd in the place.

**14** After this Jesus finds him in the temple and said to him, "Look, you have been made well. Do not sin any more, so that nothing worse happens to you."

**15** The man went away and told the Judeans that it was Jesus who had made him well.

**16** And this was why the Judeans began to hound Jesus, because he was doing these things on the Sabbath \[G-SABBATON\].

**17** But he answered them, "My Father is working until now, and I am working."

**18** This was why the Judeans were seeking all the more to kill him: not only was he breaking the Sabbath, but he was also calling God his own Father, making himself equal to God.

## Part Seven: Chapters 19 to 21, the cross, the tomb and the shore

### Chapter 19

**1** Then Pilate took Jesus and had him flogged.

**2** The soldiers twisted a crown out of thorns and put it on his head, and dressed him in a purple robe,

**3** and kept coming up to him and saying, "Hail, King of the Judeans," and slapping him in the face.

**4** Pilate went out again and says to them, "Look, I am bringing him out to you, so that you may know that I find no guilt in him."

**5** So Jesus came out, wearing the crown of thorns and the purple robe. Pilate says to them, "Look, the man."

**6** When the chief priests and the officers saw him, they shouted, "Crucify, crucify." Pilate says to them, "Take him yourselves and crucify him, for I find no guilt in him."

**7** The Judeans answered him, "We have a law, and by that law he ought to die, because he made himself the Son of God."

**8** When Pilate heard this word, he was even more afraid.

**9** He went back into the praetorium and says to Jesus, "Where are you from?" But Jesus gave him no answer.

**10** So Pilate says to him, "Do you refuse to speak to me? Do you not know that I have authority to release you, and authority to crucify you?"

**11** Jesus answered him, "You would have no authority over me at all unless it had been given you from above. For this reason the one who handed me over to you has the greater sin."

**12** From then on Pilate was seeking to release him, but the Judeans shouted, "If you release this man, you are no friend of Caesar. Everyone who makes himself a king opposes Caesar."

**13** When Pilate heard these words, he brought Jesus out and sat down on the judge's seat at a place called the Stone Pavement, in Aramaic Gabbatha.

**14** Now it was the day of Preparation for the Passover. It was about the sixth hour. He says to the Judeans, "Look, your king."

**15** They shouted, "Away with him, away with him, crucify him." Pilate says to them, "Shall I crucify your king?" The chief priests answered, "We have no king but Caesar."

**16** So then he handed him over to them to be crucified.

**17** So they took Jesus, and carrying the cross by himself he went out to the place called the Place of a Skull, which in Aramaic is called Golgotha.

**18** There they crucified him, and with him two others, one on either side, and Jesus in the middle.

**19** Pilate also wrote a notice and put it on the cross. It read, "Jesus the Nazarene, the King of the Judeans."

**20** Many of the Judeans read this notice, because the place where Jesus was crucified was near the city, and it was written in Aramaic, in Latin and in Greek.

**21** So the chief priests of the Judeans said to Pilate, "Do not write, 'The King of the Judeans,' but, 'This man said, I am King of the Judeans.'"

**22** Pilate answered, "What I have written I have written."

**23** When the soldiers had crucified Jesus, they took his garments and made four parts, one for each soldier, and the tunic also. Now the tunic was seamless, woven in one piece from the top.

**24** So they said to one another, "Let us not tear it, but cast lots for it to see whose it will be." This was to fulfil the scripture: "They divided my garments among them, and for my clothing they cast lots." So the soldiers did these things.

**25** Standing near the cross of Jesus were his mother and his mother's sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene.

**26** When Jesus saw his mother and the disciple whom he loved \[G-AGAPE\] standing near, he says to his mother, "Woman, look, your son."

**27** Then he says to the disciple, "Look, your mother." And from that hour the disciple took her into his own home.

**28** After this, knowing that all was now finished, Jesus says, in order to fulfil the scripture, "I am thirsty."

**29** A jar full of sour wine stood there. So they put a sponge full of the sour wine on a branch of hyssop and held it to his mouth.

**30** When Jesus had received the sour wine, he said, "It is finished." And he bowed his head and handed over his spirit \[G-PNEUMA\].

**31** Since it was the day of Preparation, the Judeans did not want the bodies to remain on the cross on the Sabbath \[G-SABBATON\] (for that Sabbath was a great day), so they asked Pilate to have their legs broken and the bodies taken away.

**32** So the soldiers came and broke the legs of the first, and of the other who had been crucified with him.

**33** But when they came to Jesus and saw that he was already dead, they did not break his legs.

**34** But one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once blood and water came out.

**35** The one who saw it has borne witness, and his witness is true, and he knows that he is telling the truth, so that you also may believe \[G-PISTIS\].

**36** For these things took place so that the scripture might be fulfilled: "Not one of his bones will be broken."

**37** And again another scripture says, "They will look on the one whom they have pierced."

**38** After these things Joseph of Arimathea, who was a disciple of Jesus, but secretly for fear of the Judeans, asked Pilate to let him take away the body of Jesus. Pilate gave permission. So he came and took away his body.

**39** Nicodemus also, who had first come to him by night, came bringing a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about a hundred pounds in weight.

**40** So they took the body of Jesus and bound it in linen cloths with the spices, as is the burial custom of the Judeans.

**41** Now in the place where he was crucified there was a garden, and in the garden a new tomb in which no one had yet been laid.

**42** So because of the Judeans' day of Preparation, since the tomb was near, they laid Jesus there.

**1** Now on the first day of the week Mary Magdalene comes to the tomb early, while it was still dark, and sees that the stone has been taken away from the tomb.

**3** So Peter and the other disciple went out, and they were going to the tomb.

**4** The two were running together, but the other disciple ran ahead faster than Peter and reached the tomb first.

**5** And bending down to look in, he sees the linen cloths lying there, but he did not go in.

**6** Then Simon Peter came, following him, and went into the tomb. He sees the linen cloths lying there,

**8** Then the other disciple, who had reached the tomb first, also went in, and he saw and believed \[G-PISTIS\].

**9** For as yet they did not understand the scripture, that he must rise from the dead.

**13** They say to her, "Woman, why are you weeping?" She says to them, "They have taken away my Lord, and I do not know where they have laid him."

**15** Jesus says to her, "Woman, why are you weeping? Whom are you looking for?" Supposing him to be the gardener, she says to him, "Sir, if you have carried him away, tell me where you have laid him, and I will take him away."

**22** And after saying this he breathed on them and says to them, "Receive the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\].

**23** If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them. If you retain the sins of any, they are retained."

**24** Now Thomas, one of the twelve, called the Twin, was not with them when Jesus came.

**25** So the other disciples told him, "We have seen the Lord." But he said to them, "Unless I see in his hands the mark of the nails, and put my finger into the mark of the nails, and put my hand into his side, I will never believe \[G-PISTIS\]."

**27** Then he says to Thomas, "Put your finger here and see my hands, and put out your hand and put it into my side. Do not be unbelieving, but believing."

**29** Jesus says to him, "Have you believed \[G-PISTIS\] because you have seen me? Blessed \[G-MAKARIOS\] are those who have not seen and yet have believed."

### Chapter 21

**1** After these things Jesus showed himself again to the disciples at the Sea of Tiberias, and he showed himself in this way.

**2** Simon Peter, Thomas called the Twin, Nathanael of Cana in Galilee, the sons of Zebedee, and two others of his disciples were together.

**5** So Jesus says to them, "Children, do you have any fish?" They answered him, "No."

**7** That disciple whom Jesus loved \[G-AGAPE\] says to Peter, "It is the Lord." When Simon Peter heard that it was the Lord, he put on his outer garment (for he was stripped for work) and threw himself into the sea.

**8** But the other disciples came in the boat, dragging the net full of fish, for they were not far from the land, only about a hundred metres.

**9** When they got out on land, they saw a charcoal fire in place, with fish laid on it, and bread.

**10** Jesus says to them, "Bring some of the fish that you have just caught."

**11** So Simon Peter went aboard and hauled the net ashore, full of large fish, a hundred and fifty-three of them. And although there were so many, the net was not torn.

**12** Jesus says to them, "Come and have breakfast." Now none of the disciples dared to ask him, "Who are you?" They knew that it was the Lord.

**13** Jesus comes and takes the bread and gives it to them, and so with the fish.

**14** This was now the third time that Jesus showed himself to the disciples after he was raised from the dead.

**15** When they had finished breakfast, Jesus says to Simon Peter, "Simon, son of John, do you love \[G-AGAPE\] me more than these?" He says to him, "Yes, Lord, you know that I am fond of you." He says to him, "Feed my lambs."

**16** He says to him again a second time, "Simon, son of John, do you love \[G-AGAPE\] me?" He says to him, "Yes, Lord, you know that I am fond of you." He says to him, "Shepherd my sheep."

**17** He says to him the third time, "Simon, son of John, are you fond of me?" Peter was grieved because he said to him the third time, "Are you fond of me?" and he said to him, "Lord, you know everything. You know that I am fond of you." Jesus says to him, "Feed my sheep.

**18** Amen, amen, I tell you, when you were young, you used to dress yourself and walk where you wanted. But when you are old, you will stretch out your hands, and another will dress you and carry you where you do not want to go."

## Appendix A: Footnotes

- **1:1** "At the beginning" translates *en arche*, as in Genesis 1:1. "The Word" translates *logos*, which may also be read "word", "reason" or "message". See entry G-LOGOS. "In God's company" translates *pros ton theon*, literally "toward God", which may also be read "with God". "The Word was God" translates *theos en ho logos*, in which "God" has no article.
- **1:3** The sentence is divided here as in the critical text, with "what has come into being" at the end of verse 3. Some early writers join it to verse 4: "what has come into being in him was life".
- **1:5** "Has not overpowered" translates *katelaben*, which may also be read "has not grasped" or "has not understood".
- **1:9** "Shines on" translates *photizei*. The Greek may also be read "the true light, which shines on everyone, was coming into the world" with "coming" of the light, as here, or of everyone.
- **1:12** "Took him in" translates *elabon auton*, and "the right" translates *exousian*.
- **1:13** "Desire" translates *thelematos*.
- **1:14** "Pitched his tent" translates *eskenosen*, from the word for a tent, and it echoes the tent of meeting (see Exodus 25:8-9). "Glory" translates *doxan*, "one-of-a-kind" translates *monogenous*, which may also be read "only" or "unique", "grace" translates *charitos*, and "truth" translates *aletheias*. See entries G-DOXA, G-CHARIS and G-ALETHEIA.
- **1:15** "Has taken a place ahead of me" translates *emprosthen mou gegonen*.
- **1:16** "Grace on top of grace" translates *charin anti charitos*.
- **1:17** "The Law" translates *ho nomos*. See entry G-NOMOS.
- **1:18** "The one-of-a-kind God" translates *monogenes theos*, the reading of the critical text, and the Byzantine tradition has "the one-of-a-kind Son". "At the Father's breast" translates *eis ton kolpon tou patros*, literally "into the bosom of the Father", and "has told us" translates *exegesato*, which may also be read "has made known" or "has interpreted".
- **1:19** "The Judeans" translates *hoi Ioudaioi*. The rendering is open to the team's decision.
- **1:21** "The Prophet" is the prophet of Deuteronomy 18:15.
- **1:23** The words are from Isaiah 40:3.
- **1:28** "Bethany across the Jordan" is the reading of the critical text, and some manuscripts have "Bethabara".
- **1:29** "The Lamb of God" translates *ho amnos tou theou*. See Isaiah 53:7 and Exodus 12.
- **1:32** "Stayed" translates *emeinen*, from *menein*, which is rendered "stay" throughout John.
- **1:34** Some manuscripts have "the chosen one of God" in place of "the Son of God".
- **1:38** "Rabbi" is from the Hebrew, "my teacher", and "staying" translates *meneis*.

- **18:13** "Annas" had been high priest, and "Caiaphas" was high priest that year.
- **18:15** "Another disciple" is not named, and may be the disciple whom Jesus loved.
- **18:17** "The slave girl" translates *paidiske*.
- **18:20** "Synagogues" translates *sunagoge*, in the singular in the critical text, "in synagogue", and the sense is the plural.
- **18:24** Some manuscripts and versions place this verse after verse 13.
- **18:28** "The praetorium" translates *praitorion*, the governor's residence, and "the Passover" may mean the festival meal or the whole festival.
- **18:31** "Your own Law" translates *kata ton nomon humon*. See entry G-NOMOS.
- **18:36** "My kingdom" translates *he basileia he eme*, and "my servants" translates *hoi huperetai hoi emoi*. See entry G-BASILEIA.
- **18:37** "You say that I am a king" translates *su legeis hoti basileus eimi ego*, which may also be read "you say it, for I am a king".
- **18:38** "What is truth?" is rendered as written. See entry G-ALETHEIA.
- **18:40** "A robber" translates *lestes*.
- **19:1** "Flogged" translates *emastigosen*.
- **19:2** "A purple robe" translates *himation porphuroun*.
- **19:5** "Look, the man" translates *idou ho anthropos*, and in the Latin it is *ecce homo*.
- **19:7** See Leviticus 24:16.
- **19:11** "From above" translates *anothen*, and "the one who handed me over" may be Caiaphas or Judas.
- **19:13** "The Stone Pavement" translates *Lithostroton*, and "Gabbatha" is the Aramaic name.
- **19:14** "About the sixth hour" differs from Mark 15:25, which has "the third hour".
- **19:17** "The Place of a Skull" translates *Kraniou Topon*, and "Golgotha" is the Aramaic name.
- **19:19** "The Nazarene" translates *ho Nazoraios*.
- **19:23** "The tunic" translates *chiton*, and "seamless" translates *araphos*.
- **19:24** The words are from Psalm 22:18.
- **19:25** "Clopas" translates *Klopa*.
- **19:26** "Loved" translates *egapa*. See entry G-AGAPE. "Woman" translates *gunai*, as in 2:4.
- **19:28** "I am thirsty" translates *dipso*. See Psalm 69:21.
- **19:29** "Hyssop" translates *hussopo*, and some think the word was originally "a javelin" (*hussoi*).
- **19:30** "It is finished" translates *tetelestai*, and "handed over his spirit" translates *paredoken to pneuma*, which may also be read "gave up his breath". See entry G-PNEUMA.
- **19:31** "The Sabbath" translates *sabbatou*. See entry G-SABBATON.
- **19:34** "Blood and water" is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **19:35** "The one who saw it" is not named here, and 21:24 connects a witness with the beloved disciple.
- **19:36** The words are from Exodus 12:46, Numbers 9:12 or Psalm 34:20.
- **19:37** The words are from Zechariah 12:10.
- **19:39** "About a hundred pounds" translates *hos litras hekaton*, about thirty-three kilograms.
- **19:40** "Linen cloths" translates *othoniois*.
- **20:2** "The one whom Jesus loved" translates *hon ephilei ho Iesous*, from *phileo*, and it is not tagged G-AGAPE.
- **20:7** "The face cloth" translates *soudarion*.
- **20:8** "Believed" translates *episteusen*. See entry G-PISTIS.
- **20:16** "Rabboni" is the Aramaic for "my teacher".
- **20:17** "Do not hold on to me" translates *me mou haptou*, which may also be read "do not keep touching me". "My brothers" translates *tous adelphous mou*, and it is literal.
- **20:19** "Peace be with you" translates *eirene humin*. See entry G-EIRENE.
- **20:22** "Breathed on them" translates *enephusesen*, as in Genesis 2:7 and Ezekiel 37:9. See entry G-PNEUMA.
- **20:23** "Forgive" translates *aphete*, and "retain" translates *kratete*.
- **20:24** "The Twin" translates *Didumos*.
- **20:28** "My Lord and my God" is rendered as written, with no comment.
- **20:29** "Blessed" translates *makarioi*. See entry G-MAKARIOS.
- **20:31** "That you may believe" is the reading of the critical text in the present tense (*pisteuete*), and some manuscripts read the aorist (*pisteusete*). "The Christ" translates *ho christos*, and "life" translates *zoen*. See entries G-CHRISTOS and G-ZOE.
- **21:1** "The Sea of Tiberias" is as in 6:1.
- **21:2** "The sons of Zebedee" are not named elsewhere in John.
- **21:7** "His outer garment" translates *ependuten*.
- **21:8** "A hundred metres" translates *hos apo pechon diakosion*, about two hundred cubits.
- **21:9** "A charcoal fire" translates *anthrakian*.
- **21:11** "A hundred and fifty-three" is the Greek of the text.
- **21:15** "Do you love me" translates *agapas me*, and "I am fond of you" translates *philo se*. See entry G-AGAPE. Many readers hear no difference of sense in the change of verb. Some manuscripts differ in the noun "lambs" (*arnia*), and in verses 16 and 17.
- **21:16** "Shepherd" translates *poimaine*.
- **21:17** "Are you fond of me" translates *phileis me*.
- **21:18** "Stretch out your hands" translates *ekteneis tas cheiras sou*.
- **21:19** "Glorify" translates *doxasei*. See entry G-DOXA.
- **21:22** "Stay" translates *menein*, as elsewhere in John.
- **21:23** "The brothers" translates *tous adelphous*, and it is literal.
- **21:24** "We know" is in the first person plural, and the note is in all manuscripts.
- **21:25** "I suppose" translates *oimai*, in the first person singular.
