# WABV: Jude

*Word Alive Bible Version, draft 1. Words in square brackets, such as \[G-PISTIS\], point to entries in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. Footnotes and centre references are in the appendices at the end. Jude is the twenty-sixth book of the New Testament in the order that the WABV text follows, and the last of the seven general letters. It is written in Greek, and the WABV text is drafted from a critical Greek text, with the Byzantine tradition noted in the footnotes where it differs, as the Charter requires. It is a short letter of twenty-five verses from "Jude, a slave of Jesus Christ and brother of James" (verse 1), who may be Jude the brother of Jesus (Mark 6:3), to those who are called, and it urges them to contend for the faith that was handed down once for all and warns against people who have crept in, with examples from the judgements of old, and it ends with a call to build up, to pray, and to show mercy, and a doxology that is among the best known in the New Testament (verses 24-25). It shares most of its wording with 2 Peter 2 (compare verses 4-13 with 2 Peter 2:1-18), and the WABV text does not decide which letter used the other. It quotes the Jewish book of Enoch (verses 14-15, 1 Enoch 1:9) and refers to a tradition about Michael and the body of Moses (verse 9) that is probably from a book that is now lost, the Assumption of Moses, and the footnote records the matter. The WABV text of Jude is drafted with the voice standard (Part 46 of the review package). Where the text of a critical edition is not certain, the draft says so in the footnote and asks the team to confirm the reading. It makes a number of fixed choices that follow from its Greek, and they are written down here so that they are used throughout. *Hapax* (verses 3, 5) is rendered "once for all". *Doxai* (verse 8) is rendered "the glorious ones" and is tagged G-DOXA, and means heavenly beings, as in 2 Peter 2:10. *Agapai* (verse 12) is rendered "love feasts" and is not tagged G-AGAPE, because it means the shared meals. *Hagios* is rendered "holy" and is tagged G-HAGIOS. *Pistis* is rendered "faith" and is tagged G-PISTIS. *Agape* is rendered "love" and is tagged G-AGAPE. *Eleos* is rendered "mercy" and is tagged G-ELEOS. *Eirene* is rendered "peace" and is tagged G-EIRENE. *Soteria* is rendered "salvation" and is tagged G-SOZO. *Charis* is rendered "grace" and is tagged G-CHARIS. *Pneuma* is rendered "Spirit" or "spirit" and is tagged G-PNEUMA. *Zoe* is rendered "life" and is tagged G-ZOE. *Doxa* is rendered "glory" and is tagged G-DOXA. *Christos* is rendered "Christ" and is tagged G-CHRISTOS. The markers beginning with G- (for Greek words) are defined in the Biblical Concepts and Language System. The wording of the WABV text is drafted from the Greek and is not taken from any other English translation.*

## The letter, verses 1 to 25

**1** Jude, a slave of Jesus Christ \[G-CHRISTOS\] and brother of James, to those who are called, beloved \[G-AGAPE\] in God the Father and kept for Jesus Christ:

**2** may mercy \[G-ELEOS\], peace \[G-EIRENE\] and love \[G-AGAPE\] be multiplied to you.

**3** Beloved, although I was very eager to write to you about our common salvation \[G-SOZO\], I found it necessary to write appealing to you to contend for the faith \[G-PISTIS\] that was once for all delivered to the holy ones \[G-HAGIOS\].

**4** For certain people have crept in unnoticed who long ago were designated for this condemnation, ungodly people, who pervert the grace \[G-CHARIS\] of our God into sensuality and deny our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ.

**5** Now I want to remind you, although you once fully knew it, that Jesus, who saved a people out of the land of Egypt, afterward destroyed those who did not believe \[G-PISTIS\].

**6** And the angels who did not stay within their own position of authority, but left their proper dwelling, he has kept in eternal chains under gloomy darkness until the judgement of the great day,

**7** just as Sodom and Gomorrah and the surrounding cities, which likewise indulged in sexual immorality and pursued unnatural desire, serve as an example by undergoing a punishment of eternal fire.

**8** Yet in the same way these people also, relying on their dreams, defile the flesh, reject authority, and blaspheme the glorious ones \[G-DOXA\].

**9** But when the archangel Michael, contending with the devil, was disputing about the body of Moses, he did not presume to pronounce a blasphemous judgement, but said, "The Lord rebuke you."

**10** But these people blaspheme all that they do not understand, and by those things that they know by instinct, like unreasoning animals, they are destroyed.

**11** Woe to them. For they walked in the way of Cain and abandoned themselves for the sake of gain to Balaam's error and perished in Korah's rebellion.

**12** These are hidden reefs at your love feasts, as they feast with you without fear, shepherds feeding themselves, waterless clouds, swept along by winds, autumn trees without fruit, twice dead, uprooted,

**13** wild waves of the sea, casting up the foam of their own shame, wandering stars, for whom the gloom of utter darkness has been reserved for ever.

**14** It was also about these that Enoch, in the seventh generation from Adam, prophesied, saying, "Look, the Lord comes with ten thousands of his holy \[G-HAGIOS\] ones,

**15** to execute judgement on all and to convict all the ungodly of all their deeds of ungodliness that they have committed in such an ungodly way, and of all the harsh things that ungodly sinners have spoken against him."

**16** These are grumblers, malcontents, following their own sinful desires. Their mouths speak loud boasts, flattering people to gain advantage.

**17** But you must remember, beloved, the predictions of the apostles of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**18** They said to you, "In the last time there will be scoffers, following their own ungodly passions."

**19** It is these who cause divisions, worldly people, devoid of the Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\].

**20** But you, beloved, build yourselves up in your most holy \[G-HAGIOS\] faith \[G-PISTIS\], pray in the Holy Spirit \[G-PNEUMA\],

**21** keep yourselves in the love \[G-AGAPE\] of God, waiting for the mercy \[G-ELEOS\] of our Lord Jesus Christ that leads to eternal life \[G-ZOE\].

**22** And have mercy \[G-ELEOS\] on those who doubt.

**23** Save \[G-SOZO\] others by snatching them out of the fire. To others show mercy with fear, hating even the garment stained by the flesh.

**24** Now to him who is able to keep you from stumbling and to present you blameless before the presence of his glory \[G-DOXA\] with great joy,

**25** to the only God, our Saviour, through Jesus Christ our Lord, be glory, majesty, dominion, and authority, before all time and now and for ever. Amen.

*Review note: Jude was drafted in WABV wording, from the Greek, using the fixed choices set out in the introduction to this book, and with the WABV voice standard (Part 46 of the review package) applied. It has the greeting (verses 1-2), the call to contend for the faith (3-4), the examples of the judgements of old: the people who did not believe, the angels, and Sodom and Gomorrah (5-7), the character of the false teachers and the example of Michael (8-10), the woe and the images (11-13), the prophecy of Enoch (14-16), the call to remember the apostles (17-19), the call to build up, to pray and to show mercy (20-23) and the doxology (24-25). The numbering is the usual numbering. No new G- marker is used, and the markers are G-CHRISTOS (1), G-AGAPE (1-2, 21), G-ELEOS (2, 21-22), G-EIRENE (2), G-SOZO (3, 23), G-PISTIS (3, 5, 20), G-HAGIOS (3, 14, 20), G-CHARIS (4), G-DOXA (8, 24), G-PNEUMA (19-20) and G-ZOE (21). Several readings are drafted with some uncertainty and are to be confirmed against the critical edition in use, since the manuscripts of Jude differ more than most: verse 1 ("beloved" or "made holy"), verse 5 ("Jesus", or "the Lord", or "God", and where "once for all" belongs), verse 18 ("in the last time" or "at the last time"), verses 22-23 (the number of the clauses, two or three, differs in the manuscripts, and the draft follows the form with two groups) and verse 25 (the Byzantine adds "wise" before "God", and the manuscripts differ on "through Jesus Christ our Lord", "before all time" and "majesty"). The main points are these, and the footnotes carry the Greek and the sources: verse 5 the example of the people saved out of Egypt and destroyed is rendered as written; verse 6 "the angels who did not stay within their own position of authority" is rendered as written, and the footnote records the link with Genesis 6 and with the Jewish book of Enoch; verse 9 "the archangel Michael ... was disputing about the body of Moses" is rendered as written, and the footnote records that the story is not in the Old Testament and is probably from the Assumption of Moses, which is lost; verses 14 and 15 are a quotation of 1 Enoch 1:9, and the footnote records the matter; verse 12 "love feasts" translates *agapais* and is not tagged G-AGAPE; verse 8 "the glorious ones" is tagged G-DOXA and means heavenly beings. There are no departures from the critical Greek text in Jude. The Greek is hard in some places, including verses 1, 5, 9, 12, 22 and 23. The words on contending for the faith (3), the angels who left their dwelling (6), Sodom and Gomorrah (7), Michael and the body of Moses (9), the images of the false teachers (12-13), Enoch (14-15), the call to build up (20-21), mercy for those who doubt (22-23) and the doxology (24-25) are rendered as written, with no comment on how they are read. The section needs a check against the Greek by a scholar and an originality check before it is locked. With this the book of Jude is complete in first draft.*

## Appendix A: Footnotes

- **1** "Jude, a slave of Jesus Christ and brother of James" is rendered as written, and may be Jude the brother of Jesus (Mark 6:3). "Beloved in God the Father" is the reading that the draft follows (*ēgapemenois*), and some manuscripts read "made holy" (*hegiasmenois*), and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use. See entries G-CHRISTOS and G-AGAPE.
- **2** "May mercy, peace and love be multiplied to you" is rendered as written. See entries G-ELEOS and G-EIRENE.
- **3** "Our common salvation" is the reading that the draft follows, and some manuscripts read "our common salvation and life", and "the faith that was once for all delivered to the holy ones" is rendered as written, and "once for all" translates *hapax*. See entries G-SOZO, G-PISTIS and G-HAGIOS.
- **4** "Designated for this condemnation" translates *proegegrammenoi eis touto to krima*, and may be read "written about beforehand", and "deny our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ" may be read of one person or of God and Christ. See entry G-CHARIS.
- **5** "Jesus, who saved a people out of the land of Egypt" is the reading that the draft follows (*Iesous*), and the manuscripts differ widely ("the Lord", "God", "Christ"), and "once for all" is placed in some manuscripts before "knew" and in others before "saved", and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use. See entry G-PISTIS.
- **6** "The angels who did not stay within their own position of authority" is rendered as written, and is linked with Genesis 6:1-4 and the Jewish book of Enoch (1 Enoch 6 to 16), and "eternal chains under gloomy darkness" is as in 2 Peter 2:4.
- **7** "Sodom and Gomorrah ... pursued unnatural desire" translates *opiso sarkos heteras*, literally "after other flesh", and is rendered as written, and see Genesis 19.
- **8** "The glorious ones" translates *doxas*, and may mean angels or heavenly beings, and is as in 2 Peter 2:10. See entry G-DOXA.
- **9** "The archangel Michael ... was disputing about the body of Moses" is rendered as written, and the story is not in the Old Testament, and is probably from the Assumption of Moses, which is lost, and the words "The Lord rebuke you" are as in Zechariah 3:2.
- **11** "The way of Cain", "Balaam's error" and "Korah's rebellion" are rendered as written, and see Genesis 4, Numbers 22 to 24 and Numbers 16.
- **12** "Love feasts" translates *agapais*, and is not tagged G-AGAPE, because it means the shared meals, and some manuscripts read "deceptions" (*apatais*) as in 2 Peter 2:13, and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use.
- **13** "Wandering stars" is rendered as written, and see 1 Enoch 18 and 21.
- **14** "Enoch, in the seventh generation from Adam" is as in Genesis 5:18-24, and the words in verses 14 and 15 are a quotation of 1 Enoch 1:9, and "ten thousands of his holy ones" may mean angels or holy people. See entry G-HAGIOS.
- **16** "Grumblers, malcontents" is rendered as written.
- **17** "The predictions of the apostles of our Lord Jesus Christ" is rendered as written.
- **18** "In the last time there will be scoffers" is the reading that the draft follows, and the manuscripts differ in the form of "in the last time", and the words are not found word for word in a known text, and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use.
- **19** "Worldly people, devoid of the Spirit" translates *psuchikoi pneuma me echontes*, and "worldly" may be read "natural", as at 1 Corinthians 2:14, and it is not tagged G-PSYCHE. See entry G-PNEUMA.
- **20** "Build yourselves up in your most holy faith, pray in the Holy Spirit" is rendered as written. See entries G-HAGIOS, G-PISTIS and G-PNEUMA.
- **21** "Keep yourselves in the love of God, waiting for the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ that leads to eternal life" is rendered as written. See entries G-AGAPE, G-ELEOS and G-ZOE.
- **22** The verses 22 and 23 are among the hardest in the Greek text, and the manuscripts differ in the number of the groups (two or three) and in the verbs ("have mercy on those who doubt" or "convict those who doubt"), and the draft follows the form with two groups, and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use.
- **23** "Save others by snatching them out of the fire" and "hating even the garment stained by the flesh" are rendered as written. See entry G-SOZO.
- **24** "Able to keep you from stumbling and to present you blameless" is rendered as written. See entry G-DOXA.
- **25** "The only God, our Saviour, through Jesus Christ our Lord" is the reading that the draft follows, and the Byzantine tradition adds "wise" before "God", and the manuscripts differ on "through Jesus Christ our Lord" and on "before all time" and "majesty", and the team should confirm the reading against the critical edition in use.
